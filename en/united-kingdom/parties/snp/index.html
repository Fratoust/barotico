United Kingdom

Constitutional monarchy, parliamentary system · about 69 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.

Parliament dissolves automatically five years after it first met (9 July 2024), and polling day follows 25 working days later. The Prime Minister can ask the King for an earlier election at any time. Changing Prime Minister does not require an election: Andy Burnham took over from Keir Starmer in July 2026 without one. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Some kept, some stretched.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Never tested An independence referendum, with an SNP majority in May 2026 as the mandate (John Swinney's campaign line): it won 58 of 129 seats, short of that majority; untested source
  • Broken Cut emissions by 75% by 2030, a target its government presented as world-leading: dropped in April 2024 after the Climate Change Committee said it was no longer credible; net zero by 2045 stays in law source
  • Kept, made a difference The Scottish Child Payment for low-income families, in place since 2021: a University of Glasgow-led study (June 2026) finds material deprivation and food insecurity would be 8 to 9 points higher without it, with no fall in employment source · measured effect
  • Partly kept No one waiting more than a year for treatment by March 2026 (Scottish Government pledge): missed; at the deadline 16,090 outpatient and 17,882 inpatient or day-case waits were over a year, despite 'significant progress' (Public Health Scotland) source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the SNP, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its government called its 2030 climate target world-leading, then dropped it in April 2024, citing budgets and the limits of devolution. The Climate Change Committee pointed to 8 missed annual targets in 12 years and a delayed climate plan.

No one, it pledged, would wait more than a year for treatment by March 2026. The pledge was missed.

Independence is still waiting. In 2022 the Supreme Court ruled that Holyrood cannot hold a referendum on its own, and in May 2026 the SNP won 58 of 129 seats, short of the majority it called a mandate.

To be fair, its Scottish Child Payment works: without it, material deprivation and food insecurity would be 8 to 9 points higher (University of Glasgow-led study, 2026).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Seats are those of the House of Commons 'state of the parties' page, consulted on 15 November 2026; the page carries no date. It lists 13 independents and 3 vacant seats without naming them, and this update could not identify the vacancies. Nine cards are shown, by seats, under the seats-or-3% rule and the nine-card cap. The SDLP (2 MPs), Alliance, the UUP, the TUV, Your Party and Restore Britain (1 each) have no card and appear only in the seat chart, where their place is indicative. 'Greens' means the Green Party of England and Wales; the Scottish Greens are a separate party.

Left-right positions are an expert-consensus estimate, since no Chapel Hill Expert Survey page for the UK was opened; Reform UK is labelled far right by the position rule. Party finance uses the Electoral Commission's 2024 accounts: totals only, as no breakdown by source is published, and none for Labour, which filed late. Programme links are the 2024 general election manifestos, or the 2026 Holyrood and Senedd manifestos for the SNP and Plaid Cymru; the DUP's is an archive copy held by Ulster University.

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel; PA dispatches republished by regional titles with a visible credit count as PA. Claims reported only elsewhere were rewritten from official sources or dropped. Cases are listed only with an official act; allegations, private conduct and spent convictions are left out. The homicide rate covers England and Wales only.
