Egypt

Presidential republic under the 2014 Constitution (amended 2019) · about 109.5 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 37 monthsNext national vote: presidential election, due before President Sisi's term ends in 2030 (no date set yet)
Abdel Fattah al-Sisi's third term runs to 2030. The 2019 amendments lengthened terms to six years and reset his count, and the Constitution allows two terms, so he cannot run again unless it is amended. On 14 October 2026, after pro-government voices called for a fourth term, he said: 'No one can touch' the Constitution. The 2023 vote was held in December for a term starting in April, so the countdown uses December 2029 as an estimate. The House (term 2026-2031) and the Senate are next due in 2030. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. The number of cases partly reflects a party's size and years in power.

Programme links are left empty: we could not open a current national programme on any party's own site. Stated values come from founding statements, congresses, campaign events and parliamentary positions reported by the Egyptian press. All nine parties ran candidates on the same National List in 2025, so 'government' and 'opposition' here describe how each party presents itself, not two competing camps at the ballot box.

On the posterSocial protection for the poorest households
Half-wayThe House it leads passed the Social Security Law, published on 3 April 2025 as Law 12 of 2025: the Takaful and Karama fund replaces the 2010 social security fund, and current beneficiaries keep their benefits while their files are aligned within a year. We found no independent measurement of its effect yet. source
On the posterDo not displace the elderly in the old-rent reform (its bloc's demand, 2025)
Half-wayOn 2 July 2025 the House passed the government's old-rent law: frozen leases end after 7 years (5 for non-residential units), rents rise 10 to 20 times and then 15% a year, and tenants who agree to leave get priority for state housing. Majority leader Abdelhadi El-Qasabi thanked the government for answering the bloc's demand on the elderly; the majority rejected an amendment exempting original tenants and their families. CAPMAS: actual rents up 27.7% year on year in October 2026. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterA new Criminal Procedure Code with stronger guarantees for the accused (passed by its majority)
Half-wayIts majority passed the code on 29 April 2025. On 21 September 2025 the President sent 8 articles back, asking for more alternatives to pretrial detention, guarantees for the inviolability of homes and the rights of the accused, and clearer wording; the House rewrote them and the code was ratified in November 2025. The Egyptian Social Democratic bloc was the only one to reject the whole text. Amnesty International says the final code still falls short of international law. source · 2 · 3
On the posterBring young people and women into politics
Half-wayIn the 2025 House election it put 61 women among its 120 main National List candidates, the 50% the law requires for lists, and 1 woman among its 125 individual-seat candidates. The Egyptian Center for Women's Rights notes that the list met the quota without exceeding it. source
On the posterPrice stability and average growth above 5% (government programme it backs, 2024-2027)
Half-wayCAPMAS: annual inflation fell from 27.4% in May 2024 to 12% in October 2026. The IMF forecasts growth of 4.6% in 2025-26 and 4.4% in 2026-27, below the 5% target. source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • Its majority passed the new Criminal Procedure Code; on 21 September 2025 the President sent it back, objecting among other things that article 112 allowed detention 'without a maximum period or a reasoned judicial order'. source
  • Led the National List, which ran unopposed for the House list seats in 2025; in the individual races, after a presidential statement asking for results that reflect the voters' will, 19 districts were voided by the electoral authority and 29 by the Supreme Administrative Court. source
  • Promises social protection; on 2 July 2025 the House it dominates passed the old-rent law ending frozen leases after 7 years, as 24 opposition and independent MPs walked out. source
To be fair
  • The revised Criminal Procedure Code, ratified in November 2025, adds alternatives to pretrial detention such as travel bans and electronic tracking (Amnesty)
  • Annual inflation down from 27.4% in May 2024 to 12% in October 2026 under the government it backs (CAPMAS)
  • Unemployment at 5.8% in the second quarter of 2026 (CAPMAS)
