Poland

Parliamentary republic with a directly elected president · EU member since 2004

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 11 monthsNext Sejm and Senate election: autumn 2027, on a Sunday chosen by the President
The Sejm elected on 15 October 2023 sits for four years from its first sitting in November 2023. Under Article 98 of the Constitution the President orders the election at least 90 days before the term ends, for a non-working day within the last 30 days of the term, so between mid-October and early November 2027. The date shown is our estimate. The President who will set it, Karol Nawrocki, was backed by the opposition PiS. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Poland

Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: 39% of Poles trust most news most of the time, down 8 points in a year; TVN leads television news, and Kanał Zero is the most visible digital challenger. CBOS (June 2026, 991 people): television is the main news source for 59% and news websites for 50%; Polsat and Polsat News are the most credible TV brand (32% credible, 17% not), TVN and TVN24 are split (28% against 30%), public TV news is trusted by 18% and distrusted by 35%, TV Republika by 11% against 31%. source

Recommended for factual news

Polsat News

Private news channel of the Polsat group; the most credible TV news brand in CBOS's June 2026 survey (32% credible, 17% not), trusted by 51% of those who know it in the Digital News Report 2023, the latest brand figures we could open.

Added on 15 November 2026.

Video explainers

Kanał Zero

YouTube channel run by Krzysztof Stanowski (himself a 2025 presidential candidate), with long interviews of politicians from every camp; the most visible digital challenger to established media (Digital News Report 2026) and rated slightly more positively than negatively in CBOS's June 2026 survey.

source

Who owns what

  • State: public broadcasters TVP and Polskie Radio (in liquidation since December 2023, PLN 1.2 billion of budget money in the first half of 2026) and the news agency PAP
  • State-controlled energy group Orlen: majority of the Polska Press network of local papers, bought under the previous government
  • Warner Bros. Discovery (US): TVN and TVN24; RSF warns that a takeover by Paramount Skydance could put TVN24's independence at risk
  • Private Polish groups: Polsat (Polsat, Polsat News); the government designated TVN and Polsat companies of strategic importance to shield them from hostile takeovers

source

Documented facts to know

  • RSF 2026: Poland 27th of 180 (75.52), up from 31st. RSF says public media served as propaganda tools for the former majority in 2015-2023 and are struggling to rebuild credibility, and that verbal attacks and SLAPPs against private outlets have decreased since 2024. source
  • National Broadcasting Council KRRiT (July 2026): calls the public media liquidation a sham; it refused the 2025 public-mission reports of TVP and Polskie Radio and transferred PLN 241.3 million of licence fees to public broadcasters in the first half of 2026. source
  • CBOS (June 2026): credibility balance of +15 points for Polsat, -2 for TVN, -17 for public TV news and -20 for TV Republika. source
  • Freedom House 2026: Poland scores 3 of 4 for free and independent media and 2 of 4 for judicial independence. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Seats by club are the Polsat News count of 29 July 2026: the Sejm's club page and the government's cabinet page could not be read for this update, so later moves of single MPs are not shown, and the four seats that count leaves unassigned appear as such in the seat chart. Nine groups are shown, by seats; Konfederacja Korony Polskiej (three MPs) is left out under the nine-card cap.

Left-right positions are our estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey 2024, which could not be opened; Rozwój Plus and Centrum are too new to be in it. PiS and Razem are labelled far right and far left because The PopuList classifies them so. Rozwój Plus is an association and Centrum a club: neither has a programme or audited accounts yet, and party finance is broken down only where we opened the PKW resolution (PiS, PSL).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the news agency PAP, the EFCSN fact-checker Demagog and the most trusted broadcasters (Polsat News, RMF FM, TVN24). The 2026 Reuters brand-trust table could not be read, so its 2023 figures are used. Claims that only other outlets reported were rewritten from official sources or dropped, among them a count of presidential vetoes, a newspaper's tally of the '100 concretes' and one 2025 first-instance conviction.

No independent promise tally could be opened, so the tracker shows none. The homicide rate comes from a data site republishing UNODC figures. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, indictment, ruling, immunity vote); allegations alone are left out.
