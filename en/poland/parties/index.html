Poland

Parliamentary republic with a directly elected president · EU member since 2004

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 11 monthsNext Sejm and Senate election: autumn 2027, on a Sunday chosen by the President
The Sejm elected on 15 October 2023 sits for four years from its first sitting in November 2023. Under Article 98 of the Constitution the President orders the election at least 90 days before the term ends, for a non-working day within the last 30 days of the term, so between mid-October and early November 2027. The date shown is our estimate. The President who will set it, Karol Nawrocki, was backed by the opposition PiS. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterGenerous family benefits: 800+ (2023 campaign)
DeliveredIts government raised the child benefit from PLN 500 to 800 a month from January 2024. Extreme poverty fell from 6.6% of people in 2023 to 5.2% in 2024, as household incomes rose (GUS), then edged up to 5.3% in 2025. source · 2
On the posterHelp victims of crime through the Justice Fund
ContradictionIn 2017 its justice ministry moved PLN 25 million from the fund for victims of crime to the anti-corruption bureau CBA, which bought Pegasus spyware. In October 2025 prosecutors indicted the deputy minister who signed it, now PiS MP Michał Woś, saying he knew the CBA did not qualify for such funding. Woś says the CBA could legally be funded from outside the state budget and calls the case political repression; the indictment rests on exactly that point, and a court has yet to rule. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRepair the courts: a 'good change' in the judiciary
⚠ Rights alertIts laws let the Sejm choose the judges on the judicial council KRS and created a Disciplinary Chamber at the Supreme Court. On 22 July 2021 the European Court of Human Rights (Reczkowicz v. Poland) found that the Disciplinary Chamber was not a tribunal established by law, in breach of the right to a fair trial (Art. 6). The European Commission had opened its Article 7 rule-of-law procedure in December 2017, and in December 2021 it started infringement proceedings over serious concerns about the Constitutional Tribunal and its case law. source · 2 · 3
On the posterProtect unborn life
⚠ Rights alertOn 22 October 2020, on a motion signed by 119 MPs, about 106 of them from PiS, the Constitutional Tribunal struck out the ground allowing abortion for severe foetal defects. On 14 December 2023 the ECtHR (M.L. v. Poland) found a breach of the right to respect for private and family life (Art. 8) of a woman who had to travel abroad, noting that the Tribunal panel included judges appointed with serious irregularities; Poland was ordered to pay her €16,004. source · 2 · 3
On the posterProtected borders
⚠ Rights alertIn 2016-2017, under its government, border guards at the Terespol crossing turned back Chechen families dozens of times without registering their asylum requests, sending them back to Belarus. On 23 July 2020 the ECtHR (M.K. and Others v. Poland) found breaches of the ban on inhuman treatment (Art. 3), the ban on collective expulsion and the right to an effective remedy, and that Poland had ignored the Court's interim measures. The authorities had argued that the applicants were seeking work, not protection. source · 2 · 3
