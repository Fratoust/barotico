Voting in Poland

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Citizens aged 18 and over are registered automatically at their permanent address. To vote elsewhere in Poland you can ask to be added to the register where you are staying (also online, via gov.pl) or obtain a certificate to vote at any polling station. Poles abroad register with a consulate before each election. Voters with disabilities can vote by post or by proxy.

Last turnout: 74.38% in the October 2023 Sejm election, the highest since 1989; 71.63% in the June 2025 presidential run-off source

The electoral system

  • Voting is voluntary from 18. The Sejm is elected by proportional representation (D'Hondt) in 41 constituencies, with a 5% threshold for parties and 8% for coalitions; national-minority lists are exempt.
  • The 100 senators are elected by first-past-the-post in single-member districts.
  • The President is elected in two rounds; in 2025 Nawrocki won the run-off by 369,591 votes, with 71.63% turnout.

Sources: PKW: official results of the 2023 Sejm election (41 constituencies) · Constitution, art. 98 and chapter IV · PKW: 2025 presidential election · PKW: first-round results 2025

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Poland, there is no blank vote: a ballot without any cross is an invalid vote, counted with the others.

What it is

  • Invalid vote: no cross next to any candidate, crosses next to candidates of different lists, or a cross only for a list whose registration was cancelled. source

How it is counted

  • The electoral commission publishes invalid votes by reason; they go to no list. source
  • A national referendum is binding only if more than half of the voters take part. source

The figures

  • 1.69%of votes were invalid in the October 2023 Sejm election (370,217), including 144,870 ballots with no cross at all, with a turnout of 74.38%. source
  • 40.91%turnout in the referendum held the same day: below 50%, so the result was not binding. source

What it changes for the result

  • In 2023 the same voters received a Sejm ballot and a referendum ballot: in a referendum with a turnout quorum, not taking part and taking part do not have the same effect. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the news agency PAP, the EFCSN fact-checker Demagog and the most trusted broadcasters (Polsat News, RMF FM, TVN24). No independent promise tally could be opened, so the tracker shows none. The homicide rate comes from a data site republishing UNODC figures. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, indictment, ruling, immunity vote); allegations alone are left out.
