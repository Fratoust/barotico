Poland

Parliamentary republic with a directly elected president · EU member since 2004

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 11 monthsNext Sejm and Senate election: autumn 2027, on a Sunday chosen by the President
The Sejm elected on 15 October 2023 sits for four years from its first sitting in November 2023. Under Article 98 of the Constitution the President orders the election at least 90 days before the term ends, for a non-working day within the last 30 days of the term, so between mid-October and early November 2027. The date shown is our estimate. The President who will set it, Karol Nawrocki, was backed by the opposition PiS. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who governs in Poland?

Who's at the wheel

Prime Minister Donald Tusk (KO) since December 2023, governing with PSL, Polska 2050 and Lewica; the Centrum club, formed from a split of Polska 2050 in February 2026, keeps the climate ministry. President Karol Nawrocki (backed by PiS) since 6 August 2025.

In October 2023 PiS came first (35.38%, 194 seats) but lost its majority; KO (157 seats), Trzecia Droga (65) and Lewica (26) together won 248 of 460 seats, with 74.38% turnout. After the 2024 Razem exit and the 2026 splits, the coalition clubs total about 239 MPs (Polsat News count, July 2026).

Sources: Gov.pl: composition of the Council of Ministers · Coalition agreement, 10 November 2023 (RMF FM) · PAP: Polska 2050 after the split, Centrum keeps the climate ministry · PKW: official results of the 2023 Sejm election · Polsat News: seats by club (29 July 2026) · CBOS: attitudes to the government, June 2026

Who holds power

President: Karol Nawrocki (backed by PiS, in office since 6 August 2025). Prime Minister: Donald Tusk (KO, since December 2023).

In government: 239 absolute majority: 231

The Council of Ministers led by Donald Tusk rests on KO, PSL, Lewica and Polska 2050; the Centrum club, formed in February 2026 by MPs who left Polska 2050, kept the climate ministry (PAP). The official composition page could not be read for this update, so no count of ministers by party is shown.

Seats: Polsat News count of 29 July 2026, after Rozwój Plus left PiS; the Sejm's own club page (sejm.gov.pl) could not be read for this update, so later moves of single MPs are not shown. The groups listed add up to 456; the 4 remaining seats (460 minus the groups) are not assigned in that count and are shown separately.

Centrum is counted with the government because it kept the climate ministry, although it has not signed the 2023 coalition agreement. Konfederacja Korony Polskiej and the unaffiliated MPs have no card; their place in the chart is indicative. A veto override needs three-fifths of the votes cast: 276 if all 460 MPs vote.

What the government promised

  • Coalition agreement of KO, Trzecia Droga (PSL and Polska 2050) and Lewica, signed 10 November 2023 (full text published by RMF FM)
  • KO's '100 concretes for the first 100 days' (2023 campaign), listed by Demagog

What independent promise trackers found

  • Demagog, an EFCSN-verified fact-checker, reviews the '100 concretes' promise by promise; its two-year review (December 2025) and live tracker could not be opened for this update, so no figure is shown. We found no other independent tally in a validated source. source
  • Not independent: on 19 August 2026 Donald Tusk said the PLN 60,000 tax-free allowance, one of the 100 concretes, was 'hardly realistic' in 2027 and 2028 because of the defence effort, while 'not giving it up' (Polsat News). source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Statistics Poland (GUS): real GDP grew 3.6% in 2025; consumer prices were 2.2% higher year on year in January 2026; registered unemployment was 6.0%. source
  • GUS (October 2026 notification): general government deficit of 7.2% of GDP in 2025 (PLN 283.6 billion); public debt at 59.6% of GDP. source
  • Energy regulator (URE, December 2025): household electricity averages PLN 495.16/MWh net in 2026, about 1% below the frozen 2025 price; with distribution charges, a typical bill rises about 3% a month. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Seats by club are the Polsat News count of 29 July 2026: the Sejm's club page and the government's cabinet page could not be read for this update, so later moves of single MPs are not shown, and the four seats that count leaves unassigned appear as such in the seat chart. Nine groups are shown, by seats; Konfederacja Korony Polskiej (three MPs) is left out under the nine-card cap.

Left-right positions are our estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey 2024, which could not be opened; Rozwój Plus and Centrum are too new to be in it. PiS and Razem are labelled far right and far left because The PopuList classifies them so. Rozwój Plus is an association and Centrum a club: neither has a programme or audited accounts yet, and party finance is broken down only where we opened the PKW resolution (PiS, PSL).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the news agency PAP, the EFCSN fact-checker Demagog and the most trusted broadcasters (Polsat News, RMF FM, TVN24). The 2026 Reuters brand-trust table could not be read, so its 2023 figures are used. Claims that only other outlets reported were rewritten from official sources or dropped, among them a count of presidential vetoes, a newspaper's tally of the '100 concretes' and one 2025 first-instance conviction.

No independent promise tally could be opened, so the tracker shows none. The homicide rate comes from a data site republishing UNODC figures. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, indictment, ruling, immunity vote); allegations alone are left out.
