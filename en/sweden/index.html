Sweden

Parliamentary constitutional monarchy · 10.6 million people

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Next vote1402 daysNext general election: 8 September 2030 (an extra election could come first)
Ordinary elections to the Riksdag, the 21 regions and the 290 municipalities are held together on the second Sunday of September every four years. If the Riksdag rejects the Speaker's candidate for prime minister four times, an extra election must be held; in Sweden it does not change the date of the next ordinary election. Three of the four attempts remained at the end of October 2026, the next vote being set for 3 November. source

Reading codes

  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

248 MPs voted against Magdalena Andersson as prime minister on 15 October 2026, including the three parties she had hoped to govern with.

Sweden uses 'negative parliamentarism': a candidate becomes prime minister unless 175 of the 349 MPs vote no. Andersson won the 99 votes of her own party; the Left, the Greens and the Centre joined the four Tidö parties in voting no. The Speaker has four attempts before an extra election.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Residents are on the electoral roll automatically and receive a voting card by post. Early voting runs anywhere in Sweden for about two and a half weeks before election day (26 August to 13 September in 2026), with ID. A messenger can carry your vote if you cannot reach a polling station. Swedes abroad vote by post or at embassies and consulates that accept votes.

Last turnout: 84.9% in the September 2026 general election (6,834,413 voters), up from 84.2% in 2022 source

Who decides what

European Union21 Swedish MEPs elected every five years (next in 2029); EU law applies directly.
NationalThe King is a ceremonial head of state with no political role. The Riksdag (one chamber, 349 MPs) elects the prime minister, who appoints the government.
Regions (21)Elected regional councils run healthcare and regional public transport, funded largely by their own income tax.
Municipalities (290)Elected councils run schools, elderly care, social services and planning, and set the largest part of each person's income tax.

How people vote

  • Citizens aged 18+ vote on the same day for the Riksdag, their region and their municipality, using separate ballot papers.
  • Proportional representation: a party needs 4% nationally (or 12% in one constituency) to win seats; 39 adjustment seats make the result proportional nationwide.
  • Voters can mark one candidate on the party list; a candidate with personal votes from at least 5% of the party's voters in the constituency moves to the top.

How a law is passed

  1. The government drafts a bill after an inquiry and a public consultation (remiss); the Council on Legislation (Lagrådet) reviews important bills.
  2. A Riksdag committee prepares a report; MPs and parties can table motions and amendments.
  3. The chamber votes by simple majority; there is no second chamber and no presidential veto.
  4. The government promulgates the law in the Swedish Code of Statutes (SFS). Constitutional changes need two identical votes with an election in between.

Why it's complicated

The 13 September 2026 election left the opposition S, V, C and MP with 176 seats and the Tidö parties with 173, but the four opposition parties do not agree on a government: V will vote no to any government it is not part of, and the Centre leader found very weak support for V joining a government. Andersson lost a vote on 15 October (248 no). C's attempt at a centre government found no majority. Ulf Kristersson leads a caretaker government, and the Speaker has three attempts left before an extra election, the next on 3 November.

Why it's like thisSweden's 1974 Instrument of Government made the Riksdag a single chamber and kept the King out of politics. Its 'negative parliamentarism' lets a prime minister take office unless a majority votes against, which is why minority governments are common, from the Social Democrats' long reign to the Tidö arrangement. Since the 1990s politics has been organised in two blocs, and the rise of the Sweden Democrats has made majorities harder to build.

Who's at the wheel

Caretaker government of Ulf Kristersson (M), with KD and L, since 17 September 2026: the prime minister resigned after the election and ministers stay on for ongoing business until a new prime minister is elected. From October 2022 to September 2026 it governed with the Sweden Democrats' support under the Tidö agreement.

Election result confirmed on 19 September 2026: opposition 176 seats, Tidö parties 173. On 15 October the Riksdag rejected Magdalena Andersson (S), 99 yes and 248 no. On 26 October the Centre leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist reported that there is no majority for a centre government. The Speaker then put her forward for a vote set for 3 November; if it fails, the task is expected to pass to Ulf Kristersson.
