Sweden

Parliamentary constitutional monarchy · 10.6 million people

Next vote1392 daysNext general election: 8 September 2030 (an extra election could come first)
Ordinary elections to the Riksdag, the 21 regions and the 290 municipalities are held together on the second Sunday of September every four years. If the Riksdag rejects the Speaker's candidate for prime minister four times, an extra election must be held; in Sweden it does not change the date of the next ordinary election. By mid-November 2026 two of the four attempts had failed (15 October and 3 November); after a fourth failure, the extra election must be held within three months. source

The systemThe governmentThe partiesVotingKey figuresWatch listMedia

248 MPs voted against Magdalena Andersson as prime minister on 15 October 2026, including the three parties she had hoped to govern with.

Sweden uses 'negative parliamentarism': a candidate becomes prime minister unless 175 of the 349 MPs vote no. Andersson won the 99 votes of her own party; the Left, the Greens and the Centre joined the four Tidö parties in voting no. The Speaker has four attempts before an extra election.

Sources: Riksdag: vote on Magdalena Andersson, 15 October 2026 (99 yes, 248 no, by party) · TT (via Dagens PS): Andersson's single-party proposal and V's reasons

Who decides what

European UnionThe EU is a level of its own on top of the three Swedish ones; Sweden elects members of the European Parliament (next election in 2029).
NationalThe King is head of state with no political power. The Riksdag (349 MPs) passes laws; the Speaker proposes a prime minister, who takes office unless more than half of the MPs vote no, and then appoints the government.
Regions (21)Elected regional councils run healthcare (about 90% of their spending) and, with the municipalities, public transport; they have their own power to tax.
Municipalities (290)Elected councils run most local public services: preschools, schools, social services and elderly care.

Sources: Government Offices: the three levels, 290 municipalities and 21 regions · Government Offices: the regions' tasks and taxing power · Riksdag: fundamental laws and the head of state · Riksdag: how a prime-minister proposal is approved or rejected

How a law is passed

  1. The government usually appoints an inquiry, sends its report out for consultation (remiss), and most draft bills then go to the Council on Legislation (Lagrådet) for a legal check.
  2. The government tables a bill (proposition); MPs can table counter-proposals (motions), and a Riksdag committee prepares a report (betänkande).
  3. The single chamber decides, in most cases by simple majority. The King has no political power, so there is no veto.
  4. The government publishes the law in the Swedish Code of Statutes (SFS). Constitutional changes need two identical votes with an election in between: on 11 November 2026 the new Riksdag gave its second approval to amendments on abortion, citizenship and freedom of association.

Sources: Riksdag: how laws are made · Riksdag: amending the fundamental laws · Riksdag: constitutional amendments approved, 11 November 2026

Political and economic context

The 13 September 2026 election gave S, V, C and MP 176 seats and the four Tidö parties 173, but the four do not agree on a government: V says it will vote no to any government it is not part of. Two prime-minister votes have failed: Magdalena Andersson on 15 October (99 yes, 248 no) and the Centre leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist on 3 November (25 yes, 324 no). Ulf Kristersson leads a caretaker government and has been exploring a new majority since 3 November; on 13 November C received rival policy packages from him and from Andersson. Two attempts remain before an extra election.

Sources: Riksdag: election 2026, seats and government formation · Riksdag: vote of 15 October 2026 · Riksdag: vote of 3 November 2026 · Riksdag: Kristersson's exploratory task (3 November 2026) · SVT: V's condition on ministerial posts · SVT live coverage: C receives both policy packages (13 November 2026)

Why it's like thisSweden's 1974 Instrument of Government left the King without political power and kept parliamentarism, first written into the constitution in 1969. A prime minister takes office unless more than half of the MPs vote no, which allows minority governments such as Magdalena Andersson's single-party cabinet of 2021 or the Tidö arrangement of 2022. Majorities have become harder to build: in 2018 the Riksdag rejected Ulf Kristersson before a government could be formed, in 2021 Andersson had to be elected twice in a week, and in 2026 two candidates were rejected.
