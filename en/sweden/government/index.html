Sweden

Parliamentary constitutional monarchy · 10.6 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote1392 daysNext general election: 8 September 2030 (an extra election could come first)
Ordinary elections to the Riksdag, the 21 regions and the 290 municipalities are held together on the second Sunday of September every four years. If the Riksdag rejects the Speaker's candidate for prime minister four times, an extra election must be held; in Sweden it does not change the date of the next ordinary election. By mid-November 2026 two of the four attempts had failed (15 October and 3 November); after a fourth failure, the extra election must be held within three months. source

Who governs in Sweden?

Who's at the wheel

Caretaker government of Ulf Kristersson (M), with KD and L, since 17 September 2026: the prime minister resigned after the election and ministers stay on for ongoing business until a new prime minister is approved; two candidates have been rejected since (15 October and 3 November). From October 2022 to September 2026 it governed with the Sweden Democrats' support under the Tidö agreement.

Final result confirmed on 19 September 2026: S, V, C and MP 176 seats, the Tidö parties 173. On 15 October the Riksdag rejected Magdalena Andersson (S), who proposed a Social Democratic government alone: 99 yes, 248 no, with V, C and MP voting no alongside M, SD, KD and L. On 3 November it rejected the Centre leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist, put forward by the Speaker for a centre coalition of C, S, KD, MP and L: 25 yes, 324 no. The same day Ulf Kristersson (M) received the exploratory task; on 6 November the Speaker extended it 'because of new circumstances', and Kristersson reports on 17 November. On 13 November C received rival policy packages from Kristersson and from Andersson, who says V would join negotiations if C chooses S. Two of the four attempts remain.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Brå: 84 confirmed cases of lethal violence in 2025 (0.8 per 100,000), against 116 in 2022; cases with firearms fell from 63 to 42. source
  • SCB: 6,737 people sought asylum in 2025, against 16,825 in 2022 and 162,877 in 2015. source
  • Trafikverket: road-traffic emissions rose 18% in 2024 (12.8 to 15.1 Mt CO2e), mainly because the biofuel blending obligation was cut to 6%. source
  • Fiscal Policy Council (Feb 2026): the 2026 budget produces the largest deficit in 30 years, the pandemic year 2020 aside. source
  • Riksbank (Sept 2026): policy rate held at 1.75%; CPIF inflation 0.7% in August, held down largely by temporary fiscal measures; rate rises expected. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page covers the eight parties in the Riksdag elected on 13 September 2026; no other party reached 3% (others: 1.6%). Seats and vote shares are the final results confirmed on 19 September 2026. The government section describes a caretaker government and an unfinished formation as of 15 November 2026: two of the four prime-minister votes have failed, and the next step was due on 17 November.

Left-right positions are a rescaled reading of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey and were not re-checked against the 2024 wave; the far-left and far-right labels for V and SD follow The PopuList. The government gauge is confidence in the outgoing prime minister measured before the election (Novus, August 2026), since no later poll of trust in the government was found. The corruption rank is inferred from Transparency International's published scores.

Party finances use the latest per-party breakdown found in a national source (Kammarkollegiet filings for 2016, compiled by SVT); newer filings exist but were not broken down by a validated source. No independent promise tracker for the Tidö agreement was found; the government's own closing report is listed as not independent.

Press links are limited to the validated outlets listed in the media panel (TT, SVT and Sveriges Radio, Källkritikbyrån); TT dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as TT. Claims that only other outlets reported were rewritten to what official records confirm, or dropped. Investigations without an official act and acquittals or fines removed from the criminal record are left out.
