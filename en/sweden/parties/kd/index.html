Sweden

Parliamentary constitutional monarchy · 10.6 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote1392 daysNext general election: 8 September 2030 (an extra election could come first)
Ordinary elections to the Riksdag, the 21 regions and the 290 municipalities are held together on the second Sunday of September every four years. If the Riksdag rejects the Speaker's candidate for prime minister four times, an extra election must be held; in Sweden it does not change the date of the next ordinary election. By mid-November 2026 two of the four attempts had failed (15 October and 3 November); after a fourth failure, the extra election must be held within three months. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.

  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.

  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for KD, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It campaigns to nationalise healthcare. In 2025 the parliamentary inquiry declined to propose it, with only the KD and SD members in favour; its own health minister welcomed the report all the same.

As energy minister, its leader promised new nuclear reactors. By June 2026 the result was a state stake in Videberg Kraft, with the EU state-aid decision expected in autumn 2027.

It preaches stewardship for future generations. Road-traffic emissions rose 18% in 2024, after the biofuel cut it backed.

To be fair, that same cut made diesel about SEK 4.6 a litre cheaper (Konjunkturinstitutet).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page covers the eight parties in the Riksdag elected on 13 September 2026; no other party reached 3% (others: 1.6%). Seats and vote shares are the final results confirmed on 19 September 2026. The government section describes a caretaker government and an unfinished formation as of 15 November 2026: two of the four prime-minister votes have failed, and the next step was due on 17 November.

Left-right positions are a rescaled reading of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey and were not re-checked against the 2024 wave; the far-left and far-right labels for V and SD follow The PopuList. The government gauge is confidence in the outgoing prime minister measured before the election (Novus, August 2026), since no later poll of trust in the government was found. The corruption rank is inferred from Transparency International's published scores.

Party finances use the latest per-party breakdown found in a national source (Kammarkollegiet filings for 2016, compiled by SVT); newer filings exist but were not broken down by a validated source. No independent promise tracker for the Tidö agreement was found; the government's own closing report is listed as not independent.

Press links are limited to the validated outlets listed in the media panel (TT, SVT and Sveriges Radio, Källkritikbyrån); TT dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as TT. Claims that only other outlets reported were rewritten to what official records confirm, or dropped. Investigations without an official act and acquittals or fines removed from the criminal record are left out.
