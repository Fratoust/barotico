Sweden

Parliamentary constitutional monarchy · 10.6 million people

Ordinary elections to the Riksdag, the 21 regions and the 290 municipalities are held together on the second Sunday of September every four years. If the Riksdag rejects the Speaker's candidate for prime minister four times, an extra election must be held; in Sweden it does not change the date of the next ordinary election. By mid-November 2026 two of the four attempts had failed (15 October and 3 November); after a fourth failure, the extra election must be held within three months. source

Voting in Sweden

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Residents are on the electoral roll automatically and receive a voting card by post. Early voting runs anywhere in Sweden for about two and a half weeks before election day (26 August to 13 September in 2026), with ID. A messenger can carry your vote if you cannot reach a polling station. Swedes abroad vote by post or at embassies and consulates that accept votes.

Last turnout: 84.9% in the September 2026 general election (6,834,413 voters), up from 84.2% in 2022 source

The electoral system

  • Swedish citizens aged 18 or over vote every four years, on the second Sunday of September, for the Riksdag and, on the same day, for their regional and municipal councils.
  • Proportional representation: a party needs 4% nationally (or 12% in one constituency) to win seats; 310 constituency seats are topped up by 39 adjustment seats so that the result is proportional nationwide.
  • Voters can mark one candidate on the party list; a candidate with personal votes from at least 5% of the party's voters in the constituency moves to the top.

Sources: Riksdag: elections to the Riksdag (thresholds, seats, personal votes)

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Sweden, you vote blank with a blank ballot paper put alone in the envelope. Blank votes are published, but they are invalid votes.

What it is

  • Polling stations must offer ballot papers with only the election name on them: you can write a party on them, or leave them empty to vote blank. source
  • Careful: an envelope with no ballot inside is not a blank vote; it is recorded as “invalid – other”. source

How it is counted

  • A ballot without a party name is invalid. Blank votes count in turnout but not among valid votes, so they do not affect the 4% threshold. source

The figures

  • 0.95%of the 6.55 million votes in the 2022 Riksdag election were blank: just over 62,000 of the 70,000 invalid votes. source

What it changes for the result

  • Since blank votes stay outside valid votes, a party's share and the 4% bar are calculated without them. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Barotico does not recommend voting blank, nor avoiding it. These questions are there to help you decide with the facts in hand.

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Party finances use the latest per-party breakdown found in a national source (Kammarkollegiet filings for 2016, compiled by SVT); newer filings exist but were not broken down by a validated source. Press links are limited to the validated outlets listed in the media panel (TT, SVT and Sveriges Radio, Källkritikbyrån); TT dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as TT. Claims that only other outlets reported were rewritten to what official records confirm, or dropped. Investigations without an official act and acquittals or fines removed from the criminal record are left out.
