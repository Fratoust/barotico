How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and where things get stuck.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterSeal the border (2024 platform)
DeliveredIn fiscal 2025 (October 2024 to September 2025; Trump took office in January), the Border Patrol made nearly 238,000 arrests at the southern border, the lowest annual total since 1970 and almost ten times fewer than the 2.2 million of fiscal 2022 (DHS data reported by CBS News). source · 2
On the posterEnd automatic citizenship for children born in the US to undocumented or temporary immigrants
⚠ Rights alertTrump signed the order on his first day back in office (20 January 2025); lower courts blocked it and it never took effect. On 30 June 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in Trump v. Barbara that it violates the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause, the right of anyone born on US soil to be a citizen (five justices on that ground, Justice Kavanaugh on federal law only). Trump contests it: he called the ruling 'too bad for our Country' and said Congress could fix it. source · 2 · 3
On the posterNo tax on tips
DeliveredThe July 2025 law created a deduction for tip income until 2028. More than 7.5 million filers used it in 2026, with an average deduction above $7,000, about $840 of tax saved at a 12% rate (Treasury figures checked by FactCheck.org). Payroll taxes still apply to tips, and the Tax Policy Center estimates about 3% of households benefit. source · 2
On the poster'End inflation, and make America affordable again'
Half-wayConsumer prices rose 3.7% in the 12 months to September 2026, against 3.0% in January 2025 when it took office; core inflation, which leaves out food and energy, eased from 3.3% to 2.6% over the same period (BLS). PolitiFact rates the promise to lower the price of everyday goods 'stalled'. source · 2
On the posterMedicaid 'is not going to be touched' (Trump, February 2025)
ContradictionOn 3 July 2025 the House passed the tax and spending law 218-214 with Republican votes only. The CBO estimates it cuts federal health spending by more than $1 trillion over ten years, $326 billion from the Medicaid work requirement alone, and leaves 10 million more people uninsured by 2034. Speaker Johnson said 'we're not cutting Medicaid', only fraud, waste and abuse, with work requirements for able-bodied adults; the CBO's savings come mainly from enrolling fewer people. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • Its 2024 platform presented tariffs as the way to lower Americans' taxes. The President imposed them under an emergency-powers law (IEEPA), citing drug trafficking, migration and the trade deficit; on 20 February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that this law does not allow tariffs, a taxing power the Constitution gives to Congress, where the party holds both chambers and had never voted them. The administration replaced them with a new 10% tariff under another law. source
  • Promised to 'end inflation' and bring prices down; in February 2026 PolitiFact rated the promise to lower the price of everyday goods 'stalled', with groceries, electricity, housing and medical care costing more than when Trump took office. source
  • Promised that Medicaid would not be cut, then passed on its own votes, 218-214 in the House, a July 2025 law that the CBO says reduces federal health spending by more than $1 trillion over ten years, $326 billion of it from a Medicaid work requirement, leaving 10 million more people uninsured by 2034. Its leaders said they were cutting only fraud, waste and abuse; the CBO's savings come mainly from covering fewer people. source
To be fair
  • Made the 2017 tax cuts permanent in its July 2025 law, a promise PolitiFact rates as kept
  • Border Patrol apprehensions at the southern border fell in fiscal 2025 to their lowest level since 1970 (CBP data)
  • On its watch murders fell by an estimated 18% in 2025 and the murder rate, 4.1 per 100,000, tied the lowest ever recorded (FBI)

If you're considering voting for the Republicans, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The party sold tariffs as a way to cut your taxes. The Supreme Court struck them down, because Congress, run by the same party, had never voted them.

Groceries, electricity and housing, meanwhile, cost more than when Trump took office, PolitiFact found.

On its promises: tips are now deductible, and tax-free Social Security became a deduction for seniors. Removals are closing in on the 2013 record. Inflation stands at 3.7%.

On the posterDefend democracy against those who deny election results
ContradictionIn the 2022 Republican primaries its House campaign committee spent $425,000 on an ad boosting John Gibbs, who had questioned the 2020 result, against Rep. Peter Meijer, one of ten Republicans who voted to impeach Trump; the Democratic Governors Association booked almost $1.2 million promoting Dan Cox in Maryland. Nancy Pelosi said such choices were made 'in furtherance of our winning the election': a seat calculation, made for a candidate who disputed the very result the party says it defends. source · 2 · 3
On the posterAffordable health care for working families
DeliveredIts 2021 and 2022 laws enlarged ACA premium subsidies: Marketplace enrollment rose from 11.4 million in 2020 to a record 24.3 million in 2025, and KFF estimates subsidised enrollees would otherwise have paid 79% more in 2024 ($1,593 a year instead of $888). The subsidies lapsed at the end of 2025; in opposition, its House members forced through a three-year extension (230-196, with 17 Republicans) in January 2026, which has not become law. source · 2
On the posterSecure the border while fixing immigration humanely
Half-wayBorder Patrol arrests peaked at nearly 250,000 in December 2023. After the bipartisan border bill failed twice in the Senate in 2024, nearly all Republicans voting no after Trump told senators to 'blame it on me', Biden suspended most asylum claims in June 2024 when crossings are high, saying Republicans had left him 'no choice'; crossings fell to about 47,300 in December 2024, close to their lowest level since July 2020 (CBP figures reported by AP). source · 2 · 3
On the posterRestore the right to abortion nationwide
Half-wayHolding the Senate 50-50, with the Vice President's tie-breaking vote, it brought the Women's Health Protection Act to the Senate floor; it failed 49-51 on 11 May 2022, short of the 60 votes needed to end a filibuster, with one Democrat, Joe Manchin, voting no. No national abortion law has passed since. source
On the posterGun safety: limit where guns can be carried
⚠ Rights alertIn Hawaii, governed by Democrat Josh Green, a 2023 law (Act 52) barred permit holders from carrying guns onto private property open to the public, such as shops and hotels, without the owner's express permission. On 25 June 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in Wolford v. Lopez that this violates the Second Amendment right to bear arms. Hawaii Democrats contest it: Rep. Jill Tokuda called the ruling 'wrong for Hawaii, wrong for public safety'. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • On 1 December 2024 President Joe Biden pardoned his son Hunter, convicted on gun charges and having pleaded guilty to tax charges; in June 2024 he had ruled out a pardon. Biden said his son had been 'selectively, and unfairly, prosecuted' and that 'raw politics' had led to 'a miscarriage of justice'. source
  • Promises a humane immigration system. In June 2024 President Biden barred most asylum claims at the southern border whenever crossings exceed 2,500 a day, using the same legal provision Donald Trump had used; he said he would have preferred a law but 'Republicans left me no choice', after they had blocked the bipartisan border bill. The ACLU called the ban illegal 'just as it was when Trump unsuccessfully tried it'. source
  • Calls election deniers a threat to democracy, yet in the 2022 Republican primaries its House campaign committee spent $425,000 on an ad boosting John Gibbs, a Trump-backed candidate who had questioned the 2020 result, against a Republican who had voted to impeach Trump. Nancy Pelosi said such choices were made 'in furtherance of our winning the election'. source
To be fair
  • Passed the Inflation Reduction Act (2022), which lets Medicare negotiate some drug prices and caps insulin at $35 a month for Medicare patients
  • Passed the bipartisan infrastructure law of 2021 for roads, bridges, broadband and water pipes
  • Expanded Affordable Care Act subsidies in 2021, lowering premiums for millions of people who buy their own health insurance

If you're considering voting for the Democrats, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The party calls election deniers a threat to democracy. In 2022, it paid for ads helping one of them win a Republican primary, 'in furtherance of our winning the election'.
