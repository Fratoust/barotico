The 300 members elected in April 2024 sit for four years. By law (Public Official Election Act, art. 34) the vote falls on the first Wednesday from the 50th day before the term ends, which points to 12 April 2028. Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Some kept, some stretched.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept on paper Separate investigation from indictment: its majority amended the Criminal Procedure Act in 2026; too early for a measured effect, and the opposition contests a new dismissal clause source
  • Kept on paper Stable housing: real estate policy is the top reason for disapproval and draws more negative than positive ratings (Gallup Korea, October 2026); no official measure of an improvement found in this review source
  • Partly kept A four-year presidential term with one re-election: debated in September 2026, but the ruling camp (179 seats) lacks the 200 votes a constitutional amendment needs source
  • Kept, made a difference Reform the national pension so it lasts: the March 2025 reform, passed by the DP-led Assembly in a deal with the People Power Party, raises contributions from 9% to 13% and the replacement rate to 43%; the National Assembly Budget Office pushes fund depletion from 2057 back to 2065 source · measured effect

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you’re considering voting for the Democratic Party, it’s worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised stable housing for ordinary families. In autumn 2026, real estate policy became the first reason people gave for disapproving of the government it leads, and presidential approval slipped below 40% for the first time.

It promised to finish prosecution reform, and its majority amended the Criminal Procedure Act. The opposition notes that the new dismissal clause sits next to the president’s own trials, which have had no hearing dates since he took office.

To be fair, Freedom House raised the country’s score from 81 to 83 in 2026, with government back to normal functioning and protests proceeding without undue restrictions after martial law.

Polarizationnot measured yet

How often its leaders attack opponents personally or present them as enemies rather than rivals. Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Four parties have a card: those with a parliamentary group or at least 3% of the vote in the 2024 election. The Progressive, Basic Income and Social Democratic parties (six seats in all) appear only in the seat chart. Seats come from the June 2026 by-election count, adjusted for one seat lost in July 2026; the eight independents are worked out from the 300 total, because the National Assembly's own page could not be opened.

Left-right positions are an editorial estimate: the Chapel Hill expert survey does not cover South Korea. Press sources are limited to Yonhap, the national news agency, and to the five broadcasters with the highest trust scores in the Reuters Institute report (MBC, JTBC, SBS, YTN, KBS).

Party finance shares, programme links (except the Reform Party's) and official 2024 vote counts are missing: the National Election Commission's accounts and results pages could not be reached. The homicide rate is worked out from police victim counts. No independent promise tracker or poll on parties people would never vote for was found.

The court record lists only official acts. Cho Kuk's conviction and pardon are left out until an official text can be opened.
