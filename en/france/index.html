How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and where things get stuck.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How a law is passed

  1. National Assembly
  2. Senate
  3. Joint committee if they disagree
  4. Final word to the Assembly
  5. Constitutional Council if referred

Why it's complicated

The two-round system is designed to produce a clear majority. Since 2024 the Assembly has been split into three blocs with no majority: the government looks for votes bill by bill, under constant threat of a censure motion.

Why it's like thisThe Fifth Republic (1958) was built to avoid the instability of the Fourth: a strong executive and a voting system that manufactures majorities. When voters split into three blocs, the machine runs on empty.

Documented facts to know

  • CNews (private): fined €60,000 and €20,000 by the regulator Arcom in 2024; formal notice in June 2026 for a “manifest and lasting imbalance”. CNews disputes it and has appealed. source
  • C8 (private): broadcast licence not renewed, upheld by the Conseil d'État in February 2025, after repeated breaches. source
  • Radio France (public): formal notice from Arcom in June 2026 because the RN was under-represented in daytime; about 60% of its speaking time on France Inter aired between midnight and 6 a.m. Franceinfo says RN leaders decline its invitations. source
  • Parliamentary inquiry into public broadcasting (May 2026): the report alleges “militant biases”; adopted 12 to 10 with 8 abstentions, it was criticised by the Prime Minister and by the inquiry's own chair. source
  • Measured trust (2025): BFMTV 40% trust vs 38% distrust; CNews 41% vs 35%. source
