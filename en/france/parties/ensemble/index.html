Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Its promises, set against its actions

Each line puts a promise next to what the party actually did on the same subject. The colour gives the outcome: DeliveredHalf-wayConsistentContradiction⚠ Rights alert

Delivered: done, with an effect measured by an independent body. Half-way: partly done, or pushed by the party but blocked by others. Consistent: it voted for its own promise and lost the vote. Contradiction: it acted against its own promise (three sources required). ⚠ Rights alert: a court or independent authority found the measure contrary to fundamental rights.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterSound public finances
ContradictionIn power since 2017. Public debt reached 115.6% of GDP (€3,460bn) at the end of 2025, with a deficit of 5.1%, above the EU's 3% ceiling (Insee). It cites the cost of protecting jobs during Covid; the Cour des comptes found the end of crisis spending in 2023 was not used to cut spending, and the public-finance council that tax cuts had not been offset. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterRetirement at 65 (2022 programme)
Half-wayRaised the legal age to 64 in 2023. On 12 November 2025 the Assemblée voted 255 to 146 to suspend the reform until the presidential election, a concession its own government made to avoid a Socialist censure motion; most Ensemble MPs abstained to let the compromise through, saying they still stood by the 2023 choice. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterPurchasing power: abolish the taxe d'habitation (2017)
DeliveredAbolished in stages on main homes. The 2019 step alone raised disposable income by about €200 a year for the 18.3 million households concerned, with no household losing out (Insee). source
On the posterEnd glyphosate “within three years at the latest” (2017)
ContradictionNot done. In January 2022 the president said “on glyphosate, I did not succeed”, blaming a France-only exit and saying it had to be done at EU level. At the 2023 EU votes France abstained, saying it did not oppose glyphosate in substance, and the herbicide was renewed for ten years. source · 2 · 3
On the poster“Global security that preserves freedoms” (2021 law)
⚠ Rights alertBill of its MPs Jean-Michel Fauvergue and Alice Thourot. On 20 May 2021 the Conseil constitutionnel struck down on substance the new offence of inciting the identification of police officers (an offence must be clearly defined) and the rules on police drones and on video surveillance of people in custody (right to privacy); five other articles fell as legislative riders. Fauvergue said “we will not let go” and planned to bring the offence back in other bills. source · 2 · 3
On the posterControl immigration, improve integration (2024 law)
⚠ Rights alertIts government's law. Of the 35 articles the Conseil constitutionnel struck out on 25 January 2024, 32 were legislative riders, not judged on substance; 3 fell on substance, and only one on a right: taking foreigners' fingerprints and photos by force, without consent, censured for individual liberty (no magistrate's authorisation, not limited to cases where it is the only means of identification, no lawyer). A 2025 law brought it back in a narrower form, which was upheld. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterRestore authority over young offenders (Attal law, 2025)
⚠ Rights alertBill of its group leader Gabriel Attal. On 19 June 2025 the Conseil constitutionnel struck out five articles on substance: fast-track trial from 16, wider single-hearing trials, longer pre-trial detention for under-16s, reduced sentences for minors made the exception, and 12-hour holds ordered by a police officer without a judge. Ground: the constitutional principle that juvenile justice must be adapted to minors and aim at their rehabilitation. A sixth fell as a rider. Attal “took note” and announced a new text. source · 2 · 3 · 4

In short

The card in a few facts, then a few ways to make up your own mind.

The barometer's verdict

If you're considering voting for Ensemble, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Full employment was the horizon. Unemployment, meanwhile, has climbed back to 8.3%, its highest level since 2020.

The deficit was due back under 3%. The High Council of Public Finance now pencils in “2031 at best”, for a debt that has reached 115.6% of GDP.

Leaving glyphosate was meant to take three years. Emmanuel Macron then handed the matter to Europe, where France abstained on the day the EU renewed it for ten more.

To be fair, scrapping the housing tax did leave about €200 a year with the households covered by the 2019 stage (Insee).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards: France has eight cards, for the parties with a group in the National Assembly or at least 3% of the vote, ordered by seats. Smaller groups such as LIOT and the UDR appear in the seat chart without a card.

Left-right positions come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey, rescaled from -2 to +2. "Far left" and "far right" use the same threshold on both sides (1.5) or The PopuList classification.

Democracy barometer: a party's real weight is its votes divided by all registered voters at the June 2024 European election, the last national vote in which every party ran under its own name (the 2024 legislative election was fought in alliances). Rejection comes from a single Odoxa poll (December 2025) asking the same question about every party. Pluralism and polarisation use V-Party data from 2017, when some parties had other names (LaREM, FN).

Figures: the homicide rate is our calculation from the 887 homicides recorded by Eurostat and a population of about 68 million. Party finance shares are calculated from the 2024 accounts filed with the CNCCFP; for Ensemble, public funding flows through a separate association, so its share is calculated.

Judicial records list only official acts (investigations, charges, referrals, convictions, sanctions) concerning the party, its leaders, its ministers of the last ten years and its current MPs. Complaints, press allegations and private life are left out, and acquittals or dismissals older than 12 months are removed.
