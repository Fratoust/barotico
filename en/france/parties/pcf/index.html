How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept, made a difference Small farm pensions at 85% of the minimum wage (Chassaigne law, 2020): paid since December 2021, €1,035.57 gross a month for a full career (MSA) source · measured effect
  • Kept on paper Repeal the 2023 pension reform: its resolution passed 198 to 35 in June 2025, with no legal force; the reform kept applying source
  • Never tested Retirement at 60 for all (2027 project)

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for PCF, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

A pro-nuclear party, in 2022 it joined an alliance in favour of a gradual exit, giving up its referendum but keeping a free vote for its MPs.

It signed the New Popular Front with LFI, then ruled out any further alliance with it.

Its 2020 law did raise small farm pensions to 85% of the minimum wage.

In June 2025, its call to repeal retirement at 64 passed by 198 votes to 35. A clear victory, with no legal force whatsoever: the reform still applies.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?

Real weightnot measured yet

Its votes divided by everyone registered to vote, not only by the votes cast. A party can top the polls and win many seats with a minority of the electorate: this shows how many citizens really chose it, compared with the noise it makes.

Rejectionnot measured yet

The share of people ready to vote for a rival just to keep this party out. A high figure means the party divides more than it convinces, whatever its score.

Pluralismnot measured yet

How far the party accepts the rules of democracy, as rated by independent experts: free and fair elections, respect for opponents, rights of minorities, rejection of political violence. 100 = fully pluralist, 0 = not at all. Green is reassuring, red is alarming.

Not covered by the V-Party expert survey.

Polarizationnot measured yet

How often its leaders attack opponents personally or present them as enemies rather than rivals. It splits society into camps, makes any compromise look like betrayal, fuels fear of the other and pushes facts and solutions out of the debate. Rated by independent experts: 0 = never, 100 = always. Green is reassuring, red is alarming.

Not covered by the V-Party expert survey.

Measured by others, never by us: official results, a national poll, and the V-Party expert survey of the V-Dem Institute (University of Gothenburg). A loud party is not necessarily a big one: compare its real weight with the noise.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards: France has eight cards, for the parties with a group in the National Assembly or at least 3% of the vote, ordered by seats. Smaller groups such as LIOT and the UDR appear in the seat chart without a card.

Left-right positions come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey, rescaled from -2 to +2. "Far left" and "far right" use the same threshold on both sides (1.5) or The PopuList classification.

Democracy barometer: a party's real weight is its votes divided by all registered voters at the June 2024 European election, the last national vote in which every party ran under its own name (the 2024 legislative election was fought in alliances). Rejection comes from a single Odoxa poll (December 2025) asking the same question about every party. Pluralism and polarisation use V-Party data from 2017, when some parties had other names (LaREM, FN).

Figures: the homicide rate is our calculation from the 887 homicides recorded by Eurostat and a population of about 68 million. Party finance shares are calculated from the 2024 accounts filed with the CNCCFP; for Ensemble, public funding flows through a separate association, so its share is calculated.

Judicial records list only official acts (investigations, charges, referrals, convictions, sanctions) concerning the party, its leaders, its ministers of the last ten years and its current MPs. Complaints, press allegations and private life are left out, and acquittals or dismissals older than 12 months are removed.
