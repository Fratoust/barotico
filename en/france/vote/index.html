How to vote in practice, and what your vote changes, even a blank one.

Voting in France

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

You are registered automatically at 18 if you did the citizenship census; otherwise register online (service-public.fr) up to the sixth Friday before the vote. Vote at your polling station with an ID, or give a proxy online (maprocuration.gouv.fr). French people abroad vote at their consulate, and online for the legislative elections.

Last turnout: 66.6% in the second round of the July 2024 legislative elections (Interior Ministry) source

The electoral system

  • President: direct vote, two rounds, 5-year term.
  • Deputies: one constituency each, two rounds.
  • Senators: elected by local officials (indirect vote).

Sources: Constitution, Art. 6, 7 and 24 (Élysée) · National Assembly: election of deputies · Senate: the essentials

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

You go to the polling station, but you choose none of the candidates. Some see it as a protest, others as a refusal of the choice on offer, others as a way to vote without deciding. Here is what it means in practice in France.

What it is

  • Blank vote: an empty envelope, or a blank sheet of paper in the envelope. No blank ballot is provided at the polling station.
  • Spoilt vote: a ballot that is annotated, torn, or not valid.
  • Abstention: not voting at all.
  • What the three have in common: none of them counts for a candidate.
  • Careful: a word, a crossing-out, a drawing or a sign on the paper, or a torn ballot, makes it spoilt, not blank. To vote blank, use an empty envelope or a sheet with nothing written on it. source

How it is counted

  • Since the law of 21 February 2014 (in force since 1 April 2014), blank votes are counted separately from spoilt votes and published with the results. The presidential election has applied the same rule since 2017. source
  • But they stay outside the “votes cast” (suffrages exprimés): percentages, majorities and the 5% threshold for campaign reimbursement are all calculated without them. source
  • The President is elected “by an absolute majority of the votes cast” (Constitution, art. 7). Counting blank votes as votes cast could leave the second round with no winner: that would require a constitutional amendment. source
  • In parliamentary elections, two thresholds are calculated on registered voters: a quarter of them to win in the first round, 12.5% to stay in the second. On those thresholds, a blank vote weighs the same as an abstention. source

The figures

  • 8.5%of voters cast a blank ballot in the 2017 presidential run-off: about 3 million, a record under the Fifth Republic. source
  • 6.4%in the 2022 run-off: 2.2 million blank ballots. source
  • 1.5%in the first round of 2022. Blank votes rise when the choice narrows to two names. source
  • 86%of French people supported counting blank votes as votes cast (Ifop, 2017, cited by the National Assembly report). source

What it changes for the result

  • An example: in the 2022 run-off, Emmanuel Macron won 58.55% of votes cast and Marine Le Pen 41.45%. Had the 2.2 million blank ballots been counted, it would have been 54.8% and 38.8%. The winner does not change; the percentage does. source
  • In 2012, François Hollande would have fallen to 48.6%, below half, with blank votes counted (an argument raised in the 2023 committee debate). source
  • In a run-off, the gap between A and B is made only of votes cast for A or B. A blank ballot adds to neither: it leaves the decision to those who chose. The closer the race, the more each vote cast weighs on the outcome.

Why some vote blank (voices from 2017)

  • Rejecting both finalists, even while fearing one of them.
  • Refusing the “useful vote” after having made it before.
  • Preferring blank to abstention, so as not to look indifferent.
  • A narrower choice in the second round, says political sociologist Jérémie Moualek: blank votes rise at every run-off.

source

The debate: should it count?

For counting it
  • Tell a refusal of the candidates apart from indifference, and bring voters back to the polls (arguments of the LIOT group's 2023 bill).
  • Give an effect to a disagreement that the 2014 law publishes but does not count; its supporters say that law changed little.
Against
  • No absolute majority in the presidential run-off (art. 7); if blank votes passed 50%, elections would have to be rerun, leaving seats empty (arguments of Renaissance and the RN in committee).
  • Thresholds such as 5% become harder to reach, and seat calculations under proportional representation more complex (points raised in committee, citing the Constitutional Council).

Where it stands: the blank-vote article of the LIOT bill was rejected in committee in May 2023; another bill (no. 1251, May 2023) was referred to committee with no vote recorded. In Europe, only Spain counts blank votes as valid votes. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? In France, blank is the only way to make it appear in the results.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the other?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count? A blank vote does the second, not the first.
  • Is the race close? The smaller the expected gap, the more the outcome rests on the votes cast alone.
  • Is the country going through a crisis, or could the election tip one way or the other? Then a blank vote leaves the other voters to decide in your place.
  • First or second round? The first round offers a wide choice; blank votes are most frequent in the second.
  • Who will read my message? The blank-vote rate is published and discussed on election night; an abstention is not told apart from indifference or being unable to vote.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: all three leave the decision to those who vote for a candidate. Only blank is counted as a deliberate choice not to choose.

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards: France has eight cards, for the parties with a group in the National Assembly or at least 3% of the vote, ordered by seats. Smaller groups such as LIOT and the UDR appear in the seat chart without a card.

Left-right positions come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey, rescaled from -2 to +2. "Far left" and "far right" use the same threshold on both sides (1.5) or The PopuList classification.

Democracy barometer: a party's real weight is its votes divided by all registered voters at the June 2024 European election, the last national vote in which every party ran under its own name (the 2024 legislative election was fought in alliances). Rejection comes from a single Odoxa poll (December 2025) asking the same question about every party. Pluralism and polarisation use V-Party data from 2017, when some parties had other names (LaREM, FN).

Figures: the homicide rate is our calculation from the 887 homicides recorded by Eurostat and a population of about 68 million. Party finance shares are calculated from the 2024 accounts filed with the CNCCFP; for Ensemble, public funding flows through a separate association, so its share is calculated.

Judicial records list only official acts (investigations, charges, referrals, convictions, sanctions) concerning the party, its leaders, its ministers of the last ten years and its current MPs. Complaints, press allegations and private life are left out, and acquittals or dismissals older than 12 months are removed.
