How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and where things get stuck.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who governs in France?

Who's at the wheel

President: Emmanuel Macron (since 2017). Prime Minister: Sébastien Lecornu, reappointed on 10 October 2025.

Lecornu II government, a minority in the Assembly: it survived censure in October 2025 and passed the 2026 budget using Article 49.3.

Government barometer

Democracy80 / 100

V-Dem's Liberal Democracy Index: free and fair elections, freedom of expression and association, independent courts, limits on the executive. 100 = fully democratic. V-Dem also sorts countries into four regimes: closed autocracy (no real elections, e.g. China, North Korea), electoral autocracy (elections without fair competition), electoral democracy and liberal democracy. Rated by thousands of country experts (V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg).

Liberal democracy · V-Dem, 2025. source

Pluralism of those in power97 / 100

How far the party or parties in power accept the rules of democracy, according to the V-Party expert survey: free elections, respect for opponents and minorities, rejection of political violence. 100 = fully pluralist. It is the same measure as on each party card.

Ensemble · V-Party, 2017. source

Popularity17 %

Share of people who approve of, or are satisfied with, the head of the executive in the latest reputable national poll. Green from 55%, yellow 40 to 54%, orange 25 to 39%, red below 25%. In countries V-Dem classes as autocracies the bar stays grey: polls there are too constrained to read as reassuring or alarming.

satisfied with President Emmanuel Macron; 25% with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu (Ifop-Endrix, October 2026). source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • 2017-2022 tax and benefit measures: living standards +1.9% on average; +0.8% for the poorest 5%, +3.3% for the richest 1%; about 24% of people lost out (IPP). source
  • 2026 measures: the richest 1% bear the largest loss (higher levy on some capital income); in-work benefit reform +0.3% for lower-middle incomes; large single-parent families −1.3% (IPP). source
  • Redistribution in 2024: the richest 10% have 21.1 times the income of the poorest 10% before taxes and benefits, 6.2 times after (INSEE). source
