New Zealand

Constitutional monarchy, parliamentary system · 5.36 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 36 monthsNext general election: expected in late 2029 (date to be set)
The 7 November 2026 election is not final yet: the figures on this page are the preliminary count of election night. The official result, including about 399,365 special votes (some 14% of the total), is due on 27 November 2026 and the writ is returned on 3 December. Parliament's term stays at three years, since the government dropped its planned referendum on a four-year term in February 2026, so the next election is expected in late 2029; the Prime Minister sets the date. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the poster100,000 affordable KiwiBuild homes in ten years (2017)
Half-wayIn September 2019, with 258 homes built, Housing Minister Megan Woods dropped the target as 'overly ambitious', saying it had produced 'perverse outcomes', and turned to shared-equity schemes and 5% deposits. By April 2024, 2,335 KiwiBuild homes had been built. source · 2
On the posterReduce child poverty
DeliveredPassed the Child Poverty Reduction Act (2018), with targets measured by Stats NZ: the share of children in low-income households (before housing costs) fell from 16.5% in 2018 to 12.0% in 2022. source
On the posterA fairer tax system
Half-wayIn April 2019 Jacinda Ardern dropped a capital gains tax, saying she could not get NZ First's support, and ruled it out for as long as she led. Chris Hipkins also ruled one out before the 2023 election; in October 2025 Labour announced a 28% tax on gains from property sales, excluding the family home and farms, from July 2027, to fund three free GP visits a year. source · 2
On the posterCovid-19: keep New Zealanders safe at the border
⚠ Rights alertIts government allocated most managed isolation (MIQ) places through a 'virtual lobby' lottery. On 27 April 2022 the High Court found that, from September to December 2021, this system unjustifiably limited citizens' right to enter New Zealand under the Bill of Rights Act, while ruling MIQ itself lawful. Chris Hipkins, then Covid-19 Response Minister, said the lobby 'may have infringed' that right; the government did not appeal. source · 2 · 3
On the posterA fair go for working people
ConsistentIn opposition, it voted against the Equal Pay Amendment passed under urgency by the coalition in May 2025, which dropped 33 pay equity claims covering thousands of workers; Chris Hipkins said the change was 'not getting the scrutiny it deserves'. source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • Before the 2023 election its leader Chris Hipkins ruled out introducing a wealth tax or a capital gains tax as prime minister; in October 2025 he announced Labour would campaign on a 28% capital gains tax on investment property. source

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept, made a difference Cut child poverty (Child Poverty Reduction Act 2018): the share of children in low-income households (before housing costs) fell from 16.5% in 2018 to 12.0% in 2022, Stats NZ source · measured effect
  • Kept on paper A fees-free first year of tertiary study (2017): introduced in 2018, but an AUT study of 250,000 school leavers found it did not appear to increase participation nor narrow the gap for students from poorer schools source
  • Broken 100,000 KiwiBuild homes in ten years (2017): target abandoned in 2019; 2,335 homes built source
  • Never tested A 28% capital gains tax on investment property bought after 1 July 2027, to fund three free GP visits a year (2026): untested source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Labour, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised 100,000 KiwiBuild homes and built 2,335.

It promised light rail to Mt Roskill in four years, and spent $228m without laying a metre of track.

In 2022 the High Court found that its MIQ booking lottery had unjustifiably limited citizens' right to come home. Its government did not appeal.

To be fair, Stats NZ measured the share of children in low-income households falling from 16.5% to 12.0% between 2018 and 2022.
