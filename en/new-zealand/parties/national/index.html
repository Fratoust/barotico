Before you read

  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.

  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 36 monthsNext general election: expected in late 2029 (date to be set)
The 7 November 2026 election is not final yet: the figures on this page are the preliminary count of election night. The official result, including about 399,365 special votes (some 14% of the total), is due on 27 November 2026 and the writ is returned on 3 December. Parliament's term stays at three years, since the government dropped its planned referendum on a four-year term in February 2026, so the next election is expected in late 2029; the Prime Minister sets the date. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterWith rights come responsibility: no vote for sentenced prisoners
⚠ Rights alertReinstate a total ban on sentenced prisoners voting (Paul Goldsmith, April 2025): enacted in the Electoral Amendment Act passed on 17 December 2025 with the coalition's votes, which bars prisoners serving a sentence from voting (not those on remand or in home detention). On 9 November 2018 the Supreme Court (Attorney-General v Taylor) upheld the High Court's declaration that the 2010 law with the same content, a ban on all sentenced prisoners voting, is inconsistent with the right to vote (s 12 of the Bill of Rights Act). The declaration did not invalidate that law. National maintains the ban: 'with rights come responsibility', says Goldsmith, who disagrees with the reviews that recommended restoring prisoners' votes. source · 2 · 3
On the posterTax relief for the 'squeezed middle': up to $250 a fortnight for families, partly funded by a tax on foreign buyers
Half-wayIts tax package took effect in July 2024, but the foreign buyer tax, meant to raise about $740m a year, was dropped in the November 2023 deal with NZ First, which kept the ban on foreign buyers. The unions' council estimates fewer than 50 families get the full $252 a fortnight; 249 families consistently received the full FamilyBoost childcare rebate, against 21,000 first estimated by IRD, whose estimate Nicola Willis blamed (April 2025). source · 2 · 3
On the posterCut the use of emergency motel housing by 75% by 2030
Half-wayTarget met in December 2024: 471 households in emergency housing in December 2025, against 3,141 in December 2023 (government figures). Over the same period, the share of emergency housing applications declined rose from 4% to 32% after stricter rules in August 2024, and community providers counted rough sleepers in Auckland rising from 426 to 809 (The Spinoff, August 2025). source · 2
On the posterLaw and order: at least 500 new frontline police within two years (coalition agreement)
Half-wayThe deadline of 26 November 2025 was missed; Treasury projected the target would be reached around September 2026. Associate Police Minister Casey Costello (NZ First) called the timing 'ambitious' and said the extra constables 'will be delivered'. source
On the posterRestore law and order: ban gang patches in public
⚠ Rights alertIts Gangs Act passed in September 2024 with National, ACT and NZ First votes. On 30 July 2026 the High Court (B v Attorney-General) declared the ban on displaying gang insignia in public inconsistent with freedom of expression (s 14 of the Bill of Rights Act) and not justified, because a narrower ban could have met its aim. The declaration leaves the law in force; Justice Minister Paul Goldsmith says it will not change. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • Cancelled the iReX Cook Strait ferry project in 2023 over cost overruns; KiwiRail put the cost of cancelling at $671m, and the replacement ferries ($1.86bn programme) are due in 2029. The government says it saves $2.3bn compared with iReX. source
  • Agreed in its coalition deals to support ACT's Treaty Principles Bill to the select committee stage, a bill it said it would not pass; after more than 300,000 written submissions (90% opposed), it voted it down 112-11 at second reading in April 2025. source
  • Campaigned in 2023 to rebuild the economy; in June 2026 unemployment reached 5.6%, the highest in 11 years (Stats NZ). source
To be fair

  • Adjusted income tax thresholds from July 2024 for the first time since 2010
  • Passed the Fast-track Approvals Act (December 2024), with 149 listed projects

  • Partly kept At least 500 new frontline police within two years (coalition agreement): the deadline was moved for the fourth time in November 2025; Treasury expected the target to be reached around September 2026 source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for National, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised families up to $250 a fortnight. By the unions' estimate, fewer than 50 receive it in full.

In July 2026 the High Court declared its ban on gang patches in public an unjustified limit on freedom of expression. The government says the law will not change.

It cancelled ferries to save money, at a cost of $671m. Its 500 extra police, meanwhile, slipped four times.

To be fair, households in emergency motels fell by 85%, from 3,141 to 471, in two years.
