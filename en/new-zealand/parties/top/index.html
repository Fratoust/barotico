New Zealand

Constitutional monarchy, parliamentary system · 5.36 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.

The 7 November 2026 election is not final yet: the figures on this page are the preliminary count of election night. The official result, including about 399,365 special votes (some 14% of the total), is due on 27 November 2026 and the writ is returned on 3 December. Parliament's term stays at three years, since the government dropped its planned referendum on a four-year term in February 2026, so the next election is expected in late 2029; the Prime Minister sets the date. source
How to read this country profile

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Also worth knowing
  • Promises to clean up politics; until August 2026 its constitution let a 75% majority of its unelected board overrule caucus decisions. That clause was dropped after questions on Q+A, but the board can still remove the leader and must approve any governing deal by 75%; Qiulae Wong says the rules will be reviewed in 2027. source
  • Says anyone currently on a benefit will be at least as well off under its Tax Reset; a Victoria University of Wellington researcher found that a beneficiary who adds a few hours of work could be worse off, and that a single adult with no income would gain $27 a year. source
  • Refuses to 'slap a left or right label' on itself; in an August 2026 Talbot Mills poll, 47% of voters who knew the party well placed it on the left and 22% on the right. source

  • In 2026 became the first new party not formed by current or former MPs to enter Parliament under MMP

The barometer's verdict

Promise-o-meterTake its word for it

0 made a difference0 on paper0 partly0 broken3 never tested

Never in a position to keep or break these promises. Impossible to check, which is convenient.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Never tested A tax-free Citizen's Income of up to $19,400 a year for every adult citizen and resident (2026): never in government, untested source
  • Never tested A land value tax of 1.75% a year on urban land and 0.5% on rural land (2026): untested source
  • Never tested An independent Anti-Corruption Commission 'with the teeth to enforce the rules' (2026): untested source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Opportunity, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promises that anyone on a benefit will be at least as well off under its Tax Reset. An independent researcher found a single adult with no income would gain $27 a year, and single adults earning over about $30,000 typically $11,000 or more.

It wants to clean up politics. Until August 2026 its unelected board could overrule its MPs, and any governing deal still needs 75% of that board.

It refuses the labels 'left' and 'right'. Among voters who know it well, 47% put it on the left.

To be fair, its sums are public enough to be checked: the same researcher found a sole parent with no other income $2,869 a year better off.

Democracy barometer

Real weightnot measured yet

Its votes divided by everyone registered to vote, not only by the votes cast. A party can top the polls and win many seats with a minority of the electorate: this shows how many citizens really chose it, compared with the noise it makes.

Share of all registered voters who voted for it: no comparable national result yet.

Rejectionnot measured yet

The share of people ready to vote for a rival just to keep this party out. A high figure means the party divides more than it convinces, whatever its score.

No comparable national poll found yet.

Pluralismnot measured yet

How far the party accepts the rules of democracy, as rated by independent experts: free and fair elections, respect for opponents, rights of minorities, rejection of political violence. 100 = fully pluralist, 0 = not at all. Green is reassuring, red is alarming.

Not covered by the V-Party expert survey.

Polarizationnot measured yet

How often its leaders attack opponents personally or present them as enemies rather than rivals. It splits society into camps, makes any compromise look like betrayal, fuels fear of the other and pushes facts and solutions out of the debate. Rated by independent experts: 0 = never, 100 = always. Green is reassuring, red is alarming.

Not covered by the V-Party expert survey.

Measured by others, never by us: official results, a national poll, and the V-Party expert survey of the V-Dem Institute (University of Gothenburg). A loud party is not necessarily a big one: compare its real weight with the noise.
