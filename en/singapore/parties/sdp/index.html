How to read this country profile

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

The barometer's verdict

Promise-o-meterTake its word for it

0 made a difference0 on paper0 partly0 broken3 never tested

Never in a position to keep or break these promises. Impossible to check, which is convenient.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Never tested Cut GST to 7% or 5% and remove it from everyday essentials (2025)
  • Never tested A Minimum Wage Act
  • Never tested One national health insurance in place of Medisave, MediShield Life and CareShield

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you’re considering voting for SDP, it’s worth looking beyond the slogans.

It campaigns to protect free speech and rein in POFMA. When its 2019 posts on local PMET jobs drew three corrections, it went to the Court of Appeal, which upheld the law, two directions and half of the third.

It wants GST at 7% or even 5%, and one national health insurance. Its 2025 campaign pages put no figure on the cost.

On the promise-o-meter: three promises, all untested; its leader came within 6.4 points of a seat in Sembawang West.

To be fair, it has never governed, so none of its promises has yet faced a measured test.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards cover every party that won a seat or at least 3% of the valid votes in May 2025: PAP, Workers’ Party, Progress Singapore Party, Red Dot United and Singapore Democratic Party. Smaller parties, led by the People’s Alliance for Reform (2.51%), have no card. Vote shares and the “real weight” figures are our own sums of the Elections Department’s constituency results; the 131,820 electors of the uncontested Marine Parade-Braddell Heights GRC cast no vote and are counted only among registered voters.

Left-right positions for the PAP and the Workers’ Party come from the V-Party expert survey (2015, its latest Singapore year), rescaled to our scale; the other three are our judgement, because no recent expert survey covers them.

No recognised national poll of satisfaction with or trust in the government was found, so those gauges and the popularity bar are empty, and no “would never vote for” poll exists. No party publishes an income breakdown, so party finance is shown as unknown. No independent promise tracker exists. Parliamentary voting records could not be retrieved, so some cards have fewer said-vs-done rows than usual.

Press sources are limited to the most trusted brands in the Reuters Institute’s 2026 survey (CNA, The Straits Times, Mediacorp news, Lianhe Zaobao); CNA reports are read through a syndicated copy carrying CNA’s credit. An MP’s resignation for personal reasons is used only to explain the vacant seat.
