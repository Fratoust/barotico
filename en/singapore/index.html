Singapore

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

131,820 voters in Marine Parade-Braddell Heights cast no ballot in May 2025: no team stood against the PAP in their constituency.

The PAP team won that group constituency by walkover. Everywhere else, 92.83% of the 2,627,026 registered electors voted.

Sources: Elections Department: 2025 general election results (walkover in Marine Parade-Braddell Heights) · Elections Department: total votes cast and turnout (15 May 2025)

Who decides what

GovernmentPrime Minister Lawrence Wong (PAP), who is also Minister for Finance, and his Cabinet; the government brings the Budget to Parliament.
ParliamentOne chamber: 96 elected MPs today (97 seats), plus 2 Non-Constituency MPs from the opposition and 9 Nominated MPs.
Head of stateThe President appoints the Nominated MPs for two and a half years, on the recommendation of a parliamentary committee chaired by the Speaker.
ElectionsThe Elections Department is part of the Prime Minister’s Office; it runs the vote and declares the NCMPs.

Sources: Parliament of Singapore: Members of Parliament (elected, NCMP, NMP) · Government of Singapore: Budget 2026, delivered by the Prime Minister and Minister for Finance · Elections Department (Prime Minister’s Office): declaration of NCMPs, May 2025

How a law is passed

  1. Ministers bring bills and the annual Budget to Parliament: Prime Minister Lawrence Wong, as Minister for Finance, delivered Budget 2026 on 12 February 2026.
  2. Parliament debates and votes. The PAP holds 86 of the 96 elected seats, enough for a majority on its own.
  3. Two opposition NCMPs and nine non-partisan Nominated MPs also sit in the chamber and take part in debates.
  4. Parliament also rules on its own members: on 14 January 2026 it adopted a motion declaring the Leader of the Opposition unsuitable for that post.

Sources: Government of Singapore: Budget 2026 statement in Parliament (12 February 2026) · Parliament of Singapore: composition of the 15th Parliament · PMO: Leader of the House on the motion of 14 January 2026

Political and economic context

The PAP holds 86 of the 96 elected seats; the Workers’ Party has 10, plus 2 NCMPs. Since 21 January 2026 there has been no Leader of the Opposition: after Parliament declared Pritam Singh unsuitable for the post following his final conviction for lying to a parliamentary committee, the Prime Minister ended his designation, and the Workers’ Party declined to nominate another MP. Freedom House scores the electoral framework 1 out of 4 for fairness.

Sources: Parliament of Singapore: 96 elected MPs, 2 NCMPs, 9 NMPs · PMO: the Leader of the Opposition post stays vacant (21 January 2026) · Workers’ Party: statement on the Leader of the Opposition post (21 January 2026) · Freedom House: Freedom in the World 2026, Singapore

Why it's like thisThe People’s Action Party has dominated politics since 1959 (Freedom House) and has governed continuously since 1965 (RSF). The schemes added since then widen representation inside that system: group constituencies require each team to include a minority-race candidate, Non-Constituency seats guarantee a minimum opposition presence, and since 1990 up to nine Nominated MPs bring non-partisan views. Freedom House finds that the framework “constrains the growth of opposition parties”.

Sources: Freedom House: Freedom in the World 2026, Singapore · RSF: Singapore country page (political context) · Parliament of Singapore: why GRCs, NCMPs and NMPs exist

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards cover every party that won a seat or at least 3% of the valid votes in May 2025: PAP, Workers’ Party, Progress Singapore Party, Red Dot United and Singapore Democratic Party. Smaller parties, led by the People’s Alliance for Reform (2.51%), have no card. Vote shares and the “real weight” figures are our own sums of the Elections Department’s constituency results; the 131,820 electors of the uncontested Marine Parade-Braddell Heights GRC cast no vote and are counted only among registered voters.

Left-right positions for the PAP and the Workers’ Party come from the V-Party expert survey (2015, its latest Singapore year), rescaled to our scale; the other three are our judgement, because no recent expert survey covers them.

No recognised national poll of satisfaction with or trust in the government was found, so those gauges and the popularity bar are empty, and no “would never vote for” poll exists. No party publishes an income breakdown, so party finance is shown as unknown. No independent promise tracker exists. Parliamentary voting records could not be retrieved, so some cards have fewer said-vs-done rows than usual.

Press sources are limited to the most trusted brands in the Reuters Institute’s 2026 survey (CNA, The Straits Times, Mediacorp news, Lianhe Zaobao); CNA reports are read through a syndicated copy carrying CNA’s credit. An MP’s resignation for personal reasons is used only to explain the vacant seat.
