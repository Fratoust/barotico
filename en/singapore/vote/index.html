Voting in Singapore

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Voting is compulsory: a non-voter’s name is struck off the register, and restoring it costs a $50 fee without a valid reason (such as being overseas or ill). Voters mark an X, 8 am to 8 pm, with their NRIC or passport. Singaporeans living abroad can register as overseas electors and vote in person at an overseas polling station (10 in 2025) or by post: in 2025, 5,966 voted in person abroad and 3,363 postal ballots were counted. In a walkover constituency there is no poll.

Last turnout: 92.83% in May 2025 (2,438,610 votes, 42,945 of them rejected, out of 2,627,026 electors in contested constituencies) source

The electoral system

  • Voting is compulsory. MPs are elected first-past-the-post, in single-member constituencies (SMCs) or group constituencies (GRCs): 15 SMCs and 18 GRCs in 2025.
  • In a GRC, each party fields a team of 3 to 5 candidates, at least one from a minority race; the team with the most votes takes every seat.
  • Non-Constituency seats go to the best-placed losing opposition candidates: 12 minus the number of opposition MPs elected. In 2025, 12 − 10 = 2, both for the Workers’ Party.
  • If only one team is nominated, there is no poll: in 2025 the PAP won Marine Parade-Braddell Heights GRC (131,820 electors) by walkover.

Sources: Parliament of Singapore: SMCs, GRCs and NCMPs · Elections Department: NCMP seats in 2025 (12 minus 10) · Elections Department: 2025 results by constituency · Elections Department: voting is compulsory · Elections Department: how to vote

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Singapore, voting is compulsory but there is no blank-vote option: an unmarked ballot is rejected. Rejected votes count in turnout, not in the result.

What it is

  • How to vote: mark one box clearly with an “X”. Do not sign or write anything that could identify you. source
  • A ballot is rejected if it is unmarked, marks more than one candidate or team, carries writing that identifies the voter (a signature, an NRIC number), lacks the complete official mark, or is void for uncertainty. A mark other than a cross still counts if the choice is clear. source

How it is counted

  • Rejected votes are part of the votes cast but not of the result: the PAP’s 65.57% in 2025 is a share of the 2,395,665 valid votes, not of the 2,438,610 votes cast. The 2% recount threshold is also calculated without rejected votes. source
  • Voting is compulsory: non-voters are struck off the register and can neither vote nor stand in later elections until restored. Restoration is free with a valid reason (work or study abroad, holiday overseas, illness, childbirth); otherwise it costs a $50 fee. source

The figures

  • 1.76%of votes cast were rejected in the general election of May 2025 (42,945 of 2,438,610), with a turnout of 92.83% in contested constituencies. source

What it changes for the result

  • With first-past-the-post, a rejected ballot changes no seat: the candidate or team with the most valid votes wins the constituency. source
  • Where only one team is nominated there is no poll at all: in 2025, the 131,820 electors of Marine Parade-Braddell Heights GRC could cast neither a valid nor a rejected vote. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards cover every party that won a seat or at least 3% of the valid votes in May 2025: PAP, Workers’ Party, Progress Singapore Party, Red Dot United and Singapore Democratic Party. Smaller parties, led by the People’s Alliance for Reform (2.51%), have no card. Vote shares and the “real weight” figures are our own sums of the Elections Department’s constituency results; the 131,820 electors of the uncontested Marine Parade-Braddell Heights GRC cast no vote and are counted only among registered voters.

Left-right positions for the PAP and the Workers’ Party come from the V-Party expert survey (2015, its latest Singapore year), rescaled to our scale; the other three are our judgement, because no recent expert survey covers them.

No recognised national poll of satisfaction with or trust in the government was found, so those gauges and the popularity bar are empty, and no “would never vote for” poll exists. No party publishes an income breakdown, so party finance is shown as unknown. No independent promise tracker exists. Parliamentary voting records could not be retrieved, so some cards have fewer said-vs-done rows than usual.

Press sources are limited to the most trusted brands in the Reuters Institute’s 2026 survey (CNA, The Straits Times, Mediacorp news, Lianhe Zaobao); CNA reports are read through a syndicated copy carrying CNA’s credit. An MP’s resignation for personal reasons is used only to explain the vacant seat.
