Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

How to vote, in practice

Voting is compulsory from 18 for the Chamber and regional parliaments (16 for the European election). You can give a proxy for a valid reason (illness, work, studies, travel) with a certificate; otherwise justify your absence to the justice of the peace. Belgians abroad register with their consulate.

Last turnout: About 87.5% in June 2024: 12.5% of voters stayed away, a record despite compulsory voting source

How people vote

  • Proportional representation by constituency, separate lists by language.
  • Compulsory voting for federal and regional elections.
  • Federal, regional and European elections on the same day, every 5 years.

How a law is passed

  1. Chamber of Representatives
  2. Senate for institutional texts
  3. Coordination between entities for shared powers
  4. Constitutional Court if referred

Why it's complicated

No party runs nationwide: Flemish and French-speaking parties are separate. Forming a federal government means building majorities on both sides of the language border.

Why it's like thisThis split lets French, Dutch and German speakers live in the same state without one community imposing its choices on the others. The price is slowness.
