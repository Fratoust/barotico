Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Political and economic context

The main parties are either Flemish or French-speaking and stand only in their own part of the country; the PTB-PVDA presents itself as a national party. A federal government therefore brings together parties from both sides of the language border: in 2024-2025 it took 239 days, from the 9 June election to the swearing-in on 3 February.

Sources: Dandoy and De Decker (ULB): Belgium's party systems · BX1: Raoul Hedebouw calls the PTB “the fourth national party” (11 June 2024) · FPS Foreign Affairs: elections of 9 June 2024 · Belgium.be: the De Wever government, sworn in on 3 February 2025

Why it's like thisThe state reforms of 1970 to 2014 turned a unitary state into a federal one made up of communities (language, people) and regions (territory), so that French, Dutch and German speakers run part of their own affairs. Each level has its own government, and decisions that touch several powers need agreement between them.

Sources: Chamber: the genesis of Belgium (state reforms) · European Committee of the Regions: shared powers
