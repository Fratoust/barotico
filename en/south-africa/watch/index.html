Country profile · edited 14 Nov 2026

South Africa

Parliamentary republic with an executive president · 63.5 million people · coalition government since 2024

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 31 monthsNext national and provincial elections: due in 2029 (date to be set)
The National Assembly is elected for five years (section 49 of the Constitution); the current one first sat on 14 June 2024, so its term ends in June 2029. The President must proclaim an election to be held within 90 days of the end of the term, or earlier after a dissolution: the window runs to about mid-September 2029, and the month shown is an estimate. The local government elections of 4 November 2026 are over; the IEC declared the results on 8 November. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.

  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

What to watch in South Africa

Watch list

  • 4 Nov 2026 · pastHeld: in the local government elections the ANC fell to 30.89% nationally, ahead of the DA (24.60%), MK (9.61%), the EFF (6.25%) and ActionSA (5.68%); turnout 52% (45.9% in 2021). 127 councils are hung; the ANC has an outright majority in 51 municipalities, the DA in 12 (IEC declaration, 8 November).
  • 16 Nov 2026 · in 2 daysDeadline for the final report of the Madlanga Commission on criminality, political interference and corruption in the criminal justice system, set up after police commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi's allegations.
  • 22 Nov 2026 · in 8 daysDeadline for the first sittings of the newly elected municipal councils: section 29 of the Municipal Structures Act requires them within 14 days of the council being declared elected, and results were declared on 8 November.
  • 22 Nov 2026 · in 8 daysCoalition talks in the hung metros. Only Cape Town has a majority (DA, 133 of 235 seats). The DA leads without a majority in Johannesburg (91 of 270; ANC 63, ActionSA 43), Tshwane (74 of 214; ActionSA 48, ANC 44), Ekurhuleni (66 of 224; ANC 59, ActionSA 40) and Nelson Mandela Bay (53 of 120; ANC 32); MK leads eThekwini (86 of 223; DA 57, ANC 37); the ANC leads Buffalo City (42 of 100) and Mangaung (35 of 101, DA 34).
  • Date to be setWestern Cape High Court judgment on President Ramaphosa's review of the Section 89 panel report on Phala Phala (heard 2-4 September 2026); public impeachment hearings have been interdicted since 24 July 2026.
  • Date to be setWestern Cape High Court judgment on the DA, AfriForum, Vaderland Stigting and IRR challenges to the Expropriation Act (argued in August 2026).
  • Date to be setAppeal of EFF leader Julius Malema against his firearm convictions and five-year sentence, before a full bench of the Eastern Cape High Court in Makhanda.
  • 1 Feb 2027 · in 79 daysStart of the arms-deal corruption trial of former president Jacob Zuma (MK leader) and Thales in Pietermaritzburg, after the court ruled against further delaying applications.
  • Date to be setANC decision on its national partnership with the DA after its 30.89% local result: its national executive was due to discuss ending it, and some officials want ActionSA (6 seats) to take the DA's place, which would give a reshaped unity government 206 of 400 seats if the other partners stayed (Bloomberg via BusinessTech, 9 November 2026).
  • Date to be setNational and provincial elections due in 2029; the new DA leader has set the goal of leading the national government by then.
