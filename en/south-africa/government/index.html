South Africa

Parliamentary republic with an executive president · 63.5 million people · coalition government since 2024

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.

The National Assembly is elected for five years (section 49 of the Constitution); the current one first sat on 14 June 2024, so its term ends in June 2029. The President must proclaim an election to be held within 90 days of the end of the term, or earlier after a dissolution: the window runs to about mid-September 2029, and the month shown is an estimate. The local government elections of 4 November 2026 are over; the IEC declared the results on 8 November. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in South Africa?

Who's at the wheel

President Cyril Ramaphosa (ANC) heads a Cabinet of 32 ministers from the ten-party unity government (ANC, DA, IFP, PA, FF Plus, UDM, Rise Mzansi, Al Jama-ah, GOOD, PAC). Key posts: Enoch Godongwana (Finance, ANC), John Steenhuisen (Agriculture, DA), Leon Schreiber (Home Affairs, DA), Siviwe Gwarube (Basic Education, DA), Velenkosini Hlabisa (Cooperative Governance, IFP), Gayton McKenzie (Sport, Arts and Culture, PA), Pieter Groenewald (Correctional Services, FF Plus).

After losing its majority on 29 May 2024, the ANC signed a statement of intent with the DA and others; ten parties with 287 seats joined, and Ramaphosa announced the Cabinet on 30 June 2024. Disputes go to a GNU 'clearing house'. The DA changed leader in April 2026 (Geordin Hill-Lewis), but Steenhuisen stayed in Cabinet.

What the government promised

  • Medium-Term Development Plan 2024-2029: inclusive growth and jobs, reducing poverty and the cost of living, a capable and ethical state
  • President Ramaphosa's Opening of Parliament address (July 2024) setting out the GNU priorities

What independent promise trackers found

  • ACT Ubumbano and the SIVIO Institute (independent, Sept. 2025): of 121 promises tracked from the State of the Nation addresses and the development plan, none fully implemented after one year, 9% overall fulfilment progress, climate promises with no progress. source
  • Not independent: Operation Vulindlela (Presidency and National Treasury), Q1 2026/27 report: 60% of electricity reforms delayed, 60% of rail and port reforms on track, water 25% completed. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Stats SA: official unemployment 33.6% in Q2 2026 (8.5 million people), up from 32.7% in Q1; 16.7 million employed. source
  • Stats SA: GDP contracted about 0.2% in Q2 2026 after 0.4% growth in Q1, with manufacturing (-1.8%) and mining (-3.0%) down. source
  • CSIR: energy shed by load-shedding fell 82%, from 4,126 GWh in the first half of 2024 to 749 GWh in the first half of 2025. source
  • Auditor-General (March 2026): 151 of 417 national and provincial auditees (36%) obtained clean audits in 2024-25; fruitless and wasteful expenditure fell to R1.4bn from R3.5bn. source
