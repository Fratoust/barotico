South Africa

Parliamentary republic with an executive president · 63.5 million people · coalition government since 2024

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 31 monthsNext national and provincial elections: due in 2029 (date to be set)
The National Assembly is elected for five years (section 49 of the Constitution); the current one first sat on 14 June 2024, so its term ends in June 2029. The President must proclaim an election to be held within 90 days of the end of the term, or earlier after a dissolution: the window runs to about mid-September 2029, and the month shown is an estimate. The local government elections of 4 November 2026 are over; the IEC declared the results on 8 November. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterRescue South Africa from an ANC-EFF 'doomsday coalition' (Moonshot Pact, 2023)
Half-wayIn 2023 it built the Moonshot Pact to unseat the ANC and accused the PA, Al Jama-ah and GOOD of being ANC fronts. After the ANC fell to 40% in May 2024, it joined a ten-party unity government with the ANC, the PA, Al Jama-ah and GOOD, and its ministers took seats in the Cabinet announced on 30 June 2024. source · 2
On the posterNo expropriation without compensation; secure property rights
ConsistentIn opposition, it voted against the Expropriation Bill, which the ANC passed with its majority on 27 March 2024, and announced a legal challenge. The President signed the Act in January 2025, after the DA had joined the government; its challenge was argued in the Western Cape High Court in August 2026 and judgment is pending. source · 2
On the posterAmend clauses 4 and 5 of the BELA Act on school language and admissions
Half-wayThe President delayed the two clauses for talks, then in December 2024 put the Act fully into force without amending them. The DA called the outcome a 'win-win compromise', and its Basic Education minister was put in charge of implementing the Act. source
On the posterNo tax rises on struggling households: stop the VAT increase
Half-wayIt voted against the fiscal framework with a 0.5-point VAT rise, which passed 194 to 182 on 2 April 2025, and went to court with the EFF; the finance minister withdrew the rise on 24 April. In July 2025 it voted for the final budget, which kept income tax brackets unadjusted for inflation, a measure Treasury expects to raise R18bn. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterAbolish cadre deployment
ConsistentIts bill to end cadre deployment, tabled in 2021 by Leon Schreiber, was rejected on 19 September 2023 by 202 votes (ANC and PAC) to 193. The ANC said public hearings showed it clashed with other legislation and that two government bills covered similar ground. source

  • Partly kept Amend BELA clauses 4 and 5 on school language and admissions: Act in force unamended since December 2024; its minister issues the guidelines source
  • Partly kept No expropriation without compensation: Act signed in January 2025; its court challenge heard in August 2026, judgment pending source
  • Never tested Halve violent crime (2024 manifesto): police is an ANC portfolio; untested at national level

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the DA, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It built a pact to keep the ANC out, then joined the ANC in Cabinet.

It voted against the Expropriation Act in 2024. The Act was signed in 2025 by the government it had joined.

It blocked the 2025 VAT rise, then voted for a budget that left tax brackets unadjusted for inflation. On school language, it called an unamended Act a win-win.

To be fair, the city it runs, Cape Town, was the only metro with a clean audit in 2023-24 (Auditor-General).
