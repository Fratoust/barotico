South Africa

Parliamentary republic with an executive president · 63.5 million people · coalition government since 2024

The National Assembly is elected for five years (section 49 of the Constitution); the current one first sat on 14 June 2024, so its term ends in June 2029. The President must proclaim an election to be held within 90 days of the end of the term, or earlier after a dissolution: the window runs to about mid-September 2029, and the month shown is an estimate. Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. On the posterCreate jobs and grow an inclusive economy
Half-wayIn government throughout the decade. Official unemployment was 33.6% in the second quarter of 2026 (8.5 million people), up from 32.7% in the first, and GDP shrank by about 0.2% in that quarter (Stats SA). source · 2
On the posterLower the cost of living
Half-wayIts finance minister put a 0.5-point VAT rise in the March 2025 budget. The fiscal framework passed 194 to 182 on 2 April, with the DA, MK and the EFF against; on 24 April the minister withdrew the rise, saying he lacked political support, and the Western Cape High Court suspended it on 27 April at the request of the DA and the EFF. Income tax brackets stayed unadjusted for inflation, which Treasury expects to raise R18bn. source · 2 · 3
On the posterOpen national elections to independent candidates (Electoral Amendment Act, 2023)
⚠ Rights alertThe Act, passed in 2023 when it held a majority in the National Assembly, required independent candidates and unregistered parties to gather signatures equal to 15% of the votes needed for a seat, roughly 11,000 to 14,000. On 4 December 2023 the Constitutional Court (One Movement South Africa v President) declared the requirement invalid as an unjustified limit on freedom of association and on the right to stand for office, and set an interim threshold of 1,000 signatures per region. Its Home Affairs minister, Aaron Motsoaledi, accepted the ruling. source · 2 · 3
On the posterManage migration with stricter asylum rules (Refugees Act amendments in force since 2020)
⚠ Rights alertAmendments in force since 1 January 2020, under its government, treated an asylum claim as abandoned if the applicant's visa was not renewed within a month of expiry. On 12 December 2023 the Constitutional Court unanimously confirmed that these provisions (sections 22(12) and 22(13)) are unconstitutional: they exposed applicants to deportation without a hearing on the merits, against the principle of non-refoulement, and limited the rights to dignity, to freedom and security of the person and of children. The invalidity runs back to 2020; the Home Affairs minister was ordered to pay the costs. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • Opened national elections to independent candidates with its 2023 Electoral Amendment Act, but required them to gather signatures equal to 15% of a seat's quota; on 4 December 2023 the Constitutional Court declared that requirement an unjustified limit on freedom of association and set a threshold of 1,000 signatures. source
  • Promised to ease the cost of living; in March 2025 its finance minister announced a 0.5-point VAT increase, withdrawn in April after a court challenge by the DA and the EFF, and the budget had to be tabled again. source
  • Did not click 'submit as final' for 181 candidates in six municipalities; the Constitutional Court put them back on the ballot in October 2026 while 2,093 candidates of 44 other parties stayed excluded. Its secretary-general then said it would review the DA's place in the GNU because the DA had joined the case. source
To be fair
  • Energy lost to load-shedding fell 82% between the first halves of 2024 and 2025 (CSIR), under its electricity minister
  • The murder rate fell from 40.7 to 36.6 per 100,000 between 2024/25 and 2025/26 (SAPS figures)
  • Its finance minister lowered the inflation target to 3% with the Reserve Bank (November 2025)

Who picks up the bill

No breakdown of income is published. Represented parties receive public money from the IEC-managed funds and must declare large donations. For 2025/26 the IEC knew of at least R21m in donations to the ANC, including R10.5m declared late (largest: R10m from a company linked to Patrice Motsepe, R7.6m and R2m from Chancellor House); in April-June 2026 it flagged R15.27m, mainly R15m from the Batho Batho Trust.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the ANC, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its 2023 electoral law opened the ballot to independent candidates. The Constitutional Court then struck down the signature quota it set for them as an unjustified limit on their rights.

The same Court struck down its 2020 asylum rule, which treated a late visa renewal as giving up the claim.

It promised jobs. Official unemployment reached 33.6% in mid-2026, and the VAT rise its minister proposed in 2025 was withdrawn within weeks.

To be fair, energy lost to load-shedding fell 82% in a year (CSIR).
