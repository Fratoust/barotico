South Africa

Parliamentary republic with an executive president · 63.5 million people · coalition government since 2024

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Register with the Electoral Commission (IEC) using a South African identity document; you vote at the station of the voting district where you are registered. The roll for the 4 November 2026 local elections closed on 7 August 2026. Voters who cannot reach their station can apply to the IEC for a special vote. Citizens abroad can register and vote at South African missions in national elections, not in local ones.

Last turnout: 58.61% in the national and provincial elections of 29 May 2024 source

Who decides what

President and CabinetThe National Assembly elects the President from among its members; he is head of state and of government, appoints the Cabinet (32 ministers since June 2024) and can be removed by an impeachment vote under section 89 of the Constitution. Since 2018: Cyril Ramaphosa (ANC), re-elected in June 2024.
ParliamentTwo chambers: the National Assembly (400 members elected by proportional representation) passes laws and the budget and holds the executive to account; the National Council of Provinces represents the nine provinces. A third of the Assembly can ask the Constitutional Court to review a new law within 30 days of its signature.
ProvincesNine provinces, each with an elected legislature and a premier. In KwaZulu-Natal, where MK won 37 of 80 seats in 2024, an IFP-ANC-DA-NFP coalition elected IFP premier Thami Ntuli by 41 votes to 39; the DA governs the Western Cape.
Municipalities257 municipalities, including eight metros, run water, electricity distribution and local services. Only 39 obtained a clean audit in 2024-25, 21 of them in the Western Cape. After the November 2026 elections, Cape Town (DA) is the only metro where one party holds a majority; the other seven councils need coalitions.
Courts and watchdogsThe Constitutional Court has the last word: it jailed former president Zuma for contempt in 2021 and in May 2026 set aside Parliament's 2022 vote on the Phala Phala report. The 'Chapter 9' institutions include the Public Protector, the Auditor-General and the Electoral Commission (IEC), which also polices party funding.

How people vote

  • Voting is voluntary from 18 for citizens registered on the IEC roll. The 400 members of the National Assembly are elected by proportional representation; since 2024 independent candidates may also stand. Turnout was 58.61% in 2024.
  • No party won a majority in 2024: the ANC fell to 40.18%, ahead of the DA (21.8%), MK (14.59%) and the EFF (9.52%). Voters do not elect the President directly: the Assembly does.
  • In local elections, voters choose a ward councillor and a party list for the council; councils then elect mayors, which is why coalitions in the metros change often.

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled in the National Assembly by a minister, a committee or an MP; it goes to a portfolio committee, which holds public hearings.
  2. After the Assembly vote, the National Council of Provinces debates it; constitutional amendments need special majorities (75% of the Assembly for some sections, which the 2021 land amendment failed to reach with 204 of 267 votes).
  3. The President signs it or sends it back; in a coalition he may also delay parts, as with clauses 4 and 5 of the BELA Act in 2024.
  4. Once signed, a law can be challenged before the courts, as the DA, AfriForum and others are doing with the Expropriation Act signed in January 2025.

Why it's complicated

Since June 2024 South Africa has been run by a 'government of national unity' of ten parties holding 287 of 400 seats, led by an ANC that fell to 40% and partnered with the DA, its main rival. They disagree on much: the 2025 budget had to be tabled three times after the DA and EFF stopped a VAT rise in court; the DA is suing over the Expropriation Act signed by the government it sits in; partners differ on foreign policy. The ANC's national general council endorsed the coalition in December 2025 as a 'tactical intervention', and in October 2026 its secretary-general threatened to review the DA's place. Meanwhile the Constitutional Court revived an impeachment inquiry into President Ramaphosa over the Phala Phala farm cash, now paused by an interdict while he challenges the panel report; a commission is examining claims of criminal infiltration of the police; GDP shrank 0.2% in the second quarter of 2026 and official unemployment is 33.6%. In the 4 November 2026 local elections the ANC fell to 30.89% nationally, ahead of the DA (24.60%) and MK (9.61%); 127 councils are hung, including seven of the eight metros.

Why it's like thisThe system was negotiated at the end of apartheid. After the first all-race elections in 1994, the 1996 Constitution made itself supreme over Parliament, guaranteed rights enforced by a Constitutional Court, and created independent 'Chapter 9' watchdogs. Proportional representation was chosen so that small communities would be represented, and the President is elected by Parliament rather than by direct vote. For thirty years the ANC's majorities made coalitions a local matter; the 2024 result made them national. The Zondo Commission on 'state capture' under President Zuma (2018-2022) shaped the current debates on corruption and on the independence of prosecutors and police.

Who's at the wheel

President Cyril Ramaphosa (ANC) heads a Cabinet of 32 ministers from the ten-party unity government (ANC, DA, IFP, PA, FF Plus, UDM, Rise Mzansi, Al Jama-ah, GOOD, PAC). Key posts: Enoch Godongwana (Finance, ANC), John Steenhuisen (Agriculture, DA), Leon Schreiber (Home Affairs, DA), Siviwe Gwarube (Basic Education, DA), Velenkosini Hlabisa (Cooperative Governance, IFP), Gayton McKenzie (Sport, Arts and Culture, PA), Pieter Groenewald (Correctional Services, FF Plus).

After losing its majority on 29 May 2024, the ANC signed a statement of intent with the DA and others; ten parties with 287 seats joined, and Ramaphosa announced the Cabinet on 30 June 2024. Disputes go to a GNU 'clearing house'. The DA changed leader in April 2026 (Geordin Hill-Lewis), but Steenhuisen stayed in Cabinet.

On the posterManage migration with stricter asylum rules (Refugees Act amendments in force since 2020)
