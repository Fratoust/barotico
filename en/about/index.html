About

Why Barotico exists

Many people have turned away from politics because speeches seem far from real life. Barotico puts what parties say next to what they do, with sources, so that everyone can judge for themselves. A smile helps: satire makes facts easier to share, never to bend.

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Neutral by design

Same rules, same caps and the same effort for every party, left and right, government and opposition. No party colours, no labels such as “extremist” or “populist”: we describe acts. Left-right positions come from expert surveys, not from us.

Far left, far right: a warning, not a label

A party at either end of the axis (expert-survey score of 1.5 or more out of 2, left or right, the same threshold for both) is marked “far left” or “far right”. Its verdict does not end with “to be fair” but with “before you vote”: a sourced warning, often the gap between its promises and its votes once it could act. Ends of the spectrum often thrive on crises and contradictions; we show the acts, you decide. Where a single party rules without competition (China), no party is placed on the axis at all: the scale would measure nothing, and we say so on the card.

Promises that change lives

A promise counts as kept only if an independent body measured its effect on people. Passing a law that changes nothing, announcing a scheme that reaches a few hundred people or reversing it a year later counts as “kept on paper”. The aim is not to find something good everywhere, but to give you the keys.

Said vs done: a contradiction must be proven

Each card sets a few promises against what the party did on the same subject: a glimpse, not the full record. We only write “Contradiction” when three independent sources agree: the vote or act, the reason the party gave at the time, and an outside analysis. If the reason holds up (a flawed text, an alternative tabled), it is a political choice, not a contradiction, and we leave it out. “Consistent” means an opposition party voted for its own promise and lost. Parties in power leave more acts behind, so they often have more contradictions: that reflects the record, not a preference.

Rights alert

When a court or an independent human-rights body (constitutional court, European Court of Human Rights, UN committees, Council of Europe) rules that a measure breaches fundamental rights, the line carries a ⚠ Rights alert, whoever passed it, left or right. Keeping such a promise is not an achievement. We never flag a policy goal itself, only a measure found to breach rights, and procedural rulings do not count.

Facts with a source

Every claim links to a recognised source from the country, in its own language: courts, parliaments, statistics offices, public broadcasters, newspapers of record, certified fact-checkers. A complaint or a rumour never appears in the court proceedings section; only an official act does, with the presumption of innocence recalled.

Informed choices, not voting advice

Barotico never tells you whom to vote for and never matches you with a party. The values mirror only shows how values usually split between left and right; then it is up to you to compare with what parties actually do.

Measured, not felt

Country figures come from UNDP, UNODC, RSF, Freedom House and Transparency International. Each carries a badge saying whether it is measured, rated by experts or based on perception, and each country is compared with its own region.

A giant summary

Politics is far more complex than one page. Barotico wants to spark curiosity and lead to the sources. It does not replace reading the programmes, the press and the decisions themselves.

Independent

Barotico is funded by its readers' contributions. It accepts no money from political parties, governments or campaigns.

Corrections and right of reply

Spotted a mistake or an outdated status? Every profile shows its update date: send us the source and we correct it. Parties are invited to reply; their documented answers are added.

Languages

The original site is in English. If your browser uses another language, Barotico offers it automatically: French and Spanish are translated by us, other languages by your browser's own translator.

Method

How a country profile is built

  1. We cover the last ten years, in the country's own language, from national or official sources, never Wikipedia. Every fact links to its source.
  2. Every country follows the same model, piloted on France: the system, the government, the parties, key figures, a dated watch list and the media.
  3. The government has its own barometer (democracy, pluralism, popularity, trust, promises kept, integrity), measured by independent institutes, and, where the data exist, twenty years of public spending, debt and growth.
  4. For each party: its place on the left-right axis, its age and experience of power, then its promises set against its votes and actions. Each row has a status: delivered (with a measured effect), half-way, consistent, contradiction (at least three sources) or rights alert (a ruling by an independent authority).
  5. A democratic watch point flags a promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.
  6. Each party also has a democratic barometer: its real weight among registered voters, the rejection it meets in polls, and the pluralism and polarisation scores of the V-Party expert survey.
  7. Verdicts are written as a chronicle: the facts side by side, with no judging adjectives. The reader draws the conclusion.
  8. The court-cases section holds only official acts (rulings, indictments, sanctions), with their exact status and the same limits for everyone. No verdict, contradiction or promise status ever rests on an ongoing trial.
  9. Every profile is dated, checked by a weekly watch and after each election. The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. A review by people of opposing views and a right of reply for parties are being set up.
