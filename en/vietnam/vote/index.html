Vietnam

One-party socialist republic led by the Communist Party of Vietnam · about 102.3 million people (2025)

No national office is filled by a free, competitive vote: Freedom House scores Vietnam 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework. Power is allocated inside the Communist Party: its 14th Congress closed on 23 January 2026 and the new Central Committee re-elected Tô Lâm General Secretary the same day. Voters then elected a 500-seat National Assembly on 15 March 2026, which in April chose the President, the Prime Minister and the heads of the courts for 2026-2031. The next Congress and the next Assembly election are due when that term ends; no date has been set, so the countdown uses January 2031, by analogy with January 2026, as an estimate. Before that, the Assembly's current session closes on 20 November 2026. source

Voting in Vietnam

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

The 2026 election was held at more than 72,000 polling places in the 34 provinces and cities, two months earlier than usual after the outgoing Assembly shortened its own term (Resolution 199/2025/QH15). Voters aged 18 or over must vote in person and may not ask anyone to vote for them; a voter who cannot write may have someone fill in the ballot. The UN Human Rights Committee notes that proxy voting persists and weighs on women's participation.

Last turnout: No competitive vote. Official turnout in the National Assembly election of 15 March 2026: 99.70% of 76,423,940 registered voters source

The electoral system

  • Voters elect the National Assembly and the local people's councils directly every five years. On 15 March 2026, two months earlier than usual, 863 candidates stood for 500 seats; the official turnout was 99.70% of 76,423,940 registered voters.
  • Candidates are screened through Fatherland Front consultations; no party other than the Communist Party may exist. The UN Human Rights Committee also points to proxy voting and asks the authorities to 'redouble' efforts against it.
  • The Assembly then elects the President, the Prime Minister and the heads of the courts and prosecution. In April 2026 Tô Lâm, already General Secretary, was elected President by 495 votes of 495 voting, and then himself presented the nominations for Prime Minister and the other posts.

Sources: Đại biểu Nhân dân: official result, 99.70% turnout, 76,423,940 voters, 863 candidates for 500 seats (21 March 2026) · UN Human Rights Committee (July 2025), para. 52: Fatherland Front negotiations and proxy voting · Tạp chí Công Thương: Tô Lâm elected President, 495 votes of 495 (7 April 2026) · National Assembly press release No. 3: Tô Lâm presents the nominations (7 April 2026) · Supreme People's Court journal: voting rules for 15 March 2026 (in person, no proxy, valid and invalid ballots)

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Vietnam there is no blank vote: a ballot with every name struck out is invalid, and with no competitive national election and no party other than the Communist Party allowed, no ballot choice changes who holds power.

What it is

  • Voters choose among candidates vetted through Fatherland Front consultations; the UN Human Rights Committee notes the ban on other parties and that independent candidates must go through 'multiple rounds of negotiations' with the Front. source
  • There is no blank vote. Voters strike through the full name of each candidate they reject; a ballot on which every candidate's name is struck out is invalid (Election Law, art. 74, and Home Affairs Circular 21/2025/TT-BNV). source
  • Also invalid: a ballot not issued by the polling team or without its stamp, one leaving more names than there are seats (so an untouched ballot with surplus candidates is invalid), and one with an added name, mark or any other content. Striking out only part of a name still counts as a vote for that candidate. source
  • Voters must vote in person and may not ask anyone to vote for them; doubtful ballots are decided by the whole polling team, which may not alter them. source

How it is counted

  • Results are announced as turnout and the list of those elected: 500 deputies from 863 candidates in March 2026. No breakdown of votes by party exists, since candidates do not run under party labels, and no count of invalid ballots was published with the 2026 results. source

The figures

  • 99.7%of 76,423,940 registered voters took part in the National Assembly election of 15 March 2026, according to the official result. source

What it changes for the result

  • Freedom House scores Vietnam 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework; a blank vote, like any other, does not decide who governs. source
  • The UN Human Rights Committee asks Vietnam to 'redouble its efforts to combat proxy voting', which it says weighs on women's participation. source

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Vietnam has no competitive national election, so this page has a single party card, the Communist Party's; the Vietnam Fatherland Front is not a party and is described in the system blocks. The left-right axis does not apply: no expert survey places a ruling single party, so the card has no position. Vote shares and a rejection poll are left empty because candidates run without party labels and no independent political polling is published; the seat chart counts Party members as the 500 deputies minus the 18 officially announced non-Party deputies.

Several figures are missing or dated. The homicide gauge uses the latest available UNODC figure (2011). Population is the Population Department's 102.3 million. No approval or trust poll exists, the Party publishes no audited accounts, no independent promise tracker exists, and no official count of invalid ballots was published for 2026. The cabinet block does not link ministers to the Party card because the official list does not state party membership.

Press: with no independent domestic media, state outlets (the national news agency, Party, Assembly, government, court and inspectorate organs) are cited only for what the authorities announce, such as votes, verdicts and results. Findings on rights and the regime come from UN treaty bodies, Freedom House, RSF, UNDP and Transparency International. The judicial record lists only cases with an official act from the last ten years, within the site's caps, and notes the UN finding that accountability for officials is often applied selectively and for political ends; resignations without a criminal or disciplinary act are left out.
