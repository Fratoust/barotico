Vietnam

One-party socialist republic led by the Communist Party of Vietnam · about 102.3 million people (2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
no free national vote≈ 50 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national steps are the Communist Party's 15th Congress and a new National Assembly election, both due in 2031 at the end of the 2026-2031 term
No national office is filled by a free, competitive vote: Freedom House scores Vietnam 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework. Power is allocated inside the Communist Party: its 14th Congress closed on 23 January 2026 and the new Central Committee re-elected Tô Lâm General Secretary the same day. Voters then elected a 500-seat National Assembly on 15 March 2026, which in April chose the President, the Prime Minister and the heads of the courts for 2026-2031. The next Congress and the next Assembly election are due when that term ends; no date has been set, so the countdown uses January 2031, by analogy with January 2026, as an estimate. Before that, the Assembly's current session closes on 20 November 2026. source

Đảng Cộng sản Việt Nam (Communist Party of Vietnam)

Leaders
Tô Lâm (General Secretary)
Seats
Ruling party; about 5.6 million members; 482 of 500 National Assembly deputies elected in 2026 are Party members
Founded
1930 · 96 years old Long-established partyas Đảng Cộng sản Đông Dương (Indochinese Communist Party, from October 1930), then Đảng Lao động Việt Nam (Workers' Party of Vietnam), renamed in 1976 · source
In power
Has led the national government

Not placed on the left-right axisIn a single-party system with no competitive election, expert surveys do not place parties on a left-right scale. The Party's doctrine (Marxism-Leninism, a 'socialist-oriented market economy') and its economic record (markets, foreign investment, a growing private sector) point in different directions, so we give it no position and no 'far left' or 'far right' label.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterFight corruption with 'no forbidden zones and no exceptions'
Half-wayIn the 2021-2026 term 174 centrally managed officials were disciplined and 66 prosecuted (Tô Lâm, December 2025), and 7 Politburo members left their posts. Transparency International counts Vietnam among the countries whose score rose steadily since 2012, to 41 in 2025, crediting 'effective management of petty corruption'. In July 2025 the UN Human Rights Committee found that accountability mechanisms for officials are 'often selectively applied and politically motivated' and asked for independent, transparent anti-corruption bodies. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterGrowth of 10% a year or more over 2026-2030, and double-digit growth in 2026
Half-wayGDP grew 9.01% over the first nine months of 2026, with 9.95% in the third quarter (Statistics Office). The IMF, ADB and AMRO forecast 8.2%, 7.8% and 7.5% for the full year. Consumer prices rose 4.52% on average over the same period. source · 2
On the posterA 'lean, strong' state apparatus and a two-tier local government
Half-wayThe National Assembly merged 63 provinces into 34 and abolished the district level from 1 July 2025; about 10,035 communes became roughly 3,320, and the government said at least 230,000 officials and employees would retire early or leave the public sector. No independent body has yet published an evaluation of savings or service quality. source · 2
On the posterFreedom of expression, of the press and of association, guaranteed by the Constitution
⚠ Rights alertIts Assembly passed the 2018 Law on Cybersecurity and keeps Criminal Code articles 109, 116, 117 and 331; the government added Decrees 53/2022, 147/2024 and 126/2024. On 25 March 2019 and again on 15 July 2025 the UN Human Rights Committee found these provisions overly broad, restricting freedom of expression (Covenant, art. 19) and of association (art. 22), and asked for them to be amended or abolished. The government's delegation replied that the cybersecurity law 'did not hinder human rights'. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterElections in which the people choose their representatives
⚠ Rights alertIn March 2026, 863 candidates vetted through the Fatherland Front stood for 500 seats and the official turnout was 99.70%. On 15 July 2025 the UN Human Rights Committee, reviewing the right to take part in public affairs (Covenant, art. 25), was concerned by the ban on parties other than the Communist Party, the Fatherland Front negotiations imposed on independent candidates and proxy voting, and asked for 'transparent, fair and free elections' and 'genuine political pluralism'. The government's delegation said there had been 'no cases of serious violation of electoral regulations'. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • The Party has a rule on leaders 'setting an example'; on 20 March 2024 its Central Committee found that President Võ Văn Thưởng had breached it, as well as the rules on what members must not do, and agreed that he leave all his posts, about a year after taking office. source
  • The Party presents elections as the people choosing their representatives; the UN Human Rights Committee (July 2025) notes the ban on all other parties, that independent candidates must go through 'multiple rounds of negotiations' with the Fatherland Front, and that at least three independent candidates were detained before the 2021 election. In 2026, 18 of the 500 deputies elected are not Party members. source
  • In July 2025 the government told the UN that its policy safeguards freedom of expression, which is 'not an absolute right'; the Human Rights Committee counted at least 160 people reportedly jailed for peaceful expression and asked for Criminal Code articles 109, 116, 117 and 331 to be amended or abolished. source
To be fair
  • Human Development Index up 53.5% since 1990, to 0.766 in 2023, in the 'high human development' group (UNDP, 2025)
  • GDP growth of 8.02% in 2025, the second-highest rate since 2011, with GDP per head reaching about US$5,026 (Statistics Office)
  • Abolished the death penalty for eight offences in the 2025 Criminal Code amendment, a step welcomed by the UN Human Rights Committee

A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're wondering what this party has delivered, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its January 2026 congress report criticised a habit of 'talking a lot and doing little'. The same congress set growth of 10% a year or more; the first nine months of 2026 came in at 9.01%.

It promised a fight against corruption with 'no forbidden zones': 174 senior officials disciplined in five years and seven Politburo members gone. In 2025 the UN Human Rights Committee found such accountability 'often selectively applied and politically motivated'.

In March 2026, 99.70% of voters turned out. Of the 500 deputies they elected, 18 are not Party members.

To be fair, Vietnam's Human Development Index has risen 53.5% since 1990, to 0.766 (UNDP).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Vietnam has no competitive national election, so this page has a single party card, the Communist Party's; the Vietnam Fatherland Front is not a party and is described in the system blocks. The left-right axis does not apply: no expert survey places a ruling single party, so the card has no position. Vote shares and a rejection poll are left empty because candidates run without party labels and no independent political polling is published; the seat chart counts Party members as the 500 deputies minus the 18 officially announced non-Party deputies.

Several figures are missing or dated. The homicide gauge uses the latest available UNODC figure (2011). Population is the Population Department's 102.3 million. No approval or trust poll exists, the Party publishes no audited accounts, no independent promise tracker exists, and no official count of invalid ballots was published for 2026. The cabinet block does not link ministers to the Party card because the official list does not state party membership.

Press: with no independent domestic media, state outlets (the national news agency, Party, Assembly, government, court and inspectorate organs) are cited only for what the authorities announce, such as votes, verdicts and results. Findings on rights and the regime come from UN treaty bodies, Freedom House, RSF, UNDP and Transparency International. The judicial record lists only cases with an official act from the last ten years, within the site's caps, and notes the UN finding that accountability for officials is often applied selectively and for political ends; resignations without a criminal or disciplinary act are left out.
