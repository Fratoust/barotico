Serbia

Parliamentary republic · about 6.55 million people (RZS estimate, 2025)

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Aleksandar Vučić resigned as President on 27 September 2026 to campaign for parliament 'without holding office'. Ana Brnabić, Speaker of the dissolved Assembly, is acting President for at most three months. The Law on the President requires the vote within three months of the vacancy, so 27 December at the latest, and it must be called 30 to 60 days in advance. Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Adult citizens living in Serbia are on the single voter register automatically and vote in person at their polling station with an identity document. Citizens abroad must apply in advance to vote at a diplomatic mission. People who cannot move because of illness can ask to vote at home.

Last turnout: About 68.8% in the October 2026 snap parliamentary election (RIK preliminary count). source

Who decides what

StateA single chamber, the Narodna skupština (250 MPs), passes laws and elects the government led by the Prime Minister. The President of the Republic is directly elected for five years (two terms at most), proposes the Prime Minister and can return a law once.
Courts and control bodiesThe Constitutional Court (Ustavni sud) reviews laws and decrees; the Supreme Court tops the courts. The State Audit Institution audits public money, the Agency for the Prevention of Corruption receives party accounts and asset declarations, and the Fiscal Council assesses the budget.
ProvincesVojvodina, in the north, is an autonomous province with its own assembly and government. The Constitution also names Kosovo as a province; Kosovo declared independence in 2008, which Serbia does not recognise.
Cities and municipalitiesBelgrade and the other cities and municipalities elect their assemblies, which then choose the mayor.
European UnionSerbia is an EU candidate country and negotiates accession; it is not a member and uses the dinar.

How people vote

  • Every four years, or earlier if the Assembly is dissolved, citizens aged 18 and over elect 250 MPs from closed national lists in a single constituency. Lists need 3% of the votes cast; national-minority lists are exempt from the threshold.
  • Seats are shared in proportion to votes. In the October 2026 snap election, the Student List took about 53% of the vote and 141 seats, an absolute majority, on the count of 28 October.
  • The President is elected separately by direct vote in up to two rounds.

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled by the government, an MP, the Assembly of Vojvodina or 30,000 voters.
  2. The relevant committee examines it, then the Assembly debates it in general and article by article.
  3. Vote: a majority of MPs present, with at least half of all MPs in the chamber; some laws need an absolute majority of all 250.
  4. The President promulgates it or returns it once; the Constitutional Court can later annul it.

Why it's complicated

On 1 November 2024, 16 people died when the canopy of the renovated Novi Sad railway station collapsed. Student-led protests followed for two years. Prime Minister Miloš Vučević resigned in January 2025 after an attack on students in Novi Sad, and Đuro Macut's government followed in April. Vučić announced snap elections more than 20 times before dissolving the Assembly on 9 September 2026; he then resigned the presidency to lead his party's list. On 25 October the Student List won an absolute majority and Vučić conceded. Observers noted strong competition but misuse of public resources and broadcasters favouring the ruling list. As of 31 October, voting must be repeated at about 50 polling stations, Macut's government stays in office until a new one is elected, no prime minister has been named, and a presidential election is due by 27 December.

Why it's like thisMultiparty elections returned in 1990, under Slobodan Milošević. After the October 2000 protests that ended his rule, a parliamentary system with a directly elected president was kept. The current Constitution dates from 2006, the year Montenegro left the union with Serbia. Proportional lists in one national constituency were meant to give every vote the same weight, and minority lists were exempted from the threshold so that Hungarians, Bosniaks, Albanians and others are represented.

Who's at the wheel

Đuro Macut, Prime Minister since 16 April 2025, now in office until a new government is elected. Ana Brnabić has been acting President since 28 September 2026.

The Assembly elected in December 2023 approved Macut's cabinet by 153 votes to 46, after Vučević's resignation. A no-confidence motion failed in July 2026. On 7 September the government proposed dissolution, and on 9 September Vučić dissolved the Assembly. In the 25 October vote his list lost its majority: on the count of 28 October, the Student List had 141 seats and United Serbia 103.
