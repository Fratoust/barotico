Serbia

Parliamentary republic · about 6.55 million people (RZS estimate, 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 1 monthsPresidential election (first round by 27 December 2026, not yet called on 13 November)
Aleksandar Vučić resigned as President on 27 September 2026 to campaign for parliament 'without holding office'. Ana Brnabić, Speaker of the outgoing Assembly, is acting President for at most three months. The vote must take place within three months of the vacancy, so 27 December at the latest, and it must be called 30 to 60 days in advance. On 30 October Brnabić gave 27 November as the deadline to call it, and said someone else could take over as acting President if the new institutions were formed quickly. The Assembly elected on 25 October 2026 holds its first session on 17 November and sits for four years. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. The number of cases partly reflects a party's size and years in power.

Only two lists passed the 3% threshold in October 2026. Five national-minority lists won seven seats between them: two each for the Alliance of Vojvodina Hungarians (0.8%) and the Albanian Coalition, one each for 'Rasim Ljajić – Ljudski', SDA Sandžaka – Čuvari Sandžaka and Usame Zukorlić's list; none reaches 3% or a parliamentary group, so they have no card. SPS, in government since 2012, ran as 'Ivica Dačić – Faktor stabilnosti' and won 2.1% and no seats. The opposition parties of the outgoing Assembly ran no lists of their own; some, such as Boris Tadić's SDS, backed the Student List.

Half-wayThe blockades led the government to publish documents: 195 by December 2024, against the 857 held by prosecutors, by the students' count. On the posterFree and fair elections, with a full audit of the voter register (its programme)
Half-wayThe student movement behind the list called for snap elections from May 2025, saying the government had been 'deaf' to its demands, and the Assembly was dissolved on 9 September 2026. The full audit did not happen before the vote. An independent review of the register was introduced, but ODIHR reported in July 2026 doubts that it would produce results in time, and in October observers noted persistent distrust in the register. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • It says a seat is a public duty that belongs to the citizens. In July 2026 the actress Bojana Maljević withdrew from the list because future candidates had to sign a statement. 'You cannot defend democracy with mechanisms that limit the freedom of a mandate', she wrote, citing 'authoritarian patterns' in the movement. The Niš students denied having vetoed her. The list's rules require candidates to respect its programme, to protect 'the integrity of the list', and some students said the wording could be changed (Vreme). source
  • It says its candidates must answer to voters and explain their decisions. It went to the polls without naming a candidate for prime minister. On 10 November, five days after RIK's final results, none had been named. List candidate Dejan Šoškić said students decide on the new government 'in communication with' people on the list, and that there would be several candidates for prime minister and for each ministry. source
  • At the start of the blockades in late 2024, protesters said they wanted to change 'the system, not the government' and kept their distance from parties. By May 2025 student accounts were demanding snap elections and a change of government, saying there had been 'enough time to meet the demands' and that the government had shown itself 'deaf' to them. In October 2026 their list won an absolute majority. source
To be fair
  • Its demands led the government to raise 2025 funding for universities' material costs by 20% (decision of 12 December 2024).
  • Its demand for transparency led the government and the railway infrastructure company to publish canopy reconstruction documents (195 by December 2024).
  • Its main demand since 2025, a snap parliamentary election, was met when the Assembly was dissolved on 9 September 2026.

If you're considering voting for Studentska lista, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It says a mandate belongs to the citizens. In July 2026 an actress left its list because candidates had to sign a statement she compared to an 'imperative mandate'. The list's rules say candidates must respect its programme.

It rejects any cult of personality. On 25 October it won an absolute majority, 140 seats of 250, without naming a candidate for prime minister. By 10 November, final results in hand, it still had not.

Its programme, from the voter-register audit to halting the Jadar mine, is untested: it has never governed. Its own rules, meanwhile, bar students from standing as its candidates.

To be fair, the blockades behind it obtained 20% more money for state universities' material costs in 2025 (government decision of 12 December 2024).

Leaning right

On the posterOn the record
On the posterAn average wage above €1,400 by 2027 ('Srbija 2027' plan)
Half-wayWages did rise: RZS measured net wages up 8.5% in real terms in January–August 2026. But the average net wage was RSD 117,282 in August 2026, about €1,000, with a median of RSD 93,325. The finance minister repeated the €1,400 target in early 2026; Vučić's new 'Srbija 2030' plan aims for €1,320 by 2030. source · 2
On the posterA 'full stop' to the Jadar lithium mine (January 2022)
ContradictionAfter mass protests, PM Ana Brnabić annulled the project's spatial plan on 20 January 2022: 'we have met all the demands and put a full stop to Rio Tinto'. She repeated the 'full stop' in the November 2023 campaign; after the December vote, Vučić said he 'would dig lithium tomorrow' if it were up to him. On 16 July 2024 its government restored the plan, citing a Constitutional Court ruling that the 2022 decree exceeded the government's powers. The Court had said its decision does not revive the plan and leaves further steps to the government; Brnabić had by then recast 2022 as a 'pause'. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the posterA 'more efficient and fairer' judiciary (2026 judicial laws)
Half-wayOn 28 January 2026 its majority passed, by urgent procedure, SNS MP Uglješa Mrdić's amendments to five judicial laws. In April the Venice Commission's urgent opinion accepted the efficiency goal but found that the changes 'individually and cumulatively remove' existing safeguards of prosecutorial autonomy. The government redrafted them: on 16 June the Commission found seven of its nine key recommendations implemented, and the Assembly adopted the new amendments on 25 June. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterODIHR recommendations 'fully applied in every election process' (PM Brnabić, December 2023)
Half-wayODIHR found that conditions in the December 2023 election 'were not equal'. Since then the election laws have taken up a number of its recommendations, capped campaign spending and introduced an independent review of the voter register. In July 2026 ODIHR said key recommendations were still not implemented, notably on misuse of state resources, pressure on voters, campaign-finance oversight and balanced media. In October observers again found that the ruling list benefited from misuse of public resources. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterShow 'our European face' and help refugees, with no fences (Vučić, 2015)
⚠ Rights alertIn February 2017, with Vučić as prime minister, border police drove 17 Afghans, seven of them minors, to the Bulgarian border at night, took their documents and made them cross, although a court in Pirot had ordered them placed in an asylum centre. Serbia's Constitutional Court had already found their rights violated when, in O.H. and Others v. Serbia (February 2026), the ECtHR found a collective expulsion, inhuman treatment (Article 3) and unlawful detention (Article 5). We found no reaction from the party. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • In May 2025 party president Miloš Vučević called on members and supporters to blockade the court in Novi Sad, saying 'no one will be able to enter the building', until four SNS activists were released from detention. They were held on suspicion of beating two students. Aleksandar Vučić backed the idea and accused courts of releasing 'terrorists and coup plotters'. Days later the party dropped the plan, Vučević saying it did not want to disrupt citizens' lives (Insajder). source
  • In 2023 ODIHR found that 'conditions were not equal', mainly because the President campaigned for the ruling list. In 2026 Vučić resigned the presidency to campaign 'without holding office'. Observers still found that the ruling party benefited from the misuse of public resources, and that public and private broadcasters favoured its list. source
  • Vučić's 'Srbija 2027' plan promised an average wage of €1,400 by 2027, and the finance minister repeated it in early 2026. RZS measured €1,057 in December 2025, with a median of €773. The new 'Srbija 2030' plan targets €1,320 three years later. source
To be fair
  • Inflation was down to 4.1% year on year in September 2026, in line with the central bank's expectations (RZS / NBS).
  • The average wage reached RSD 124,089 (€1,057) in December 2025 (RZS).
  • Public debt stood at 43.7% of GDP at the end of May 2026, well below the EU average (Ministry of Finance).
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Integrity
  • Investigation Tomislav Momirović (former transport minister in SNS-led governments): investigated by the Prosecutor's Office for Organised Crime since 1 August 2025. Prosecutors suspect that annexes to the Novi Sad–Kelebija railway contract let the contractor bill the state for about US$115.6 million of damage. The Belgrade Higher Court ordered 30 days' detention in August 2025. He denies guilt. No indictment as of January 2026; presumption of innocence applies.
  • Investigation Goran Vesić (former construction, transport and infrastructure minister in SNS-led governments): investigated in the same organised-crime case on the Novi Sad–Kelebija railway (suspected corruption offences, alleged damage over US$115 million). Prosecutors said in January 2026 that police and other state bodies were slowing the probe. No indictment; presumption of innocence applies.
  • Other
  • Trial Goran Vesić (same): referred to trial with 12 others for a serious crime against public safety over the Novi Sad canopy collapse. The Novi Sad Court of Appeal confirmed the indictment on 15 June 2026, overturning the Higher Court; no trial date yet. Presumption of innocence applies.
