Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights.   • "We stoppen met het huisvesten van statushouders in de sociale sector en geven Nederlanders altijd voorrang" (stop housing refugees with a residence permit in social housing and always give priority to Dutch nationals; 2025 programme, p. 23).On 22 September 2025 the Council of State's advisory division advised the cabinet not to submit its bill banning priority in social housing for refugees with a residence permit (statushouders), finding unequal treatment contrary to the Constitution (art. 1); on 24 June 2026 it gave the same advice on an identical initiative bill. source · 2 · 3Guarantee at stake: equality before the law

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you’re considering voting for FvD, it’s worth looking beyond the slogans.

It wants voters to decide directly, through binding referendums and recall. After the March 2026 local elections, it kept candidates who had won preference votes out of their seats in four municipalities, calling one of them ‘list filler’.

It won 299 council seats and entered no coalition, demanding not one new asylum place; several parties had also ruled it out.

In May 2025 the Tweede Kamer, by 100 votes to 40, reprimanded its founder Thierry Baudet and suspended him conditionally for not registering his roles in three companies. In 2023 it had suspended him and Gideon van Meijeren for a week over an undeclared side activity.

Before voting, it’s worth asking: do the party’s actions match its promises?

Left-right positions are a rescaled reading of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that could not be re-checked against the 2024 wave; DNA has no expert score and is placed with the PVV. In the seat chart, groups without a card are placed by our own estimate.

Party finances come from the 2024 filings to the Interior Ministry archived by DNPP; no income breakdown could be read for VVD, CDA and PVV, and DNA has no accounts yet. No independent promise tracker was found; the CPB/PBL analysis of the coalition agreement is shown instead. The satisfaction gauge is Ipsos I&O's September 2026 figure, the latest that gives a satisfied share. The homicide rate is CBS victims divided by CBS population.
