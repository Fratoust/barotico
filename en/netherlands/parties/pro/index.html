Before you read

How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterScrap the cuts to unemployment, sickness and old-age benefits
Half-wayIn April 2026 the Senate adopted, by a broad left-to-right majority, its senator Paul Rosenmöller’s motion asking the cabinet to drop the faster rise in the state-pension age. On 26 May the cabinet withdrew that bill and shelved the unemployment (WW) and disability (WIA) cuts for now, while keeping a savings target of about €2.7 billion a year. source · 2
On the posterWork for everyone, including people with disabilities (Participation Act, 2015)
Half-wayThe act of PvdA state secretary Jetta Klijnsma, passed in February 2014 with VVD, PvdA, D66, CU, SGP and CDA votes, took effect in 2015. The SCP planning office found in 2019 that it had hardly raised job chances: young people with disabilities more often had work (38% against 29% three years in), but with lower incomes and more temporary contracts; for welfare recipients chances went from 7% to 8%, and former sheltered workers’ chances fell. source · 2
On the posterFight benefit fraud (childcare benefit, SyRI)
⚠ Rights alertPvdA sat in Rutte II (2012–2017); its social affairs minister Lodewijk Asscher submitted the SyRI law and quit as leader over the affair in 2021. The national human-rights institute (College voor de Rechten van de Mens) found that the tax office’s fraud approach hit parents of non-Dutch origin far harder in 2014 and 2018, and in October 2023 formally ruled it indirect discrimination by origin. In February 2020 the Hague District Court ruled the SyRI fraud-detection law a breach of the right to private life (Art. 8 ECHR); the State did not appeal. The government said it deeply regretted the discrimination. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you’re considering voting for PRO, it’s worth looking beyond the slogans.

It wants the wealthiest and big polluters to pay for everything, from defence to dropped benefit cuts. The amount is not named.

In September 2026 Jesse Klaver refused to cooperate with ‘demolition policy’. By late October, he saw room for a deal.

In 2025 it ran as GroenLinks-PvdA under Frans Timmermans, who stepped down on election night. It has since renamed itself PRO, a name that was not on the ballot.

To be fair, after the Senate adopted its motion in April 2026, the cabinet withdrew the faster rise in the state-pension age.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page covers the nine largest groups in the Tweede Kamer by seats, as listed on the chamber's group page on 15 November 2026. The eight others (DENK, SGP, PvdD, ChristenUnie, SP, 50PLUS, Volt and Mona Keijzer's one-member group, each with three seats or fewer) have no card under the nine-card cap. PRO is the former GroenLinks-PvdA, and the DNA card covers the seven former PVV MPs who sit as Groep Markuszower.

Left-right positions are a rescaled reading of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that could not be re-checked against the 2024 wave; DNA has no expert score and is placed with the PVV. In the seat chart, groups without a card are placed by our own estimate.

Party finances come from the 2024 filings to the Interior Ministry archived by DNPP; no income breakdown could be read for VVD, CDA and PVV, and DNA has no accounts yet. No independent promise tracker was found; the CPB/PBL analysis of the coalition agreement is shown instead. The satisfaction gauge is Ipsos I&O's September 2026 figure, the latest that gives a satisfied share. The homicide rate is CBS victims divided by CBS population. The result of the 12 November 2026 Tax Plan vote could not be confirmed from an official source for this update.

Press links come only from the validated outlets in the media panel; Parlement.com and DNPP are used as documentation references. Criminal complaints without an official act appear only in the watch list; acquittals or dismissals older than twelve months and private conduct are left out.
