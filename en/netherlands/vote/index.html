Voting in the Netherlands

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Residents are registered automatically: a voting pass arrives by post and is shown with ID at any polling station in your municipality. You may vote by proxy. Dutch citizens abroad must register once with the municipality of The Hague and can then vote by post or at some embassies.

Last turnout: 78.3% in the October 2025 general election (10.64 million of 13.59 million voters) source

The electoral system

  • Voters choose a candidate on a party list; the 150 seats are allocated nationally.
  • No legal threshold: a seat (the kiesdeler) is worth the total of valid votes divided by 150, about 0.67%. In 2025, 15 parties entered parliament.
  • Preference votes count: a candidate with more than a quarter of the kiesdeler moves ahead of the list order.
  • The Eerste Kamer (75) is elected indirectly by the members of the twelve provincial councils.

Sources: Parlement.com: seat allocation and preference votes · DNPP (Groningen): 15 parties in 2025 · Senate: how the Eerste Kamer is elected

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In the Netherlands, a blank vote counts in turnout but not for seats. It must be handed in with nothing written on it.

What it is

  • Blank vote: the ballot put in the box with nothing filled in. source
  • Careful: writing anything on it, even “blank”, or a name or signature, makes the vote invalid, not blank. A mark other than a red-filled box is also invalid. source

How it is counted

  • Blank votes are listed separately in the official result. They count in turnout but are not shared out among parties. source

The figures

  • 0.38%of votes cast were blank in October 2025: 40,128, against 28,206 invalid votes, with a turnout of 78.3%. source
  • 0.19%of valid votes were blank in 2023 (19,655): blank votes doubled in two years. source

What it changes for the result

  • With proportional representation, every valid vote counts towards the sharing of seats; a blank vote counts only in turnout. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Barotico does not recommend voting blank, nor avoiding it. These questions are there to help you decide with the facts in hand.

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page covers the nine largest groups in the Tweede Kamer by seats, as listed on the chamber's group page on 15 November 2026. The eight others (DENK, SGP, PvdD, ChristenUnie, SP, 50PLUS, Volt and Mona Keijzer's one-member group, each with three seats or fewer) have no card under the nine-card cap. PRO is the former GroenLinks-PvdA, and the DNA card covers the seven former PVV MPs who sit as Groep Markuszower.

Left-right positions are a rescaled reading of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that could not be re-checked against the 2024 wave; DNA has no expert score and is placed with the PVV. In the seat chart, groups without a card are placed by our own estimate.

Party finances come from the 2024 filings to the Interior Ministry archived by DNPP; no income breakdown could be read for VVD, CDA and PVV, and DNA has no accounts yet. No independent promise tracker was found; the CPB/PBL analysis of the coalition agreement is shown instead. The satisfaction gauge is Ipsos I&O's September 2026 figure, the latest that gives a satisfied share. The homicide rate is CBS victims divided by CBS population. The result of the 12 November 2026 Tax Plan vote could not be confirmed from an official source for this update.

Press links come only from the validated outlets in the media panel; Parlement.com and DNPP are used as documentation references. Criminal complaints without an official act appear only in the watch list; acquittals or dismissals older than twelve months and private conduct are left out.
