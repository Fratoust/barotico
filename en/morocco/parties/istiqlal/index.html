How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterA law on conflicts of interest
Half-wayIts group tabled a conflict-of-interest bill in September 2020, in opposition. It then spent 2021-2026 in government without such a law being adopted; the 2026-2031 programme of the government it joined again commits to 'a specific law on conflicts of interest'. source · 2
On the posterFight rents and protect citizens' rights
⚠ Rights alertIts government's civil procedure code, carried by Justice minister Abdellatif Ouahbi (PAM), passed the House on 23 July 2024 by 104 votes to 35. On 4 August 2025 the Constitutional Court struck down several provisions on substance: powers letting the Justice minister move cases between courts and run court IT and judge assignments breached judicial independence; a prosecutors' right to seek annulment of final judgments breached legal certainty and fair trial. Ouahbi first welcomed the ruling, then said in December 2025 that the Court had 'no right' to rule on provisions not contested. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterFight rents and protect citizens' rights
⚠ Rights alertIts government's law reorganising the National Press Council passed the House on 22 July 2025 by 87 votes to 25. On 22 January 2026 (decision 261/26), on a referral by 96 deputies, the Constitutional Court struck down five provisions: publishers' majority over elected journalists breached equality and press self-regulation (art. 28), a rule letting one organisation take every publisher seat breached pluralism, and a disciplinary set-up breached impartiality. It upheld the rest of the law. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • In March 2025, while minister in the Akhannouch government, its leader said livestock importers had made huge profits at the population's expense, about 13 billion DH, from subsidies granted by that same government. source
  • Campaigned in 2026 for a 40% corporate tax on monopoly rents; the government programme it signed with the PAM left it out, keeping only its anti-rent wording 'nearly verbatim'. source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Istiqlal, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It fights rents in its slogans. At the coalition table, it dropped its 40% tax on them, in the name of 'realism of execution'.

From inside the cabinet, its leader denounced the importers enriched by the cabinet's subsidies.

It tabled a conflict-of-interest bill in opposition in 2020. After five years in government, the law is back where it started: in a programme.

To be fair, the government it sat in paid direct social aid to 4.18 million households.
