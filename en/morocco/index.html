Morocco

Constitutional monarchy · about 37 million people · new government since October 2026

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

97 seats out of 395 are enough to lead the government.

Article 47 of the Constitution has the King appoint the head of government from the party that comes first, whatever its share. The PAM came first in 2026 with under a quarter of the seats, then needed three partners to reach 207. In 2021 the RNI had led with 102; five years later it sits in opposition with 66.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Register on the general electoral lists (listeselectorales.ma or your local authority) during the annual revision; for 2026 the deadline was 13 June. Bring your electronic national ID card on polling day. Moroccans abroad registered on the lists vote by proxy: they designate a voter registered in Morocco through an online platform (elections.ma), open until the day before the vote.

Last turnout: 38.02% in the legislative elections of 23 September 2026, against 50.18% in 2021 (when local and regional elections were held the same day) source

Who decides what

The KingUnder the 2011 Constitution, King Mohammed VI is head of state, Commander of the Faithful and supreme commander of the armed forces. He appoints the head of government from the party that came first and the ministers on her proposal, chairs the Council of Ministers (strategic orientations, organic laws, senior appointments), presides the Superior Council of the Judiciary and may dissolve parliament. Freedom House notes that the 2011 reforms shifted some authority to parliament while the palace keeps substantial formal powers.
Head of government and cabinetThe head of government directs the administration, chairs the Government Council and is invested when the House of Representatives approves her programme by absolute majority. Since October 2026: Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri (PAM) and 31 other members.
ParliamentTwo chambers: the House of Representatives (395 members elected directly for five years), which has the last word on most laws and can bring down the government, and the House of Councillors (120 members chosen indirectly by local councillors, professional chambers and unions).
CourtsThe Constitutional Court (12 members: six appointed by the King, three elected by each chamber) rules on electoral disputes and strips the seats of MPs who become ineligible, as it did for several deputies in 2024. The Court of Audit (Cour des comptes) audits public spending and party accounts.
Regions and communesTwelve regions and about 1,500 communes have elected councils (last elected in 2021); walis and governors appointed by the State oversee them.

How people vote

  • Voting is voluntary from 18, but you must be on the general electoral lists: for 2026, new registrations closed on 13 June. On polling day the electronic national identity card is required.
  • The 395 deputies are elected by proportional list voting: 305 in local constituencies and 90 on regional lists. A party needs 20 deputies to form a parliamentary group, which is why small parties team up (UC and MDS formed a 25-member group in 2026).
  • The head of government is not elected directly: the King appoints her from the party that came first, and she then needs a majority of 198 deputies.

How a law is passed

  1. Bills come from the head of government or from members of parliament; government bills are adopted in the Government Council, and those on strategic matters or organic laws also go to the Council of Ministers chaired by the King.
  2. Both chambers examine the text in committee and in plenary; if they disagree, the House of Representatives decides in second reading, as with the penal procedure code adopted on 22 July 2025 by 130 votes to 40.
  3. Organic laws must be checked by the Constitutional Court before promulgation; ordinary laws can be referred to it.
  4. The law enters into force once promulgated by royal dahir and published in the Bulletin officiel.

Why it's complicated

Freedom House rates Morocco Partly Free (37/100): regular multiparty elections, but it finds that the King and the palace keep dominance through substantial formal powers, and its score for the chance of an opposition winning real power is 1 of 4. Turnout fell to 38.02% in September 2026. In autumn 2025 the youth-led GenZ 212 movement protested over hospitals and schools; three people died at Leqliaa during what the authorities described as an attack on a gendarmerie post, and prosecutors said 2,480 people were prosecuted. Some thirty MPs of the 2021-2026 legislature faced the courts, among them members of the RNI, PAM, Istiqlal, MP, USFP, PPS and UC. The RNI, which led the outgoing government, fell from 102 to 66 seats and is now in opposition to its former partners PAM and Istiqlal.

Why it's like thisMorocco became independent in 1956 as a monarchy with several parties, among them the Istiqlal of the independence struggle and later the USFP. Constitutions from 1962 onward kept the King at the centre. In 1998 the King named the socialist Abderrahmane Youssoufi prime minister in an 'alternance' government. After the February 20 Movement protests of 2011, a new Constitution adopted by referendum created a head of government drawn from the party that comes first and widened parliament's powers, while keeping the King's role in strategic areas. The Islamist PJD led governments from 2011 to 2021; the RNI from 2021 to 2026; the PAM since 2026.

Who's at the wheel

Head of government Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri (PAM), the first woman in the post, appointed by the King on 29 September 2026. Her 32-member government, appointed on 16 October, rests on the PAM, Istiqlal, USFP and PPS (207 of 395 seats). Fouzi Lekjaa (PAM) holds Economy and Finance, Abdellatif Ouahbi stays at Justice, Nabil Benabdallah (PPS) takes Education.

The PAM came first on 23 September 2026 with 97 seats. Under Article 47 the King appointed its coordinator; the four-party alliance was announced on 12 October, and on 22 October the House of Representatives approved the 2026-2031 programme by 204 votes to 127, the RNI voting against.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • HCP: unemployment 12.3% in 2021 and 13.0% in 2025 (1.621 million unemployed); 37.2% for 15-24 year-olds; 193,000 net jobs created in 2025 after 82,000 in 2024. source
  • HCP: consumer prices rose 6.6% in 2022, then 0.8% in 2025. source
  • Cour des comptes: health-insurance (AMO) coverage rose from 60% to 70% between 2022 and 2024 (25.62 million people with open rights), but more than 11 million remain outside, and several schemes are in deficit. source
