Austria

Federal parliamentary republic · 9.2 million people · 9 Länder

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterFairness: family allowance for children abroad at local prices
⚠ Rights alertA flagship of its coalition with the ÖVP, in force since January 2019: family allowance and child tax credits for children living in other EU states followed local price levels. On 16 June 2022 the EU Court of Justice (C-328/20) ruled this indirect discrimination on grounds of nationality, in breach of EU social-security rules and the free movement of workers. Austria had repaid about €337 million for 143,705 children by October 2022. On the posterOn the record
On the posterSound public finances
ContradictionIn April 2024 the Fiscal Council forecast a 2024 deficit of 3.4% of GDP, above the EU's 3% limit, mainly because of new government measures: a longer electricity price brake, suspended energy taxes and a housing package. Its finance ministry called that 'not comprehensible', held to 2.9% and raised its forecast above 3% only in October, after the election. The deficit reached 4.6% (Statistik Austria) and Austria is under an EU excessive deficit procedure. Finance minister Magnus Brunner cited the crises of recent years and said he should have been stricter with the Greens. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterLower non-wage labour costs: work must pay
Half-wayThe 2027-28 double budget passed by its coalition on 10 July 2026 cuts the employer contribution to the family fund from 3.7% to 2.7% from 2028, worth about €1.5 billion a year. Meanwhile the reduced unemployment-insurance rates of 1.1 million low earners, 70% of them women, are phased out by 2032, and from 2027 employers pay these contributions up to the statutory retirement age. source · 2
On the posterFirm against political Islam: no headscarf for young girls
⚠ Rights alertIts MPs and the FPÖ passed a headscarf ban in primary schools on 15 May 2019; its MP Rudolf Taschner called the headscarf a symbol of oppression. On 11 December 2020 the Constitutional Court struck it down: it singled out Islam without justification, breaching equality and the state's religious neutrality, which rests on freedom of religion. Its ministers said the ruling must be accepted while regretting it. In December 2025 its coalition with SPÖ and NEOS banned headscarves for girls under 14; in July 2026 the court dismissed early challenges as premature, without ruling on substance. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterWork must pay: tougher social assistance (2019)
⚠ Rights alertIts government with the FPÖ passed the social-assistance framework law in 2019: full benefits only with German at B1 or English at C1, and steeply falling rates for each extra child. On 17 December 2019 the Constitutional Court (G 164/2019) struck down both as breaches of the principle of equality: the child scale could leave large families without what they need to live, and many people can work without that language level. Its group leader August Wöginger said the party could not understand the ruling, but that it was final in a state under the rule of law. source · 2 · 3
On the posterFairness: family allowance for children abroad at local prices
⚠ Rights alertA flagship of its government with the FPÖ under family minister Juliane Bogner-Strauß, in force since January 2019 and meant to save €114 million a year: payments for children living in other EU states followed local price levels. On 16 June 2022 the EU Court of Justice (C-328/20) ruled this indirect discrimination on grounds of nationality, breaching EU social-security rules and the free movement of workers. Minister Susanne Raab said the ruling must be accepted, though she still found the idea fair; her coalition with the Greens lifted the indexation and had repaid about €337 million for 143,705 children by October 2022. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • It preaches sound finances, yet in the 2017 campaign it spent €12.96 million, nearly €6 million over the €7 million legal cap; the Parties Transparency Senate fined it €800,000 in 2020. source
  • It led the previous government with the Greens: the public deficit reached 4.6% of GDP in 2024, well above the EU's 3% limit, and Austria is now under an EU excessive deficit procedure. source

To be fair
  • Its coalition with the Greens passed the Freedom of Information Act ending official secrecy; Freedom House raised Austria's transparency score for it in 2026.
  • Its government cut the deficit from 4.6% to 4.2% of GDP in 2025.
  • Asylum applications fell 36% to 16,284 in 2025, the lowest since 2020, after its interior minister suspended family reunification (ministry figures).

On the posterCut non-wage labour costs
Half-wayThe 2027-28 double budget it passed with ÖVP and SPÖ on 10 July 2026 cuts the employer contribution to the family fund from 3.7% to 2.7%, but only from 2028. Meanwhile the reduced unemployment-insurance rates of 1.1 million low earners are phased out by 2032, and from 2027 employers pay these contributions up to the statutory retirement age. source · 2
On the posterHalve party funding (2024 campaign)
Half-wayIn government it could not get a cut past the ÖVP: in June 2026 the coalition agreed only not to raise party, parliamentary-group and academy funding in 2027 and 2028. Its MP Veit Dengler, who still calls for halving it, asked for a separate vote on that budget item; the request found no support, and the coalition, NEOS included, passed the budget on 10 July 2026. source · 2 · 3
On the posterCivil liberties: no state spyware
Half-wayIn opposition its MPs, with the SPÖ, took the ÖVP-FPÖ 'Bundestrojaner' to the Constitutional Court, which struck it down in December 2019 for breaching the right to private life. In government it refused the first draft of messenger surveillance, then backed a revised law passed on 9 July 2025: an order by a three-judge panel, a legal-protection officer with more time and staff, and a special report above 30 uses a year. Group leader Yannick Shetty cited the overhaul; its MPs Nikolaus Scherak and Stephanie Krisper voted against. source · 2 · 3
On the posterFairness between generations: reform pensions
Half-wayIts coalition's July 2025 reform brought in a partial pension from 2026, cut partial retirement from five years to three and added a mechanism that forces measures if pension spending overshoots a set path by 2030. The statutory retirement age was not raised: in June 2026 its social spokesman Johannes Gasser called for an open debate on it, and the SPÖ replied that the matter was settled for this term. source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • It promised lower non-wage labour costs; the April 2026 budget deal it signed cuts them only from 2028 and raises unemployment-insurance contributions for many workers first. source
  • It campaigns against political privileges; in 2025 its state secretary Sepp Schellhorn was given an Audi A8 as official car instead of the usual A6, which the Greens and the FPÖ criticised. source
  • It campaigned for sound budgets; in government the deficit was still 4.2% of GDP in 2025, and the Fiscal Council forecasts 3.9% for 2026, above the EU's 3% limit. source
To be fair
  • Its coalition passed a pension reform in July 2025: a partial pension from 2026 and partial retirement limited to three years instead of five.
  • Voted for the 2025 freeze on rent rises in regulated flats.
  • Its coalition capped high special pensions in 2025, a law passed unanimously.

Leaning left

On the posterOn the record
On the posterA climate law with binding targets
Half-wayAustria has had no climate protection law since the old one expired at the end of 2020. In government with the ÖVP it said it wanted one by autumn 2024; in September 2023 there was still no jointly agreed draft, with the ÖVP described as hesitant, and the coalition ended without one. source
On the posterAn affordable climate ticket for all public transport
DeliveredIts climate ministry launched the KlimaTicket in October 2021: 243,754 annual passes were sold in 2023, about twice the 124,000 forecast, with €520 million in federal subsidies to the end of 2024. The Court of Audit found that the expected shift from car to rail saves about 0.2% of transport emissions. source
On the posterPut a price on CO2
DeliveredIts eco-social tax reform with the ÖVP introduced a CO2 price. Greenhouse-gas emissions fell about 15% in three years, to 66.6 million tonnes in 2024; the ministry names CO2 pricing among the causes, along with renewables, an exceptionally warm year and a weaker economy. source
On the posterTake in refugee children from Moria (2020)
Half-wayAfter the Moria camp burned in September 2020, vice-chancellor Werner Kogler wanted Austria to take in children; the ÖVP refused and offered €1 million in aid on site. On 14 September its MPs did not back the SPÖ and NEOS motions to admit children, which failed; group leader Sigrid Maurer said there was no majority and that it would be a breach of the coalition. source · 2 · 3
On the posterTax inherited millions
ConsistentIn opposition, its urgent motion to bring back an inheritance and gift tax on million-euro inheritances was rejected in February 2026: only the Greens voted for it, against ÖVP, SPÖ, NEOS and FPÖ. source
Also worth knowing
  • In government from 2020 to 2025 it wanted a new climate law with binding targets; Austria has had none since the old one expired at the end of 2020, and the ÖVP-Greens coalition never passed one. source
  • Its flagship KlimaTicket needed €520 million in federal subsidies from October 2021 to 2024; the Court of Audit found the expected shift from car to rail saves about 0.2% of transport emissions. source
  • It accuses the government of making cuts at the expense of the middle class, yet in October 2025 it voted for the pension adjustment that gives full inflation compensation only up to €2,500. source
To be fair
  • The KlimaTicket sold about 243,754 annual passes in 2023, twice the forecast (Court of Audit).
  • Greenhouse-gas emissions fell about 15% in three years, to 66.6 million tonnes in 2024 (Umweltbundesamt inventory).
  • Co-wrote the Freedom of Information Act ending official secrecy, which Transparency International Austria calls a 'milestone'.
