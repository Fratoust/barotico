The Federal President is elected directly for exactly six years. Alexander Van der Bellen's second term began on 26 January 2023, so his successor must be elected in 2028, probably in the autumn; he cannot run a third time in a row. The next Nationalrat election is due in 2029. source

On the posterOn the record
On the posterLet the people decide: binding referendums
Half-wayIts 2017 coalition deal with the ÖVP made referendums automatic only from 2022, and only above 900,000 signatures; Strache had wanted 250,000. In October 2018 the 'Don't smoke' petition drew 881,569 signatures; its group leader Walter Rosenkranz ruled out a referendum, citing the coalition agreement, and said even a million signatures would not have changed that. The government fell in 2019 and the reform was never passed. source · 2
On the posterLess welfare as a pull factor for immigration (2019 social-assistance law)
⚠ Rights alertIts government with the ÖVP passed the framework law in 2019: full benefits only with German at B1 or English at C1, and steeply falling rates for each extra child. On 17 December 2019, on a challenge by SPÖ members of the Bundesrat, the Constitutional Court (G 164/2019) struck down both as breaches of the principle of equality: the child scale could leave large families without what they need to live, and many people can work without that language level. Herbert Kickl said the court had set 'the magnet for unqualified immigration' back to maximum. source · 2 · 3
On the posterFairness: family allowance for children abroad at local prices
⚠ Rights alertA flagship of its coalition with the ÖVP, in force since January 2019: family allowance and child tax credits for children living in other EU states followed local price levels. On 16 June 2022 the EU Court of Justice (C-328/20) ruled this indirect discrimination on grounds of nationality, in breach of EU social-security rules and the free movement of workers. Austria had repaid about €337 million for 143,705 children by October 2022. Herbert Kickl said Austria should pay 'not a cent' for children abroad, and the FPÖ was the only party to vote against lifting the indexation. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterMake amends for the Covid measures: refund the fines (Lower Austria)
Half-wayIn Lower Austria, where it has governed with the ÖVP since 2023, the coalition set up a €31.3 million Covid fund in May 2023; the FPÖ's Reinhard Teufel called it 'the heart of reconciliation'. When it closed on 1 September 2025 it had paid about €4.6 million, 15% of the total, including €157,000 for 480 refunded fines. The Court of Audit found no traceable basis for the €31.3 million and said that refunding lawful fines sent a questionable signal for the rule of law. source · 2 · 3
On the posterPensions that fully keep up with inflation
ConsistentOn 16 October 2025 only its MPs voted for its motion to adjust all pensions fully to inflation. It was rejected, and the coalition's adjustment, passed with the Greens, gave the full 2.7% only up to €2,500 a month. source
On the posterDefend our culture: no headscarf for young girls at school
⚠ Rights alertIts MPs and the ÖVP passed a headscarf ban in primary schools on 15 May 2019. On 11 December 2020 the Constitutional Court struck it down: it singled out Islam without justification, breaching equality and the state's religious neutrality, which rests on freedom of religion. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • It promises binding referendums. In October 2018, in government, its parliamentary leader Walter Rosenkranz ruled out a referendum on the smoking ban after 881,569 people signed the 'Don't smoke' petition, citing the coalition deal that delayed binding referendums to 2022; he said even 900,000 or a million signatures would not have changed that. source
  • It campaigns against the privileges of the 'system'. The Ibiza video, filmed in 2017 and published in May 2019, showed its then leader Strache discussing state contracts for a supposed Russian investor in return for support such as a takeover of the Kronen Zeitung, and how donations could be routed through associations past the Court of Audit (prosecutors later dropped that investigation). source
  • In 2020 the Parties Transparency Senate fined it almost €300,000, including €185,000 because its parliamentary group ran Strache's Facebook page, which counts as an illegal donation to the party (appealable at the time). source

  • Never tested Asylum stop and 'Fortress Austria' (2024 campaign): never in federal government since 2019, after its talks with the ÖVP collapsed in February 2025; untested source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for FPÖ, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promises binding referendums. The last time it governed, it refused one on the smoking ban despite 881,569 signatures, citing a coalition deal that delayed them to 2022.

It rails against the privileges of the 'system'. Its then leader was filmed on Ibiza explaining how donations could slip past the Court of Audit; prosecutors later dropped that investigation.

In 2020 it was fined because its parliamentary group ran that leader's Facebook page, which counted as an illegal donation to the party. Its asylum stop and full pension indexation have never been tested in government.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?
