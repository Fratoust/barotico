Austria

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 23 monthsNext national vote: Federal President
The Federal President is elected directly for exactly six years. Alexander Van der Bellen's second term began on 26 January 2023, so his successor must be elected in 2028, probably in the autumn; he cannot run a third time in a row. The next Nationalrat election is due in 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Half-wayThey are not in the 2025 coalition programme: in March 2025 its finance minister Markus Marterbauer said they were 'not up for debate for the next five years', while still calling them sensible. In February 2026 its MPs voted against the Greens' motion to bring back an inheritance tax on millions, citing coalition discipline. source · 2 · 3
On the posterFreeze all rents until the end of 2026, then at most 2% a year (2024 programme)
Half-wayIts coalition froze regulated rents (old-building, municipal and cooperative flats) for 2025 and capped their rises at 1% in 2026 and 2% in 2027; free-market rents may pass on only half of any inflation above 3%. Rent inflation fell from 6.7% in 2024 to 4.3% in 2025, and rents including running costs rose 3.3% in the year to mid-2026 (Statistik Austria). source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the posterPensions that keep up with prices
Half-wayIn October 2025 it voted for a 2026 adjustment that gives the full 2.7% only up to €2,500 a month (about 71% of pensioners) and a flat €67.50 above. For 2027 most pensions rise 2.95%, less than the 3.3% given to the smallest ones; Birgit Gerstorfer, its representative on the Seniors' Council, said she respects the budget but still wants full compensation for all. source · 2 · 3
On the posterKeep Vienna's €365 annual transit pass (2025 city campaign)
ContradictionOn 25 February 2025, two months before the city election, its Vienna finance councillor Peter Hanke raised the 2025 deficit forecast to €3.8 billion and said 'the €365 annual pass stays'; Mayor Michael Ludwig pledged in the campaign not to raise it. On 1 September 2025 its councillors announced €467 from 2026, citing the budget situation and greater consolidation needs. That €3.8 billion forecast was already public when the pledge was made, and the 2025 deficit came in at about €2.8 billion (preliminary). source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • It campaigns for an inheritance tax on millions. In February 2026 its MPs voted against the Greens' motion to bring one back, citing coalition discipline; its finance minister Marterbauer backs the idea, but not in this parliament. source
  • It promises pensions that keep up with prices; in October 2025 it voted for an adjustment that gives the full 2.7% only up to €2,500 a month, a flat €67.50 above that, and only half in the first year for new pensioners. source
  • In 2020 the Parties Transparency Senate fined it €30,000 for overspending the 2017 campaign cap by €383,430. source
To be fair
  • Pushed the 2025 freeze on rent rises for regulated flats; its state secretary estimated savings of €140 million a year for 1.5 million people.
  • About 71% of pensioners received the full inflation adjustment for 2026, and care allowance rose at every level.
  • Its finance minister cut the deficit from 4.6% to 4.2% of GDP in 2025.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept, made a difference Stop rent rises: rents in regulated flats frozen for 2025; rent inflation fell from 6.7% in 2024 to 4.3% in 2025 (Statistik Austria) source · measured effect
  • Broken Wealth and inheritance taxes on millionaires (2024 programme): not in the coalition deal, and its MPs voted against the Greens' inheritance-tax motion in 2026 source
  • Partly kept Pensions protected from inflation: full 2.7% for 2026 only up to €2,500 a month (71% of pensioners), a flat €67.50 above source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for SPÖ, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It wants to tax inherited millions. In February 2026 its MPs voted against a Greens motion to do exactly that, citing coalition discipline; its own finance minister had already said such taxes were off the table until 2029.

It promises pensions that keep up with prices. For 2026, full compensation stops at €2,500 a month.

On the promise-o-meter: regulated rents frozen, wealth taxes shelved, pensions only partly indexed.

To be fair, in 2025, the year regulated rents were frozen, rent inflation fell from 6.7% to 4.3% (Statistik Austria).
