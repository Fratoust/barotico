Austria

Federal parliamentary republic · 9.2 million people · 9 Länder

155 days passed between the September 2024 election and the swearing-in of a government, led by the party that came second.

The FPÖ came first with 28.8%. Its leader Herbert Kickl was asked to form a government, but talks with the ÖVP broke down over the distribution of ministries and he returned the mandate on 12 February 2025. On 3 March 2025 the ÖVP, SPÖ and NEOS took office together.

Sources: Al Jazeera: three-party government sworn in (3 March 2025) · Tiroler Tageszeitung: final 2024 result · News.at: Kickl returns the mandate (12 February 2025)

Who decides what

Federal (Bund)The Nationalrat (183 members) passes federal laws; the Bundesrat represents the Länder; the Federal President is directly elected for six years.
States (Länder)9 Länder, including Vienna, each with its own parliament (Landtag), which also elects the Land's members of the Bundesrat; the governor (Landeshauptmann) also carries out federal laws.
Municipalities2,096 municipalities.
European UnionAustria is under the EU excessive deficit procedure (current phase 2025-2028).

Sources: European Committee of the Regions: Länder, Bundesrat, municipalities · Parliament: how the Federal President is elected · Parliament: the road to a seat in the Nationalrat · Fiscal Council: June 2026 report

How a law is passed

  1. A bill comes from the government, members of the Nationalrat, a committee, the Bundesrat or a citizens' initiative (Volksbegehren).
  2. The President of the Nationalrat sends it to a committee, then it is voted on in plenary.
  3. Every law passed is sent to the Bundesrat: it can only be published if the Bundesrat raises no objection or gives its consent.
  4. The Federal President certifies the law, the Chancellor countersigns it and it is published in the Federal Law Gazette. The Constitutional Court (VfGH) reviews laws.

Sources: Parliament: how a federal law is made · Constitutional Court: history

Political and economic context

The FPÖ won the 2024 election with 28.8% but sits in opposition. The ÖVP and SPÖ had 92 of the 183 seats, a majority of one: so they brought in the NEOS (18), and the coalition holds 110 seats. Austria is under the EU excessive deficit procedure; in June 2026 the Fiscal Council still forecast deficits of 3.6% to 4% of GDP a year until 2029, above the 3% limit.

Sources: Tiroler Tageszeitung: final 2024 result · Fiscal Council: June 2026 report

Why it's like thisThe 1920 Federal Constitution, drafted under the lawyer Hans Kelsen, made Austria a federal state and gave the Constitutional Court the power to review laws. In 1929 a reform strengthened the Federal President, now elected by the people, as a counterweight to parliament. From 1933 an authoritarian government sidelined parliament and paralysed the Court; in 1938 Austria was annexed by the German Reich. The Constitutional Court was re-established in 1945.

Sources: Parliament: 100 years of the Federal Constitution · Constitutional Court: history · Parliament: direct election of the President since 1929
