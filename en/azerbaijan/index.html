Country profile · 1 Nov 2026

Azerbaijan

Presidential republic · South Caucasus, on the Caspian Sea

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Next vote≈ 24 monthsNext national vote: parliamentary election, due in November 2028 (date estimated). Elections are held but are not free (Freedom House: 'Not Free', 6/100; OSCE/ODIHR: 'absence of genuine political alternatives' in 2024)
The Milli Majlis was elected early, on 1 September 2024. Under the Constitution, after a snap election the next regular vote is held in November of the fifth year, so the term can be shorter than five years; we count the first Sunday of November 2028, to be confirmed. Ilham Aliyev was re-elected in a snap presidential vote on 7 February 2024 for a seven-year term; presidential term limits were abolished by referendum in 2009. source

105 deputies voted to end their own term early. 1 wanted to stay.

On 21 June 2024 the Milli Majlis asked the President, by 105 votes to 1, to call a snap election. The ruling party cited the COP29 climate summit in Baku in November 2024, the very month the regular election was due. The vote was moved forward by two months, to 1 September.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Citizens from 18 are on the voter list of their registered address and vote at their polling station with an identity document. Citizens abroad vote at polling stations set up in embassies and consulates.

Last turnout: 37.27% in the September 2024 parliamentary election according to the election commission, the lowest since independence; 76.73% in the February 2024 presidential election source

Who decides what

PresidentHead of state and the centre of power, elected for seven years with no term limit since 2009. Ilham Aliyev, in office since 2003, was credited with 92% in February 2024. Freedom House describes power as 'heavily concentrated' in his hands and his extended family's.
Cabinet of MinistersLed by Prime Minister Ali Asadov, appointed by the President; it runs the economy and the budget and answers to the President.
Milli Majlis (parliament)125 deputies, each elected in a single-member district for five years. Since 2024: 68 for the New Azerbaijan Party, 44 independents and 13 seats shared by 10 small parties.
CourtsThe Constitutional Court confirms election results. Freedom House scores judicial independence and due process 0 out of 4 each.

How people vote

  • Voting is voluntary from 18. Each voter elects one deputy in their district by simple majority; there is no party-list vote and no minimum turnout.
  • Candidates, party members or not, must collect 400 voters' signatures in their district to register with the Central Election Commission.
  • The President is elected by direct vote, with a run-off if no one wins a majority. Constitutional changes go to referendum (2009, 2016).

How a law is passed

  1. Bills come mostly from the President, but also from deputies and other bodies named in the Constitution.
  2. The Milli Majlis debates and adopts them, usually in three readings.
  3. The President signs the law or sends it back; parliament can override with a qualified majority.
  4. Changes to the Constitution are put to a referendum: in 2016 voters approved a longer presidential term and new vice-president posts.

Why it's complicated

Azerbaijan holds elections, but Freedom House rates it 'Not Free' (6/100, with 0/40 for political rights). OSCE/ODIHR's final reports found the 2024 presidential and parliamentary votes 'took place in a restrictive environment marred by the absence of genuine political alternatives', with irregularities that 'raised serious concerns about whether ballots were counted and reported honestly'. In September 2024 the election commission's preliminary results gave the New Azerbaijan Party 68 of 125 seats, independents 44 and ten small parties 1 to 3 seats each, on a 37.27% turnout, the lowest for a parliamentary vote since independence. The Popular Front Party boycotted; Musavat ran and won nothing. A 2023 party law raised the minimum membership from 1,000 to 5,000 and forced all parties to re-register; the Venice Commission and ODIHR warned it could restrict pluralism. In January 2024 the Council of Europe's Parliamentary Assembly refused to ratify Azerbaijan's delegation, citing unmet commitments on elections and judicial independence; the delegation walked out. Since late 2023, independent newsrooms have been prosecuted: Abzas Media staff got 7.5 to 9 years (upheld by the Supreme Court in April 2026), Toplum TV staff and researchers 12 to 15 years (July 2026), and the Meydan TV trial is under way; PACE's rapporteur called the Abzas convictions 'a transparent attempt to silence independent investigative journalism', and Human Rights Watch called the Toplum TV verdict the harshest of the crackdown. Popular Front leader Ali Karimli has been detained since November 2025, charged with plotting a violent seizure of power in the same case in which Ramiz Mehdiyev, head of the Presidential Administration for 24 years, was given a 14-year suspended sentence in a closed trial in October 2026; HRW calls the charges against Karimli 'unfounded'. Party cards: we show the ruling party, REAL (the only party in parliament that calls itself opposition) and the two main extra-parliamentary opposition parties. The other nine small parties with 1 to 3 seats (Civic Solidarity 3; Justice, Law and Democracy 2; one seat each for National Independence, Democratic Reforms, Vatan, Great Order, Great Azerbaijan, National Front and Democratic Enlightenment) have no card: we found no independent record of their votes or programmes solid enough for three sourced gaps.

Why it's like thisAzerbaijan became independent from the Soviet Union in 1991, in the middle of a war with Armenia over Nagorno-Karabakh. Heydar Aliyev, a former Soviet-era leader, took power in 1993 and gave the 1995 Constitution a strong presidency; his son Ilham succeeded him in 2003. Referendums removed presidential term limits (2009) and lengthened the term and created vice-presidencies (2016); the Venice Commission said the 2016 changes upset the balance between branches of power. After the 2020 war and a September 2023 military operation, Azerbaijan took control of Nagorno-Karabakh and nearly all its ethnic Armenian population left; a peace agreement with Armenia was initialed in Washington in August 2025.

Who's at the wheel

President Ilham Aliyev, chairman of the New Azerbaijan Party, and Prime Minister Ali Asadov's cabinet, backed by a parliament where the party holds 68 of 125 seats; many of the 44 independents are believed to be linked to the government (OC Media).

Through two snap elections in 2024 that ODIHR found lacked genuine political alternatives: 92% for Aliyev in February (76.73% turnout, main opposition boycotting) and a parliamentary majority in September (37.27% turnout).

If you're considering voting for Musavat, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In February 2024 it stayed out of the presidential vote, citing the lack of a democratic environment, of prisoner releases and of basic freedoms.

In September it ran about 25 candidates for parliament, against the advice of its leading member Tofig Yagublu, then in detention. None was elected.

It then rejected the results and asked for new elections, as it had after 2020.

To be fair, it has never governed, so no statistics office has measured a result of its own; in about 25 districts it put an opposition name on a ballot that ODIHR found short of genuine alternatives.
