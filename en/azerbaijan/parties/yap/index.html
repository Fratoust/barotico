Azerbaijan

Presidential republic · South Caucasus, on the Caspian Sea

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 24 monthsNext national vote: parliamentary election, due in November 2028 (date estimated). Elections are held but are not free (Freedom House: 'Not Free', 6/100; OSCE/ODIHR: 'absence of genuine political alternatives' in 2024)
The Milli Majlis was elected early, on 1 September 2024. Under the Constitution, after a snap election the next regular vote is held in November of the fifth year, so the term can be shorter than five years; we count the first Sunday of November 2028, to be confirmed. Ilham Aliyev was re-elected in a snap presidential vote on 7 February 2024 for a seven-year term; presidential term limits were abolished by referendum in 2009. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterThe 'Great Return': about 140,000 displaced people resettled in Karabakh by the end of 2026
Half-wayThe first State Programme, under a late-2021 presidential decree, set the 140,000 target; Caucasian Knot now cites about 120,000 for its first phase. Official counts gave 13,745 returnees in May 2025 and 30,261 people in 41 settlements by 11 March 2026. Analyst Toghrul Juvarly says the goal 'will obviously not be achieved'; residents cite unresolved property issues and a lack of jobs. source · 2
On the posterA peace treaty with Armenia
Half-wayIts text was initialed in Washington on 8 August 2025. A year later it is still not signed: Baku wants the provisions it reads as territorial claims removed from Armenia's Constitution first. Meanwhile goods transit to Armenia through Azerbaijan, fuel deliveries have begun and border commissions agreed working procedures in April 2026, according to experts from both countries quoted by APA. source
On the posterA democratic, law-based state with several parties
⚠ Rights alertIn December 2022 the Milli Majlis, where it held 65 of 125 seats, passed a party law raising the minimum membership from 1,000 to 5,000 and making every party re-register. On 10-11 March 2023 the Venice Commission and OSCE/ODIHR found the 'far-reaching and intrusive' checks of member lists 'incompatible' with the necessity test for limits on freedom of association, called the 5,000 figure 'problematic' and urged dropping re-registration. In July 2023 the party re-registered 'with our 750,000 members', while the Ministry of Justice first rejected the lists of REAL, Musavat and the Popular Front. We found no response from the party to the opinion. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterCyber-security and order in the information space (2017 and 2021 laws)
⚠ Rights alertOn 10 March 2017 parliament, where it held a majority, unanimously let the authorities block websites without a court order when content 'endangers the state or society'; Vice-Speaker Bahar Muradova cited cyber-security. On 13 June 2024 the European Court of Human Rights (RFE/RL Inc. and Others v. Azerbaijan) found these provisions 'expressed in terms of unfettered power', without safeguards against arbitrariness, in breach of freedom of expression; the government had cited national security. In June 2022 the Venice Commission found that 'many provisions' of the media law passed on 30 December 2021 fail European standards on freedom of expression, and that it should not be applied 'as it stands'. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6
Also worth knowing
  • Its majority passed the 2023 party law raising the minimum membership from 1,000 to 5,000 and forcing every party to re-register, which the Venice Commission and ODIHR said could have 'a restrictive effect on pluralism'. source
  • Joined the Council of Europe in 2001 with commitments on elections and the rule of law; in January 2024 PACE refused to ratify the Azerbaijani parliament's delegation (76 votes to 10), citing unmet commitments, and the delegation left hours before the vote. source
  • In June 2024 the European Court of Human Rights ruled that blocking azadliq.org and three other news sites since 2017-18 breached freedom of expression: the blocking rules its majority passed in 2017 were 'expressed in terms of unfettered power'. source
To be fair
  • Minimum wage raised by 65 manat to 400 manat from January 2025, which also lifts public-sector pay scales tied to it
  • Peace agreement text with Armenia initialed in Washington in August 2025; goods now transit through Azerbaijan to Armenia
  • Inflation at 5.6% in March 2026, inside the central bank's target band

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept Resettle about 140,000 displaced people in Karabakh and East Zangezur by the end of 2026: 30,261 people had returned by March 2026 source
  • Partly kept A signed peace treaty with Armenia: text initialed in August 2025, not yet signed or ratified source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for New Azerbaijan Party, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The 'Great Return' was to bring about 140,000 people back to Karabakh by the end of 2026. In March 2026 the official count stood at 30,261.

Its majority raised the membership needed to run a party from 1,000 to 5,000. The ruling party re-registered 'with our 750,000 members'; the Venice Commission found the new checks on member lists 'incompatible' with freedom of association.

In January 2024 PACE refused to ratify its parliament's delegation, which had left hours before the vote. In June the European Court of Human Rights found that the website-blocking rules its majority passed in 2017 gave the authorities 'unfettered power'.

To be fair, the minimum wage rose by 65 manat to 400 in January 2025, above the 285-manat subsistence minimum.

No official programme found online yet.
