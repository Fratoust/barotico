Indonesia

Presidential republic · about 280 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 27 monthsNext national elections (president, DPR, DPD): 2029, date not set
On 26 June 2025 the Constitutional Court ruled (No. 135/PUU-XXII/2024) that national elections (president, DPR, DPD) and local elections (regional councils and heads) must be held separately, the local ones two to two and a half years later. National elections are expected in 2029 and local ones around 2031. The KPU has not set a date, and the election law must first be revised; the month shown is only an estimate. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

The systemThe governmentThe partiesVotingKey figuresMedia

104 ministers and deputy ministers were sworn into the cabinet in October 2024.

The Kabinet Merah Putih started with 48 ministers and 56 deputy ministers (detik). On a single day in February 2024, voters also handled five ballots at once: president, DPR, DPD, provincial council and local council.

Sources: detik: 48 ministers and 56 deputy ministers in the Kabinet Merah Putih (October 2024)

Who decides what

NationalThe president and vice-president are elected together by direct vote. The DPR (580 seats) passes laws and the budget with the government; the DPD represents the regions.
ProvincesEach province has an elected governor and an elected provincial council (DPRD).
Regencies and citiesAn elected regent or mayor and an elected local council (DPRD); from about 2031 these will be chosen in separate local elections.
Constitutional Court (MK)Reviews laws against the Constitution and settles election-result disputes; in 2025 it reshaped the election calendar.

Sources: DPR research centre: 2024 national results (president, DPR, DPD) · detik: the five ballots of 2024 and the Constitutional Court ruling · detik: Constitutional Court rules on the state-enterprise law (October 2025)

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is put forward by the DPR or by the government.
  2. The DPR and the government debate it together; it passes only with the approval of both.
  3. The president signs it into law and it is published in the State Gazette.
  4. Anyone affected can challenge it before the Constitutional Court; in 2025 the state-enterprise law was amended twice, and the Court dropped four pending challenges because the text had changed under them.

Sources: detik: Constitutional Court drops four challenges after the law was amended (30 October 2025)

Political and economic context

Parties backing the government hold 470 of the 580 DPR seats; PDI-P, the largest party (110), has no ministers but calls itself a balancing force rather than the opposition. In late August 2025 anger at MPs' Rp50 million monthly housing allowance turned into nationwide protests: six national human-rights institutions counted 11 deaths and about 5,147 arrests and found that disproportionate force violated rights. The flagship free-meals programme has been dogged by mass food poisoning, and the Constitutional Court has ordered a new election calendar that still needs a law.

Sources: Infopublik (KPU decision): DPR seats 2024-2029 · detik: no PDI-P or NasDem minister in the cabinet · Six national human-rights institutions: final report on the August-September 2025 protests (20 April 2026) · ANTARA: BGN reports 70 poisoning cases (September 2025)

Why it's like thisThe 2017 Election Law sets a 4% national threshold that keeps small parties out of the DPR: in 2024 the PPP missed it with 3.87% and lost its seats for the first time. In 2025 the Constitutional Court ordered national and local elections to be separated, reasoning that holding them close together pushes parties towards pragmatism and leaves them no time to train candidates for each level.

Sources: DPR research centre: the 4% threshold (Art. 414, Law 7/2017) · CNN Indonesia: Constitutional Court ruling 135/PUU-XXII/2024

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards cover every party with seats in the DPR or at least 3% of the 2024 vote: the eight parliamentary parties plus PPP (3.87%, no seats). Left-right positions use V-Party's economic left-right scale only (2019 expert survey, rescaled to -2/+2), because no Chapel Hill survey covers Indonesia and V-Party has no general left-right score; this can place religious-conservative parties such as PKS left of centre.

Press sources come only from validated outlets (ANTARA, Kompas, CNN Indonesia and detik, Liputan6 and TVRI, Tempo) and official bodies. The approval figure is the latest recognised poll found through them (February 2026); later polls exist but were not reported by these outlets. No party publishes an audited income breakdown, so funding shares are left empty; no programme files could be opened, and promises come from the 2024 presidential platforms or the government programme each party backs. No independent promise tracker or rejection poll was found.

The judicial record lists only official acts (court rulings, KPK suspect status, parliamentary sanctions) within ten years, and only for people whose party membership at the time is confirmed: cases where the party disputes membership are left out, and people who have since left the party are marked as such. The homicide rate is the latest UNODC year available (2022). The 2029 election month is an estimate: no date has been set.
