Venezuela

Federal presidential republic · about 28 million people · acting government since January 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
no free national vote— No election date set: a presidential vote depends on talks between the acting government and part of the opposition
Freedom House rates Venezuela's 2025 elections as neither free nor fair. Since August 2026 the acting government and a delegation of the opposition-led Assembly elected in 2015 have been negotiating: they agreed to replace the whole Supreme Court (new judges hoped for by December 2026), then to renew the electoral council (CNE). A US official said that once both are in place, Delcy Rodríguez should declare the presidency permanently vacant, which under current law opens a six-month window for an election. The opposition negotiator hopes for a presidential vote between late 2027 and early 2028; nothing is official. source

5 branches of power, 0 election dates.

The 1999 Constitution added a 'citizen power' (prosecutor, comptroller, ombudsman) and an 'electoral power' to the classic three. After three rounds of talks in 2026, both sides had agreed to rebuild the Supreme Court and to review four laws, but still not on when Venezuelans will vote for President.

Who decides what

PresidencyThe President is head of state and government, elected for six years with no term limit since 2009, and appoints the Executive Vice-President and ministers. Since 5 January 2026 Delcy Rodríguez, the Vice-President, has been acting President after Nicolás Maduro was captured by US forces.
National AssemblyA single chamber, elected for five years. The 2026-2031 Assembly has 285 seats, 256 held by the ruling Gran Polo Patriótico; it is presided over by Jorge Rodríguez.
Courts, citizen and electoral powersThe Supreme Tribunal of Justice (TSJ) has the last word, including on elections. The UN Fact-Finding Mission said in March 2026 that the courts, prosecutor, comptroller, ombudsman and the electoral council (CNE) had 'not been reviewed or reformed'. A full renewal of the TSJ was agreed in August 2026.
23 states and the Capital DistrictEach state elects a governor and a legislative council; in May 2025 the ruling camp was proclaimed winner in 23 of 24 regions.
MunicipalitiesAbout 335 municipalities, each with an elected mayor and council, run local services.

How a law is passed

  1. Bills can come from the President, deputies, the other branches or 0.1% of registered voters.
  2. The Assembly debates a bill twice, in first and second discussion, then votes it.
  3. The President promulgates it or sends it back; the TSJ's Constitutional Chamber can annul it, as it did with the opposition Assembly's 2016 amnesty law.
  4. Short cut: an enabling law (ley habilitante) lets the President legislate by decree for a set period.

Why it's complicated

In July 2024 the CNE proclaimed Nicolás Maduro re-elected with 51.95% without publishing table-by-table results; the opposition published over 80% of the tally sheets, which gave Edmundo González 67.1%, and the Carter Center and a UN expert panel found the count fell short of basic transparency. On 3 January 2026 US forces captured Maduro in Caracas; he awaits trial in New York. Vice-President Delcy Rodríguez became acting President with the backing of the TSJ and of Washington, reformed the oil law and passed an amnesty, yet Foro Penal still counted 322 political prisoners in October 2026. Talks with part of the opposition have produced a plan to renew the courts but no election date. María Corina Machado returned from exile on 25 October 2026. Twin earthquakes on 24 June 2026 killed 6,509 people according to the government.

Why it's like thisAfter the 1958 fall of the last military dictatorship, the Punto Fijo pact let two parties, AD and COPEI, alternate in power for 40 years on oil money. Falling oil prices, the 1989 Caracazo riots and two coup attempts in 1992 broke that system. Hugo Chávez, who led one of them, won the 1998 election and had a new Constitution adopted in 1999, with five branches of power and a strong presidency. Indefinite re-election was added in 2009. After the opposition won the Assembly in 2015, the TSJ annulled most of its acts and a Constituent Assembly elected in 2017 took over much of its role.

Who governs in Venezuela?

Who's at the wheel

Acting President Delcy Rodríguez (PSUV) since 5 January 2026, designated by the TSJ's Constitutional Chamber and sworn in before the National Assembly, presided over by her brother Jorge Rodríguez. Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores are detained in New York.

Not through a vote: after the US operation of 3 January 2026, the TSJ ordered the Vice-President to take over to ensure 'institutional continuity'. The US government said it was satisfied with how she met its demands and would oversee the country until a presidential election, without a timeline. The ruling camp holds 256 of 285 Assembly seats since the May 2025 election, which most of the opposition boycotted.

What the government promised

  • Hydrocarbons law reform passed unanimously in January 2026 to attract foreign investment; energy agreements with US officials and Shell followed
  • Amnesty law for acts linked to political crises from 2002 to 2025, passed unanimously on 19 February 2026 (excludes armed actions, human rights crimes, drug trafficking and corruption)
  • Agreement with the opposition delegation (13 August 2026) to renew all Supreme Court judges, followed by the electoral council

What independent promise trackers found

  • No independent promise tracker found. Foro Penal's weekly count is the closest check on the amnesty pledge: 322 political prisoners on 5 October 2026 (182 civilians, 140 military), down from 902 at the end of 2025. source
  • UN Fact-Finding Mission, oral update of 12 March 2026: releases welcomed but unverifiable official figures; 87 new detentions since 3 January, including 14 journalists; repressive laws still in force. source

Voting in Venezuela

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Register with the CNE using your national ID card (cédula) and check your polling centre before each vote. Voting is not compulsory. Venezuelans abroad can only vote at a consulate if registered there, which in practice requires legal residence papers in the host country; most of the millions who emigrated are not registered.

Last turnout: 42.63% in the May 2025 legislative and regional elections according to the CNE, with the main opposition coalition calling for a boycott (59.97% claimed by the CNE for the July 2024 presidential election) source

The electoral system

  • Voting is voluntary from 18. Voters must be in the CNE electoral register; voting is electronic, and each machine prints a paper receipt and a tally sheet (acta) per polling table.
  • The President is elected in a single round: whoever has the most votes wins, for six years, with no term limit.
  • Deputies are elected by a mixed system (constituency seats plus lists), with three seats reserved for indigenous peoples.
