Venezuela

Federal presidential republic · about 28 million people · acting government since January 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
no free national vote— No election date set: a presidential vote depends on talks between the acting government and part of the opposition
Freedom House rates Venezuela's 2025 elections as neither free nor fair. Since August 2026 the acting government and a delegation of the opposition-led Assembly elected in 2015 have been negotiating: they agreed to replace the whole Supreme Court (new judges hoped for by December 2026), then to renew the electoral council (CNE). A US official said that once both are in place, Delcy Rodríguez should declare the presidency permanently vacant, which under current law opens a six-month window for an election. The opposition negotiator hopes for a presidential vote between late 2027 and early 2028; nothing is official. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Half-wayOn 29 January 2026, weeks after US forces captured Maduro, its Assembly unanimously reformed the 2001 hydrocarbons law: private and foreign companies may now run exploration and production at their own risk, royalties can be lowered and disputes can go to arbitration. Its stated aim was to attract investment. OPEC secondary sources put October 2026 output at 1.16 million barrels a day, about 20% more than a year earlier; the official figure is 1.22 million, up 8%. source · 2 · 3
On the posterZero extreme poverty
Half-wayThe UCAB university's ENCOVI survey (fieldwork March-June 2025, 11,352 households) found income poverty down from 73.2% of households in 2024 to 68.5%, and extreme poverty down from 36.5% to 31.7%; multidimensional poverty stayed at 55% of people, its 2022 level. source
On the posterPeace, dialogue and 'democratic coexistence'
Half-wayOn 19 February 2026 its Assembly unanimously passed the Amnesty Law for Democratic Coexistence, covering ten periods of political unrest from the April 2002 coup to the protests after the July 2024 election, excluding human-rights crimes, corruption and drug trafficking, with a court check of each case. Foro Penal still counted 322 political prisoners on 5 October 2026 (182 civilians, 140 military). source · 2
On the poster'Peaceful coexistence and tolerance'
⚠ Rights alertIn November 2017 its Constituent Assembly passed the Law against Hatred, for Peaceful Coexistence and Tolerance (10 to 20 years in prison for incitement). On 26 October 2023 the UN Human Rights Committee voiced concern at its use to restrict expression; in October 2026 the IACHR and its Special Rapporteurship said it unduly restricts freedom of expression, citing 85 cases since 2017, and asked for its repeal. The party partly accepts this: the acting President admitted 'abusive use', and a reform replacing prison with fines passed first reading on 3 November 2026. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterSovereignty: no foreign-funded 'destabilisation'
⚠ Rights alertOn 15 August 2024 its Assembly unanimously passed a law on NGO oversight: donors must be declared, funding political organisations is banned, and NGOs can be suspended or dissolved. The government cited transparency and said at least 62 foreign-funded NGOs sought to 'destabilise'. On 13 August 2024 the IACHR found that the text would 'arbitrarily restrict' freedom of association, expression and public participation. The 2026 talks did not put the law on their list for review. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • Built its identity on oil sovereignty and anti-imperialism; weeks after the US captured Maduro, its Assembly unanimously opened oil exploration and production to private and foreign companies, with arbitration and lower royalties possible, its stated aim being to attract investment. source
  • In 2016 the TSJ struck down the opposition Assembly's amnesty law; in 2026 its own Assembly passed an amnesty unanimously, and two months later the acting President said it had 'completed its cycle' while NGOs said fewer than 30% of political detainees had benefited. source
  • Says it defends the people's vote; in 2024 the CNE proclaimed its candidate the winner without publishing results by polling table, which the UN expert panel said had 'no precedent in contemporary democratic elections'. source
On its record
  • Household income poverty fell from 73.2% in 2024 to 68.5% in 2025 (ENCOVI, UCAB)
  • Released hundreds of political detainees in 2026: Foro Penal counted 690 releases by mid-March and 322 prisoners left in October, against 902 at the end of 2025
  • Oil output back above one million barrels a day (OPEC, February 2026), from 636,000 in 2021
