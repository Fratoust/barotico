Hungary

Parliamentary republic · 9.49 million people (KSH estimate, end of 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 41 monthsParliamentary election (spring 2030, date not set)
The Országgyűlés elected on 12 April 2026 sits for four years, and the President of the Republic sets the date of the next vote, normally in April. Under the 17th amendment to the Fundamental Law (July 2026), anyone who has already been an MP for 12 years can no longer stand. HVG counted 31 sitting MPs affected: 22 from Fidesz, 6 from KDNP and 3 from Mi Hazánk. No Tisza MPs are affected, because none of them were in parliament before 2026. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Hungary

Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: only 17% of Hungarians trust most news most of the time, down 5 points and far below the 37% global average. The most trusted brands are RTL (49%), HVG (44%) and Telex (41%), ahead of 24.hu and 444 (38%). TV2, Origo and public media are trusted by 21%. 62% get news from social media. source

Köztér

YouTube interview channel launched in 2024 by Dániel Kurucz, a former Fidelitas and government communications official, with about 100,000 subscribers. Telex found that about 90% of its plays in February 2025 came from paid ads, and that the producer received about HUF 112 million of indirect state support for documentaries. Kurucz denies any Fidesz affiliation.

source

Who owns what

  • Public media (MTVA, Duna Média): state-funded; news suspended in July 2026 and resumed on 20 August under interim management, with a merger of MTVA and Duna Média planned.
  • KESMA foundation: about 500 national and local titles, set up in 2018 to serve the Orbán government according to RSF.
  • Indamédia: bought most of Swiss group Ringier's Hungarian titles in 2025; the Media Pluralism Monitor describes it as government-affiliated.
  • Media Pluralism Monitor 2026: market plurality at high risk (85%), with highly concentrated ownership and limited transparency about who the final owners are.

source

Documented facts to know

  • RSF 2026: Hungary fell from 68th to 74th of 180 (score 59.85), third lowest in the EU. RSF cites public media turned into a propaganda tool, state advertising favouring pro-government outlets, and Pegasus surveillance of journalists. source
  • Media Pluralism Monitor 2026: overall risk 75% (high), with political independence at 80%. It finds public service media 'captured' with no independent appointment mechanism, and an unfair distribution of state advertising. source
  • In July 2026, under the new government, public-media news (M1, hirado.hu) was suspended for several weeks. The appointments of an M1 news editor-in-chief and a presenter were withdrawn on 4 August after criticism, and a permanent management was expected by late October. source
  • DNR 2026: public media and the pro-government brands TV2 and Origo are each trusted by 21% of Hungarians, against 49% for RTL. source
