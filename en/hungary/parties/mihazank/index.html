Hungary

Parliamentary republic · 9.49 million people (KSH estimate, end of 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 41 monthsParliamentary election (spring 2030, date not set)
The Országgyűlés elected on 12 April 2026 sits for four years, and the President of the Republic sets the date of the next vote, normally in April. Under the 17th amendment to the Fundamental Law (July 2026), anyone who has already been an MP for 12 years can no longer stand. HVG counted 31 sitting MPs affected: 22 from Fidesz, 6 from KDNP and 3 from Mi Hazánk. No Tisza MPs are affected, because none of them were in parliament before 2026. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. Only three lists entered parliament in April 2026. The Democratic Coalition (DK, 1.1%), the Two-Tailed Dog Party (MKKP) and other lists fell below both the 5% threshold and this site's 3% rule. Fidesz and KDNP ran on a joint list and sit as one group, so they share a card.

On the posterThe right to pay in cash, written into the constitution
Half-wayDone, through others' votes: the 15th amendment of April 2025, tabled by Fidesz-KDNP after talks between Toroczkai and minister János Lázár, gave citizens a constitutional right to pay in cash, and a related bill was to require ATMs in every settlement. It passed 140 to 21 with Mi Hazánk's votes, which were not needed for the two-thirds majority. No measured effect on people has been found. source · 2
On the posterStop the 'full power' of Fidesz or Tisza and keep checks and balances (2026 campaign)
ConsistentAfter Tisza won two thirds, Toroczkai said President Tamás Sulyok should serve out his term. On 13 July 2026 its six MPs cast the only votes against the 17th amendment that ended that term, Toroczkai arguing that 'the rule of law cannot be restored by non-rule-of-law means'. Fidesz-KDNP boycotted the vote and the amendment passed 139 to 6. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • Its leader says it will be nobody's coalition partner and wants to avoid 'even the appearance' of moving closer to Viktor Orbán. In April 2025 its long-standing cash-payment proposal entered the constitution through Fidesz-KDNP's 15th amendment, after talks between Toroczkai and minister János Lázár. Mi Hazánk was the only other party to vote for it; the same amendment lets police ban Pride marches. source
  • In December 2023 Parliament adopted its own bill rewriting Budapest's election rules six months before the local vote, with 135 Fidesz-KDNP votes. Its six MPs abstained: a late governing-party amendment on joint lists, they said, amounted to a 'pact' between Fidesz and the left-liberal parties. source
  • In September 2026 it voted to lift the immunity of two former Fidesz-KDNP ministers suspected of corruption. In 2025 the State Audit Office had found that its own records could not back up the donations and other contributions it declared, and that it had not set up the supervisory board required by law. source
On its record
  • The right to pay in cash, a proposal it had long pushed, was written into the Fundamental Law in April 2025.
  • Its MPs voted with Tisza to lift the immunity of former minister Balázs Hankó, by 139 votes to 0 (September 2026).
  • The State Audit Office found its income and expense accounting and its asset records compliant for 2022-2023.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. If you're considering voting for Mi Hazánk, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its flagship right to pay in cash entered the constitution in April 2025 through Fidesz-KDNP's 15th amendment, which also lets police ban Pride. Mi Hazánk was the only other party to vote for it. No measured effect has been found.

It wants others held to account. In 2025 the State Audit Office found that its own records could not back up the donations it declared.

In 2023 Parliament upheld a HUF 5 million fine on its MP Előd Novák for saying 'Gypsy crime' in the chamber.

In July 2026 its MPs cast the only votes against the amendment that removed the President.
