Hungary

Parliamentary republic · 9.49 million people (KSH estimate, end of 2025)

14 years after Fidesz used the constitution to cut short András Baka's term as Supreme Court president, he became President of the Republic, after Tisza used the constitution to cut short his predecessor's term.

In 2011 a transitional provision of Fidesz's new Fundamental Law ended Baka's six-year term about three and a half years early; the European Court of Human Rights ruled in 2016 that this violated his rights (Index). Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Citizens living in Hungary are registered automatically and vote at their local polling station with two ballots, one for a district candidate and one for a party list. Voters away on election day can apply in advance to vote elsewhere in Hungary or at a consulate. Citizens with no address in Hungary must first register with the National Election Office (valasztas.hu), and they vote by post for a party list only.

Last turnout: 79.56% in the April 2026 parliamentary election, the highest since 1990. source

Who decides what

StateA single chamber, the Országgyűlés (199 MPs), passes laws and elects the Prime Minister, who leads the government. It also elects the President of the Republic, whose role is mostly ceremonial but who can return a law once or send it to the Constitutional Court.
Courts and control bodiesThe Constitutional Court (Alkotmánybíróság) reviews laws. The Kúria is the supreme court. The State Audit Office (ÁSZ) audits public money and party accounts, and the Fiscal Council (Költségvetési Tanács) checks the budget. Since July 2026, constitutional judges serve one nine-year term and must retire at 70.
Counties19 counties plus Budapest, with elected assemblies. The 17th amendment (2026) renamed them back from 'vármegye' to 'megye'.
MunicipalitiesMore than 3,000 towns and villages elect their mayor and council every five years (last in 2024). Budapest has a directly elected mayor and 23 district mayors.
European UnionAn EU member since 2004, Hungary keeps the forint. About €10 billion of EU recovery funds were frozen for nearly four years over rule-of-law and corruption concerns; EU ministers approved a new plan in July 2026, when the Commission also approved Hungary joining the European Public Prosecutor's Office.

How people vote

  • Every four years citizens aged 18 and over elect 199 MPs with two votes: 106 are elected in single-member districts in one round (whoever comes first wins), and 93 come from national party lists. A party needs 5% of list votes to win list seats; joint lists need more.
  • Votes that did not elect anyone in the districts are added to the list count, and so are the winners' surplus votes. This rewards the largest party: in 2026 Tisza won 53.18% of list votes and 141 of 199 seats (71%).
  • Two thirds of MPs (133) can change the Fundamental Law and the 'cardinal' laws. Tisza holds that majority alone, as Fidesz-KDNP did in 2010, 2014 (until 2015), 2018 and 2022.

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled by the government, an MP, a parliamentary committee or the President.
  2. The Országgyűlés holds a general debate, then committees examine amendments, and a legislative committee prepares the final text.
  3. Final vote: a simple majority of MPs present, or two thirds for cardinal laws and amendments to the Fundamental Law. The 17th amendment (July 2026) narrowed the list of cardinal laws.
  4. The President signs within five days, or sends the law back to Parliament once or to the Constitutional Court.

Why it's complicated

After 16 years of Fidesz-KDNP governments, Péter Magyar's Tisza won the 12 April 2026 election with 53.18% and a two-thirds majority on its own. It is now using that majority quickly. The 17th amendment (July 2026) ended President Tamás Sulyok's term. It also sent Constitutional Court judges aged 70 and over into retirement and capped MPs at 12 years. Fidesz-KDNP boycotted the vote. Amnesty, the Helsinki Committee, TASZ and HRW said the removals lacked procedural safeguards. Public-media news was suspended in July and resumed in August under interim management. Prosecutors are pursuing two former Fidesz-KDNP ministers, and Parliament also lifted the Prime Minister's own immunity at his request. Viktor Orbán gave up his seat but still leads Fidesz. The 2026 deficit target was raised to 7.5% of GDP.

Why it's like thisHungary's democracy was negotiated at the 1989 round table, when one-party rule ended. Many key laws were made to require a two-thirds majority, so that no single party could rewrite the rules. In 2010 Fidesz-KDNP won two thirds on its own and in 2011 adopted a new Fundamental Law, which it amended 15 times. In 2026 Tisza won two thirds on the same basis, and it says it will draft a new constitution with other parties and put it to a referendum.

Who's at the wheel

Péter Magyar (Tisza), Prime Minister since 9 May 2026, with a 16-member cabinet sworn in on 12 May. András Baka, elected by Parliament, has been President of the Republic since 19 August 2026.

Tisza won 141 of 199 seats, including 96 of the 106 districts, on a record 79.56% turnout. Parliament elected Magyar by 140 votes to 54 (one abstention). Viktor Orbán gave up his seat on 25 April and was re-elected Fidesz president in June. Gergely Gulyás led the Fidesz-KDNP group until July, when János Bóka took over.
