Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

Who governs in Czechia?

Who's at the wheel

Andrej Babiš (ANO), Prime Minister since December 2025, his third term. The cabinet has 15 members: 9 from ANO, 3 nominated by SPD and 3 by Motoristé sobě. Tomio Okamura (SPD) chairs the Chamber of Deputies.

ANO won the 3-4 October 2025 election with 34.5% and 80 seats and formed a majority with SPD (15) and Motoristé (13). On 15 January 2026 all 108 coalition deputies voted confidence, against 91. President Pavel refused to appoint Filip Turek (Motoristé) as environment minister, and Igor Červený (Motoristé) was appointed on 23 February 2026; Turek became a government commissioner for climate policy.

What the government promised

  • Programme statement of the Babiš government (January 2026): cheaper energy, retirement age capped at 65, deficits 'safely below 3%' of GDP, return of sales registration (EET), public media funded from the state budget
  • Summary of the programme (Forbes Česko)

What independent promise trackers found

  • Babiš government, self-assessment after six months (July 2026, not independent): 195 of 978 programme tasks completed, 774 in progress; about 35% of the programme statement fulfilled, according to Deputy PM Havlíček. source
  • National Fiscal Council (September 2026): the programme's promise to keep deficits 'safely below 3%' of GDP 'is no longer achievable in 2027'. source
  • Previous Fiala government, Demagog.cz check of 50 of its 550 programme items (September 2025): 32% fulfilled, 28% partly, 40% not fulfilled. The government called the selection 'unfair'. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • ČSÚ: general government deficit 3.0% of GDP in 2022, 3.7% in 2023, 2.0% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025; public debt 44.2% of GDP in 2025. source
  • ČSÚ: average inflation 15.1% in 2022, 10.7% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025; 2.5% year on year in September 2026. source
  • National Fiscal Council: deficit forecast at 2.7% of GDP in 2026 and above 3.5% under the 2027 draft budget (cash deficit CZK 389 billion); interest costs rise from 0.7% of GDP in 2021 to 1.5% in 2027. source
