Czechia

Parliamentary republic · 10.9 million people (ČSÚ, 30 June 2026)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 14 monthsPresidential election (first round expected in mid-January 2028)
President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. The governing coalition says it wants a joint candidate. The Chamber of Deputies elected in October 2025 sits until autumn 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Czechia

Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: 31% of Czechs trust most news most of the time (down 2 points, below the 37% global average). 41% sometimes or often avoid the news. Most trusted brands: ČT 59%, ČRo 58%, CNN Prima News 57%, Seznam Zprávy and Hospodářské noviny 55%. source

Recommended for factual news

Public broadcaster, the most trusted news brand (59%, DNR 2026). Funded by licence fees; the government plans to replace them with a state-budget contribution.

Who owns what

  • ČT and ČRo: public broadcasters funded by licence fees, raised in May 2025 after two decades; a government bill (June 2026) would fund them from the state budget with CZK 1.4 billion less.
  • MAFRA (iDNES, Lidové noviny): sold by Andrej Babiš's group in 2023; a 50% stake has since moved from Kaprain to energy businessman Pavel Tykač.
  • Czech News Center, MAFRA, Seznam and Economia: the large groups, 'owned by major economic players' (RSF).
  • Media Pluralism Monitor 2026: market plurality at high risk (74%), with opaque beneficial ownership and a weak response to consolidation.

source

Documented facts to know

  • RSF 2026: Czechia fell from 10th to 11th of 180 (score 83.01). RSF cited verbal attacks on journalists and warned that funding public broadcasters from the state budget may threaten their independence. source
  • On 15 June 2026 the government approved a bill abolishing licence fees from 2027 and giving ČT CZK 5.74 billion and ČRo CZK 2.07 billion from the budget, CZK 1.4 billion less in total; the Senate opposed abolition and staff threatened a strike. source
  • Media Pluralism Monitor 2026: overall risk 51% (medium-high), political independence 35% (medium-low), with political pressure on public-media governance and non-transparent state advertising. source
  • DNR 2026: 44% use social media for news (Facebook 44%, YouTube 25%), 9% use AI chatbots, and 13% pay for online news. source
