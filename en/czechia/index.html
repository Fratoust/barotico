Who decides what

StateThe government answers to the 200-member Chamber of Deputies. The Senate (81 members) can delay and amend laws but cannot bring down the government. The President, directly elected for five years, appoints the Prime Minister and ministers and can return laws once.
Courts and control bodiesThe Constitutional Court (Ústavní soud) can strike down laws. The Supreme Audit Office (NKÚ) audits state spending, the National Fiscal Council (NRR) checks the budget, and an independent office (ÚDHPSH) supervises party finances.
Regions14 regions, including Prague, each with an elected assembly and a governor (hejtman) who run schools, hospitals and regional roads.
MunicipalitiesMore than 6,000 municipalities, many of them very small, elect their councils every four years. Their mayors are a large part of the political class, which is why one party is called 'Mayors and Independents' (STAN).
European UnionAn EU member since 2004, Czechia keeps its own currency, the koruna. In September 2026 the European Commission suspended part of the EU rural-development payments to firms of the Agrofert group, which the Prime Minister founded.

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled by the government, a deputy, the Senate or a regional assembly.
  2. The Chamber of Deputies debates it in three readings.
  3. The Senate has 30 days to approve, amend or reject it. The Chamber can override the Senate with an absolute majority of 101 deputies, as it did for the new sales-registration system (EET 2.0) in September 2026.
  4. The President signs it or returns it once; 101 deputies can override the veto. Constitutional laws need three fifths of both chambers.

Why it's complicated

Andrej Babiš, a billionaire who founded the Agrofert group, became Prime Minister for the third time in December 2025, at the head of an ANO-SPD-Motoristé coalition with 108 of 200 seats. In March 2026 the Chamber refused to lift his immunity in the Čapí hnízdo subsidy case, which suspends his trial. He moved his Agrofert shares to a trust in February 2026, but the European Commission says he remains in a potential conflict of interest and has suspended some payments to the group. The President refused to appoint the Motoristé candidate Filip Turek as minister. The government wants to fund public TV and radio from the state budget instead of licence fees, and its 2027 draft budget plans a CZK 389 billion deficit. The opposition, which ran the country from 2021 to 2025, won every Senate seat in play in October 2026.

Why it's like thisThe 1993 Constitution was written for the new Czech Republic after Czechoslovakia split peacefully. After four decades of one-party rule, it spread power: a government answerable to an elected Chamber, a Senate as a brake, a strong Constitutional Court, and proportional voting that forces parties into coalitions. The President was elected by Parliament until 2013 and is now elected directly.

Who's at the wheel

Andrej Babiš (ANO), Prime Minister since December 2025, his third term. The cabinet has 15 members: 9 from ANO, 3 nominated by SPD and 3 by Motoristé sobě. Tomio Okamura (SPD) chairs the Chamber of Deputies.

Voting in Czechia

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

No registration needed: you vote where you live with an ID card or passport (in 2025 the eDoklady mobile ID was also accepted). A voting certificate (voličský průkaz) lets you vote anywhere else. Czechs abroad vote at an embassy or, since 2025, by post, after asking to be put on the special list abroad well before the deadline set by the Interior Ministry.

Last turnout: 68.95% in the October 2025 Chamber of Deputies election. source

The electoral system

  • Every four years citizens aged 18 and over elect the 200 deputies by proportional representation in 14 regions. A single party needs 5% nationwide; coalitions need more (11% for three or more parties). Voters can give preference votes to up to four candidates on their list.
  • The Senate is elected in 81 single-seat districts in two rounds, for six years, with one third renewed every two years (last in October 2026).
  • The President is elected directly in two rounds for five years. The current President, Petr Pavel, was elected in 2023.
