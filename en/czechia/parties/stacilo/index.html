Czechia

Next vote≈ 14 monthsPresidential election (first round expected in mid-January 2028)
President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. The governing coalition says it wants a joint candidate. The Chamber of Deputies elected in October 2025 sits until autumn 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

⚠ Rights alertThe bill of KSČM, now its main member, taxed churches' restitution compensation at 19%; it was one of KSČM's conditions for tolerating the Babiš government. ČSSD, today SOCDEM and on its lists, voted for it with ANO and SPD, and the Chamber overrode the Senate 114 to 57 on 23 April 2019. On 15 October 2019 the Constitutional Court (Pl. Also worth knowing
  • In June 2025 SOCDEM leader Jana Maláčová said there was no reason for an alliance if Stačilo! insisted on leaving the EU and NATO. In July 2025 SOCDEM joined its lists, while KSČM leader Kateřina Konečná said the programme still included a referendum on EU and NATO membership. source
  • Stačilo! campaigns against the political establishment, but KSČM, its main member, tolerated Babiš's second government in its 2018 confidence vote and backed its 2019 budget. source
  • Four parties (KSČM, SOCDEM, ČSNS and SD-SN) ran on the list of Stačilo!, registered as a single movement facing a 5% threshold, instead of the 11% that a coalition of three or more parties needs; critics said the choice sidestepped the electoral rules. source
On its record
  • In its first election, the 2024 European election, Stačilo! won two seats with just under 10%.
  • SOCDEM (then ČSSD), now on its list, pushed through paid sick leave from the first day in 2018, in the Babiš II government.
  • KSČM's support helped pass the 2019 budget, which carried the largest pension rise to that date (about CZK 900 on average).

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Never tested Referendum on EU and NATO membership: never in a position to hold one
  • Never tested Abolish VAT on basic foods: never in a position to do it
  • Never tested Nationalise ČEZ by buying out minority shareholders: never in a position to do it

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Stačilo!, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It rails against the establishment. Yet KSČM, its main member, kept Babiš's second government afloat in the 2018 confidence vote and backed its 2019 budget.

It ran four parties as one movement, facing a 5% threshold rather than the 11% a coalition of three or more parties needs.

SOCDEM had ruled out an alliance if Stačilo! insisted on leaving the EU and NATO. It then joined its lists, while Kateřina Konečná said the referendum on EU and NATO membership was still in the programme. Its promises remain untested.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?
