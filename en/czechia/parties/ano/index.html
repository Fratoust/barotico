Czechia

Parliamentary republic · 10.9 million people (ČSÚ, 30 June 2026)

Before you read

  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 14 monthsPresidential election (first round expected in mid-January 2028)
President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. The governing coalition says it wants a joint candidate. The Chamber of Deputies elected in October 2025 sits until autumn 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterCheaper energy
DeliveredFrom January 2026 its government moved the renewable-energy fee (POZE) from electricity bills to the state budget. Final electricity prices were 12.2% lower year on year in January 2026, partly also because energy itself got cheaper (Czech National Bank). source · 2
On the posterRetirement at 65 at the latest
Half-wayIn opposition its deputies challenged the Fiala government's reform raising the age towards 67; the Constitutional Court rejected the petition on 9 July 2025. In government, it postponed its own cap in June 2026: Labour Minister Aleš Juchelka said it would come only from 2028, in a later amendment, so it will not take effect this term. Babiš: 'What we said still stands, but we'll start with the professions that should be entitled to retire earlier.' source · 2
On the posterDeficits 'safely below 3%' of GDP (2026 programme)
Half-wayThe National Fiscal Council forecasts a deficit of 2.7% of GDP in 2026, but above 3.5% under the government's 2027 draft budget, with a cash deficit of CZK 389 billion. In September 2026 it wrote that the programme's promise 'is no longer achievable in 2027'. source · 2
On the posterNo conflict of interest: Agrofert placed in trust funds (2017)
ContradictionWhile Babiš was Prime Minister (2017-2021), Agrofert firms kept applying for subsidies and public contracts. In 2019 he said 'I am not in a conflict of interest', arguing that the trust funds complied with Czech law, and called the European Commission's audit 'an attack on the Czech Republic'. The final audit confirmed the conflict of interest. In 2025 the Prague Municipal Court and the Supreme Administrative Court ruled that he had controlled the firms in fact, so they were not entitled to the subsidy and contract at issue. In September 2026 the Commission found his new trust 'not sufficient'. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the posterMake church restitution fairer: tax the compensation
⚠ Rights alertIts deputies voted for KSČM's bill taxing the churches' restitution compensation at 19%, one of KSČM's conditions for tolerating its minority government with ČSSD. On 23 April 2019 the Chamber overrode the Senate 114 to 57 with ANO, SPD, ČSSD and KSČM votes. On 15 October 2019 the Constitutional Court (Pl. ÚS 5/19) struck it down as a retroactive cut to compensation already agreed, breaching legal certainty and the property right protected by Article 11 of the Charter. Babiš said he respected the ruling but still believed the income should be taxed. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • Its January 2026 programme statement promised deficits 'safely below 3%' of GDP. In September 2026 the National Fiscal Council said this 'is no longer achievable in 2027': the government's own draft budget implies a deficit above 3.5%. source
  • Capping the retirement age at 65 was a campaign and programme promise. In June 2026 the government postponed it: the cap is due only from 2028, in a later amendment, and will not take effect in this term. source
  • Babiš moved his Agrofert shares to a trust in February 2026 to end his conflict of interest. In September 2026 the European Commission said he was still in a potential conflict and the trust's set-up was 'not sufficient', and part of the EU rural-development payments to Agrofert firms was suspended. source
To be fair
  • Its government moved the renewable-energy fee (POZE) from bills to the state budget: final electricity prices were 12.2% lower year on year in January 2026, partly thanks to cheaper energy (ČNB).
  • Its government raised the state's monthly payment for state-insured people (children, pensioners) from CZK 2,188 to 2,479 and approved a higher parental allowance (2026).
  • Its Finance Ministry reports CZK 11 billion of operating savings against the original 2026 draft budget.
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Integrity
  • Trial Andrej Babiš (leader, Prime Minister): on trial with his former aide Jana Nagyová over a CZK 50 million subsidy for the Čapí hnízdo farm (2007-2008). The Prague Municipal Court acquitted them twice; the High Court annulled both acquittals and in June 2025 directed the lower court to convict. On 5 March 2026 the Chamber refused to lift his immunity, so his trial is suspended while he is an MP. He denies wrongdoing.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.
