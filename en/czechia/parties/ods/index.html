Czechia

Next vote≈ 14 monthsPresidential election (first round expected in mid-January 2028)
President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. The governing coalition says it wants a joint candidate. The Chamber of Deputies elected in October 2025 sits until autumn 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

On the posterSound public finances without tax rises (SPOLU, 2021)
Half-wayIts government's 2023 consolidation package cut spending by about CZK 80 billion over two years but also raised corporate income tax from 19% to 21% and property tax. Finance Minister Zbyněk Stanjura cited debt up 14.5 points of GDP since 2019, and Petr Fiala the war; the government's own economic advisers (NERV) had warned that consolidation was impossible without tax rises. The deficit fell from 3.7% of GDP in 2023 to 2.0% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025 (ČSÚ). ODS's Jan Skopeček: 'In my view, we broke that promise.' source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the posterNo EET sales registration
ConsistentIts government abolished EET from 2023. In opposition, it proposed in July 2026 exempting businesses with turnover up to CZK 2 million from EET 2.0 ('the exemption for small businesses doesn't really exist', said its MP Vojtěch Munzar). The coalition passed the law, and on 9 September 2026 the Chamber overrode the Senate's amendments, including an exemption for part of flat-rate taxpayers; EET returns in January 2027. source · 2
On the posterSound public finances: savings on top state salaries
⚠ Rights alertIts government froze judges' pay base for 2022 and cut the pay coefficient for 2024 from 3 to 2.822. In May 2024 the Constitutional Court ruled both unconstitutional (Pl. ÚS 15/22 and 5/24): stable pay is part of judicial independence, and the government had shown neither necessity nor proportionality and had not consulted the judiciary. It upheld the 2021 freeze because of the pandemic. Its Justice Minister Pavel Blažek said he did not consider the Court's case law on judges' pay constitutional. The government found CZK 725 million for the 2024 top-up. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • SPOLU campaigned in 2021 on not raising taxes. In 2023, citing debt up 14.5 points of GDP since 2019 and the war's economic shock, its government raised corporate tax from 19% to 21% from 2024; ODS MP Jan Skopeček said: 'In my view, we broke that promise.' source
  • The 'tax brake' in the Fiala programme was not delivered: the government dropped it in its 2023 updated statement (Demagog). source
  • Petr Fiala, its leader until January 2026, said in January 2025: 'When Russia attacked Ukraine, I should have said that no pre-election promises applied.' source
To be fair
  • Under its finance minister the deficit fell from 3.7% of GDP in 2023 to 2.0% in 2024 (ČSÚ).
  • Its government abolished the EET sales-registration system from 2023, a promise Demagog rated fulfilled.
  • Its government raised the public-media licence fee in 2025 after two decades, which RSF linked to Czechia's rise to 10th place in press freedom.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Broken No tax rises (SPOLU, 2021): corporate tax raised from 19% to 21% from 2024 source
  • Kept, made a difference Consolidate public finances: deficit down from 3.7% of GDP in 2023 to 2.0% in 2024 (ČSÚ) source · measured effect
  • Broken A 'tax brake' in law: dropped in 2023 source
  • Kept on paper Abolish EET: done from 2023, no measured effect found, and the next government brings it back in January 2027 source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for ODS, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

SPOLU campaigned in 2021 on not raising taxes. Its government then raised corporate tax from 19% to 21%, to slow the growth of debt, and in 2023 the 'tax brake' left its programme.

In January 2025 its then leader Petr Fiala said that when Russia attacked Ukraine, he 'should have said that no pre-election promises applied'. Voters heard it nearly three years later.

Its abolition of the EET sales register will have lasted four years: EET returns in January 2027.

To be fair, its finance ministry cut the deficit from 3.7% to 2.0% of GDP in a year (ČSÚ).
