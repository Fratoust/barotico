President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On its record
  • It is part of the government that moved the renewables fee to the state budget: electricity prices 12.2% lower year on year in January 2026 (ČNB).
  • The government it backs approved its demand to abolish TV and radio licence fees (bill of June 2026).
  • The government has started work on a general referendum law, without votes on EU or NATO membership.
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Remarks and violence
  • Trial Tomio Okamura (leader, Chamber speaker): indicted in August 2025 for alleged incitement to hatred over SPD posters in the 2024 regional and Senate campaign. On 5 March 2026 the Chamber refused to lift his immunity, so the case is suspended. He denies the charge.
  • Acquitted SPD (the movement as a legal entity): a CZK 3 million fine for incitement to hatred over the same posters was annulled on appeal; the Prague Municipal Court acquitted it with final effect on 1 September 2026, calling the posters 'on the edge of good taste' but not a crime.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for SPD, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised voters a referendum on leaving the EU and NATO. It dropped it in the coalition talks: 'We regret it, but that's the political reality.'

It now holds three ministries and the Chamber speaker's chair, and says it is 'highly dissatisfied' with the deficit in the 2027 draft budget of the government it sits in.

The abolition of TV and radio licence fees it pushed passed a first reading in October 2026. Coalition sources call the planned January 2027 start unrealistic.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?
