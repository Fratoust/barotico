Czechia

Parliamentary republic · 10.9 million people (ČSÚ, 30 June 2026)

Next vote≈ 14 monthsPresidential election (first round expected in mid-January 2028)
President Petr Pavel's five-year term ends on 9 March 2028, and the vote must take place in the last 60 days of the term. The Senate chairman sets the exact date, usually in the autumn before. The governing coalition says it wants a joint candidate. The Chamber of Deputies elected in October 2025 sits until autumn 2029. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterA balanced budget within four years
Half-wayThe 2027 draft budget of the government in which it holds three ministries has a CZK 389 billion deficit, the second-highest in Czech history. Party leader Petr Macinka said 'we really don't like the deficit' and warned of a 'big problem' supporting the budget without some of its proposals; budget committee chair Vladimír Pikora proposed 22 measures to save CZK 80 billion in 2028. The Chamber has not yet voted. source
On the posterNo mandatory EET (2025 programme)
Half-wayIts programme rejected the return of mandatory EET and demanded exemptions for all small businesses up to CZK 5 million of turnover if it came back. In October 2025 it agreed with ANO and SPD to restore EET. The law, passed in July 2026 and confirmed over the Senate on 9 September, applies from January 2027 with a full exemption only up to CZK 50,000 of turnover and an opt-out for first-band flat-rate taxpayers (up to CZK 1 million) who pay a higher flat tax. E15 reported that SPD and Motoristé won the biggest concessions for the self-employed. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • Its 2025 programme pledged a balanced or surplus budget within four years. The 2027 draft budget of the government it sits in has a CZK 389 billion deficit, the second-highest in Czech history according to a political scientist quoted by Seznam Zprávy. source
  • Its programme said 'we don't want mandatory EET'. The government in which it holds three ministries brought back sales registration as EET 2.0, confirmed by the Chamber on 9 September 2026 and in force from January 2027. source
  • A party against state bureaucracy, it obtained a new post of government commissioner for climate policy and the Green Deal for its honorary chairman Filip Turek after the President refused to make him minister (January 2026). source
To be fair
  • It is part of the government that moved the renewables fee to the state budget: electricity prices 12.2% lower year on year in January 2026 (ČNB).
  • Its budget-committee chair Vladimír Pikora has proposed 22 measures to save CZK 80 billion in 2028.
  • Its position against the ETS2 emission allowances was adopted by the government, and the Senate also rejected ETS2 (2026).
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Remarks and violence
  • Dropped Filip Turek (MP, honorary chairman): police dropped a probe into old social-media posts (possible approval of a crime, incitement to hatred, defamation of a group), mostly because the statute of limitations had run out (July 2026); the decision was not yet final and was to be reviewed by the prosecutor.
  • Other
  • Administrative fine Motoristé: CZK 25,000 fine from the party-finance office for unlabelled campaign material and use of a municipality's media for a pre-election event (November 2025).

  • Partly kept Balanced budget within four years: it pushes for cuts, but the 2027 draft deficit is CZK 389 billion source
  • Broken No mandatory EET: its government brings back EET 2.0 from January 2027 source
  • Kept on paper Reject ETS2 emission allowances: government and Senate positions adopted, no measured effect source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Motoristé, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised a balanced budget within four years. It sits in the government that drafted a CZK 389 billion deficit for 2027, a deficit its leader says 'we really don't like'.

Its programme said no to mandatory EET. Its government brings EET back in January 2027, with exemptions far smaller than the CZK 5 million of turnover it wanted.

It fights state bureaucracy, and obtained a new government commissioner post for its honorary chairman Filip Turek.

To be fair, its government made electricity 12.2% cheaper in a year (Czech National Bank).
