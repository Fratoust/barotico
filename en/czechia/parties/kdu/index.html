Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. On the posterSecure, fair pensions
Half-wayUnder Labour Minister Marian Jurečka, a 2023 law cut the extraordinary June 2023 indexation: the average pension rose by CZK 760 instead of 1,770; the coalition said the old mechanism would have cost up to CZK 400 billion over the coming decades. The government dropped its pledge not to change indexation (Demagog), and its 2024 reform raises the retirement age gradually to 67. The Constitutional Court upheld the indexation cut on 24 January 2024 and the reform on 9 July 2025. source · 2 · 3
On the posterSupport for families
Half-wayTo cut the deficit, the 2023 consolidation package of the government it sat in abolished the tax credits for kindergarten fees and for students and limited the spouse credit to couples caring for a child under three. Its Labour Ministry's single 'superdávka' benefit, merging housing, living and child allowances, passed the Chamber on 26 March 2025. The current coalition's 2026 tax package brings the kindergarten and student credits back. source · 2 · 3
On the posterConsolidate public finances (government programme)
⚠ Rights alertThe Fiala government it sat in froze judges' pay base for 2022 and cut the pay coefficient for 2024 from 3 to 2.822. In May 2024 the Constitutional Court ruled both unconstitutional (Pl. ÚS 15/22 and 5/24): stable pay is part of judicial independence, and the government had shown neither necessity nor proportionality and had not consulted the judiciary. It upheld the 2021 freeze because of the pandemic. ODS Justice Minister Pavel Blažek contested the Court's case law; no reaction from KDU-ČSL was found. The government found CZK 725 million for the 2024 top-up. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • The Fiala programme committed not to change the pension indexation system; the government dropped that commitment (Demagog). KDU-ČSL's Marian Jurečka was Labour Minister. source
  • SPOLU campaigned in 2021 on not raising taxes; with KDU-ČSL in government, corporate tax rose from 19% to 21% from 2024, in a package the government said was needed to slow the growth of debt. source
  • The Fiala programme promised two new national parks; Demagog rated it not fulfilled. KDU-ČSL held the Environment Ministry. source
To be fair

  • Its Labour Minister passed the 'superdávka', a single benefit meant to make work pay (2025).
  • Automatic indexation of the minimum wage, a programme promise, was delivered (Demagog).

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for KDU-ČSL, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It is the party of secure pensions. Under its Labour Minister the 2023 indexation was cut: the average pension rose by CZK 760 instead of 1,770, although the government had promised not to change the indexation system.

His reform raises the retirement age gradually to 67. The next government plans to cap it at 65.

SPOLU, to which it belongs, campaigned on no tax rises; its government raised corporate tax to 21%.

To be fair, its government cut the deficit from 3.7% to 2.0% of GDP in a year (ČSÚ).
