Benin

Presidential republic · about 14 million people · a parliament with no opposition since February 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 74 monthsNext general elections expected in 2033 (legislative and local in January, presidential in April; no date set)
The November 2025 constitutional revision gave the president, deputies, mayors and local councillors seven-year terms. The deputies and councillors elected on 11 January 2026 and the president elected on 12 April 2026 are therefore due for re-election in 2033 (month estimated from the 2026 calendar). Deputies voted in July 2026 to suspend the electoral commission (CENA) 'provisionally', citing the absence of major elections for six years; the body that will run the 2033 vote has not yet been defined. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.

  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterAppeasement after the 2019 post-election crisis: an amnesty recommended by the October 2019 political dialogue
⚠ Rights alertOn 31 October 2019 the same parliament (its predecessor and the BR) unanimously adopted Law 2019-39, an amnesty for all offences committed around the April 2019 legislative elections (April to June 2019): prosecutions ended and detainees were freed. The Constitutional Court validated it. On 4 December 2020 the African Court (Ajavon v. Benin), which found that security forces' live fire during the May 2019 protests violated the rights to life and to physical integrity, ruled that the amnesty denied victims their right to have their cause heard (art. 7(1) of the African Charter) and ordered its repeal and investigations. No position of the party on this part of the ruling was found. source · 2 · 3

Half-wayTalon's constitutional bill was rejected by the National Assembly on 5 April 2017. In November 2025 the UP-R's deputies voted for seven-year terms, renewable once. source · 2 · 3
On the posterExtend school canteens (Government Action Programme)
DeliveredThe national school feeding programme went from 2,199 schools and 425,231 pupils (about 31% coverage) in 2017-18 to 5,532 schools and 1,025,203 pupils (75%) at the end of 2021-22. In canteen schools, girls rose from 282,751 in 2019 to 326,793 in 2021 (+15.5%), according to a 2024 case study for the Research Consortium for School Health and Nutrition. source
Also worth knowing
  • Its founding figure Patrice Talon campaigned in 2016 for a single presidential term; in April 2018 he said the single term was 'no longer relevant, at least as I speak', and he went on to serve two. source
  • The March 2024 electoral code passed by the majority doubled the seat threshold to 20% of the national vote; in January 2026 the main opposition party's 16.20% became zero seats, and the UPR president was then elected Speaker by 109 votes to 0. source
  • Its deputies backed the November 2025 revision that lengthened terms from five to seven years and created a Senate of 25 unelected members; in August 2026 that Senate elected Talon, 74 days out of office, as its president. source
To be fair
  • School meals under its governments reached 1.16 million pupils in 5,351 schools in 2022, up from 31% coverage in 2017-18 (LSHTM case study)
  • Real growth of 7.5% in 2024 and 8.1% in 2025 (INStaD)
  • Public debt down to 53.4% of GDP in 2024 with a deficit at the 3% regional ceiling (UEMOA Commission)

If you're considering voting for UPR, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its founder promised one term, and served two.

He then helped turn five-year terms into seven, and a presidency into a Senate chair.

Its electoral code, meanwhile, made 16% of the vote worth zero seats.

To be fair, school meals under its governments went from 31% coverage to 1.16 million pupils, with more girls in those schools.

On the posterInclusive elections open to the opposition
ConsistentOn 6 March 2024 the majority passed an electoral code raising the threshold for any seat from 10% to 20% of the national vote and allowing deputies and mayors to sponsor only their own party's presidential candidate; the opposition voted against, and LD vice-president Éric Houndété accused the majority of wanting to 'confiscate power'. ConsistentIt opposed the revision creating a 25-member unelected Senate and seven-year terms, adopted on 15 November 2025 by 90 votes to 19 in a secret ballot. Its deputy Kolawolé Djiman Ogbon said senators would sit 'without any mandate from the people'; the party called the vote 'cheating' and announced legal challenges. The majority said it had negotiated with some deputies through the night. source · 2 · 3
On the posterAccountability for public money: a parliamentary inquiry into water and electricity funds
Half-wayAfter a minister said in June 2025 that funds had been diverted, twelve LD deputies led by Nourénou Atchadé tabled a resolution on 25 June. On 4 July 2025 the Assembly set up a ten-member inquiry (4 UP-R, 3 BR, 3 LD) into water and electricity spending in Parakou since 2016. The anti-corruption authority HCPC found no proof of embezzlement in August; no report of the parliamentary inquiry was found. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • It campaigned for inclusive elections and gathered exactly the minimum number of sponsorships for 2026; in October 2025 one of its own deputies, Michel Sodjinou, withdrew his to protest the choice of candidate, and the Constitutional Court then excluded its ticket. source
  • Its deputies attacked the new Senate's members as 'not elected' and sitting 'without any mandate from the people'; its founder Boni Yayi, who left the party and its leadership in March 2026 citing health reasons, took his seat there as a member by right in July 2026. source
  • The party of 'Democrats' chose its new leader in an overnight closed-door session in March 2026 after the interim president walked out; in October its congress confirmed him 'by consensus', without a vote count, and called for the former interim president and four other officials to be struck off. source
To be fair
  • Won 28 seats in 2023, the only opposition group in the 2023-2026 Assembly
  • Its June 2025 resolution led the Assembly to open, on 4 July 2025, a parliamentary inquiry into water and electricity spending in Parakou since 2016
  • Its deputies voted against the 2025 revision that lengthened all terms to seven years
