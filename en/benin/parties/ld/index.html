Benin

Presidential republic · about 14 million people · a parliament with no opposition since February 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 74 monthsNext general elections expected in 2033 (legislative and local in January, presidential in April; no date set)
The November 2025 constitutional revision gave the president, deputies, mayors and local councillors seven-year terms. The deputies and councillors elected on 11 January 2026 and the president elected on 12 April 2026 are therefore due for re-election in 2033 (month estimated from the 2026 calendar). Deputies voted in July 2026 to suspend the electoral commission (CENA) 'provisionally', citing the absence of major elections for six years; the body that will run the 2033 vote has not yet been defined. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

On the posterInclusive elections open to the opposition
ConsistentOn 6 March 2024 the majority passed an electoral code raising the threshold for any seat from 10% to 20% of the national vote and allowing deputies and mayors to sponsor only their own party's presidential candidate; the opposition voted against, and LD vice-president Éric Houndété accused the majority of wanting to 'confiscate power'. Outvoted: in January 2026 LD's 16.20% won no seat. source · 2
On the posterElected representatives with a mandate from the people
ConsistentIt opposed the revision creating a 25-member unelected Senate and seven-year terms, adopted on 15 November 2025 by 90 votes to 19 in a secret ballot. Its deputy Kolawolé Djiman Ogbon said senators would sit 'without any mandate from the people'; the party called the vote 'cheating' and announced legal challenges. The majority said it had negotiated with some deputies through the night. source · 2 · 3
On the posterAccountability for public money: a parliamentary inquiry into water and electricity funds
Half-wayAfter a minister said in June 2025 that funds had been diverted, twelve LD deputies led by Nourénou Atchadé tabled a resolution on 25 June. On 4 July 2025 the Assembly set up a ten-member inquiry (4 UP-R, 3 BR, 3 LD) into water and electricity spending in Parakou since 2016. The anti-corruption authority HCPC found no proof of embezzlement in August; no report of the parliamentary inquiry was found. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • It campaigned for inclusive elections and gathered exactly the minimum number of sponsorships for 2026; in October 2025 one of its own deputies, Michel Sodjinou, withdrew his to protest the choice of candidate, and the Constitutional Court then excluded its ticket. source
  • Its deputies attacked the new Senate's members as 'not elected' and sitting 'without any mandate from the people'; its founder Boni Yayi, who left the party and its leadership in March 2026 citing health reasons, took his seat there as a member by right in July 2026. source
  • The party of 'Democrats' chose its new leader in an overnight closed-door session in March 2026 after the interim president walked out; in October its congress confirmed him 'by consensus', without a vote count, and called for the former interim president and four other officials to be struck off. source
To be fair
  • Won 28 seats in 2023, the only opposition group in the 2023-2026 Assembly
  • Its June 2025 resolution led the Assembly to open, on 4 July 2025, a parliamentary inquiry into water and electricity spending in Parakou since 2016
  • Its deputies voted against the 2025 revision that lengthened all terms to seven years
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Remarks and violence
  • Convicted, 1st instance Soumaïla Sounon Boké (LD deputy in the 2023-2026 legislature; left the party in April 2026, no longer a member): sentenced by the CRIET on 12 May 2026 to five years in prison and a 10 million FCFA fine for glorifying crimes against state security and inciting rebellion, over a WhatsApp message 'c'est la fête' on the day of the 7 December 2025 coup attempt, which he says was sent by mistake; appeal possible, no appeal ruling found
  • Other
  • Convicted, final Reckya Madougou (former Justice minister, designated presidential candidate of Les Démocrates in 2021, whose candidacy was rejected): sentenced by the CRIET on 11 December 2021 to 20 years in prison for complicity in terrorist acts; she pleaded not guilty and no appeal was filed. The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention found her detention arbitrary and without legal basis and called for her release (opinion made public in November 2022)
  • Charged Alassane Tigri (LD vice-president at the time, former minister, elder brother of the alleged coup leader): charged by the CRIET in January 2026 with attack on the authority of the State, complicity in murder and treason in the 7 December 2025 coup case and placed in pretrial detention; he was not in Benin on the day; presumed innocent

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Les Démocrates, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It fought for inclusive elections, and lost its own presidential ticket because of one of its own deputies.

It denounced an unelected Senate. Its founder, who had left the party in March, took a seat there by right in July.

It chose its leader behind closed doors and 'by consensus'.

To be fair, it has never governed, so it has no measured record; in opposition, its resolution got the Assembly to open an inquiry into water and power spending in Parakou.
