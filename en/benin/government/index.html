Benin

Presidential republic · about 14 million people · a parliament with no opposition since February 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 74 monthsNext general elections expected in 2033 (legislative and local in January, presidential in April; no date set)
The November 2025 constitutional revision gave the president, deputies, mayors and local councillors seven-year terms. The deputies and councillors elected on 11 January 2026 and the president elected on 12 April 2026 are therefore due for re-election in 2033 (month estimated from the 2026 calendar). Deputies voted in July 2026 to suspend the electoral commission (CENA) 'provisionally', citing the absence of major elections for six years; the body that will run the 2033 vote has not yet been defined. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in Benin?

Who's at the wheel

President Romuald Wadagni (finance minister under Talon, sworn in on 24 May 2026) and Vice-President Mariam Chabi Talata, with 22 ministers and 3 ministers delegate named the same day: Aristide Médenou (Economy and Finance), Yvon Détchénou (Justice), Djibril Mama Cissé Moussa (Interior, minister delegate), Corinne Amori Brunet (Foreign Affairs), Gildas Agonkan (Defence, minister delegate). Backed by the UPR and the Bloc républicain, joined by the FCBE.

Elected on 12 April 2026 in the first round with 94.27% of valid votes against 5.73% for Paul Hounkpè, on a 63.57% turnout (Constitutional Court, provisional results, confirmed on 23 April with no challenge filed). The main opposition party had no candidate.

What the government promised

  • Wadagni's programme 'Plus loin, ensemble' for 2026-2033 (21 March 2026): six development regions, municipal police, defence technology, a paid national artistic excellence programme
  • Health pledges: vital emergencies treated before payment ('on vous soigne d'abord'), an international hospital in Parakou, regulated traditional medicine
  • First Council of Ministers (3 June 2026): emergency-care scheme, free schooling for girls up to the baccalaureate nationwide from 2026-27, amended 2026 budget up 302.6 billion FCFA

What independent promise trackers found

  • No independent promise tracker found. Independent survey: Afrobarometer (2024) found 62% approving Talon's performance but 52% saying the country was going in the wrong direction. source
  • Not independent: the Government Action Programme (PAG) totals as relayed in May 2026: 12,011 billion FCFA for 2021-2026 across 342 projects, after 9,039 billion for 2016-2021; the '2 million jobs' headline is not attributed to any statistical body. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • INStaD: real GDP grew 8.1% in 2025, after 7.5% in 2024; investment rose 10.7%. source
  • INStaD: about 36.2% of the population lived below the monetary poverty line in 2022, more in rural areas. source
  • UEMOA Commission and Finance Ministry figures (UMOA-Titres, 2026): public debt 53.4% of GDP in 2024, down from 54.5% in 2023; deficit 3.0% of GDP in 2024, at the regional ceiling. source
