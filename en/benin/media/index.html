Benin

Presidential republic · about 14 million people · a parliament with no opposition since February 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 74 monthsNext general elections expected in 2033 (legislative and local in January, presidential in April; no date set)
The November 2025 constitutional revision gave the president, deputies, mayors and local councillors seven-year terms. The deputies and councillors elected on 11 January 2026 and the president elected on 12 April 2026 are therefore due for re-election in 2033 (month estimated from the 2026 calendar). Deputies voted in July 2026 to suspend the electoral commission (CENA) 'provisionally', citing the absence of major elections for six years; the body that will run the 2033 vote has not yet been defined. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Media in Benin

The Reuters Institute Digital News Report does not cover Benin, and we found no recent national survey of trust in news. RSF ranks Benin 113th of 180 in 2026 (47.39), down from 92nd, citing two detained journalists, prior validation of public broadcaster content by a body including executive representatives, and a sharp decline in journalists' freedom of tone; it describes the sector as pluralist but economically fragile. source

Recommended for factual news

SRTB

Société de radio et de télévision du Bénin, the public broadcaster created in 2023 from the ORTB: three TV channels and four radio stations, mainly state-funded; RSF reports that a government-composed committee reviews TV news reports in advance

source
La Nation

National daily of record published by the state press enterprise ONIP; the State Media Monitor finds its editorial line closely aligned with the administration through structural dependency rather than overt censorship

source
Bénin Web TV

Private online outlet; published the election, court and Senate coverage cited on this page; suspended indefinitely by the regulator HAAC in March 2025 after articles on the HAAC's budget

source
La Nouvelle Tribune

Private daily and news site; covered the Assembly's 109-0 vote, the constitutional revision and the CRIET trials cited here

source

Who owns what

  • State: SRTB (TV and radio, ex-ORTB) and ONIP (daily La Nation), both classed state-controlled with no independent editorial governance
  • State news agency: Agence Bénin Presse (ABP)
  • Private: a pluralist but economically fragile sector, with no large, solid media group (RSF)
  • Since May 2026 a Ministry of Communication in charge of the media has been restored

source

Documented facts to know

  • RSF 2026: Benin 113th (47.39), down from 92nd (54.60) in 2025. source
  • HAAC, 11 March 2025: Bénin Web TV suspended indefinitely and its director's press card withdrawn over articles on the regulator's budget request; the MFWA says a 2023 agreement capped suspensions at one month and points to the HAAC president both presiding and deciding. source
  • HAAC, 21 January 2025: six outlets suspended until further notice (L'Audace Infos, Le Patriote, Reporter Médias Monde, Crystal News, Les Pharaons, TikTok account Madame Actu) for operating without authorisation and publishing false information; Le Patriote's publisher links it to articles critical of Talon. source
  • State Media Monitor 2026: SRTB and ONIP are state-controlled with no independent editorial-governance mechanism; RSF says SRTB must relay the government's message. source
