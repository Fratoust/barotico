Who governs in Switzerland?

Who's at the wheel

The seven-member Federal Council: Guy Parmelin (SVP, economy, President in 2026), Albert Rösti (SVP, environment, transport, energy), Karin Keller-Sutter (FDP, finance), Ignazio Cassis (FDP, foreign affairs), Elisabeth Baume-Schneider (SP, home affairs and health), Beat Jans (SP, justice and police) and Martin Pfister (Mitte, defence). Parmelin steps down at the end of 2026.

The Federal Assembly (both chambers sitting together) elects each Federal Councillor for four years; there is no prime minister, no coalition agreement and no vote of no confidence. Each year the Assembly elects one of the seven as President for one year. Decisions are taken collectively by seven equal members. Parmelin's successor is due to be elected on 9 December 2026 and will serve until December 2027.

Sources: Federal Council: members and departments · Parliament fact sheet: Federal Council elections · Parliament: replacement election for Guy Parmelin (9 December 2026) · Federal Chancellery: The Confederation in brief 2026: presidency and collegiality · Federal Council: legislature programme 2023-2027 · Federal Council: parties in the Federal Council

Who holds power

No head of government: the seven Federal Councillors decide together as equals. Guy Parmelin (SVP) is President of the Confederation for 2026, a one-year role elected by parliament.

In government: 166 absolute majority: 101

Concordance: since 1959 the largest parties have shared the seven seats in line with their electoral strength (the 'magic formula', 2 SVP, 2 SP, 2 FDP and 1 Mitte since 2003). There is no coalition agreement and no vote of no confidence, and parties in the Federal Council can campaign against its proposals, as the FDP and SVP do against the VAT rise for the 13th pension voted on 29 November 2026. 'In government' here means only that a party holds a Federal Council seat; the Greens and Green Liberals hold none, and no party is officially designated as the opposition.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Six party cards cover the parties with their own parliamentary group. The EVP (2.0%), the EDU (1.2%), the Lega and the MCG won less than 3% and sit in other groups, so they have no card. National Council seats in 'Who holds power' are derived from the official 2023 result and the group changes listed by parliament, because parliament's pages give no current count per group; the Council of States, which has only informal groups, is not shown.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the 2019 Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, as the survey files could not be opened; no party reaches the far-left threshold, and the SVP is labelled far right on that estimate. No national 'would never vote for' poll was found, so rejection figures are missing. Shares of registered voters are estimated from party strength times turnout, because Swiss voters can split their votes across lists and no national vote count per party exists.

There is no independent promise tracker: the promise-o-meter relies on parliamentary votes, official results and public statistics. Party finance shows declared totals only; the Federal Audit Office publishes the details, but they were not checked against a validated source. Press links come only from the public broadcaster SRG SSR (including swissinfo.ch), the national agency Keystone-SDA (also when republished with a visible credit) and official bodies; claims backed only by other outlets were rewritten or dropped. Cases without a formal act, such as refused immunity requests, are not listed.
