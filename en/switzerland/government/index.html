Reading codes

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

Who governs in Switzerland?

Who's at the wheel

The seven-member Federal Council: Guy Parmelin (SVP, economy, President in 2026), Albert Rösti (SVP, environment, transport, energy), Karin Keller-Sutter (FDP, finance), Ignazio Cassis (FDP, foreign affairs), Elisabeth Baume-Schneider (SP, home affairs and health), Beat Jans (SP, justice and police) and Martin Pfister (Mitte, defence). Parmelin steps down at the end of 2026.

Parliament elects each Federal Councillor for four years; there is no prime minister, no coalition agreement and no vote of no confidence. The presidency rotates every year among the seven. Decisions are taken collectively and defended by all, even by those who voted against. Parmelin's successor will be elected on 9 December 2026.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • SECO: real GDP growth 1.4% in 2025 (sport-event adjusted), inflation 0.2%, unemployment 2.8%; exports to the US fell by about a quarter in Q3 2025 after tariffs rose. source
  • Federal accounts 2025: surplus of CHF 0.3 billion instead of a budgeted deficit of CHF 0.8 billion; the Federal Council still expects structural deficits of CHF 2-4 billion for 2027-2029. source
  • compenswiss: the old-age pension fund (AHV) closed 2025 with a pay-as-you-go surplus of CHF 1.8 billion; the first 13th pension (CHF 4.2 billion) is due in December 2026. source
