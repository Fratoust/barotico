Switzerland

Federal republic with direct democracy · about 9.1 million people · 26 cantons

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.

Voters renew the 200-seat National Council and most of the 46-seat Council of States on 24 October 2027. In December the new parliament then elects the seven members of the Federal Council: the government is chosen by parliament, not by the people. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. Upcoming deadlines and pending cases, with their exact status on the update date.
  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Half-wayThe 13th pension, launched by the unions and the left, was approved in March 2024 with 58.2% and 15 of 23 cantons; the first payment, to about 2.6 million pensioners at a cost of more than CHF 4 billion a year, is due in December 2026. To finance it the SP backs a 0.4-point VAT rise, to 8.5%, in an alliance with the Mitte, the Greens, the EVP and the unions; the FDP and the SVP oppose it, and voters decide on 29 November 2026. source · 2 · 3
On the posterPremiums capped at 10% of income
Half-wayIts premium-relief initiative was rejected in June 2024 with 55.5% no and failed the cantons' majority. The counter-proposal, in force since 1 January 2026, obliges cantons to spend at least 3.5% to 7.5% of basic health-insurance costs on premium subsidies, about CHF 360 million more by the federal estimate (2020 data); average premiums still rise 5.0% in 2027. source · 2 · 3
On the posterThe right to asylum must not be questioned
Half-wayIts justice minister Beat Jans extended the 24-hour asylum procedure for applicants from Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia and Libya nationwide in April 2024: applications from the Maghreb fell 42% and first decisions took 17 days instead of 52 (SEM); of 37 appeals, the Federal Administrative Court had upheld 31 by September, the rest pending. SRF described the measure as aimed at deterring applicants with little chance of asylum. source · 2
On the posterA public climate fund
ConsistentWith the Greens it launched the climate fund initiative: 0.5% to 1% of GDP a year from the Confederation for climate action. Voters rejected it on 8 March 2026 by 70.7%, with no canton in favour. source
Also worth knowing
  • It says the right to asylum must not be questioned. Its own justice minister Beat Jans rolled out 24-hour asylum procedures nationwide for applicants from Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco, designed to deter people with little chance of being granted asylum. source
  • It campaigns for affordable health premiums. Its initiative to cap premiums at 10% of income was rejected in June 2024 with 55.5% no; the counter-proposal instead obliges cantons to put a minimum share of health costs into premium subsidies. source
  • With the unions it won the 13th pension at the ballot box. To pay for it, it now backs, with the Mitte, the Greens and the EVP, raising the standard VAT rate from 8.1% to 8.5%: a tax on purchases paid by everyone, with food and medicines exempt. source
To be fair
  • With the unions and the left, won the 13th AHV pension in March 2024 (58.2% yes, 15 of 23 cantons) against the right, the centre and business groups.
  • Its minister's 24-hour procedure cut Maghreb asylum applications by 42% nationwide and the time to a first decision from 52 to 17 days (SEM figures, 2024).
  • Its two Federal Councillors sit in the government that sent the EU treaty package to parliament, in line with its call for close relations with Europe.

Also worth knowing

If you're considering voting for FDP/PLR, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It is the party of less state. In March 2023 its finance minister used emergency law to back the UBS takeover of Credit Suisse with CHF 109 billion in federal guarantees, before parliament had any say. The parliamentary inquiry later acknowledged that a global financial crisis had been averted.

It rejects VAT rises, for the 13th pension and, 'in no uncertain terms', for defence, and wants savings instead.

Its delegates backed the EU package by 330 to 104 in 2025; a year later its co-president said the party had 'not yet issued a voting recommendation', as is customary before the parliamentary debate ends.

To be fair, the 2025 federal accounts closed CHF 1.1 billion better than budgeted.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Six party cards cover the parties with their own parliamentary group. The EVP (2.0%), the EDU (1.2%), the Lega and the MCG won less than 3% and sit in other groups, so they have no card. National Council seats in 'Who holds power' are derived from the official 2023 result and the group changes listed by parliament, because parliament's pages give no current count per group; the Council of States, which has only informal groups, is not shown.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the 2019 Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, as the survey files could not be opened; no party reaches the far-left threshold, and the SVP is labelled far right on that estimate. No national 'would never vote for' poll was found, so rejection figures are missing. Shares of registered voters are estimated from party strength times turnout, because Swiss voters can split their votes across lists and no national vote count per party exists.

There is no independent promise tracker: the promise-o-meter relies on parliamentary votes, official results and public statistics. Party finance shows declared totals only; the Federal Audit Office publishes the details, but they were not checked against a validated source. Press links come only from the public broadcaster SRG SSR (including swissinfo.ch), the national agency Keystone-SDA (also when republished with a visible credit) and official bodies; claims backed only by other outlets were rewritten or dropped. Cases without a formal act, such as refused immunity requests, are not listed.
