Switzerland

Federal republic with direct democracy · about 9.1 million people · 26 cantons

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote342 daysNext federal election: National Council and Council of States
Voters renew the 200-seat National Council and most of the 46-seat Council of States on 24 October 2027. In December the new parliament then elects the seven members of the Federal Council: the government is chosen by parliament, not by the people. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Switzerland

Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: 42% of Swiss adults trust most news most of the time, down 4 points (45% in German-speaking Switzerland, 37% in French-speaking Switzerland; global average 37%); 39% sometimes or often avoid the news; 19% pay for online news. Public-service brands are the most trusted in both language regions, followed by subscription newspapers; tabloids, digital-born brands and portals are trusted less. source

Recommended for factual news

SRG SSR (SRF, RTS, RSI)

Public broadcaster in four languages (SRF, RTS, RSI, RTR and swissinfo.ch), with the largest share of national opinion power (28%, Federal Office of Communications study 2025); public-service brands are the most trusted in both language regions (Reuters Institute DNR 2026).

source
Keystone-SDA

National news agency covering German, French and Italian with 12 regional desks; it receives up to CHF 4 million a year in federal support under an agreement with the Federal Office of Communications. Its dispatches republished with a visible credit (e.g. on blue News) are cited as agency copy.

source
20 Minuten / 20 minutes

Free daily and news site of TX Group, one of the most powerful brand systems in the country (Federal Office of Communications study 2025).

source

Video explainers

SRF Arena

Political debate programme of the public broadcaster where party leaders face off before votes, e.g. SVP, SP, FDP and Mitte on the EU package (October 2025). No audience figures or documented criticism were researched.

source

Who owns what

  • SRG SSR: public service broadcaster (SRF, RTS, RSI, RTR), 28% of national opinion power.
  • TX Group: 20 Minuten and German-Swiss print brands, 14%.
  • CH Media: radio, TV, online and German-Swiss print brands, 11%.
  • Ringier: 5%. NZZ group, Corriere del Ticino, Somedia and ESH Médias lag well behind; Meta (Instagram) alone reaches 9%.

source

Documented facts to know

  • The ten largest media groups control 105 of the 181 brands studied (Federal Office of Communications media monitor, survey year 2024). source
  • On 8 March 2026 voters rejected the initiative to cut the radio and TV licence fee to CHF 200 by about 62%, with no canton in favour. source
  • RSF 2026: Switzerland 8th of 180; the legal context is its weakest indicator (18th), notably Article 47 of the Banking Act, which can punish journalists using leaked bank data with up to three years in prison. source
  • Measured trust by media type (UBS Worry Barometer 2025, scale 1-7): radio 4.2, paid newspapers 4.1, television 3.9, free newspapers 3.0, YouTube and direct online sources 2.7. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Six party cards cover the parties with their own parliamentary group. The EVP (2.0%), the EDU (1.2%), the Lega and the MCG won less than 3% and sit in other groups, so they have no card. National Council seats in 'Who holds power' are derived from the official 2023 result and the group changes listed by parliament, because parliament's pages give no current count per group; the Council of States, which has only informal groups, is not shown.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the 2019 Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, as the survey files could not be opened; no party reaches the far-left threshold, and the SVP is labelled far right on that estimate. No national 'would never vote for' poll was found, so rejection figures are missing. Shares of registered voters are estimated from party strength times turnout, because Swiss voters can split their votes across lists and no national vote count per party exists.

There is no independent promise tracker: the promise-o-meter relies on parliamentary votes, official results and public statistics. Party finance shows declared totals only; the Federal Audit Office publishes the details, but they were not checked against a validated source. Press links come only from the public broadcaster SRG SSR (including swissinfo.ch), the national agency Keystone-SDA (also when republished with a visible credit) and official bodies; claims backed only by other outlets were rewritten or dropped. Cases without a formal act, such as refused immunity requests, are not listed.
