Country profile · 1 Nov 2026

Turkey (Türkiye)

Presidential republic since 2018 · about 86 million people

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Next vote548 daysNext national vote: presidential and parliamentary elections, regular date 7 May 2028 (could be brought forward)
The President and the 600 deputies are elected on the same day for five years; the last vote was on 14 May 2023, with a presidential run-off on 28 May. President Erdoğan's chief adviser Mehmet Uçum says the regular date is 7 May 2028 and has floated 16 April 2028 instead: if at least 360 deputies vote to renew the elections early, Erdoğan, who has already served two terms, could stand again without a constitutional amendment. According to Uçum, parliament could take that decision between 9 and 15 February 2028. The Supreme Election Council (YSK) has not yet published a calendar. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted

  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

91 deputies left the main opposition party on one day in July 2026, and the CHP, founded in 1923, dropped to 5th place in parliament.

On 21 May 2026 an appeals court annulled the CHP congress that had elected Özgür Özel in 2023 and put Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu back in charge pending a final ruling. On 24 July Özel and 90 deputies resigned and founded YENİ Parti, which became the second group in parliament; the CHP went from 135 seats to 44 (Evrensel).

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Citizens aged 18 and over are registered automatically from the address registration system: check your entry and update your address before the YSK closes the lists. Turks abroad vote at consulates in a set period before polling day, or at border gates.

Last turnout: 87.05% in the May 2023 parliamentary election (88.92% in Turkey, 53.80% abroad); 84.15% in the presidential run-off source

Who decides what

PresidencySince 2018 the directly elected President is both head of state and head of government: he appoints the vice-president and ministers without a confidence vote, issues presidential decrees and prepares the budget. Recep Tayyip Erdoğan won the May 2023 run-off with 52.18%.
Grand National Assembly (TBMM)A single chamber of 600 deputies passes laws and the budget and can lift members' immunity. A party needs 20 deputies to form a group. In October 2026, 592 seats were filled: AKP 281, YENİ Parti 91, DEM 56, MHP 46, CHP 44, İYİ 27, Yeni Yol 20, others and independents 27.
Courts and electoral councilThe Constitutional Court (AYM), the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) and the Council of State review laws, verdicts and administrative acts; the Supreme Election Council (YSK) runs and certifies elections. Freedom House scores judicial independence 1 out of 4 in 2026.
Provinces and municipalitiesThe 81 provinces are run by governors appointed by the central government; mayors and councils are elected every five years (last in March 2024, when the CHP came first with 37.77%). The Interior Ministry can replace removed mayors with trustees (kayyum), as in Mardin and Batman in November 2024.

How people vote

  • The President is elected by direct vote in up to two rounds; a candidate needs more than 50%. In 2023 the run-off drew 84.15% turnout.
  • Deputies are elected the same day on party lists by province; a party or its alliance needs 7% nationwide to win seats.
  • Citizens abroad vote at consulates and border gates; 56.34% of them turned out in the 2023 run-off.

How a law is passed

  1. Only deputies can table bills; the 2026 'framework law' of the peace process was submitted with about 360 signatures.
  2. A committee (for example the Justice Committee) examines the text, then the plenary votes article by article.
  3. The President signs the law or sends it back; it takes effect when published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete), as the framework law did on 18 August 2026, eight days after the vote.
  4. Short cut: the President governs many areas by presidential decree, without a vote of parliament.

Why it's complicated

Three stories run at once. First, the opposition in court: Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu was detained on 19 March 2025 and remanded in custody on 23 March, the day of the primary that made him the CHP's presidential candidate, and has been on trial since March 2026 with 435 co-defendants; prosecutors seek 828 to 2,352 years. The Council of Europe's Parliamentary Assembly said his arrest gave 'the impression of political motives', and Human Rights Watch speaks of arbitrary charges. Second, the CHP itself: on 21 May 2026 an appeals court annulled its 2023 congress and reinstated Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu; the Court of Cassation has not ruled, Özgür Özel and 90 deputies founded YENİ Parti in July, and İmamoğlu left the CHP in August. Third, peace: the PKK announced its dissolution in May 2025, a parliamentary commission adopted a report in February 2026 (47 for, 2 against), and a framework law passed 467 to 87 in August 2026; its measures wait for a National Security Council decision confirming disarmament. Meanwhile official inflation was 29.73% in September 2026, against 46.61% for the independent ENAG group. Freedom House rates Turkey 'Not Free' (32/100).

Why it's like thisThe Republic founded in 1923 was ruled by a single party until multiparty elections in 1946, then interrupted by military interventions in 1960, 1971 and 1980; the 1982 Constitution, written under military rule, still frames the state. Parliamentary governments were often short-lived coalitions. After the failed coup attempt of July 2016, the AKP and the MHP proposed an executive presidency, approved by referendum on 16 April 2017 and in force since 2018: supporters argued that it ended unstable coalitions, critics that it removed checks on the executive. Kurdish politics, a 40-year conflict with the PKK and repeated closures of pro-Kurdish parties explain why the DEM Party's predecessors changed names several times.

Who's at the wheel

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (AKP), re-elected on 28 May 2023; Vice-President Cevdet Yılmaz. The 'People's Alliance' (Cumhur İttifakı) of the AKP and the MHP holds a majority in parliament; the MHP has no ministers.

Erdoğan won the run-off with 52.18% (27,834,589 votes) against Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu (47.82%) on an 84.15% turnout. In the parliamentary vote of 14 May 2023 the AKP won 268 seats (35.62%) and the MHP 50 (10.07%); with later transfers the AKP had 281 seats in October 2026.

What the government promised

  • AKP 2023 election manifesto ('Century of Türkiye'): single-digit inflation, 6 million jobs and unemployment at 7%, 650,000 earthquake homes (319,000 within a year), a new civilian constitution
  • Medium-Term Programme 2027-2029 (6 September 2026): year-end 2026 inflation forecast raised from 16% to 28.4%; 21% in 2027, 13.5% in 2028, 9% in 2029
  • 'Terror-free Türkiye' framework law (in force 18 August 2026): sentences for PKK-related offences can be deferred once security bodies confirm disarmament

What independent promise trackers found

  • No independent promise tracker found. Against the 2023 manifesto: single-digit inflation is now planned for 2029 in the government's own programme (not independent), and TÜİK measured unemployment at 7.8% in August 2026 against a 7% pledge. source
  • Earthquake housing: 319,000 homes were promised within a year in 2023, and the overall target fell from 650,000 to 453,000 (Sözcü, November 2025). The government counted 455,357 homes and workplaces completed by 27 December 2025 (its own figure, not independent). source
