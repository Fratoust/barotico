Turkey (Türkiye)

Presidential republic since 2018 · about 86 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote538 daysNext national vote: presidential and parliamentary elections, regular date 7 May 2028 (could be brought forward)
The President and the 600 deputies are elected on the same day for five years; the last vote was on 14 May 2023, with a presidential run-off on 28 May. President Erdoğan's chief adviser Mehmet Uçum says the regular date is 7 May 2028 and has floated 16 April 2028 instead: if at least 360 deputies vote to renew the elections early, Erdoğan, who has already served two terms, could stand again without a constitutional amendment. According to Uçum, parliament could take that decision between 9 and 15 February 2028. The Supreme Election Council (YSK) has not yet published a calendar. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. Half-wayIt voted for the peace commission's report in February 2026 while objecting that Kurdish should not remain 'only a language of communication'. The report contains no language provision, and the MHP's member stressed that the commission's work involves no constitutional change. source
Also worth knowing
  • In January 2025 its group deputy chair asked how there could be 'both trustees and a solution', after five of its mayors, including Ahmet Türk in Mardin, were replaced by trustees; it stayed in the process, and in August 2026 all 55 of its deputies who voted backed the framework law. source
  • Campaigns against the presidential system's concentration of power; in October 2026 its Diyarbakır deputy Cengiz Çandar said that backing Erdoğan's new candidacy could be considered in exchange for a constitutional promise on equal citizenship (a group deputy chair called it his 'own projection'). source
  • In February 2026 it objected to the commission report calling Abdullah Öcalan a 'terrorist organisation leader', saying he started the process; the framework law it then voted for excludes 'organisation leaders' from its measures. source
To be fair
  • Voted for the commission report and the framework law of the process that followed the PKK's May 2025 dissolution decision
  • The ECtHR judgment on Selahattin Demirtaş's detention became final on 3 November 2025, after Turkey's referral request was rejected
  • Won 3 metropolitan and 7 provincial mayoralties in March 2024 (5.70%)

  • Partly kept A legal framework for peace: law in force since 18 August 2026 with its votes, but its measures wait for a security council decision on disarmament source
  • Never tested End trustee rule over elected mayors: never had the majority to change the law
  • Never tested Constitutional equal citizenship and mother-tongue rights: no amendment tabled with a majority

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for DEM Party, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In January 2025 it asked how there could be 'both trustees and a solution', after five of its mayors elected in 2024 had been replaced. It stayed in the process, and in August 2026 all 55 of its deputies present voted for the peace law.

It objected to the commission calling Abdullah Öcalan a 'terrorist organisation leader'. The law it backed excludes 'organisation leaders' from its measures.

In October 2026 one of its deputies said backing Erdoğan's next candidacy could be considered in exchange for a constitutional promise. A group deputy chair called that his 'own projection'.

To be fair, it has never governed nationally, so no statistics office has measured a result of its own; its 55 votes helped the peace law pass 467 to 87.
