Argentina

Federal presidential republic · about 46 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote342 daysNext general election: Sunday 24 October 2027 (President, half of the Chamber, a third of the Senate)
The law sets the general election on the fourth Sunday of October. In 2027 voters choose the President and Vice-President, 130 deputies, 24 senators, 21 governors and the head of government of Buenos Aires City. A run-off follows if nobody wins in the first round. The government wants to suspend the open primaries (PASO) for 2027, but in October 2026 it still lacked the 37 Senate votes needed. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Leaning left

On the posterOn the record
On the posterLegalise abortion (2019 campaign)
DeliveredIts government sent the bill and Congress passed it in December 2020. Some 245,000 voluntary and legal terminations were recorded in the public sector up to October 2023, and maternal deaths from abortion fell from 23 in 2020 to 13 in 2021 (Ministry of Health, via Chequeado). source
On the posterDefend wages and pensions
Half-wayIn December 2019 its majority suspended the pension indexation formula and replaced it with discretionary rises. In 2020 the minimum pension roughly kept pace with inflation (35%) and beat it with bonuses, while medium and high pensions lost 4% to 10%; under the suspended formula all would have risen 42% (Chequeado). In opposition, in August 2025 it voted to override Milei's veto of a pension rise, which fell 6 votes short of two-thirds. source · 2
On the posterA present State that protects the poor
Half-wayUnder its 2019-2023 government, poverty rose from 35.5% in the second half of 2019 to 40.1% in the first half of 2023 and 41.7% in the second half, with the pandemic in between (INDEC data compiled by Chequeado). source
On the posterEconomic independence and political sovereignty
Half-wayIts government negotiated the 2022 IMF programme to refinance the 2018 loan, arguing that a default would be 'much more serious'. The Chamber approved it 202-37 in March 2022; 28 of its deputies, Máximo Kirchner among them, voted against and 13 abstained. In opposition, in March 2025 it opposed Milei's decree for a new IMF deal, saying it bypassed a 2021 law requiring Congress to approve such debt; the decree was validated 129-108. source · 2
On the posterFree public education
ConsistentIn opposition it voted for the university funding laws. In October 2024 Milei's veto survived with 84 votes; in October 2025 Congress overrode the second veto (58-7 in the Senate), but Decree 759/2025 suspended the law's execution until Congress sets out how to fund it. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • Promised in 2019 to put money back in people's pockets; when its government ended, inflation for 2023 was 211.4%, the highest in three decades. source
  • Its own government negotiated the 2022 IMF agreement; its bloc leader in the Chamber, Máximo Kirchner, resigned and he and La Cámpora voted against it. source
  • Promised to defend pensions; in December 2019 it suspended their indexation formula, and in 2020 medium and high pensions lost 4% to 10% to inflation, while all would have risen 42% under the suspended formula (Chequeado). source
To be fair
  • Created the Universal Child Allowance (AUH) in 2009, a monthly payment for children of informal and unemployed workers
  • Its government sent and passed the 2020 law legalising abortion up to the 14th week
  • Restructured most of the foreign-law debt held by private creditors in 2020
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Integrity
  • Convicted, final Cristina Fernández de Kirchner (PJ president, President 2007-2015): convicted, final, of fraudulent administration to the detriment of the public administration in the Vialidad public-works case: six years in prison and a lifelong ban from public office, upheld by the Supreme Court on 10 June 2025; serving the sentence under house arrest
  • Trial Cristina Fernández de Kirchner: on trial since November 2025 before Federal Oral Court No. 7 in the Cuadernos case (alleged corruption network around public works, 2003-2015), with 86 other defendants
  • Charged Alberto Fernández (President 2019-2023): charged (procesado) with negotiations incompatible with public office and breach of duty in the state insurance (Seguros) case; upheld on appeal in November 2025
  • Remarks and violence
  • Charged Alberto Fernández: charged with minor and serious injuries aggravated by a context of gender violence, and coercive threats, against his former partner (upheld on appeal 2025); a federal judge rejected his nullity request in April 2026 and an oral trial is pending

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept, made a difference Legalise abortion (2019 campaign): law passed in December 2020; deaths from abortion down from 23 in 2020 to 13 in 2021 (Ministry of Health) source · measured effect
  • Broken Bring inflation down and put money back in people's pockets: 211.4% in 2023 source
  • Broken Reduce poverty: 35.5% in 2019, 40.1% in the first half of 2023 (INDEC) source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Unión por la Patria, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised to put money back in people's pockets. When it handed over power, inflation stood at 211%.

Its own bloc leader walked out over its own IMF deal.

It also promised to defend pensions. After it suspended their indexation formula, only the minimum pension kept up with inflation in 2020; medium and high pensions lost up to 10%, according to Chequeado.

To be fair, after its abortion law, deaths from abortion fell from 23 in 2020 to 13 in 2021, according to the Ministry of Health.
