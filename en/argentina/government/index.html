Argentina

Federal presidential republic · about 46 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote342 daysNext general election: Sunday 24 October 2027 (President, half of the Chamber, a third of the Senate)
The law sets the general election on the fourth Sunday of October. In 2027 voters choose the President and Vice-President, 130 deputies, 24 senators, 21 governors and the head of government of Buenos Aires City. A run-off follows if nobody wins in the first round. The government wants to suspend the open primaries (PASO) for 2027, but in October 2026 it still lacked the 37 Senate votes needed. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in Argentina?

Who's at the wheel

President Javier Milei (La Libertad Avanza) since 10 December 2023, with Vice-President Victoria Villarruel, Chief of Cabinet Diego Santilli (since 30 June 2026) and Economy Minister Luis Caputo.

Milei won the 2023 run-off. In the 26 October 2025 midterm La Libertad Avanza came first nationally with 40.8%, ahead of the Peronist Fuerza Patria. Its bloc grew to 95 deputies and, by February 2026, 21 senators, with the PRO as its main ally; it still needs governors and dialogue blocs to pass laws.

Who holds power

President Javier Milei (La Libertad Avanza) since 10 December 2023, with Vice-President Victoria Villarruel, Chief of Cabinet Diego Santilli (since 30 June 2026) and Economy Minister Luis Caputo.

In government: 95 absolute majority: 129

The parties in government hold 95 of 257 seats; an absolute majority is 129. In a presidential system the government does not need the chamber's confidence to stay in office, but to pass laws it needs 34 more votes from other groups. With the parties that support it (12 seats), the total is 107.

Seats as of December 2025, when the deputies elected in October 2025 took office. “Other blocs” groups the provincial and smaller blocs not on a card.

What the government promised

  • Labour modernisation law, passed by the Senate on 27 February 2026 (42-28): company-level agreements take priority, a fund to pay severance, hour banks, 75% minimum service in essential sectors during strikes
  • 2027 budget bill (15 September 2026): 18% inflation, 4% growth, 1.5% of GDP primary surplus
  • Electoral reform: suspend the PASO primaries for 2027 and change the single ballot (stalled in the Senate in October 2026)

What independent promise trackers found

  • Chequeado (independent fact-checker), December 2025: of 20 campaign promises, 2 fulfilled, 8 in progress ahead of schedule, 7 in progress but delayed (including dollarisation and closing the Central Bank) and 3 unfulfilled (including 'make the State bear the adjustment'). source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • INDEC: consumer prices up 1.9% in September 2026 and 33.4% over twelve months; 23.6% since January. source
  • INDEC: 32.3% of people below the poverty line in the first half of 2026, up from 28.2% in the second half of 2025; indigence 7.5%. source
  • INDEC: unemployment 7.9% in the second quarter of 2026, the highest since 2021. source
