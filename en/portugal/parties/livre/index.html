Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

  • Never tested More open and humane migration rules: never in government, untested
  • Never tested Ecology and a green transition: never in government, untested

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Livre, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promised a new way of doing politics. Three months after its first MP was elected in 2019, its assembly withdrew political confidence from her.

It asks the left to drop sectarianism. For the presidential election, it fielded its own candidate, Jorge Pinto.

In 2025 it missed every public target it had set itself, including doubling its group. It still grew from 4 to 6 seats.

To be fair, its four-day-week idea became a state pilot: 93% of the workers in it wanted to keep the shorter week, and the final report found less exhaustion.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has cards for the seven parties with a parliamentary group after the election of 18 May 2025. PSD and CDS-PP ran together as AD but sit as two groups, so each has its own card. BE, PAN and JPP (one seat each, under 3% of the vote) have no card and appear only in the seat chart. Seats by group are the Lusa count at the start of the legislature; the parliament's own page could not be opened for this update, and no change of group was found up to 15 November 2026.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, not re-checked against the dataset; Chega and the PCP are labelled far right and far left under that rule. BE, PAN and JPP are placed in the seat chart by judgement. The election date is an estimate: the legislature ends in autumn 2029 unless parliament is dissolved.

No party publishes a percentage breakdown of its income, so party finance gives the 2025 figures filed with the party-finance authority. No programme on the party's own site was found for Chega, the PS or Livre. No independent promise tracker exists; the government's own anti-corruption count is shown as not independent. The homicide rate is calculated from the official 2025 count and the resident population. The satisfaction gauge is the latest poll from a validated source (July 2026).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the Lusa agency, RTP, Renascença, the EFCSN fact-checker Polígrafo, and the brands trusted by 70% or more in the Digital News Report Portugal 2026, plus the regional press. Claims found only in other outlets were rewritten from validated sources or dropped, among them the vote count of 3 July 2026 on the nationality decree. Cases without an official act, private disputes, former members and dismissals older than 12 months are left out.
