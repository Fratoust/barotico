Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

On the posterBan face coverings such as the burqa in public
Half-wayIts bill became Law 58/2026, passed on 17 July with PSD, CDS and IL and in force since 23 September 2026: fines of €150 to €3,000, and up to two years in prison for forcing someone to cover their face. No figure on how many people it concerns has been published. source · 2
On the posterTight control of immigration
⚠ Rights alertIts 60 votes carried the government's foreigners law on 16 July 2025 (IL abstained). On 8 August 2025 the Constitutional Court struck down, by majority, four norms on substance (ruling 785/2025): excluding spouses from family reunification, a two-year wait and decision periods of up to 18 months breached the State's duty to protect the family, and a limit on urgent court protection breached effective judicial protection. Chega voted for the revised version passed on 30 September 2025. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterLoss of nationality for naturalised citizens who commit serious crimes
⚠ Rights alertIts votes were needed to pass the penalty on 28 October 2025. The Constitutional Court struck it down unanimously on 15 December 2025 and, in a revised version, again on 8 May 2026: it treated naturalised citizens differently from citizens by birth (equality) and was disproportionate for most of the crimes listed (rulings 1134/2025 and 409/2026). André Ventura called on Parliament to confirm it regardless, or to hold a referendum; Chega alone asked for a new vote to confirm it, while PSD and IL said they would not go against the Court (May 2026). source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • Ventura had said that the opposition leader should not also be a presidential candidate, calling it a bad sign for democracy; in September 2025 he ran anyway, saying he had not wanted to but would go if the party wished, and reached the run-off. source
  • Promised to raise the lowest pensions in stages, first to the social support index and then to the minimum wage, at a cost André Ventura put at €1.6 billion (January 2024), without saying how it would be funded. source
  • Its programme invokes the rule of law; after the Constitutional Court unanimously struck down loss of nationality as a criminal penalty (May 2026), Ventura urged Parliament to reconfirm it regardless of the Court and proposed a referendum. source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Chega, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It promises 'the biggest pension rise Portugal has ever had', at a cost André Ventura put at €1.6 billion, without saying how it would be funded. In the 2025 budget, its abstention let through a smaller permanent rise, proposed by the PS.

Its showcase law, the ban on covering the face in public, has applied since 23 September 2026. No one has yet counted the people it concerns.

Its votes carried the loss of nationality for naturalised criminals. The Constitutional Court struck it down unanimously, twice, for breaching equality and proportionality. André Ventura asked Parliament to confirm it regardless, or to hold a referendum; Chega was the only party to ask for that new vote.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has cards for the seven parties with a parliamentary group after the election of 18 May 2025. PSD and CDS-PP ran together as AD but sit as two groups, so each has its own card. BE, PAN and JPP (one seat each, under 3% of the vote) have no card and appear only in the seat chart. Seats by group are the Lusa count at the start of the legislature; the parliament's own page could not be opened for this update, and no change of group was found up to 15 November 2026.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, not re-checked against the dataset; Chega and the PCP are labelled far right and far left under that rule. BE, PAN and JPP are placed in the seat chart by judgement. The election date is an estimate: the legislature ends in autumn 2029 unless parliament is dissolved.

No party publishes a percentage breakdown of its income, so party finance gives the 2025 figures filed with the party-finance authority. No programme on the party's own site was found for Chega, the PS or Livre. No independent promise tracker exists; the government's own anti-corruption count is shown as not independent. The homicide rate is calculated from the official 2025 count and the resident population. The satisfaction gauge is the latest poll from a validated source (July 2026).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the Lusa agency, RTP, Renascença, the EFCSN fact-checker Polígrafo, and the brands trusted by 70% or more in the Digital News Report Portugal 2026, plus the regional press. Claims found only in other outlets were rewritten from validated sources or dropped, among them the vote count of 3 July 2026 on the nationality decree. Cases without an official act, private disputes, former members and dismissals older than 12 months are left out.
