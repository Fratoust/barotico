Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Rights alertIts government's foreigners law passed on 16 July 2025 with PSD, Chega and CDS votes (IL abstained). On the posterStricter nationality rules, with loss of nationality for serious crimes
⚠ Rights alertIts government's reform, voted on 28 October 2025 with Chega, IL, CDS and JPP, raised the residence required from 5 to 10 years. On 15 December 2025 the Constitutional Court struck down, among other norms, an automatic bar for anyone sentenced to two years in prison (disproportionate) and, unanimously, the loss-of-nationality penalty, for breaching equality between citizens and proportionality (rulings 1133 and 1134/2025). A revised penalty fell unanimously again on 8 May 2026 (ruling 409/2026). After the second ruling its parliamentary leader, Hugo Soares, said the PSD did not intend to provoke an institutional conflict, and the party declined to confirm the decree (May 2026). source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • Montenegro's first government fell on a lost confidence vote on 11 March 2025, amid controversy over his finances and his family firm Spinumviva; the snap election followed. Prosecutors closed a preventive inquiry into the firm in December 2025, finding no indication of a crime. source
  • Its 2024 health emergency plan promised 20 new family-health units (model C) by the end of 2024 to cover 360,000 people; tenders were only authorised at the end of 2025, and patients without a family doctor rose to 1.56 million. source
  • Its "no is no" to Chega rules out a government deal, not talks in Parliament, as Montenegro restated in May 2026; its nationality reform passed with Chega's votes in October 2025, and the Constitutional Court struck down parts of the package in December 2025 and May 2026. source

  • Unemployment fell to 6.0% in 2025, the lowest annual rate since 2011 (INE).
  • Won Lisbon and Porto in the October 2025 local elections.

  • Partly kept Tighter nationality rules, including loss of nationality for serious crimes (negotiated with Chega): the Constitutional Court unanimously struck down the loss-of-nationality penalty twice, in December 2025 and May 2026 source
  • Partly kept Revise labour law, one of the government's main reforms: rejected by Parliament on 19 June 2026 after talks with Chega failed; only PSD, CDS-PP and IL voted for it source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the PSD, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In 2024 its government promised a family doctor for everyone by the end of 2025. At the end of that year, 1,563,710 people still had none, about as many as when it took office.

It promises lower taxes on work. Its 2025 income-tax cut was worth about €500 million; the tax burden, meanwhile, rose to 35.4% of GDP. Its state guarantee helped 25,519 young people get a mortgage, as house prices jumped 17.6%.

Its foreigners and nationality laws, passed with Chega, were partly struck down by the Constitutional Court in 2025 and 2026, on family protection, equality and proportionality.

To be fair, it has run budget surpluses, debt fell to 89.7% of GDP, and unemployment fell to 6.0% in 2025, the lowest since 2011 (INE).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has cards for the seven parties with a parliamentary group after the election of 18 May 2025. PSD and CDS-PP ran together as AD but sit as two groups, so each has its own card. BE, PAN and JPP (one seat each, under 3% of the vote) have no card and appear only in the seat chart. Seats by group are the Lusa count at the start of the legislature; the parliament's own page could not be opened for this update, and no change of group was found up to 15 November 2026.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, not re-checked against the dataset; Chega and the PCP are labelled far right and far left under that rule. BE, PAN and JPP are placed in the seat chart by judgement. The election date is an estimate: the legislature ends in autumn 2029 unless parliament is dissolved.

No party publishes a percentage breakdown of its income, so party finance gives the 2025 figures filed with the party-finance authority. No programme on the party's own site was found for Chega, the PS or Livre. No independent promise tracker exists; the government's own anti-corruption count is shown as not independent. The homicide rate is calculated from the official 2025 count and the resident population. The satisfaction gauge is the latest poll from a validated source (July 2026).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the Lusa agency, RTP, Renascença, the EFCSN fact-checker Polígrafo, and the brands trusted by 70% or more in the Digital News Report Portugal 2026, plus the regional press. Claims found only in other outlets were rewritten from validated sources or dropped, among them the vote count of 3 July 2026 on the nationality decree. Cases without an official act, private disputes, former members and dismissals older than 12 months are left out.
