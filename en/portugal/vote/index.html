Voting in Portugal

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Citizens are registered automatically from 18. Portuguese living abroad elect 4 of the 230 deputies through two overseas constituencies. Practical details (polling station, early voting) are published by the Comissão Nacional de Eleições before each vote.

Last turnout: 58.3% in the May 2025 legislative election (including voters abroad); 50.0% in the February 2026 presidential run-off source

The electoral system

  • Assembly: 230 deputies elected by proportional representation (D'Hondt method) in 22 constituencies, two of them for Portuguese living abroad.
  • President: direct vote in two rounds, five-year term, at most two in a row.
  • A party concentrated in one area can win a seat: in 2025 JPP won one in Madeira.

Sources: Constitution, Art. 121-128, 149 · DNoticias: how votes are counted in legislative elections · Renascença: final 2025 results, including JPP's seat in Madeira

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Portugal, a blank vote is counted separately and counts in turnout, but not among the valid votes used to share out seats.

What it is

  • Blank vote: a ballot handed in with no mark or sign at all. source
  • Spoilt vote: more than one box marked, writing or crossings-out; also a mark for a withdrawn or non-admitted candidate still printed on the ballot. source

How it is counted

  • Only valid votes count for seats: blank and spoilt votes neither help nor harm any list directly. source

The figures

  • 1.06%of votes were blank in the first round of the January 2026 presidential election (61,226), fewer than spoilt votes (65,381, 1.13%). source

What it changes for the result

  • Blank votes are published and discussed, but a candidate or a list wins with valid votes alone. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has cards for the seven parties with a parliamentary group after the election of 18 May 2025. PSD and CDS-PP ran together as AD but sit as two groups, so each has its own card. BE, PAN and JPP (one seat each, under 3% of the vote) have no card and appear only in the seat chart. Seats by group are the Lusa count at the start of the legislature; the parliament's own page could not be opened for this update, and no change of group was found up to 15 November 2026.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, not re-checked against the dataset; Chega and the PCP are labelled far right and far left under that rule. BE, PAN and JPP are placed in the seat chart by judgement. The election date is an estimate: the legislature ends in autumn 2029 unless parliament is dissolved.

No party publishes a percentage breakdown of its income, so party finance gives the 2025 figures filed with the party-finance authority. No programme on the party's own site was found for Chega, the PS or Livre. No independent promise tracker exists; the government's own anti-corruption count is shown as not independent. The homicide rate is calculated from the official 2025 count and the resident population. The satisfaction gauge is the latest poll from a validated source (July 2026).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the Lusa agency, RTP, Renascença, the EFCSN fact-checker Polígrafo, and the brands trusted by 70% or more in the Digital News Report Portugal 2026, plus the regional press. Claims found only in other outlets were rewritten from validated sources or dropped, among them the vote count of 3 July 2026 on the nationality decree. Cases without an official act, private disputes, former members and dismissals older than 12 months are left out.
