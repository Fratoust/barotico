  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

Who governs in Portugal?

Who's at the wheel

XXV Constitutional Government (PSD/CDS-PP, "AD"), Prime Minister Luís Montenegro, sworn in on 5 June 2025. President: António José Seguro, elected on 8 February 2026 with 66.8% and in office since 9 March 2026.

Minority government. The first Montenegro cabinet fell on 11 March 2025 after losing a confidence vote linked to his family company Spinumviva; the snap election gave the AD 91 seats, without a majority. Its budgets have passed thanks to PS abstention, including the 2027 budget at first reading on 28 October 2026 (final vote scheduled for 24 November), while its labour reform was rejected by Chega, the PS and the left on 19 June 2026.

Sources: Government of Portugal: XXV Government sworn in (5 June 2025) · RTP: Constitutional Court's official result, Seguro 66.84% · Government of Portugal: the Prime Minister's message as Seguro takes office (March 2026) · Renascença: Seguro's inauguration speech, 9 March 2026 · Government of Portugal: 2027 budget approved in principle, final vote scheduled for 24 November 2026 · Notícias ao Minuto: confidence vote lost, Spinumviva (11 March 2025) · Renascença: final 2025 legislative results · DNoticias/Lusa: 2027 budget passed at first reading, PS abstaining · Renascença: labour reform rejected (19 June 2026)

Who holds power

President: António José Seguro, elected on 8 February 2026 with 66.8% and in office since 9 March 2026. Prime Minister: Luís Montenegro (PSD), heading the XXV Constitutional Government sworn in on 5 June 2025.

In government: 91 absolute majority: 116

Minority government of PSD and CDS-PP, which ran together as the Aliança Democrática (AD): 91 of 230 seats, 25 short of a majority. There is no written support agreement with any other party; bills pass with votes found case by case. The PS abstained on the 2026 budget and on the 2027 budget at first reading (28 October 2026), with PAN and JPP also abstaining on the latter; the final vote on the 2027 budget is scheduled for 24 November 2026.

Seats by group are the Lusa count published at the start of the legislature (June 2025), which matches the final 2025 results; the parliament's own composition page could not be opened for this update. No change of group was found up to 15 November 2026: a Chega MP who asked on 8 July 2026 to sit as non-attached withdrew the request three hours later and stayed in the group.

Groups are ordered by the left-right positions used on the party cards. BE, PAN and JPP have no card; their place in the chart is a judgement. No breakdown of ministers by party was checked, so no cabinet chart is shown.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has cards for the seven parties with a parliamentary group after the election of 18 May 2025. PSD and CDS-PP ran together as AD but sit as two groups, so each has its own card. BE, PAN and JPP (one seat each, under 3% of the vote) have no card and appear only in the seat chart. Seats by group are the Lusa count at the start of the legislature; the parliament's own page could not be opened for this update, and no change of group was found up to 15 November 2026.

Left-right positions are an estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey scale, not re-checked against the dataset; Chega and the PCP are labelled far right and far left under that rule. BE, PAN and JPP are placed in the seat chart by judgement. The election date is an estimate: the legislature ends in autumn 2029 unless parliament is dissolved.

No party publishes a percentage breakdown of its income, so party finance gives the 2025 figures filed with the party-finance authority. No programme on the party's own site was found for Chega, the PS or Livre. No independent promise tracker exists; the government's own anti-corruption count is shown as not independent. The homicide rate is calculated from the official 2025 count and the resident population. The satisfaction gauge is the latest poll from a validated source (July 2026).

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel: the Lusa agency, RTP, Renascença, the EFCSN fact-checker Polígrafo, and the brands trusted by 70% or more in the Digital News Report Portugal 2026, plus the regional press. Claims found only in other outlets were rewritten from validated sources or dropped, among them the vote count of 3 July 2026 on the nationality decree. Cases without an official act, private disputes, former members and dismissals older than 12 months are left out.
