Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who governs in Portugal?

Who's at the wheel

XXV Constitutional Government (PSD/CDS-PP, "AD"), Prime Minister Luís Montenegro, sworn in on 5 June 2025. President: António José Seguro, elected on 8 February 2026 with 66.8%.

Minority government. Montenegro's first cabinet fell on a lost confidence vote on 11 March 2025 over his family firm Spinumviva; the snap election gave AD more seats but no majority. Its budgets pass thanks to PS abstentions (2026 and 2027), while its labour reform was voted down by PS, Chega and the left on 19 June 2026.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Public finances (Conselho das Finanças Públicas): budget surplus of 0.7% of GDP in 2025, above all official forecasts; under unchanged policies the council projects deficits from 2027 (0.4% of GDP). source
  • Jobs and growth (INE): unemployment 6.0% in 2025, the lowest annual rate since 2011; GDP growth 1.9% (2.1% in 2024). source
  • Housing (INE): house prices rose 17.6% in 2025, the highest rate in the series; patients without a family doctor rose to 1.56 million at the end of 2025. source
