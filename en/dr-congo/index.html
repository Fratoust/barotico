Country profile · 31 Oct 2026

DR Congo

Semi-presidential republic · 26 provinces · war in the east, constitutional referendum law in the works

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Next vote771 daysCombined general elections set for 16 December 2028 (presidential, legislative, provincial and local)
The electoral commission (CENI) published its 2025-2029 roadmap on 4 April 2025 with combined polls on 16 December 2028: electoral mapping in 2025, update of the voter file in 2026, revision of the rolls in 2027. In an Ebuteli/GeoPoll survey of April 2026, 45% of respondents doubted the elections would be held on time. Parliament is meanwhile finalising a law organising referendums, which the president has linked to a possible reform of the Constitution. source

On 2 August 2025 President Tshisekedi confirmed a reshuffle with fewer than 50 members; the Suminwa II government named on 8 August counts 6 deputy prime ministers, 12 ministers of state, 24 ministers, 5 delegate ministers and 6 vice-ministers. A year later he cut Council of Ministers meetings from four to two a month for efficiency; a civil-society monitoring report put the execution rate of its April-December 2025 decisions at 25%.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Register with the CENI during an enrolment period to receive a voter card, which you bring on polling day. The CENI plans to update the voter file in 2026 and revise the rolls in 2027. In 2023 Congolese abroad could vote in the presidential election in a limited number of countries.

Last turnout: 43% in the combined elections of 20 December 2023 source

Who decides what

President of the RepublicElected by direct vote for five years. He appoints the prime minister from the parliamentary majority, chairs the Council of Ministers, commands the armed forces and promulgates laws or asks Parliament for a new deliberation (as he did with the referendum law in August 2026). Félix Tshisekedi (UDPS), in office since January 2019, was re-elected in December 2023 with 73.47% of the vote.
Prime minister and governmentThe government is accountable to the National Assembly, which can censure it or individual ministers (a no-confidence motion against the Interior minister was rejected in April 2026). Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka leads the 54-member Suminwa II government formed on 8 August 2025.
ParliamentNational Assembly of 500 seats (477 deputies proclaimed in January 2024, the vote having not taken place everywhere) and a Senate elected by the provincial assemblies, where former presidents sit for life. The Union sacrée of President Tshisekedi holds a very large majority in both: the referendum law passed the Assembly by 346 votes to 0 in October 2026. Speakers: Aimé Boji (Assembly, since November 2025, after Vital Kamerhe resigned) and Jean-Michel Sama Lukonde (Senate, since August 2024).
CourtsThe Constitutional Court settles election disputes and tries the president and prime minister (it convicted former PM Matata Ponyo in May 2025); the Court of Cassation tries MPs and ministers (former Justice minister Constant Mutamba, 2025 and 2026); the High Military Court sentenced former president Joseph Kabila to death in absentia in September 2025. Freedom House scores judicial independence 1 out of 4.
Provinces26 provinces, each with an elected provincial assembly that elects the governor and the senators. In parts of North and South Kivu, including Goma (since January 2025), Bukavu (February 2025) and Uvira (December 2025), the AFC/M23 rebels run their own administration.

How people vote

  • Elections are combined: on 20 December 2023 voters chose the president, national and provincial deputies and municipal councillors on the same day; turnout was 43%.
  • The president is elected in a single round: the candidate with most votes wins. In 2023 Tshisekedi took 73.47%, Moïse Katumbi 18.08% and Martin Fayulu 4.92%.
  • National deputies are elected by list proportional representation in constituencies, with a national representativity threshold for lists. Candidates run mostly through 'regroupements' (electoral alliances of parties): in 2023 the largest, UDPS/Tshisekedi, won 69 seats.

How a law is passed

  1. Bills come from the government; deputies and senators can table their own proposals (the 2026 referendum law was a deputies' proposal).
  2. The National Assembly and the Senate each examine and adopt the text; the referendum law was passed by the Assembly on 28 May 2026 and adopted definitively by Parliament on 16 June 2026.
  3. The Constitutional Court can review it: in July 2026 it declared the referendum law consistent with the Constitution, with reservations on some provisions.
  4. The president promulgates the law or asks for a new deliberation: in August 2026 he sent the referendum law back, and the Assembly re-adopted it on 23 October after rewording 13 articles; the Senate must now vote again.

Why it's complicated

Three crises overlap. In the east, the AFC/M23 rebellion, which Kinshasa accuses Rwanda of backing, took Goma and Bukavu in early 2025 together with Rwandan forces (Congo Research Group) and Uvira in December 2025, and holds more than 10,000 km²; the Washington agreement with Rwanda (27 June 2025) was 35% implemented in July 2026 and the Doha process with the rebels 18.6%, according to an independent barometer. On 12 October 2026 former president Joseph Kabila was announced at the head of a new politico-military movement in Goma, the FRN. In Kinshasa, the majority is preparing a referendum law that the president links to constitutional reform, while the opposition fears a third term. And the courts are hitting political figures: Kabila, whose PPRD boycotted the 2023 elections, was sentenced to death in absentia in September 2025 for treason and war crimes, a trial Human Rights Watch says 'had all the trappings of a political vendetta', held without defence counsel; former PM Augustin Matata Ponyo (LGD) got ten years of forced labour in May 2025 over the Bukanga-Lonzo project and calls it a political manoeuvre; former Justice minister Constant Mutamba was convicted of embezzlement in 2025 and again in September 2026, and calls the second verdict 'purely political'. On 15 October 2026 the Interior minister banned the activities of the PPRD, the LGD and 11 other parties nationwide, without stating reasons. Freedom House rates the DRC Not Free (18/100). This page shows six blocs of the Assembly (the four largest, the MLC and Ensemble) plus Fayulu's ECiDé (4.92% in the presidential vote); the majority's electoral regroupements AAAP, 2A/TDC and A/B50 (21 seats each) have no card, and the PPRD and LGD do not meet the criteria (no seats, 1 seat).

Why it's like thisThe 2006 Constitution was written after the wars of 1996-2003, which drew in several neighbouring armies, and after a negotiated transition. It created a strong presidency limited to two five-year terms, a parliament elected by proportional representation, and provinces with their own assemblies. Joseph Kabila, president since 2001, stayed on after his second term expired in 2016 until elections were held in December 2018; Félix Tshisekedi's inauguration in January 2019 was the country's first peaceful transfer of power. A 2011 revision reduced the presidential election to a single round. The current fight over 'revising or changing' the Constitution is the first since the 2015-2018 protests against Kabila's extension.

Who's at the wheel

President Félix Tshisekedi (UDPS), re-elected in December 2023. Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka at the head of the Suminwa II government (54 members, 8 August 2025), with deputy prime ministers including Jacquemain Shabani (Interior), Jean-Pierre Bemba (Transport, MLC) and Adolphe Muzito (Budget, a former Lamuka leader); Doudou Fwamba Likunde at Finance; Guillaume Ngefa-Atondoko at Justice since Constant Mutamba left.

The Union sacrée de la Nation, the platform built by Tshisekedi in 2020-2021, groups almost all the regroupements elected in 2023 and holds more than 400 of the 477 seats proclaimed. The president reappointed Suminwa in August 2025 with a team opened to a few opposition figures (Muzito, Floribert Anzuluni); Fayulu's camp refused. A no-confidence motion against the Interior minister, signed mostly by majority deputies, was rejected on 29 April 2026.

What the government promised

  • Free basic education (since 2019): at the UN in September 2026 the government cited more than 5 million children benefiting
  • Local development programme for the 145 territories (PDL-145T): 1,198 schools, 788 health centres, 145 administrative buildings in its first component; cost raised from 1.66 to 2.138 billion USD
  • Constitutional reform: in May 2026 the president said a revision is needed for 'certain justice reforms' required by the partnership with the United States, and would only happen by referendum

What independent promise trackers found

  • Civil-society monitoring report (published 2 June 2026, with CEPAS support): Council of Ministers decisions from April to December 2025 executed at 25%; overall execution index 47/100. source
  • Baromètre des Accords de Paix en Afrique (independent experts): Washington agreement 35% implemented in July 2026 (DRC 31.7%, Rwanda 30.6%); Doha process 18.6% after 13 months. source
  • Not independent: the prime minister reported in October 2026 that 1,098 PDL-145T projects were completed, including 649 schools and 371 health centres, out of 2,131 planned structures. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • World Bank (economic update presented in March 2026): inflation 11.7% in December 2024 and 7.8% in September 2025 (23.8% in 2023); the Congolese franc gained 30.4% year on year in December 2025. source
  • Banque centrale du Congo: 17.4% of 2023 public spending (3,976.65 billion CDF) went through the emergency procedure, against a 10% target. source
  • World Bank (2021): after primary school fees were abolished in 2019, at least 3 million additional children enrolled in primary school. source
