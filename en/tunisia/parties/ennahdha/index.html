Tunisia

Presidential republic under the 2022 Constitution · about 12 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 13 monthsNext national vote: legislative elections, expected in November or December 2027 (no official date yet)
On 23 October 2026 the spokesman of the electoral authority (ISIE), Mohamed Tlili Mnasri, told the TAP news agency that the legislative elections should take place in November or December 2027, with no precise date announced. He added that municipal elections could still be held before March 2027, but that this depends on a political decision. The 161 deputies are elected one by one in single-member constituencies, without party lists. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

ConsistentOn 30 March 2022 Ennahdha deputies took part in an online plenary of the suspended Assembly in which 116 deputies voted to annul the exceptional measures and decrees issued since 25 July 2021. The President dissolved the Assembly by decree that evening, saying the session had no legal value; Rached Ghannouchi called the dissolution 'null and void'. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • Its leader had said he would not work with a party suspected of corruption; in November 2019 he was elected Speaker after a deal that brought in Qalb Tounes, saying 'only fools never change their minds' and that a party elected by the people cannot be excluded. source
  • Says it respects campaign rules; in November 2020 the Court of Accounts found it had paid $187,215 under a contract with a foreign entity in force since the 2014 elections, while foreign financing of campaigns is banned. source
  • Defends the 2014 Constitution; as the first or second force in parliament from 2014 to 2021, it was part of assemblies that left the Constitutional Court unformed 1,698 days past the deadline, with one member elected. source
To be fair
  • Left government in January 2014 under the National Dialogue, handing over to a cabinet of independents
  • Voted the 2014 Constitution, after which Freedom House rated Tunisia 'Free' (78/100 in 2017)
  • Separated religious preaching from political activity at its 2016 congress

  • Broken No work with a party suspected of corruption (2019 campaign): its leader was elected Speaker weeks later after a deal with Qalb Tounes source
  • Partly kept Set up the Constitutional Court of the 2014 Constitution it voted for: one member elected, 1,698 days past the deadline (needed a two-thirds majority it never had alone) source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Ennahdha, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In 2019 its leader said he would not work with a party suspected of corruption. Weeks later he became Speaker thanks to a deal with Qalb Tounes: 'only fools never change their minds'.

In 2020 the Court of Accounts found it had paid $187,215 to a foreign firm under a contract dating from the 2014 elections.

It now demands back a Constitution whose Constitutional Court its parliaments left with one member out of four.

To be fair, it stepped down from government under the 2014 National Dialogue, and Freedom House then rated Tunisia 'Free' (78/100 in 2017).
