Tunisia

Presidential republic under the 2022 Constitution · about 12 million people

Before you read

  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 13 monthsNext national vote: legislative elections, expected in November or December 2027 (no official date yet)
On 23 October 2026 the spokesman of the electoral authority (ISIE), Mohamed Tlili Mnasri, told the TAP news agency that the legislative elections should take place in November or December 2027, with no precise date announced. He added that municipal elections could still be held before March 2027, but that this depends on a political decision. The 161 deputies are elected one by one in single-member constituencies, without party lists. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. The number of cases partly reflects a party's size and years in power.

Programme links are left empty: since 2022 deputies run as individuals and we could not verify a current national programme on any party's own site. Stated values come from founding statements, congresses and positions reported by the Tunisian press.

Also worth knowing
  • Puts the fight against corruption first; in June 2020 it kept backing Prime Minister Elyes Fakhfakh over suspected conflicts of interest, its minister Mohamed Abbou saying they were only suspicions and that he would ask for his resignation if they were confirmed by his own ministry's inquiry. source
  • Defends independent institutions; in February 2020 it asked for the Interior or Justice ministry and opposed keeping the sovereign ministries neutral. source
  • Disputed the President's reading of the emergency article on 26 July 2021, then said three days later that it 'understands' the exceptional measures, before moving into opposition. source
To be fair
  • Ran a ministry for public service and anti-corruption in the 2020 government (Mohamed Abbou)
  • Asked the President in July 2021 for a roadmap back to constitutional normality
  • Its candidate Ghazi Chaouachi ran against Rached Ghannouchi for Speaker in 2019 (45 votes)

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept Fight corruption from government (2020): it held the anti-corruption ministry for about five months before the government fell over the Prime Minister's conflict-of-interest case source
  • Partly kept Complete the Constitutional Court with its 22 deputies (2019-2021): still one member elected in July 2020 source
  • Never tested A roadmap back to constitutional normality (since 2021, no power to act)

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Courant démocrate, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

An anti-corruption party, in 2020 it left the inquiry into its Prime Minister's suspected conflicts of interest to its own minister, who promised results within three weeks.

It demanded the Interior or Justice ministry while praising independent institutions.

It needed three days to go from disputing 25 July to 'understanding' it.

To be fair, its candidate stood against Ghannouchi for Speaker in 2019 when the alliance that elected him was being sealed.
