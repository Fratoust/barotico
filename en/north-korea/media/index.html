North Korea

One-party state led by the Workers' Party of Korea · 687-seat assembly elected unopposed in March 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
no free national vote≈ 1 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national step is the Workers' Party Central Committee's year-end plenary meeting, expected in late December 2026 as in 2024 and 2025
No national office is filled by a competitive vote: Freedom House gives North Korea 0 out of 40 for political rights. Power is decided inside the Workers' Party: its 9th Congress (19-25 February 2026) re-elected Kim Jong Un General Secretary, and the Supreme People's Assembly, elected on 15 March 2026 with one pre-approved candidate per seat, re-elected him President of State Affairs on 22-23 March. Between congresses the Central Committee meets in plenary sessions; the 9th Congress itself was announced at the December 2024 plenum and prepared at the December 2025 one. No date has been announced for the next plenum, so the countdown uses late December 2026 as an estimate. The next Party Congress is due around 2031; the assembly's term was five years, and analysts believe the 2026 revision may have lengthened it. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in North Korea

The Reuters Institute Digital News Report does not cover North Korea, and no survey of trust in news can be carried out there. RSF ranks the country 179th of 180 in 2026 (12.67): KCNA is the only permitted news source for domestic media, the constitution's press freedom is nominal, and citizens can be sent to a camp for viewing foreign online media. For facts about the country, this page relies on UN bodies, the Bank of Korea and recognised institutes; official media are used only for what the authorities announced. source

Who owns what

  • State and Party only: the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA), the only authorised source for domestic media; Rodong Sinmun, organ of the Party's Central Committee, whose editor-in-chief is a full Central Committee member (State Media Monitor; RSF)
  • No private or independent media: Freedom House reports that the state runs all media outlets
  • Foreign agencies such as AFP and Kyodo have an official presence but work under close surveillance (RSF)

source

Documented facts to know

  • RSF 2026: 179th of 180 (12.67), ahead only of Eritrea; journalists have been arrested, deported, sent to labour camps or killed for departing from the Party line. source
  • UN Human Rights Office (September 2025): credible evidence that people were executed for distributing foreign information and media, based on interviews with 314 people who left the country. source
  • Human Rights Watch: two people in Haeju were reportedly arrested in March 2025 for watching South Korean television and face up to five years of forced labour; the authorities jammed Chinese mobile signals in 2025. source
  • Freedom House: smartphone users can reach a state-run intranet but not the global internet, and Stimson Center research points to wider digital monitoring of citizens. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

North Korea is a non-free country, and much of what this page shows elsewhere does not exist here. There is no human development index or homicide figure (UNDP calculates none, and no homicide statistics are published), no population figure from a source we could verify, no opinion poll of any kind, no party accounts, no seat or vote count by party, no Workers' Party programme text (the full 2026 congress report was not published) and no independent promise tracker.

Facts about the country come from UN bodies, the Bank of Korea's estimates and recognised research institutes. Official media (KCNA) are cited only for what the authorities themselves announced, such as election returns, which cannot be checked. Press sources are limited to the most trusted South Korean broadcasters in the Reuters Institute's 2026 survey, since no free domestic press exists.

The two satellite parties, which elect deputies but do not compete with the Workers' Party, share one card. No party is placed on the left-right axis, as in other one-party systems. Rights alerts use only findings by UN treaty bodies: the 2014 Commission of Inquiry and the UN Human Rights Office's 2025 report are cited in the facts and context, not as rights alerts, because neither is a treaty body. The judicial records are empty: no domestic court decision is published, and foreign sanctions designations are not used.
