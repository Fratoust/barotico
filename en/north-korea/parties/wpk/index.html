North Korea

One-party state led by the Workers' Party of Korea · 687-seat assembly elected unopposed in March 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
no free national vote≈ 1 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national step is the Workers' Party Central Committee's year-end plenary meeting, expected in late December 2026 as in 2024 and 2025
No national office is filled by a competitive vote: Freedom House gives North Korea 0 out of 40 for political rights. Power is decided inside the Workers' Party: its 9th Congress (19-25 February 2026) re-elected Kim Jong Un General Secretary, and the Supreme People's Assembly, elected on 15 March 2026 with one pre-approved candidate per seat, re-elected him President of State Affairs on 22-23 March. Between congresses the Central Committee meets in plenary sessions; the 9th Congress itself was announced at the December 2024 plenum and prepared at the December 2025 one. No date has been announced for the next plenum, so the countdown uses late December 2026 as an estimate. The next Party Congress is due around 2031; the assembly's term was five years, and analysts believe the 2026 revision may have lengthened it. source

Reading codes

Not placed on the left-right axisIn a one-party system with no competitive election, expert surveys do not place parties on a left-right scale. The Party's official ideas ('Kimilsungism-Kimjongilism', self-reliance, nuclear status) do not map onto the left-right debates of competitive democracies, so we give it no position and no 'far left' or 'far right' label.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterSecond five-year plan (2021-2025) 'basically completed' (9th Congress, February 2026)
Half-wayThe congress gave no figures except for housing, and the plan's '12 major targets' were never published (Asan Institute). The Bank of Korea estimates growth of 3.1% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025; the Asan Institute notes this does not offset the losses of 2021-2022, while KEI notes that output in 2025 passed its 2017 level. source · 2 · 3
On the posterModern factories in 20 cities and counties a year for ten years ('regional development 20×10', launched in 2024)
Half-wayThe policy has been folded into the 2026-2030 plan (KEI), and the 2026 congress widened it to hospitals and service centres (Asan Institute). The Bank of Korea estimates that light and heavy industry both grew in 2025 (manufacturing +6.6%), but no independent figure exists on the output of the new regional factories. source · 2 · 3
On the posterImproving living standards, 'heavy and urgent historic tasks' (9th Congress, 2026)
Half-wayBetween May 2024 and May 2026 rice prices rose 5.2 times and the won lost about three-quarters of its value against the dollar (KEI). Human Rights Watch cites reports of farmers going without food in Kumya county in May 2025. The Bank of Korea puts income per person at about 1.873 million won in 2025. source · 2 · 3
On the posterA 'people-centred socialist system' in which persons with disabilities are 'rights holders', not mere recipients of care (government delegation to the UN, August 2025)
⚠ Rights alertOn 19 August 2025 the UN Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, the treaty body of a convention North Korea has ratified, reported the arbitrary detention of persons with disabilities, including in 'psychiatric recovery centres', the placement of persons of short stature in isolated settlements, and forced abortion and sterilisation of women with disabilities: breaches of the rights to liberty, to physical integrity and to live in the community. The government contests this: its delegation said forced sterilisation and abortion are 'strictly prohibited' and accused Western countries of 'smear campaigns and fabrications'. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • Article 67 of the constitution guarantees freedom of the press; RSF ranks the country 179th of 180, and in September 2025 the UN Human Rights Office reported credible evidence of executions for distributing foreign media, under one of six laws adopted since 2015 that allow the death penalty. source
  • For decades the constitution's preamble honoured Kim Il Sung's principle of independent, peaceful unification. In January 2024 Kim Jong Un called the South the 'invariable principal enemy'; in October 2024 roads and railways to the South were destroyed; the March 2026 constitution dropped unification and now describes the territory as bordering 'the Republic of Korea' to the south. source
  • The assembly elects the President of State Affairs; its 2026 election came two years late, with one pre-approved candidate per seat (International IDEA), and the constitution it then adopted says it can no longer remove him. source
To be fair
  • Output grew by 3.1% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025 (Bank of Korea estimates), and in 2025 passed its 2017 level
  • New laws on fair-trial guarantees, two more human rights treaties ratified, and better treatment reported in some detention facilities (UN Human Rights Office, 2025)
  • A limited reopening in 2025: foreign tourists in the Rason zone (February), the first Pyongyang marathon in six years (April), and UN sanctions exemptions for aid (January), though UN humanitarian staff had not been allowed back by October (Human Rights Watch)
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • No case targeting the party identified to date.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept Complete the second five-year plan (2021-2025): declared 'basically completed', but only housing figures were published; the Bank of Korea estimates growth of 3.1%-3.7% a year in 2023-2025 after the Covid years source
  • Kept on paper Modern factories in 20 cities and counties a year ('20×10', 2024): adopted and folded into the 2026-2030 plan; no independent measure of what the new factories produce source
  • Never tested Raise industrial output 1.5 times under the 2026-2030 plan: too early to judge source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're wondering what this party has delivered, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

At its February 2026 congress it declared the 2021-2025 five-year plan 'basically completed'. Apart from housing, it published no figures.

The same congress called better living standards a 'heavy and urgent' task. Over the two years to May 2026, rice prices rose 5.2 times.

In August 2025 its government told a UN treaty body that persons with disabilities are 'rights holders'. The committee then reported forced sterilisation of women with disabilities and detention in 'psychiatric recovery centres'; the delegation said such practices are 'strictly prohibited'.

To be fair, the Bank of Korea estimates that the economy grew by more than 3% a year from 2023 to 2025, and in 2025 passed its 2017 level.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

North Korea is a non-free country, and much of what this page shows elsewhere does not exist here. There is no human development index or homicide figure (UNDP calculates none, and no homicide statistics are published), no population figure from a source we could verify, no opinion poll of any kind, no party accounts, no seat or vote count by party, no Workers' Party programme text (the full 2026 congress report was not published) and no independent promise tracker.

Facts about the country come from UN bodies, the Bank of Korea's estimates and recognised research institutes. Official media (KCNA) are cited only for what the authorities themselves announced, such as election returns, which cannot be checked. Press sources are limited to the most trusted South Korean broadcasters in the Reuters Institute's 2026 survey, since no free domestic press exists.

The two satellite parties, which elect deputies but do not compete with the Workers' Party, share one card. No party is placed on the left-right axis, as in other one-party systems. Rights alerts use only findings by UN treaty bodies: the 2014 Commission of Inquiry and the UN Human Rights Office's 2025 report are cited in the facts and context, not as rights alerts, because neither is a treaty body. The judicial records are empty: no domestic court decision is published, and foreign sanctions designations are not used.
