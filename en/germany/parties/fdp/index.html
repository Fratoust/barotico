Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext federal election (Bundestag)
Under Article 39 of the Basic Law the election must take place 46 to 48 months after the current Bundestag first met (25 March 2025): between 26 January and 25 March 2029, on a Sunday or public holiday. The Federal President sets the date. An early election needs a lost confidence vote and a dissolution, as in December 2024. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

⚠ Rights alertBill of the SPD, Green and FDP groups, passed 399 to 261 on 17 March 2023: seats capped at 630 and the rule letting a party in with three constituency wins dropped. On 30 July 2024 the Constitutional Court upheld the cap but ruled, 7 to 1, that the 5% threshold without that rule breached equal voting rights and parties' equal opportunities (Articles 38 and 21 of the Basic Law); the old rule applied again in 2025. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • While FDP leader Christian Lindner was finance minister, the government shifted €60 billion of unused Covid borrowing to the climate fund, although the Federal Court of Audit had told parliament it saw constitutional concerns; the Constitutional Court declared it void in November 2023. source
  • In November 2024 party officials called reports of a planned coalition exit 'a flat lie'; the FDP then published its own 'D-Day' paper scripting that exit, and its general secretary resigned. source
  • The 2021 coalition it signed promised a 'Klimageld' to give carbon-price revenue back to citizens; in 2024 finance minister Lindner said whether to pay it would be decided only after the next election. source
To be fair
  • Its justice minister Marco Buschmann had the ban on doctors informing about abortion (§219a) repealed in 2022, with past convictions annulled.
  • Its MPs, with CDU/CSU MPs, brought the case in which the Constitutional Court struck down Berlin's rent cap in 2021.
  • Stayed consistent on the debt brake: it voted against the March 2025 constitutional change allowing extra borrowing.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept Keep the debt brake: as finance minister Lindner kept it, but his €60 billion transfer to the climate fund was voided in 2023; in 2025 it made its Rhineland-Palatinate government abstain in the Bundesrat, and the brake was loosened anyway source
  • Partly kept Less bureaucracy: a fourth bureaucracy-relief law passed in 2024, but the Normenkontrollrat put the follow-up costs of new federal laws at a record €23.7 billion in 2023, €9.3 billion more than a year earlier source
  • Broken Give carbon-price revenue back to citizens ('Klimageld', 2021 coalition agreement it signed): its finance minister deferred payment to after the next election; nothing was paid source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for FDP, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It is the party of the debt brake. The €60 billion budget shift made by its finance minister Christian Lindner was declared void in Karlsruhe in 2023.

It promises fair play. Its own 'D-Day' paper scripted the coalition exit that its officials had called 'a flat lie'.

On the promise-o-meter: climate money never returned to citizens, and bureaucracy costs at a record in 2023.

To be fair, its inflation-adjustment law left about €14 billion with taxpayers in 2023 (IW), even if IW notes it only caught up with 2022's bracket creep.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

This page has seven party cards: the five groups in the Bundestag plus the BSW and the FDP, which won more than 3% in February 2025 but no seats. The SSW, a minority party with one seat, has no card. Seats are the official 2025 result; the seat chart in 'Who holds power' is derived from it, minus two MPs whom the Bundestag lists as non-attached, because the Bundestag's seat page could not be opened.

Left-right positions are our estimate of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey 2024, which could not be opened. The AfD is labelled far right by position; Die Linke is labelled far left by an editorial choice following The PopuList. The homicide rate is calculated from Eurostat's count and Destatis's population; the corruption score comes via ZDF.

Party finance shows only total income and the share of state funds: the Bundestag summaries give no further breakdown. Programme links are missing for the five parties outside government. No independent promise tracker was found; ZDF's one-year review is used instead.

Press links come only from the validated list in the media panel (dpa and epd, CORRECTIV, ARD and ZDF, regional dailies), chosen on the 2025 Reuters Institute trust figures. Legal-news sites and other outlets were replaced by court, parliament or government sources, or dropped: one 2026 acquittal and a former MP's first-instance conviction left the page for that reason. Cases without an official act and private-life matters, including the stated reason for a 2026 resignation, are left out.
