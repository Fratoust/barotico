Germany

Federal parliamentary republic · 83.5 million people · 16 Länder

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext federal election (Bundestag)
Under Article 39 of the Basic Law the election must take place 46 to 48 months after the current Bundestag first met (25 March 2025): between 26 January and 25 March 2029, on a Sunday or public holiday. The Federal President sets the date. An early election needs a lost confidence vote and a dissolution, as in December 2024. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Leaning right

On the posterOn the record
On the posterNo loosening of the debt brake
ContradictionIn January 2024 Friedrich Merz ruled out his group backing any loosening; in November 2024 he said it could be reformed for investment, not for consumption. On 18 March 2025, three weeks after the election, the outgoing Bundestag voted 512 to 206, with CDU/CSU votes, to let defence above 1% of GDP be borrowed and to create a €500 billion investment fund on credit. Merz cited the threats to freedom and peace in Europe: 'whatever it takes'. In March 2026 the president of the Federal Court of Audit said institutes found that 80 to 90% of the fund's first-year credit money had plugged budget gaps. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterMajorities only in the political centre
ContradictionOn 13 November 2024 Merz proposed that only motions agreed with SPD and Greens be put to a vote, to avoid 'chance majorities with the AfD'. On 29 January 2025 his group's migration motion passed 348 to 344, with 75 AfD votes making the majority. Merz said it was 'about the substance' and denied breaking his word; former chancellor Angela Merkel (CDU) said it was wrong no longer to feel bound by the November proposal. The motion was not binding, and the bill that followed failed two days later. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterLimit irregular migration
Half-wayFirst asylum applications halved, from 229,751 in 2024 to 113,236 in 2025 (interior ministry). Interior minister Alexander Dobrindt (CSU) credits turn-backs at the border and the suspension of family reunification; experts also cite the border controls in place since October 2023 under his SPD predecessor and fewer Syrians after Assad's fall. A Bundestag document counts 993 asylum seekers turned back at the border in 2025. source · 2
On the posterMake foreign drivers pay for German roads (CSU car toll)
⚠ Rights alertIts grand coalition with the SPD passed the toll, offset for German owners by a car-tax cut; the Bundestag adopted its amended version 397 to 135 on 24 March 2017. On 18 June 2019 the EU Court of Justice (C-591/17) ruled that it discriminated indirectly on grounds of nationality, since the burden fell almost only on drivers from other EU states, and breached the free movement of goods and services. CSU transport minister Andreas Scheuer accepted that the toll was 'off the table in this form'. After he cancelled the operators' contracts, the federal government agreed in 2023 to pay them €243 million. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterFewer incentives for irregular migration (2019 asylum benefits reform)
⚠ Rights alertIts grand coalition with the SPD passed the reform on 7 June 2019 (358 to 172); from September 2019 single adults in shared accommodation got 10% less, on the assumption that they share costs like couples. On 19 October 2022 the Constitutional Court (1 BvL 3/21) found that this breached the fundamental right to a minimum subsistence consistent with human dignity; the government had argued that residents could cook together. source · 2 · 3
On the posterClimate protection through its 2019 Climate Act
⚠ Rights alertIts grand coalition with the SPD passed the Climate Protection Act in December 2019. In a decision published on 29 April 2021 the Constitutional Court ruled that its targets and emission budgets to 2030 breached the complainants' fundamental freedoms, because they pushed the heavy cuts after 2030 into the future with no path. The SPD said CDU economy minister Peter Altmaier had blocked post-2030 targets in 2019, which he denied. On 12 May 2021 the government raised the targets: 65% less greenhouse gas by 2030 and neutrality by 2045. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • The CSU's car toll, run by its transport minister Andreas Scheuer, was stopped by the EU Court of Justice in 2019; after the contracts were cancelled, the federal government agreed in arbitration to pay €243 million. source
  • During the 2025 campaign Merz argued against loosening the debt brake; three weeks after the election the outgoing Bundestag changed the Basic Law with CDU/CSU votes to exempt defence above 1% of GDP and create a €500 billion investment fund on credit. Merz cited the threats to freedom and peace in Europe: 'whatever it takes'. source
  • On 13 November 2024 Merz proposed avoiding 'chance majorities with the AfD'; on 29 January 2025 his group's migration motion passed 348 to 344, with 75 AfD votes making the majority. He said it was 'about the substance'. source
To be fair
  • Its coalition cut electricity tax, abolished the gas-storage levy and fixed the pension level at 48% until 2031.
  • The public deficit fell to 2.4% of GDP in 2025, below the EU's 3% limit.
  • Its coalition passed the federal Tariftreuegesetz in February 2026: federal contracts only for firms with collectively agreed or equivalent working conditions.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept Cut electricity tax for everyone to the EU minimum (coalition agreement 2025): cut in 2026 for industry and farming only; in October 2026 the SPD finance minister still saw no room to extend it to households source
  • Kept on paper Food in restaurants back to 7% VAT, in force since January 2026: in the first month restaurant prices still rose (+3.9% in Bavaria, +3.8% in Brandenburg on a year earlier, state statistics offices) source
  • Partly kept Turn asylum seekers back at the borders: 993 people turned back in 2025, while 106,298 first asylum applications were still filed by the end of November (BAMF figures) source
  • Broken No new debt, keep the debt brake (2025 campaign): three weeks after the election it voted to exempt defence and create a €500 billion fund on credit source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for CDU/CSU, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In the 2025 campaign, Friedrich Merz argued against new debt. Three weeks after the win, the outgoing Bundestag loosened the debt brake, with CDU/CSU votes.

The CSU's car toll was stopped by the EU Court of Justice. It ended with €243 million paid out by the federal government.

On the promise-o-meter: the electricity tax was cut for industry, not for households; restaurant VAT was cut while menu prices kept rising; 993 asylum seekers were turned back at the border.

To be fair, CSU-run Bavaria still has the lowest unemployment of all Länder: 4.2% in September 2026 (Federal Employment Agency).
