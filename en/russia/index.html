Russia

Federal semi-presidential republic · about 146 million people (Rosstat count, which includes Crimea)

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

How to vote, in practice

Citizens from 18 vote at the polling station of their registered address, with their passport. You can ask to vote elsewhere in advance, through the Gosuslugi portal or a public service centre; online voting (DEG) also requires prior registration on Gosuslugi. Russians abroad vote at embassies and consulates.

Last turnout: 59.8% in the September 2026 State Duma election according to the election commission (67.4 million voters); independent analysts estimate real turnout at around 40% source

Who decides what

PresidentHead of state, elected for six years. Vladimir Putin (in power as President or Prime Minister since 1999) was credited with 87.3% in March 2024. The President appoints the Prime Minister with the Duma's consent, directs foreign and security policy and can rule by decree.
GovernmentLed by Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin, it runs the economy and the budget and carries out the President's 'national goals'.
Federal AssemblyThe State Duma (450 deputies, five years: 225 by party lists with a 5% threshold, 225 in single-member districts) and the Federation Council (two members per region plus members appointed by the President).
CourtsThe Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court sit at the top. After Navalny's death, the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights pointed to the 'instrumentalization of the court system for political purposes'.
Regions89 regions in Russia's own count, including Crimea and four Ukrainian regions whose annexation the Duma ratified unanimously in October 2022; each has a governor and a legislature.

How people vote

  • Voting is voluntary from 18. Ballots are cast over up to three days, at a polling station or, in many regions, by remote electronic voting (DEG); 3.3 million people used the federal DEG platform in 2026.
  • For the Duma, each voter has two ballots: a party list (5% threshold) and a candidate in a single-member district (first past the post).
  • The President is elected by direct vote, with a run-off if no one wins 50%. People designated as 'foreign agents' cannot run for office.

How a law is passed

  1. Bills come from the President, the government, deputies, the Federation Council, regional legislatures or the top courts.
  2. The Duma votes in three readings; the 2022 law on 'fakes' about the army passed its second and third readings on the same day, with no deputy speaking against it.
  3. The Federation Council approves or rejects it.
  4. The President signs it or vetoes it; a veto can be overridden by two thirds of both chambers.

Why it's complicated

Russia holds elections, but Freedom House rates it 'Not Free' (12/100): genuine opposition candidates are barred, 'foreign agents' cannot stand, and the OSCE's election office did not observe the September 2026 Duma vote, which Moscow said it would not work with. The election commission credited United Russia with about 58% of list votes and 349 of 450 seats on a 59.8% turnout. An iStories analysis estimated that 18.4 million of its roughly 30 million paper votes may have been falsified and that its real score was no more than 35%; observers' group Atlas counted some 1,500 observers removed on the first day, and experts say the 4 million online votes cannot be checked. The co-chair of the vote-monitoring movement Golos, Grigory Melkonyants, was sentenced to five years in 2025 on charges Human Rights Watch calls bogus. Alexei Navalny died in prison in February 2024; the UN human rights office said his detention appeared arbitrary, and five European governments said in 2026 he had been poisoned, which the Kremlin rejects. Vladimir Kara-Murza, sentenced to 25 years for treason in 2023 and considered a prisoner of conscience by Amnesty International, was freed in a prisoner exchange in August 2024. Since the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine, wartime laws punish 'fakes' about the army with up to 15 years; Memorial counted 482 people deprived of liberty in political cases in the first quarter of 2026 alone. Rosstat has stopped publishing many demographic data.

Why it's like thisThe 1993 Constitution was adopted by referendum after a violent standoff between President Boris Yeltsin and parliament, and gave the presidency strong powers over a weaker legislature. Vladimir Putin, appointed Prime Minister in 1999, has led the country since 2000 as President or Prime Minister. Terms were lengthened in 2008 (six years for the President, five for the Duma), and 2020 constitutional amendments, followed by a 2021 law, reset his term count so he can run again in 2024 and 2030.

Who's at the wheel

President Vladimir Putin and Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin's government, backed by United Russia, which holds 349 of 450 Duma seats since September 2026. Putin ran as an independent but the party presents his national goals as its programme.

Through elections that independent observers do not consider free: Putin was credited with 87.3% in 2024, and United Russia won 209 of 225 single-member districts in 2026 plus 140 list seats.
