Federal semi-presidential republic · about 146 million people (Rosstat count, which includes Crimea)

  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 40 monthsNext national vote: presidential election, due in March 2030. Elections are held but are not free (Freedom House: 'Not Free'; no OSCE observation in 2026)
The State Duma was elected on 18-20 September 2026; the next one is due in 2031. Vladimir Putin's current term runs to 2030, and a 2021 law resetting his term count allows him to run again in 2030 and to stay in office until 2036. The 2024 presidential vote was held in mid-March, so the countdown uses March 2030 as an estimate. The OSCE's election office (ODIHR) did not observe the 2026 vote; the Foreign Ministry said it had 'established itself as an instrument of double standards'. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. On the posterLive to 78 by 2024 (2018 national goal)
Half-wayLife expectancy reached a record 73.41 years in 2023, then fell to 72.84 in 2024 (Rosstat, preliminary). A July 2020 decree had already moved the 78-year goal to 2030, and the May 2024 decree keeps it, adding 81 by 2036. Independent demographer Alexei Raksha, quoted by The Moscow Times (in exile), blames the war and strong alcohol and says the real 2024 figure is several months lower. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterPoverty below 7% by 2030 (2024 decree)
DeliveredRosstat counted 9.8 million people, 6.7% of the population, below the poverty line in 2025 (preliminary), a record low that already meets the 2030 goal; it credits wages, business and property income and targeted support. Novaya Gazeta Europe (in exile, banned as 'undesirable' in Russia) notes that a new method has applied since 2021 and that the old one would count about three million more poor people. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRestore pension indexation for working pensioners (2024)
Half-wayFrozen since 2016, indexation resumed in January 2025 under a law passed in June 2024; Speaker Vyacheslav Volodin said it would reach 7.87 million people. In the Social Fund's example, a working pensioner's payment rose by 2,653 roubles a month. The bill, tabled by almost its entire faction, pays no compensation for the frozen years (iStories, in exile). source · 2 · 3
On the posterSovereignty: protect Russia from foreign interference
⚠ Rights alertThe Duma, where it holds a constitutional majority, extended the 'foreign agents' law to media (2017) and to individuals (2019-2020), then in 2022 to anyone 'under foreign influence', a bill led by its deputy Vasily Piskarev, who called liberalisation 'unacceptable'. On 22 October 2024 the European Court of Human Rights ruled for 107 applicants designated before September 2022 that this legislation breached freedom of expression and association and, for individuals, private life: not necessary in a democratic society, it bore 'the hallmarks of a totalitarian regime'. Russia no longer executes such rulings; in 2022 Speaker Vyacheslav Volodin called the Court 'an instrument of political struggle' against the country. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterSupport the army and the 'special military operation'
⚠ Rights alertOn 4 March 2022 the Duma passed unanimously, in second and third reading the same day, laws punishing 'fakes' about the army with up to 15 years in prison and 'discrediting' it with fines. On 11 February 2025 the European Court of Human Rights ruled in Novaya Gazeta and Others v. Russia (180 applicants) that this breached freedom of expression: courts criminalised statements contradicting the official account of the 'special military operation', with no balancing against the public interest. Russia does not execute the ruling; Speaker Vyacheslav Volodin had called the Court 'an instrument of political struggle' in 2022. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • Backed the 2018 national goal of a life expectancy of 78 by 2024; Rosstat measured 72.84 years in 2024, and the 78-year target had been moved to 2030 in 2020. source
  • Its majority backed the VAT rise from 20% to 22% from 2026, which the Finance Ministry proposed to fund 'defence and security'; the bill passed its first reading on 22 October 2025. source
  • Took credit for restoring pension indexation for working pensioners from 2025, after it was frozen in 2016; almost its entire faction then tabled a bill ruling out any compensation for the frozen years. source

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Broken Life expectancy of 78 years by 2024 (2018 national goal): 72.84 years in 2024, target moved to 2030 source
  • Kept, made a difference Poverty below 7% by 2030 (2024 decree): 6.7% in 2025 according to Rosstat's preliminary figure source · measured effect

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for United Russia, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its 2018 national goal was a life expectancy of 78 by 2024. Rosstat measured 72.84 years in 2024; a 2020 decree had already moved the target to 2030.

It puts families and births first. In December 2025 the President put the fertility rate at about 1.4, against a goal of 1.6 by 2030. Rosstat, meanwhile, no longer publishes the number of births.

It promises sovereignty and support for the army. In 2024 and 2025 the European Court of Human Rights ruled that the 'foreign agents' and wartime 'fakes' laws passed by the Duma it controls breached freedom of expression.

To be fair, Rosstat counted poverty at a record low of 6.7% in 2025, already below its 2030 goal.

No official programme found online yet.
