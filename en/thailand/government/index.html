Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in Thailand?

Who's at the wheel

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul (Bhumjaithai), in office since September 2025 and re-elected by the House on 19 March 2026 by 293 votes to 119, with 86 abstentions. Coalition of Bhumjaithai, Pheu Thai and smaller parties (about 292 seats). A cabinet of 35 was listed on 23 March 2026; Anutin is also Interior Minister, and Pheu Thai holds Labour (its leader Julapun Amornvivat), Education, Agriculture and Higher Education, with Yodchanan Wongsawat as deputy PM.

On 29 August 2025 the Constitutional Court removed Prime Minister Paetongtarn Shinawatra (Pheu Thai). On 5 September Anutin was elected with 311 votes, backed from the opposition by the People’s Party on condition of a dissolution within four months and a referendum. The House was dissolved on 12 December; Bhumjaithai came first on 8 February 2026, and Anutin was re-elected 39 days later.

Sources: Thai PBS: Paetongtarn removed (29 August 2025) · Thai PBS: Anutin elected with 311 votes (5 September 2025) · Thairath: dissolution decree (December 2025) · Thairath: Anutin re-elected (19 March 2026) · Thai PBS: the 35 ministers (23 March 2026)

Who holds power

Head of state: the King. Prime Minister: Anutin Charnvirakul (Bhumjaithai), also Minister of the Interior.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • NESDC (17 August 2026): GDP grew 1.9% year on year in Q2 2026 and shrank 0.2% on the previous quarter, as the Middle East conflict raised energy costs; private investment rose 13.4%, the fastest in 54 quarters; 2026 growth forecast 2.2%. source
  • Energy Regulatory Commission (August 2026): with the 3-baht cap on the first 200 household units from September bills, the average charged rate for all users falls from 3.95 to 3.86 baht a unit (before VAT). source
  • Transparency International, CPI 2025: Thailand scores 33, below the Asia-Pacific average of 45, with a consistent downward trend since 2012. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Seats come from the Election Commission’s certified results as published by Thai PBS. Official party-list vote totals per party were not found in a validated source, so the page uses the unofficial count at 94% of ballots. No approval rating for the government was found: NIDA Poll and Suan Dusit Poll measure preferred prime minister and expectations instead. No independent promise tracker exists.

The homicide rate is our calculation: murder cases recorded by the Royal Thai Police (October 2022 to September 2023) divided by about 66 million inhabitants. These are cases, not victims; the UN series stops in 2011. Left-right positions are editorial estimates, as no expert survey (CHES) covers Thailand; no party reaches the far-left or far-right threshold.

Cards cover parties with at least 5 seats or 3% of the list vote. Thai Ruam Phalang (6 seats), Prachachat (5) and Palang Pracharath (5) are left out for lack of validated sources. Programme links could not be verified; promises come from the costings parties filed with the Election Commission. Income breakdowns by source are not published, so party finance shows declared donations only.

Three judicial records (Kla Tham, Democrats, Setthakij) are empty: no official act was found within the scope and with a validated source, which reflects research limits, not a finding. Press sources are limited to the outlets listed in the Media tab.
