How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Spain

33% of Spaniards trust the news (+2 points in 2026). Regional and local press is the most trusted (54%). source

Recommended for factual news

EFE · EFE Verifica

State-owned news agency; its fact-checking unit is a verified EFCSN member.

Newtral

Verified EFCSN member until July 2027; a 2023 study found no significant difference in its verdicts by party, though PP statements were checked more often.

source
RTVE · Antena 3

Both at 52% trust, but polarised in opposite directions: RTVE 71% on the left vs 38% on the right; Antena 3 59% on the right and centre vs 39% on the left.

source
Regional press and regional public media

The most trusted category in Spain (54%). Used on this page: regional dailies (e.g. Diari de Tarragona, Última Hora, Diario de León, Deia, Noticias de Navarra, Ara) and regional public broadcasters and agencies (3Cat, RTVC, Catalan News).

source

Video explainers

VisualPolitik

Expert-run political explainer channel; about 2.1 million subscribers in 2021, among the most followed of its kind in a University of Valladolid study.

source
Newtral · Maldita

Fact-checkers explaining news on video. Vox called them “Gestapo” in 2020; Maldita replied that half of its checks over that period targeted the government.

source
El Orden Mundial

Independent explainer outlet funded by subscriptions.

source

Who owns what

  • PRISA: El País, Cadena SER. Planeta: Antena 3, laSexta, La Razón, Onda Cero.
  • MFE (Berlusconi family): Telecinco, Cuatro. Unidad Editorial: El Mundo. Vocento: ABC. Godó: La Vanguardia.
  • COPE: majority-owned by the Spanish Bishops' Conference. elDiario.es: owned by its journalists and staff. OKDiario and El Confidencial do not publish their beneficial owners.
  • Media Pluralism Monitor 2026: overall medium-high risk (53%).

source

Documented facts to know

  • Trust higher on the right: COPE 63% vs 28% on the left; ABC 58% vs 30%; El Mundo 56% vs 34%. Trust higher on the left: RTVE 71% vs 38%; SER 61% vs 41%; laSexta 57% vs 31%; El País 57% vs 42%. source
  • Most polarised brand (2025): OKDiario, 22% trust on the left vs 51% on the far right. source
  • RTVE (public): a 2024 reform eased the majority needed to appoint its board; the European Commission's 2026 Rule of Law report rates public media independence at medium-high risk. source
  • July 2023: the Central Electoral Board found that an RTVE report about Vox included “misleading information” and asked the broadcaster for “the utmost rigour” during the campaign. source
