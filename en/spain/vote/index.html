Voting in Spain

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Every citizen aged 18 is on the electoral roll automatically. Vote at your assigned polling station with an ID, or by post: apply at Correos within the official deadline after the election is called. Spaniards living abroad (CERA) vote by post or at their consulate.

Last turnout: 70.4% in the July 2023 general election, among voters living in Spain (Interior Ministry, via Maldita) source

The electoral system

  • Proportional lists in each province (D'Hondt method).
  • Favours large parties and regional parties concentrated in one territory.
  • The head of government must win an investiture vote in the Congress.
Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Spain, a blank vote is a valid vote: it enters the percentages and the bar that gives access to seats. But it must be cast the right way.

What it is

  • Blank vote: an empty envelope. “An envelope containing no ballot is considered a blank but valid vote” (electoral law, art. 96.5). source
  • Careful: a blank sheet of paper inside the envelope is a spoilt vote, as the Central Electoral Board recalled in 2021. source
  • Spoilt vote: for example, ballots of different lists in the same envelope, or a ballot marked or altered. source

How it is counted

  • Blank votes are counted with the valid votes. A list needs at least 3% of valid votes in its constituency to win Congress seats, so blank votes raise the number of votes it needs. source
  • In theory this penalises small parties; in practice, says political scientist Manuel Arias, it rarely changes anything, as few parties sit right at the 3% line. source

The figures

  • 0.81%of voters cast a blank vote in the 2023 general election: 199,392 envelopes. source
  • 1.06%cast a spoilt vote (261,078), more than blank votes. source

What it changes for the result

  • Unlike in France, a Spanish blank vote is in the denominator: it lowers every party's percentage a little and raises the 3% bar. source
  • It still elects no one: seats go only to lists. A blank vote does not leave a seat empty. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. Some states (Maharashtra, Haryana) rerun local elections when that option comes first. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.
