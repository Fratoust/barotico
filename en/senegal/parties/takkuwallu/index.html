Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

On the posterRepublican stability and the rule of law
⚠ Rights alertOn 5 February 2024 a bill tabled by PDS deputies and voted by Benno Bokk Yakaar (APR) and Wallu deputies, 105 to 1 after opposition deputies were removed by gendarmes, pushed the presidential election back to 15 December 2024 and kept President Sall in office until his successor took over. On 15 February 2024 the Constitutional Council (decision 1/C/2024) ruled the law unconstitutional because it extended the presidential term beyond five years (articles 27 and 103). The PDS had cited suspicions about the Council's judges; the vote took place on 24 March 2024. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterThe rule of law (Sall governments, 2012-2024)
⚠ Rights alertThe Assembly, where its camp held the majority, adopted citizen sponsorship of candidacies on 19 April 2018. On 28 April 2021 the ECOWAS Court of Justice found it violated the principle of free participation in elections and ordered its removal within six months. Former Justice minister Ismaïla Madior Fall replied that sponsorship is in the Constitution and should be 'corrected, not removed'; it was applied again for the July 2022 legislative elections. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRepublican stability and public order
⚠ Rights alertDuring the protests of June and July 2023 the Sall government cut mobile internet and social networks, citing public order and 'subversive' messages. On 14 May 2025 the ECOWAS Court found the cuts had no clear legal basis, were neither legitimate nor proportionate, and violated freedom of expression and access to information (African Charter art. 9, ICCPR art. 19); it awarded damages and ordered Senegal to refrain from arbitrary cuts in future. source · 2 · 3
On the posterSincere public accounts and a record to defend
ContradictionIn February 2025 the Cour des comptes recounted the 2023 deficit reported by the Sall government at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%, and end-2023 debt at 99.67% instead of 74.41%. In September 2026 the APR still called the 'hidden debt' a 'political construction' and disputed the debt figure. The IMF mission chief had described a 'conscious decision to underestimate the debt stock', with about 5 billion dollars not properly reported in 2019-2024; the outgoing Cour des comptes president noted that his report never uses the words 'hidden debt'. source · 2 · 3 · 4

Also worth knowing
  • In September 2026 the APR called the 'hidden debt' a 'political construction' and again disputed the debt figure; the Cour des comptes had recounted the Sall government's 2023 deficit at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%, and the IMF mission chief had spoken of a 'conscious decision to underestimate the debt stock'. source
  • It claims republican stability; on 5 February 2024 its deputies and the PDS's voted a law pushing the presidential election back ten months and keeping Macky Sall in office, which the Constitutional Council struck down on 15 February as an extension of the term beyond five years. source
  • It invokes the rule of law; in April 2021 the ECOWAS Court found that the candidate-sponsorship rule adopted by its majority in 2018 violated free participation in elections and ordered its removal within six months; the rule was applied again for the 2022 legislative elections. source
To be fair
  • Sall accepted the Constitutional Council's 2024 ruling and organised the election his camp lost, then handed over power
  • The oil and gas projects developed under its governments, in production since 2024, account for about 4.5 points of 2025's 6.7% growth (ANSD)
  • Abstained rather than voting against when the Pastef majority rejected the 2026 amended budget in committee

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Takku Wallu, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its government reported a 4.9% deficit for 2023, which the Cour des comptes recounted at 12.3%. In September 2026 the APR still called the affair a 'political construction'.

In February 2024 its deputies voted to push the presidential election back ten months, until the Constitutional Council ruled the law unconstitutional.

The ECOWAS Court later found that its candidate-sponsorship rule (2021 ruling) and its 2023 internet cuts (2025 ruling) violated, respectively, free participation in elections and freedom of expression.

To be fair, the oil and gas projects developed under its governments supplied about 4.5 of the 6.7 points of growth in 2025 (ANSD).
