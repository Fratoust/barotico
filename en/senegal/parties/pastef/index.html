Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Pastef

Patriotes africains du Sénégal pour le travail, l'éthique et la fraternité (Pastef – Les Patriotes)

Leaning left

On the posterOn the record
On the posterLower the cost of living
DeliveredOn 13 June 2024 its government cut the prices of broken rice (-40 FCFA a kilo), sugar (-50 FCFA a kilo), sunflower oil (-100 FCFA per 5 litres) and the baguette (-15 FCFA), 'small cuts' by the government's own account. ANSD measured average inflation at 0.8% in 2024, after 5.9% in 2023; by the fourth quarter of 2025 prices were up 2.5% year on year. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRepeal the 2024 amnesty 'so that everyone, including me, goes to answer' (Sonko, December 2024)
⚠ Rights alertRather than a repeal, which jurists said could not reach acts already amnestied, its majority passed an 'interpretation' law on 2 April 2025 (126 to 19), meant to exclude 'blood crimes'. On 23 April 2025 the Constitutional Council (decision 1/C/2025) struck down its article 1: it breached the non-retroactivity of harsher criminal law and, by leaving murder or torture committed in the exercise of a public freedom inside the amnesty, Senegal's commitments on imprescriptible crimes. Pastef's group said the ruling echoed its demand that blood crimes not go unpunished. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the poster'Cultural self-defence': harsher penalties for same-sex acts (bill of the Sonko government)
⚠ Rights alertLaw 05/2026, a bill of the Sonko government, passed on 11 March 2026 by 138 votes with none against, on Pastef's majority: it doubles the maximum prison term for consensual same-sex acts to 10 years and extends the offence to their 'promotion' and financing. In its concluding observations of 31 October 2019 the UN Human Rights Committee had asked Senegal to repeal the paragraph of article 319 criminalising sexual acts between consenting adults (Covenant articles 2, 17 and 26, among others). Majority deputies called the law 'cultural self-defence'; on 12 March 2026 the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights urged the president not to promulgate it. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRenegotiate oil, gas and mining contracts
Half-wayRenegotiation was launched in 2024. On 3 April 2025 President Faye called the interim results 'more than satisfactory' and said they would be made public 'at the appropriate time', without giving figures or a date. source
Also worth knowing
  • Its 2024 slogan was 'Diomaye, c'est Sonko'. Twenty-six months later President Faye dismissed Prime Minister Sonko, and the party now uses its 130 seats against a government appointed by the president it got elected. source
  • In December 2024 Sonko, then prime minister, called for the March 2024 amnesty law to be repealed 'so that everyone, including me, goes to answer'; in April 2025 its majority passed an 'interpretation' law instead, whose key article the Constitutional Council struck down for bringing murder and torture linked to protests within the amnesty. source
  • On 3 November 2026 its deputies threw out the amended 2026 budget without debate, through a preliminary motion tabled by Pastef deputy Amadou Ba (109 to 20; 126 once 17 proxies were counted), citing a deficit revised to 7.6% of GDP and missing documents; the finance minister says the dispute is about the recovery plan (PRES) drawn up under the Sonko government. source
To be fair
  • Its government cut the prices of rice, oil, sugar and bread in June 2024; ANSD inflation fell to 0.8% in 2024 from 5.9% in 2023
  • Under its government the Cour des comptes audit of 2019-2024 was published, recounting the 2023 deficit at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%
  • Abolished the HCCT and the CESE, as promised, by 134 votes to 8 in December 2024
Court proceedings (official acts)
  • Remarks and violence
  • Convicted, final Ousmane Sonko (party president, Speaker of the Assembly): convicted of defaming former minister Mame Mbaye Niang, six months suspended prison sentence and 200 million FCFA to pay; the Supreme Court rejected his last appeal on 1 July 2025. His lawyers argue the 2024 amnesty law covers the offence; he has called for a retrial

Who picks up the bill

No audited breakdown published. Under law 81-17 (amended in 1989) parties may be funded only by dues, gifts and bequests from national members and sympathisers and by their own events; there is no public funding scheme in force. In April 2026 the Interior minister said only 20 of 397 parties had filed their 2025 accounts, without naming them.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Pastef, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It campaigned on 'Diomaye is Sonko'. Diomaye then dismissed Sonko.

It promised to repeal the amnesty 'so that everyone goes to answer'. Its interpretation law was struck down instead by the Constitutional Council, which found it retroactive and at odds with Senegal's commitments on crimes such as murder and torture.

In March 2026 its government doubled the maximum sentence for same-sex acts to 10 years; in 2019 the UN Human Rights Committee had asked Senegal to repeal that offence.

To be fair, its June 2024 price cuts came as inflation fell from 5.9% to 0.8% (ANSD).
