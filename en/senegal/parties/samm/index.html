Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Also worth knowing
  • It promised a real balance of powers; on the April 2026 electoral-code reform (articles L29-L30), passed by Pastef, its deputy Anta Babacar Ngom voted against while Pape Djibril Fall abstained, and on 4 May the non-attached deputies, Ngom included, decided not to file a legal challenge, citing constraints 'beyond their control'. source
  • Its list head said in November 2024 that Sonko's government 'had kept none of its promises' in seven months; the June 2024 staple-price cuts had come as inflation fell to 0.8% for the year (ANSD). source
  • It formed in September 2024 to win 'a strong parliamentary majority' and impose cohabitation; a month later the PUR had to deny leaving the alliance, and the coalition won 3 of 165 seats. source
To be fair
  • Brought seven opposition parties and movements onto a single list for the 2024 election
  • Put a woman, Anta Babacar Ngom, second on its national list
  • Its list head contested his removal as Dakar mayor in court; the Supreme Court ruled against him in September 2025

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Sàmm Sa Kaddu, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The seven-party alliance set out to win a strong majority and impose cohabitation. It won three seats of 165.

In November 2024 its leader said the government had kept none of its promises, months after its price cuts.

In April 2026 its two best-known deputies split on the electoral-code reform: one against, one abstaining.

To be fair, it has never governed nationally as a coalition, so it has no measured record yet, good or bad.
