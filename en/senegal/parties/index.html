Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterLower the cost of living
DeliveredOn 13 June 2024 its government cut the prices of broken rice (-40 FCFA a kilo), sugar (-50 FCFA a kilo), sunflower oil (-100 FCFA per 5 litres) and the baguette (-15 FCFA), 'small cuts' by the government's own account. ANSD measured average inflation at 0.8% in 2024, after 5.9% in 2023; by the fourth quarter of 2025 prices were up 2.5% year on year. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRepeal the 2024 amnesty 'so that everyone, including me, goes to answer' (Sonko, December 2024)
⚠ Rights alertRather than a repeal, which jurists said could not reach acts already amnestied, its majority passed an 'interpretation' law on 2 April 2025 (126 to 19), meant to exclude 'blood crimes'. On 23 April 2025 the Constitutional Council (decision 1/C/2025) struck down its article 1: it breached the non-retroactivity of harsher criminal law and, by leaving murder or torture committed in the exercise of a public freedom inside the amnesty, Senegal's commitments on imprescriptible crimes. Pastef's group said the ruling echoed its demand that blood crimes not go unpunished. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the poster'Cultural self-defence': harsher penalties for same-sex acts (bill of the Sonko government)
⚠ Rights alertLaw 05/2026, a bill of the Sonko government, passed on 11 March 2026 by 138 votes with none against, on Pastef's majority: it doubles the maximum prison term for consensual same-sex acts to 10 years and extends the offence to their 'promotion' and financing. In its concluding observations of 31 October 2019 the UN Human Rights Committee had asked Senegal to repeal the paragraph of article 319 criminalising sexual acts between consenting adults (Covenant articles 2, 17 and 26, among others). Majority deputies called the law 'cultural self-defence'; on 12 March 2026 the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights urged the president not to promulgate it. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRenegotiate oil, gas and mining contracts
Half-wayRenegotiation was launched in 2024. On 3 April 2025 President Faye called the interim results 'more than satisfactory' and said they would be made public 'at the appropriate time', without giving figures or a date. source
Also worth knowing
  • Its 2024 slogan was 'Diomaye, c'est Sonko'. Twenty-six months later President Faye dismissed Prime Minister Sonko, and the party now uses its 130 seats against a government appointed by the president it got elected. source
  • In December 2024 Sonko, then prime minister, called for the March 2024 amnesty law to be repealed 'so that everyone, including me, goes to answer'; in April 2025 its majority passed an 'interpretation' law instead, whose key article the Constitutional Council struck down for bringing murder and torture linked to protests within the amnesty. source
  • On 3 November 2026 its deputies threw out the amended 2026 budget without debate, through a preliminary motion tabled by Pastef deputy Amadou Ba (109 to 20; 126 once 17 proxies were counted), citing a deficit revised to 7.6% of GDP and missing documents; the finance minister says the dispute is about the recovery plan (PRES) drawn up under the Sonko government. source
To be fair
  • Its government cut the prices of rice, oil, sugar and bread in June 2024; ANSD inflation fell to 0.8% in 2024 from 5.9% in 2023
  • Under its government the Cour des comptes audit of 2019-2024 was published, recounting the 2023 deficit at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%
  • Abolished the HCCT and the CESE, as promised, by 134 votes to 8 in December 2024
Court proceedings (official acts)

His lawyers argue the 2024 amnesty law covers the offence; he has called for a retrial

⚠ Rights alertOn 5 February 2024 a bill tabled by PDS deputies and voted by Benno Bokk Yakaar (APR) and Wallu deputies, 105 to 1 after opposition deputies were removed by gendarmes, pushed the presidential election back to 15 December 2024 and kept President Sall in office until his successor took over. On 15 February 2024 the Constitutional Council (decision 1/C/2024) ruled the law unconstitutional because it extended the presidential term beyond five years (articles 27 and 103). The PDS had cited suspicions about the Council's judges; the vote took place on 24 March 2024. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterThe rule of law (Sall governments, 2012-2024)
⚠ Rights alertThe Assembly, where its camp held the majority, adopted citizen sponsorship of candidacies on 19 April 2018. On 28 April 2021 the ECOWAS Court of Justice found it violated the principle of free participation in elections and ordered its removal within six months. Former Justice minister Ismaïla Madior Fall replied that sponsorship is in the Constitution and should be 'corrected, not removed'; it was applied again for the July 2022 legislative elections. source · 2 · 3
On the posterRepublican stability and public order
⚠ Rights alertDuring the protests of June and July 2023 the Sall government cut mobile internet and social networks, citing public order and 'subversive' messages. On 14 May 2025 the ECOWAS Court found the cuts had no clear legal basis, were neither legitimate nor proportionate, and violated freedom of expression and access to information (African Charter art. 9, ICCPR art. 19); it awarded damages and ordered Senegal to refrain from arbitrary cuts in future. source · 2 · 3
On the posterSincere public accounts and a record to defend
ContradictionIn February 2025 the Cour des comptes recounted the 2023 deficit reported by the Sall government at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%, and end-2023 debt at 99.67% instead of 74.41%. In September 2026 the APR still called the 'hidden debt' a 'political construction' and disputed the debt figure. The IMF mission chief had described a 'conscious decision to underestimate the debt stock', with about 5 billion dollars not properly reported in 2019-2024; the outgoing Cour des comptes president noted that his report never uses the words 'hidden debt'. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterDefend the record of the 'Plan Sénégal Émergent'
Half-wayANSD measured the poverty rate at 37.8% in 2018-19 and 37.5% in 2021-22, while the number of poor people rose by nearly 500,000 over the same period. source
Also worth knowing
  • In September 2026 the APR called the 'hidden debt' a 'political construction' and again disputed the debt figure; the Cour des comptes had recounted the Sall government's 2023 deficit at 12.3% of GDP instead of 4.9%, and the IMF mission chief had spoken of a 'conscious decision to underestimate the debt stock'. source
  • It claims republican stability; on 5 February 2024 its deputies and the PDS's voted a law pushing the presidential election back ten months and keeping Macky Sall in office, which the Constitutional Council struck down on 15 February as an extension of the term beyond five years. source
  • It invokes the rule of law; in April 2021 the ECOWAS Court found that the candidate-sponsorship rule adopted by its majority in 2018 violated free participation in elections and ordered its removal within six months; the rule was applied again for the 2022 legislative elections. source
To be fair
  • Sall accepted the Constitutional Council's 2024 ruling and organised the election his camp lost, then handed over power
  • The oil and gas projects developed under its governments, in production since 2024, account for about 4.5 points of 2025's 6.7% growth (ANSD)
  • Abstained rather than voting against when the Pastef majority rejected the 2026 amended budget in committee

A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Sàmm Sa Kaddu, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The seven-party alliance set out to win a strong majority and impose cohabitation. It won three seats of 165.

In November 2024 its leader said the government had kept none of its promises, months after its price cuts.

In April 2026 its two best-known deputies split on the electoral-code reform: one against, one abstaining.

To be fair, it has never governed nationally as a coalition, so it has no measured record yet, good or bad.
