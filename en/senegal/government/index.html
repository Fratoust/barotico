Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source

Who governs in Senegal?

Who's at the wheel

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye (elected March 2024 as Pastef's candidate; in July 2026 he launched his own party, Kiiraay – Les Patriotes Républicains). Prime Minister Ahmadou Al Aminou Lô since 25 May 2026, at the head of 30 ministers named on 1 June: Cheikh Diba (Economy, Finance and Planning), Moussa Sarr (Justice), Mohamedou Makhtar Cissé (Interior), Cheikh Niang (Foreign Affairs), Yankhoba Diémé (Armed Forces). Pastef, the majority party, is not part of it.

Faye dismissed Sonko by decree on 22 May 2026 and appointed Lô three days later. Pastef refused to take part, saying it wanted guarantees on justice, contract renegotiation and debt and at least half the portfolios. Lô gave his general policy statement on 8 September 2026; no censure motion was tabled, but Pastef rejected his amended 2026 budget in committee on 23 and 30 October, and the plenary threw it out without debate on 3 November 2026.

No party on our cards sits in the government of Prime Minister Lô: Pastef, which holds 130 of 165 seats, refused to take part after President Faye dismissed Ousmane Sonko in May 2026. The government therefore has no party majority of its own in the Assembly.

What the government promised

  • General policy statement of PM Lô (8 Sept. 2026): structural adjustment acknowledged, IMF programme, local elections on 17 January 2027, legislative elections in 2029
  • Economic and Social Recovery Plan 'Jubbanti Koom' (PRES, 1 Aug. 2025, Sonko government): rely on domestic resources, fiscal discipline, under the 'Vision Sénégal 2050' in three phases
  • Faye's 2024 campaign commitments: abolish the HCCT and CESE, replace 'political funds' with special funds voted by parliament, independent audit bodies, renegotiate contracts, exit plan from the CFA franc

What independent promise trackers found

  • No independent promise tracker found. In April 2025 the president said contract renegotiation was 'following its normal course' with 'more than satisfactory' results, without publishing any. source
  • Not independent: PRES results reported from tax and customs administrations (DGID, Douanes) and the DPEE: tax revenue up 12% in 2025 to about 2,915 billion FCFA (Sud Quotidien, March 2026). source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Cour des comptes (Feb. 2025): debt of 99.67% of GDP at end-2023, not the 74.41% announced; 2023 deficit of 12.3% of GDP, not 4.9%; 2,517 billion FCFA of bank debt contracted outside the budget. source
  • IMF figures reported in September 2026: public debt about 132% of GDP at end-2024 and 125% at end-2025; about a quarter of tax revenue goes to interest; energy subsidies could exceed 800 billion FCFA in 2026 against about 300 billion budgeted. source
  • ANSD: GDP grew 6.7% in 2025 (6.5% in 2024), but only 2.2% excluding hydrocarbons. source
  • ANSD: broad unemployment (including discouraged job-seekers) at 23.3% in Q4 2025, up from 20.0% a year earlier; 27.4% among 15-35 year-olds. source
