Country profile · edited 14 Nov 2026

Senegal

Presidential republic · about 18 million people · president and parliament at odds since May 2026

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 28 monthsNext presidential election expected in March 2029 (local elections overdue, early legislative elections possible)
The president is elected for five years; Bassirou Diomaye Faye won on 24 March 2024, so the next vote is due in early 2029 (month estimated). Two earlier votes may come first: local councillors elected on 23 January 2022 see their terms expire on 23 January 2027, but the decree convening voters for the announced 17 January 2027 date was not signed by the 19 October deadline; and from early December 2026 the president may dissolve the National Assembly, which would trigger legislative elections within 60 to 90 days. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

What to watch in Senegal

Watch list

  • 3 Nov 2026 · pastAmended 2026 budget: rejected in committee on 23 and 30 October, then thrown out in plenary without debate on 3 November 2026 through a preliminary motion tabled by a Pastef deputy (109 to 20 with 3 abstentions; 126 in favour once 17 proxies were counted). The government must now find another legal route to adjust the 2026 budget, with the IMF programme still awaiting Board approval.
  • 2 Dec 2026 · in 16 daysTwo years after the Assembly's installation: from early December 2026 the president may dissolve it; elections would follow within 60 to 90 days. The PM has warned the executive could use its constitutional prerogatives, and Kiiraay, the president's party, is calling for dissolution; dissolved on 2 December, the Assembly would be re-elected on a Sunday between 31 January and 28 February 2027.
  • Date to be setIMF Executive Board vote on the 36-month, 2.2-billion-dollar Extended Credit Facility agreed at staff level on 1 September 2026, conditional on corrective measures after the misreporting case; the authorities intend to request debt treatment.
  • Date to be setHigh Court of Justice: verdicts awaited for former ministers Ismaïla Madior Fall (attempted corruption, trial opened July 2026) and Aïssatou Sophie Gladima (Covid-19 funds); its rulings cannot be appealed.
  • 23 Jan 2027 · in 68 daysTerms of mayors and departmental councillors expire. No decree convening voters had been signed by the 19 October 2026 deadline for the announced 17 January date; the citizen collective Ñoo Lank plans to go to the ECOWAS Court.
  • 25 Mar 2029 · in 860 daysPresidential election (estimated date). An April 2026 reform of electoral code articles L29-L30 lets a judge, rather than the law, decide whether a conviction such as defamation removes eligibility; the opposition calls it a law tailored for Sonko.
