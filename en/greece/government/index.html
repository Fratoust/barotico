How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who governs in Greece?

Who's at the wheel

Kyriakos Mitsotakis (New Democracy), Prime Minister since 2019, re-elected in June 2023.

Single-party majority: 156 of the 300 seats (297 filled, November 2026).

Sources: ERT News: the June 2023 election · ERT News: seats of each group (3 Nov 2026) · MARC for Proto Thema: survey report (July 2026) · KEFiM: tracker of the 2023 programme · Hellenic Fiscal Council: spring report 2026

Who holds power

President of the Republic: Konstantinos Tasoulas, sworn in on 13 March 2025. Prime Minister: Kyriakos Mitsotakis (New Democracy), since July 2019, re-elected in June 2023.

In government: 156 absolute majority: 151

Single-party government: New Democracy holds 156 seats, above the 151 needed for a majority of the 300-seat chamber.

The Parliament's list by group could not be opened on 15 November 2026. ERT reported on 3 November 2026: New Democracy 156, PASOK 36, KKE 21, SYRIZA 12, Elliniki Lysi 11, Niki 8, Plefsi Eleftherias 5, independents 48. On 9 November independent MP Andreas Panagiotopoulos (elected for SYRIZA) resigned his seat; on 13 November his successor was sworn in as a SYRIZA MP, bringing SYRIZA to 13 and the independents to 47.

Three Spartiates seats annulled by the Supreme Special Court in June 2025 have not been refilled, so 297 MPs sit. Independents are placed in the middle of the chart for display only; they have no common position.

What the government promised

  • Government plan to the 2027 election: €23 bn National Development Programme 2026-2030 (October 2026), after the measures announced at the September 2026 Thessaloniki Fair

What independent promise trackers found

  • KEFiM (Centre for Liberal Studies, a private think tank) tracker, June 2026: of 156 checkable 2023 pledges, 20.5 % fully and 28.8 % partly implemented, 42.3 % in progress, 3.9 % abandoned. Weakest areas: health (30 %), sustainability, infrastructure. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Hellenic Fiscal Council (July 2026): GDP growth 2.1 % in 2025, a general-government surplus of 1.7 % of GDP, public debt down to 146.1 % of GDP; it flags high youth unemployment and low productivity. source
  • Corruption Perceptions Index 2025: Greece scores 50/100 and ranks 56th, up 14 points since 2012, one of Europe's biggest improvers (Transparency International). source
  • Press freedom 2026: 86th of 180 (89th in 2025); RSF describes a 'systemic crisis since 2021'. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards: one per party with a group in Parliament or at least 3% in June 2023, so seven groups plus Spartiates (no group since 2025). ELAS, founded by Alexis Tsipras in May 2026, and Nea Aristera have no group and no 2023 result, so no card. Left-right positions are an editorial reading of the national expert consensus, because the Chapel Hill Expert Survey could not be consulted.

Seats: the official list by group could not be opened. Figures are the Parliament's numbers as reported by ERT on 3 November 2026, updated for one resignation and one swearing-in up to 13 November. Three seats annulled in June 2025 are still vacant, so 297 MPs sit.

Gauges: the homicide rate is calculated from 79 homicides in 2022 (Hellenic Police, reported by ERT) divided by ELSTAT's population. The corruption rank comes from a parliamentary document. No poll asking the same 'would never vote' question about every party was found.

Party funding: amounts are the first official 2025 allocation of state funding (Interior Ministry, February 2025). No income breakdown is published; debt figures reported for ND and PASOK were dropped because no validated source confirms them. Programme links were not verified.

Press: besides official sources, only the validated list is used: EFCSN fact-checkers, ERT and brands trusted by at least 45% in the Digital News Report 2026. Complaints without an official act, and a conviction legally deemed erased, are left out.
