Greece

Parliamentary republic · single-chamber Parliament of 300 seats

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who decides what

President of the RepublicHead of state elected by Parliament for 5 years; mostly ceremonial, signs laws and calls elections.
Parliament and government300 MPs in one chamber; the Prime Minister needs its confidence and runs the country.
Regions13 regions, each with an elected regional governor and council (last elected in October 2023).
MunicipalitiesElected mayors and councils for local services; Mount Athos has its own self-governing status.

Sources: Constitution of Greece (2019 text, Hellenic Parliament): Art. 30 (President), 101-102 (local government), 105 (Mount Athos) · Ministry of the Interior: Law 4804/2021 on electing municipal and regional authorities · ERT News: the October 2023 local and regional elections in the 13 regions

How a law is passed

  1. A ministry drafts the bill and posts it for public online consultation.
  2. One or more standing committees of Parliament examine it.
  3. The plenary votes article by article; a simple majority is enough for most laws.
  4. The President signs it and it is published in the Government Gazette (ΦΕΚ).
  5. Changing the Constitution needs votes in two successive Parliaments: the current one voted its proposals in July and September 2026, and the Parliament elected in 2027 will decide.

Sources: ERT News: a bill posted for public consultation on opengov.gr (31 Jan 2026) · ERT News: the same bill tabled and sent to two committees (17 Feb 2026) · ERT News: plenary vote article by article (4 Mar 2026) · Constitution of Greece: Art. 42 (promulgation) and 110 (revision) · ERT News: the 2026 constitutional revision and the next Parliament's role (23 Jul 2026) · ERT News: second vote of the proposing Parliament (16 Sep 2026)

Political and economic context

One party governs alone (New Democracy, 156 seats) facing a scattered opposition: six other groups and 47 independents. SYRIZA, the main opposition after 2023, fell to 13 MPs (November 2026), and its founder Alexis Tsipras launched a new party, ELAS, in May 2026, which October 2026 polls place second although it has no group in Parliament. Three cases weigh on the term: the Tempi rail crash (trial of 36 defendants since March 2026), the OPEKEPE farm-subsidy file investigated by the European Public Prosecutor, and the Predator spyware case. Since June 2025 Parliament has sat with 297 MPs because three annulled seats were never refilled.

Sources: ERT News: seats of each group (3 Nov 2026) · thebest.gr (regional press): SYRIZA back to 13 MPs (13 Nov 2026) · ERT News: Alexis Tsipras founds ELAS (26 May 2026) · ERT News: Pulse and Metron Analysis polls (October 2026) · ERT News: the Tempi trial opens with 36 defendants · ERT News: immunity of 13 ND MPs lifted (OPEKEPE) · ERT News: the Predator court ruling · Hellenic Parliament: written question on the three vacant seats

Why it's like thisThe 1975 Constitution was written after the military junta (1967-1974): a strong Parliament, a President with limited powers, and electoral laws that reward the winner so that governments are stable. Its Article 86 says ministers can only be prosecuted if Parliament decides so, which is why the Tempi and OPEKEPE cases are fought out in parliamentary votes as well as in court.

Sources: Constitution of Greece (2019 text): Art. 86 on ministers' criminal liability, Art. 110 on revision · ERT News: Parliament votes to refer a former minister over Tempi (July 2025) · ERT News: Parliament rejects preliminary inquiries in the OPEKEPE case (July 2025)

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards: one per party with a group in Parliament or at least 3% in June 2023, so seven groups plus Spartiates (no group since 2025). ELAS, founded by Alexis Tsipras in May 2026, and Nea Aristera have no group and no 2023 result, so no card. Left-right positions are an editorial reading of the national expert consensus, because the Chapel Hill Expert Survey could not be consulted.

Seats: the official list by group could not be opened. Figures are the Parliament's numbers as reported by ERT on 3 November 2026, updated for one resignation and one swearing-in up to 13 November. Three seats annulled in June 2025 are still vacant, so 297 MPs sit.

Gauges: the homicide rate is calculated from 79 homicides in 2022 (Hellenic Police, reported by ERT) divided by ELSTAT's population. The corruption rank comes from a parliamentary document. No poll asking the same 'would never vote' question about every party was found.

Party funding: amounts are the first official 2025 allocation of state funding (Interior Ministry, February 2025). No income breakdown is published; debt figures reported for ND and PASOK were dropped because no validated source confirms them. Programme links were not verified.

Press: besides official sources, only the validated list is used: EFCSN fact-checkers, ERT and brands trusted by at least 45% in the Digital News Report 2026. Complaints without an official act, and a conviction legally deemed erased, are left out.
