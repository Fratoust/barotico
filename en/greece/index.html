Greece

Parliamentary republic · single-chamber Parliament of 300 seats

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Registration is automatic from the civil registry; you vote in person at the polling station of your municipality of registration. Voting is formally compulsory. Since the March 2026 law, voters living abroad can vote by post in national elections after registering.

Last turnout: 52.8 % in the June 2023 parliamentary election (record abstention of 47.2 %) source

Who decides what

President of the RepublicHead of state elected by Parliament for 5 years; mostly ceremonial, signs laws and calls elections.
Parliament and government300 MPs in one chamber; the Prime Minister needs its confidence and runs the country.
Regions13 elected regions (roads, regional development, some EU funds).
MunicipalitiesElected mayors and councils for local services; Mount Athos has its own self-governing status.

How people vote

  • Every 4 years at most, MPs are elected by proportional representation in constituencies plus a national list; a party needs 3% of the national vote to win seats.
  • The first party gets a seat bonus (20 seats from 25% of the vote, up to 50), designed to produce a one-party majority.
  • You can vote from age 17. A law of March 2026 lets Greeks abroad vote by post in national elections; a 3-seat diaspora constituency was also voted, but without a two-thirds majority it only applies from 2031.

How a law is passed

  1. A ministry drafts the bill and posts it for public online consultation.
  2. A standing committee of Parliament examines it and hears experts.
  3. The plenary votes article by article; a simple majority is enough for most laws.
  4. The President signs it and it is published in the Government Gazette (ΦΕΚ).
  5. Changing the Constitution needs votes in two successive Parliaments.

Why it's complicated

One party governs alone (New Democracy, 156 seats) facing a scattered opposition: six other groups and 47 independents. SYRIZA, the main opposition after 2023, fell to 11 MPs, and its founder Alexis Tsipras launched a new party, ELAS, in May 2026, which mid-2026 polls place second although it has no group in Parliament. Three cases weigh on the term: the Tempi rail crash (trial of 36 defendants since March 2026), the OPEKEPE farm-subsidy file investigated by the European Public Prosecutor, and the Predator spyware case. Since June 2025 Parliament has sat with 297 MPs because three annulled seats were never refilled.

Why it's like thisThe 1975 Constitution was written after the military junta (1967-1974): a strong Parliament, a President with limited powers, and electoral laws that reward the winner so that governments are stable. Its Article 86 says ministers can only be prosecuted if Parliament decides so, which is why the Tempi and OPEKEPE cases are fought out in parliamentary votes as well as in court.

Who's at the wheel

Kyriakos Mitsotakis (New Democracy), Prime Minister since 2019, re-elected in June 2023.

Single-party majority: 156 of the 300 seats (October 2026).
