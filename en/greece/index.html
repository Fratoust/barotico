Greece

Parliamentary republic · single-chamber Parliament of 300 seats

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Who decides what

President of the RepublicHead of state elected by Parliament for 5 years; mostly ceremonial, signs laws and calls elections.
Parliament and government300 MPs in one chamber; the Prime Minister needs its confidence and runs the country.
Regions13 elected regions (roads, regional development, some EU funds).
MunicipalitiesElected mayors and councils for local services; Mount Athos has its own self-governing status.

How a law is passed

  1. A ministry drafts the bill and posts it for public online consultation.
  2. A standing committee of Parliament examines it and hears experts.
  3. The plenary votes article by article; a simple majority is enough for most laws.
  4. The President signs it and it is published in the Government Gazette (ΦΕΚ).
  5. Changing the Constitution needs votes in two successive Parliaments.

Why it's complicated

One party governs alone (New Democracy, 156 seats) facing a scattered opposition: six other groups and 47 independents. SYRIZA, the main opposition after 2023, fell to 11 MPs, and its founder Alexis Tsipras launched a new party, ELAS, in May 2026, which mid-2026 polls place second although it has no group in Parliament. Three cases weigh on the term: the Tempi rail crash (trial of 36 defendants since March 2026), the OPEKEPE farm-subsidy file investigated by the European Public Prosecutor, and the Predator spyware case. Since June 2025 Parliament has sat with 297 MPs because three annulled seats were never refilled.

Why it's like thisThe 1975 Constitution was written after the military junta (1967-1974): a strong Parliament, a President with limited powers, and electoral laws that reward the winner so that governments are stable. Its Article 86 says ministers can only be prosecuted if Parliament decides so, which is why the Tempi and OPEKEPE cases are fought out in parliamentary votes as well as in court.

Trust in news has stayed low for a decade (peak of 32 % in 2021, Reuters Institute data analysed by diaNEOsis). In 2026, 8 in 10 Greeks say they sometimes or often avoid the news (6th of 48 countries) and 35 % name social media as their main source. source

Who owns what

  • Large private groups such as Alter Ego Media sit alongside hundreds of news websites.
  • RSF: a few entrepreneurs own most outlets while holding stakes in other heavily regulated sectors.
  • Public service media (ERT) are state-owned and supervised by the government spokesperson.

Documented facts to know

  • Press freedom: 86th of 180 in 2026, up from 89th in 2025 (RSF). source
  • Predator case: on 26 February 2026 an Athens court convicted four businessmen; journalist Thanasis Koukakis was among the people targeted. source
  • The court ordered new investigations, including for espionage, and noted that a SIM card used to send the infected messages was topped up near intelligence service (EYP) facilities. source
  • The 2021 murder of crime reporter Giorgos Karaivaz remains unsolved; two suspects were acquitted in 2024 (RSF). source
