How to read this country profile

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for PASOK, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It presents itself as the party of renewal. Elected with 32 MPs in 2023, its group has grown to 36 by taking in MPs elected on other lists, without a new vote.

On Tempi and OPEKEPE it left the chamber before both July 2025 votes, calling the Tempi charge 'constructed' to lead to acquittal and saying the OPEKEPE vote lacked a quorum. Its own OPEKEPE inquiry requests fell 78 to 3.

In opposition it voted against most of the 13-hour working-day law and for the Tempi censure motion, and lost both times.

To be fair, it was its leader's application that led the Council of State in 2024 to rule unconstitutional the 2021 ban on telling people they had been under surveillance.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Cards: one per party with a group in Parliament or at least 3% in June 2023, so seven groups plus Spartiates (no group since 2025). ELAS, founded by Alexis Tsipras in May 2026, and Nea Aristera have no group and no 2023 result, so no card. Left-right positions are an editorial reading of the national expert consensus, because the Chapel Hill Expert Survey could not be consulted.

Seats: the official list by group could not be opened. Figures are the Parliament's numbers as reported by ERT on 3 November 2026, updated for one resignation and one swearing-in up to 13 November. Three seats annulled in June 2025 are still vacant, so 297 MPs sit.

Gauges: the homicide rate is calculated from 79 homicides in 2022 (Hellenic Police, reported by ERT) divided by ELSTAT's population. The corruption rank comes from a parliamentary document. No poll asking the same 'would never vote' question about every party was found.

Party funding: amounts are the first official 2025 allocation of state funding (Interior Ministry, February 2025). No income breakdown is published; debt figures reported for ND and PASOK were dropped because no validated source confirms them. Programme links were not verified.

Press: besides official sources, only the validated list is used: EFCSN fact-checkers, ERT and brands trusted by at least 45% in the Digital News Report 2026. Complaints without an official act, and a conviction legally deemed erased, are left out.
