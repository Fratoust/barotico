Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote≈ 38 monthsNext general election (Dáil Éireann) — due by January 2030 at the latest
The Dáil elected on 29 November 2024 first met in December 2024 and can last at most five years; the election must follow within 30 days of dissolution. The Taoiseach can call it earlier. Under the coalition deal, Simon Harris (Fine Gael) is due to replace Micheál Martin (Fianna Fáil) as Taoiseach in November 2027 without an election. source
How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  7. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

If you're considering voting for Labour, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It wants universal healthcare. In its last spell in government, with Fine Gael, it brought in free GP care for children under six in 2015; their daytime visits rose 29% in a Dublin study. Free GP care for everyone did not follow.

After the 2024 election it proposed a common centre-left bloc; the Social Democrats declined, and Labour talked with Fianna Fáil and Fine Gael before leaving over 'a policy gulf'.

Its leader called a merger with the Social Democrats 'possible'; they ruled it out, and in September 2026 one of its MEPs suggested the Greens instead.

To be fair, the 2015 marriage-equality referendum, held while it governed with Fine Gael, has since allowed 5,956 same-sex couples to marry (CSO, to 2024).

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Eight parties have a card: every party with at least 3% of first preferences or a Dáil group of its own after the November 2024 election. People Before Profit–Solidarity (2.8%, 3 TDs) and 100% Redress (1 TD) appear only in the seat chart, as do the 15 independents. The Greens are kept at 3.0%, a borderline figure from an unofficial results database, which is also the source of the smaller parties' vote shares.

Seats are the official 2024 results adjusted for the May 2026 by-elections; the seven independents shown as backing the Government are the nine who signed up in January 2025 minus the two Healy-Raes, who voted against it in April 2026. No official seat count by party could be opened.

Left-right positions are our estimates, as no Chapel Hill 2024 figures were found. The homicide rate is our calculation (recorded homicide incidents divided by the CSO population). Income breakdowns are shown only for Sinn Féin and Aontú; for the others, SIPO publishes compliance reports but no breakdown we could find.

Press links come only from the validated list (RTÉ, The Irish Times, the Irish Independent, TheJournal and the Irish Examiner with regional press), chosen by trust scores in the Reuters Institute's 2026 report. No independent line-by-line tracker of the 2025 programme exists, so Fiscal Council and CSO figures are used instead. Claims without an official act or a party-level record (such as a party's vote in a confidence motion) were left out.
