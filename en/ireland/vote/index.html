Voting in Ireland

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Check or update your registration at voter.ie (run by local councils). Applications must reach the council 15 days before polling day, not counting Sundays and public holidays. You vote in the constituency where you are registered.

Last turnout: 45.8% in the October 2025 presidential election, with a record 213,738 spoiled ballots (12.9%) source

The electoral system

  • Single transferable vote (STV): 43 constituencies elect at least three TDs each (174 in all). You number the candidates 1, 2, 3… in order of preference, as far down as you like.
  • A quota is set (valid votes ÷ (seats + 1), plus one: 23,947 of 47,893 valid votes for the single seat in Galway West in May 2026). Anyone above it is elected and their surplus passes to next preferences; if nobody reaches it, the last-placed candidate is eliminated and their votes move on. Repeat until all seats are filled, so a vote is rarely wasted.
  • Voters must be 18+ and ordinarily resident. Irish citizens vote in Dáil, local and European elections and referendums; British citizens in Dáil and local elections; other EU citizens in European and local elections; everyone else in local elections only.

Sources: Electoral Commission: bye-elections, preferences, quota and transfers · Constitution of Ireland, Art. 16.2 (STV, at least three seats per constituency) · Houses of the Oireachtas: members (174 TDs, 43 constituencies) · Oireachtas: Galway West bye-election result (quota 23,947) · gov.ie: voting in Ireland (who can vote in which election)

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Ireland, there is no blank vote: an unmarked ballot is a spoilt vote. In 2025, spoiling became a protest on a scale never seen before.

What it is

  • You rank candidates: 1 for your first choice, 2 for the next, and so on. You may stop after one choice. source
  • Spoilt votes: blank ballots, ticks or crosses instead of a “1”, an unclear order, illegible writing, unstamped ballots, or marks that identify the voter. source

How it is counted

  • Spoilt votes are counted and published, but do not count towards the result. source

The figures

  • 12.9%of ballots were spoilt in the October 2025 presidential election (213,738), against 1.2% in 2018: a record. source

What it changes for the result

  • A polling-day survey linked most of those spoilt ballots to a lack of choice, protest, or support for a would-be candidate who was not on the ballot. A campaign to spoil had spread online. source
  • The winner was still elected with the valid votes alone: a spoilt ballot sends a message but changes no result. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Barotico does not recommend voting blank, nor avoiding it. These questions are there to help you decide with the facts in hand.

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Eight parties have a card: every party with at least 3% of first preferences or a Dáil group of its own after the November 2024 election. People Before Profit–Solidarity (2.8%, 3 TDs) and 100% Redress (1 TD) appear only in the seat chart, as do the 15 independents. The Greens are kept at 3.0%, a borderline figure from an unofficial results database, which is also the source of the smaller parties' vote shares.

Seats are the official 2024 results adjusted for the May 2026 by-elections; the seven independents shown as backing the Government are the nine who signed up in January 2025 minus the two Healy-Raes, who voted against it in April 2026. No official seat count by party could be opened.

Left-right positions are our estimates, as no Chapel Hill 2024 figures were found. The homicide rate is our calculation (recorded homicide incidents divided by the CSO population). Income breakdowns are shown only for Sinn Féin and Aontú; for the others, SIPO publishes compliance reports but no breakdown we could find.

Press links come only from the validated list (RTÉ, The Irish Times, the Irish Independent, TheJournal and the Irish Examiner with regional press), chosen by trust scores in the Reuters Institute's 2026 report. No independent line-by-line tracker of the 2025 programme exists, so Fiscal Council and CSO figures are used instead. Claims without an official act or a party-level record (such as a party's vote in a confidence motion) were left out.
