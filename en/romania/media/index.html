Romania

Semi-presidential republic · 19.0 million residents (INS, 1 January 2026)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote≈ 25 monthsParliamentary elections (late 2028, date not yet set)
The Chamber of Deputies and the Senate elected on 1 December 2024 serve four years. The head of the Permanent Electoral Authority expects the parliamentary vote in November or December 2028, after local elections in early summer 2028. The government sets the exact dates. The next presidential election is due in 2030. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Media in Romania

Only 23% of Romanians trust the news most of the time, 43rd of 48 countries in the Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026; it is the lowest level since measurement began in 2015. For the first time, social and video platforms overtook TV and news sites as the main way to get news, though TV is still the main source for 58%. source

Recommended for factual news

TVR

Public television: more than half of respondents trust TVR (Digital News Report 2026); 17% watched it in the week before the survey. Funded from the state budget since 2016.

source
Radio România

Public radio: with TVR, one of the two public institutions that more than half of respondents trust (Digital News Report 2026).

source
Digi24

All-news channel of the RCS&RDS group (Digi Communications, listed in Bucharest): 55% of its viewers cite its credibility, and 29% of Romanians get news from it at least three times a week (Reuters Institute data reported by Digi24).

source
Factual.ro

Romania's first political fact-checking site, launched in 2014 and run by the NGO Funky Citizens. It checks politicians' statements. Its IFCN/EFCSN status was not verified.

source

Video explainers

Recorder

Independent video-investigation outlet funded by readers. Its December 2025 documentary 'Justiție capturată' reached nearly 1.9 million YouTube views in a day and an average 305,000 viewers when TVR 1 aired it, about four times the slot's usual audience. Critics, such as BZI, say it brought no new evidence.

source

Who owns what

  • TVR and Radio România: public broadcasters funded from the state budget. Parliament nominates most of their board members and can dismiss the board by rejecting its annual report.
  • RCS&RDS / Digi Communications (listed on the Bucharest Stock Exchange): Digi24.
  • Titluri Quality: bought the Mediafax agency (January 2025) and the G4Media group (August 2025).
  • Maricel Păcuraru: Realitatea Plus, the channel the regulator has fined most often.

source

Documented facts to know

  • Expert Forum: of about 244 million lei that parties spent from state subsidies in 2025, about 117 million (48%) went on 'press and propaganda', the largest category. AEP does not disclose who receives it. source
  • CNA (audiovisual regulator), November 2025: fined Realitatea Plus 200,000 lei, partly for political advertising outside the campaign in favour of a Bucharest mayoral candidate and partly for misreporting a defence minister's statement. It also fined România TV and Nașul TV 50,000 lei each. source
  • IPI Media Capture Monitoring Report 2025: Parliament nominates 6 of the CNA's 11 members, in October 2024 it appointed a former PSD senator among others, and the report rates the regulator's effective independence as absent. source
  • RSF 2026: Romania rose from 55th to 49th of 180. RSF describes a pluralistic media landscape with low public trust, which it links to opaque media funding, including public money that parties pass on to outlets. source
