Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Also worth knowing
  • In October 2024 the Constitutional Court rejected its leader Diana Șoșoacă's presidential candidacy, finding that her statements disregarded her duty to respect the Constitution, including the democratic order and Romania's EU and NATO membership. She said the court had relied on press articles, in breach of the Constitution and the ECHR; PSD, USR and AUR leaders also questioned the ruling, which is final. source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for SOS România, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It entered Parliament in December 2024 as a single anti-system bloc. Within a year it had lost 13 of its deputies and its Senate group.

It received about 19.8 million lei of state subsidy in 2025. When Expert Forum analysed party finances, its 2025 financial statements had yet to be published.

In 2025 it backed the censure motions against the VAT rise and the austerity packages, which failed. Its own programme, from reviving the Cantacuzino Institute to a tougher criminal code, has never been tested in government.

Before voting, it's worth asking: do the party's actions match its promises?
