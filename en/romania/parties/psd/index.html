Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

On the posterHigher, fairer pensions: recalculate them all (2023 law)
DeliveredUnder the 2023 pension law of its government with PNL, labour minister Simona Bucura-Oprescu (PSD) completed the recalculation in August 2024: 3.8 million of 4.69 million pensions rose from September, by 622 lei on average. The average pension rose 22.6% in 2024, or 14.4% after inflation (INS). source · 2
On the posterProtect pensions and incomes
Half-wayThe pension point was then frozen at 81 lei: for 2025 by an emergency ordinance of Marcel Ciolacu's PSD-led government in December 2024, and for 2026 by Law 141/2025 of the Bolojan coalition it sat in; the government later said both freezes were needed to cut the deficit. In October 2025 PSD said it did not accept a freeze in 2026 and asked for a 7% indexation. Annual inflation reached 10.42% in June 2026 (INS). source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterA 5% deficit in 2024 (its government's budget)
ContradictionPrime Minister Marcel Ciolacu's PSD-PNL government built the 2024 budget on a 5% deficit, then raised the target to 6.94% in September, saying '84% of the deficit goes to investment'. The year closed at 8.65% of GDP in cash terms (Finance Ministry), 9.3% under EU rules (Eurostat). Investment does not explain the slippage on its own: the Fiscal Council traced it to public pay rises of 10% to 30%, the new pension law and unpaid bills, and PNL finance minister Marcel Boloș counted 64.4 billion lei of spending outside the budget, from pensions and wages to health and investment overruns. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
On the posterCurb the anti-corruption prosecutors' 'excess of authority' (2018)
⚠ Rights alertIn February 2018 justice minister Tudorel Toader, 'a minister in my government' according to PSD prime minister Viorica Dăncilă, proposed removing DNA chief prosecutor Laura Codruța Kövesi. The President refused; on the Government's referral the Constitutional Court ruled in May that he had to sign, and she was removed. On 5 May 2020 the European Court of Human Rights unanimously found that the removal breached her right of access to a court and her freedom of expression, since she had criticised the government's justice reforms. Toader said the President had acted on the Court's decision, not on his proposal. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6
Also worth knowing
  • PSD sat in the Bolojan cabinet that raised VAT to 21% in August 2025. On 23 April 2026 its ministers resigned, and on 5 May it co-filed the censure motion with AUR, attacking 'absurd' cuts and doubled inflation. Five months later it was back in government with 11 ministries. source
  • On 28 April 2026 PSD and AUR, who together held 7 of the 13 seats on the Chamber's Permanent Bureau, cancelled the Bureau's own decision of a week earlier and dissolved the group of POT dissidents, ten of whom reportedly did not want to vote for the censure motion. source
  • In 2024, under its prime minister Marcel Ciolacu, whose coalition included PNL, the public deficit reached 9.3% of GDP (Eurostat). In 2026 the party campaigned against the cuts made to reduce it. source
To be fair
  • The 2024 pension recalculation raised the average pension by 22.6% in a year, 14.4% after inflation (INS).
  • After resigning, its ministers kept supporting the EU-funded (PNRR) bills during the 2026 crisis.
  • Its MPs voted to lift the immunity of its own minister Adrian Chesnoiu at the DNA's request (2022).

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for PSD, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

In 2024, under its prime minister Marcel Ciolacu, the government planned a 5% deficit and ended the year at 9.3% of GDP (Eurostat). He said most of it went to investment; the Fiscal Council pointed instead to public pay rises and the new pension law.

In 2025 it signed the coalition plan to cut that deficit, including a 20% cut in public jobs, which came to under 2%. In April 2026 it left the coalition; in May it brought the government down with AUR, over 'absurd cuts'.

Five months later it was back in government, with 11 ministries.

To be fair, its 2024 pension recalculation raised the average pension by 14.4% after inflation (INS).
