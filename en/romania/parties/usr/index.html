The Chamber of Deputies and the Senate elected on 1 December 2024 serve four years. Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterAbolish the special section for investigating magistrates (SIIJ)
Half-wayIn government in 2021, its justice minister Stelian Ion had an abolition bill approved by the Government in February; it stalled in the coalition, which he blamed on UDMR. Back in opposition, USR said the PSD-PNL-UDMR coalition's 2022 bill looked like a re-establishment, not an abolition; all its amendments were rejected, and the Senate adopted the law 86 to 37 on 28 February 2022. The section was abolished, and cases against senior magistrates went to a section of the Prosecutor General's office. source · 2 · 3
On the posterCut spending before raising taxes (2025)
Half-wayOn 19 June 2025 Dominic Fritz said USR had been against VAT rises but accepted that the European Commission seemed to firmly require one; on 22 June he confirmed that the standard rate would not rise 'this summer'. The coalition USR had joined raised it from 19% to 21% on 1 August 2025. The deficit fell from 9.3% to 7.9% of GDP in 2025 (Eurostat), mainly through tax rises and a wage and pension freeze, while under 2% of public jobs were cut (Euronews România). source · 2 · 3 · 4

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept on paper Abolish special pensions: only magistrates' pensions were reformed (February 2026), phased in, with no measured effect yet source
  • Kept, made a difference Cut the deficit as part of the 2025 coalition: from 9.3% to 7.9% of GDP (Eurostat) source · measured effect
  • Kept on paper End the cumulation of public pension and salary: 'stayed on paper' (April 2026) source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for USR, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

On 22 June 2025 its leader Dominic Fritz confirmed that VAT would not rise that summer. Three days earlier he had said the European Commission seemed to firmly require it. On 1 August, with USR in government, VAT went from 19% to 21%.

After ten months in government with PSD, it voted against the Niculescu cabinet because it is 'made up of PSD members'.

The special-pensions reform of the coalition it sat in covers magistrates only. In November 2025 its defence minister resigned after the university on his old CV said he had never studied there.

To be fair, the coalition it sat in cut the deficit from 9.3% to 7.9% of GDP (Eurostat).
