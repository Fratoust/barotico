Romania

Semi-presidential republic · 19.0 million residents (INS, 1 January 2026)

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

No registration needed: vote at the polling station for your address with a valid ID card or passport. People who are ill or disabled can ask for a mobile ballot box. Romanians abroad vote at polling stations opened over the weekend; check AEP's site for postal voting rules and deadlines before each election.

Last turnout: About 52% in the December 2024 parliamentary election (51.9% half an hour before polls closed, AEP data). In the May 2025 presidential runoff, 11.64 million people voted. source

Who decides what

StateThe President, directly elected for five years (two terms at most), names the Prime Minister, leads defence and foreign policy and can return laws once. The Government needs a confidence vote of both chambers sitting together. Parliament has two chambers, the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate.
Courts and control bodiesThe Constitutional Court (CCR) reviews laws and validates presidential elections. In December 2024 it annulled one. The anti-corruption directorate (DNA) prosecutes high-level corruption, and the High Court (ÎCCJ) tries ministers and MPs.
Counties41 counties plus Bucharest. Each has an elected county council and president, plus a prefect appointed by the Government.
MunicipalitiesCommunes, towns and cities elect their mayors in a single round and a local council every four years. The new government wants communes of 1,500 people or fewer to merge voluntarily.
European UnionAn EU member since 2007, Romania is under EU budget surveillance. In 2025 it had the highest public deficit in the Union (7.9% of GDP), and part of its investment is paid for by the EU recovery plan (PNRR).

How people vote

  • Every four years citizens aged 18 and over elect 331 deputies and 134 senators (2024) from closed party lists, by proportional representation in each county. A party needs 5% nationally to enter Parliament.
  • National minorities: each recognised minority organisation that misses the threshold can still win one deputy seat. In 2024 there were 19 such seats, sitting as one group.
  • The President is elected in two rounds. The first-round result of November 2024 was annulled by the Constitutional Court, and the May 2025 rerun was won by Nicușor Dan with 53.6% against George Simion (AUR).

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled by the Government, by MPs or by 100,000 citizens.
  2. It goes first to one chamber and then to the 'decisional' chamber, which depends on the subject and has the final say.
  3. Shortcut: the Government can 'assume responsibility' for a bill. It then becomes law unless a censure motion passes within days. The 2025 austerity package (VAT up to 21%) passed this way.
  4. The President promulgates the law, can return it once or send it to the Constitutional Court, and it is then published in the Monitorul Oficial.

Why it's complicated

In December 2024 the Constitutional Court annulled the presidential election after independent candidate Călin Georgescu topped the first round. He was barred from the 2025 rerun and is now on trial in two cases. AUR backed him in 2025, and its leader pledged to make him Prime Minister. The May 2025 rerun elected Nicușor Dan, and Ilie Bolojan's PNL-PSD-USR-UDMR coalition raised VAT to 21% to cut a 9.3% deficit. PSD left the coalition in April 2026 and brought the government down with AUR in May. After a five-month crisis, Luca Niculescu's PSD-and-independents cabinet took office on 20 October. The economy is in technical recession, and AUR leads the polls (39.7%, INSCOP, September 2026).

Why it's like thisThe 1991 Constitution was written after the Ceaușescu dictatorship. It borrows from the French model: a directly elected President alongside a Prime Minister who answers to Parliament, plus a Constitutional Court to arbitrate between them. In the 2000s, to join the EU, Romania built strong anti-corruption prosecutors (DNA). Today, governing coalitions between rival parties are the norm.

Who's at the wheel

Luca Niculescu (independent, former state secretary at the Foreign Ministry), Prime Minister since 20 October 2026. PSD holds 11 ministries plus a delegate minister, and the rest, including Finance, went to independents. PNL, UDMR, the national minorities and the 'Uniți pentru România' group support it from outside.

On 5 May 2026 a PSD-AUR censure motion brought down Ilie Bolojan's government with 281 votes. Three attempts then failed: Eugen Tomac withdrew, and Siegfried Mureșan (182 votes) and Adrian Veștea (189) fell short of the 233 needed. On 5 October President Dan designated Niculescu, who won 280 votes on 20 October. PNL has pledged not to file or back a censure motion until 1 March 2027.

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Expert Forum: of about 244 million lei that parties spent from state subsidies in 2025, about 117 million (48%) went on 'press and propaganda', the largest category. AEP does not disclose who receives it. source
  • CNA (audiovisual regulator), November 2025: fined Realitatea Plus 200,000 lei, partly for political advertising outside the campaign in favour of a Bucharest mayoral candidate and partly for misreporting a defence minister's statement. It also fined România TV and Nașul TV 50,000 lei each. source
  • IPI Media Capture Monitoring Report 2025: Parliament nominates 6 of the CNA's 11 members, in October 2024 it appointed a former PSD senator among others, and the report rates the regulator's effective independence as absent. source
