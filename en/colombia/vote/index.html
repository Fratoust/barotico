Voting in Colombia

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Vote where your cédula is registered; to vote somewhere else, register the cédula with the Registraduría before the deadline (registration for the 2027 regional vote opened on 31 October 2026). Bring your cédula on the day. Colombians abroad register at a consulate and can vote there in national elections.

Last turnout: 63.59% in the June 2026 presidential run-off, a record for a Colombian run-off (historically 50-55%) source

The electoral system

  • The President must win more than half of the vote in the first round; otherwise the top two face each other three weeks later.
  • Congress was elected on 8 March 2026: the Senate in a single national constituency, with a 3% threshold, and the House by territorial and special constituencies.

Sources: Constitution, Art. 171, 176, 190, 263 (Judiciary) · Senate: the 8 March 2026 election

Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Colombia, the blank vote is a box on the ballot and a valid vote. If it wins the first round, the election is held again with other candidates.

What it is

  • Blank vote: a dedicated box on the ballot, counted separately and added to no candidate. source
  • Unlike France or Italy, it is a valid vote: it counts in the total of valid votes. source

How it is counted

  • If blank votes are a majority of valid votes, the election is held again once: in the first presidential round, and for governors, mayors and elected bodies. In single-seat races the same candidates cannot run again; lists below the threshold cannot either. source
  • In the presidential run-off the box is still there, but it is symbolic: there is no rerun, the candidate with the most votes wins. source

The figures

  • 1.71%of valid votes went blank in the first presidential round of 31 May 2026: 406,830 votes, more than eight candidates each received. source
  • 1.02%of votes were spoilt in that round (245,339). source

What it changes for the result

  • Here, a blank vote can do more than send a message: if it wins a majority, it removes every candidate from the rerun. source
  • Below a majority, it changes no winner: in the run-off and in most races, the person with the most votes is elected. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • First round or run-off? In the first round, a majority of blank votes forces a new election with other candidates; in the run-off, the blank box is symbolic.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis? Below a majority of blank votes, the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.

Barotico does not recommend voting blank, nor avoiding it. These questions are there to help you decide with the facts in hand.

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • ItalyBlank ballots do not count as valid votes, but in abrogative referendums they count towards the turnout quorum: voting blank helps reach it, abstaining helps defeat it. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. It cannot cancel an election: the candidate with the most votes still wins. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.
