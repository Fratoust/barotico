Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in Colombia?

Who's at the wheel

President Abelardo de la Espriella since 7 August 2026, sworn in in Cali, with Vice-President José Manuel Restrepo, Finance Minister Miguel Gómez Martínez and Interior Minister Rodrigo Lara.

De la Espriella came first in the 31 May first round (43.77% in the preliminary count) and beat Iván Cepeda (Pacto Histórico) in the runoff, 49.66% to 48.70%. He has no party of his own in Congress: the Conservative Party, Centro Democrático, La U, Salvación Nacional, Cambio Radical and the Liberal Party declared themselves government parties; the Pacto Histórico leads the opposition; Alianza Verde, MIRA, Dignidad y Compromiso and Nuevo Liberalismo are independent. The 2027 budget was passed on 20 October 2026; in the Senate, a motion to shelve it was rejected by 56 votes to 28.

Sources: Infobae: inauguration and cabinet, Cali, 7 August 2026 · Portafolio: first-round results (31 May 2026) · Forbes Colombia: second-round results · El Universal: parties for and against the government · Senate: 2027 budget approved (20 October 2026)

What the government promised

  • 2027 budget (634.95 trillion pesos), approved on 20 October 2026: every entity must cut at least 3%, and the government has 90 days to send a fiscal-adjustment ('rescue') law
  • Fiscal 'rescue' bill filed on 29 October 2026: corporate income tax from 35% to 33% in 2027 and 31% from 2028, wealth tax abolished from 2031, a 20% cut in state payroll by 2028 and a 50% cut in service contracts in 2027
  • Inauguration pledges (Aug. 2026): a decree cutting state spending by 1%, 'responsible' fracking and a stronger Ecopetrol

What independent promise trackers found

  • La República (April 2026), Petro's four years: kept, no fracking and no new oil-exploration contracts, the labour and 2022 tax reforms; half-done, land reform (712,205 hectares acquired) and renewables (1.9 GW connected of 6 GW promised); unmet, 'total peace' and informality. source
  • Portafolio (July 2026): Congress passed Petro's tax (2022), total-peace, pension, labour and higher-education reforms and the agrarian jurisdiction; it archived the health reform, two financing laws and the political reform. No independent tracker yet covers De la Espriella's promises. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • Fiscal Rule Committee (CARF, April 2026): primary deficit expected at 3.7% of GDP in 2026 against the 2.1% announced, public debt at 60.3% of GDP; it called the official strategy 'not credible'. source
  • DANE: monetary poverty fell from 31.8% in 2024 to 28.0% in 2025 (1.8 million fewer poor people); extreme poverty from 11.7% to 9.6%. source
  • DANE: consumer prices up 6.29% over twelve months in September 2026, the highest since July 2024; the central bank's target is 3%. source
  • DANE: unemployment 8.0% in September 2026, down from 8.2% a year earlier, with 24.25 million people employed (322,000 more). source
