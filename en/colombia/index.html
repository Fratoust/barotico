Who decides what

Nation: President and CongressThe President is head of state and government for four years and cannot be re-elected. Congress has two chambers: for 2026-2030, 103 senators (102 elected, including 2 indigenous seats, plus 1 seat for the runner-up in the presidential race) and 183 representatives, including 16 peace seats for victims of the conflict.
High courts and the JEPThe Supreme Court investigates and tries members of Congress; the Constitutional Court reviews laws and protects rights (tutela); the Council of State can strip a member of Congress of their seat. The JEP, created by the 2016 peace agreement, judges the most serious crimes of the armed conflict.
Control and electoral bodiesThe Procuraduría disciplines officials, the Contraloría audits public money, the Fiscalía prosecutes. The Registraduría runs elections and the National Electoral Council (CNE) oversees parties and campaign money.
DepartmentsEach has a governor and an assembly elected for four years.
MunicipalitiesEach has a mayor and a council elected for four years; mayors cannot be immediately re-elected.

Sources: Constitution, Art. 86, 171, 176, 184, 190, 197, 235, 241, 250, 263-267, 277, 299, 303, 312-314 (Judiciary) · RCN Radio: the 2026-2030 Congress · EU Asylum Agency: the JEP

How a law is passed

  1. Bills can come from the government, members of Congress, the high courts, the control bodies or citizens (5% of the electoral roll).
  2. A bill must pass a committee debate and a plenary debate in each chamber.
  3. The President signs it or objects to it; Congress can insist with an absolute majority, and objections on constitutional grounds go to the Constitutional Court.
  4. The Constitutional Court reviews laws: in August 2026 it upheld most of the pension reform but sent several articles back to Congress.

Sources: Constitution, Art. 155, 157, 165-167, 241 (Judiciary) · RCN: Constitutional Court ruling on the pension reform (25 August 2026)

Political and economic context

Colombia went from its first left-wing government (Gustavo Petro, 2022-2026) to Abelardo de la Espriella, a right-wing lawyer elected outside the parties, who won the 21 June 2026 runoff with 49.66% against Iván Cepeda (48.70%). Petro got a tax reform, a labour reform and a pension reform through Congress, but not his health reform. Medicina Legal counted 14,780 homicides in 2025, the highest figure in a decade. Congress is fragmented: the Pacto Histórico is the largest party in both chambers but in opposition, while six main parties have declared themselves government parties.

Sources: Forbes Colombia: second-round results · Portafolio: Petro's reforms passed and shelved · El Colombiano: 14,780 homicides in 2025 (Medicina Legal) · RCN Radio: seats by party, 2026-2030 · El Universal: government, opposition and independent parties (8 September 2026)

Why it's like thisThe 1991 Constitution was drafted by a Constituent Assembly elected in December 1990, after the demobilisation of the M-19 guerrilla and amid drug-trafficking violence. It created the tutela, which lets anyone ask a judge for urgent protection of a right, and the Constitutional Court. Presidential re-election is barred. The 2016 peace agreement with the FARC added the JEP and temporary peace seats in the House for victims.

Sources: Semana: how the 1991 Constitution was born · Constitution, Art. 86 and 197 (Judiciary) · EU Asylum Agency: the JEP and the 2016 agreement · RCN Radio: 16 peace seats (CITREP)
