Colombia

Unitary presidential republic · about 53 million people

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Vote where your cédula is registered; to vote somewhere else, register the cédula with the Registraduría before the deadline (registration for the 2027 regional vote opened on 31 October 2026). Bring your cédula on the day. Colombians abroad register at a consulate and can vote there in national elections.

Last turnout: 63.59% in the June 2026 presidential run-off, a record for a Colombian run-off (historically 50-55%) source

Who decides what

Nation: President and CongressThe President is head of state and government for four years, with no re-election. Congress has two chambers: for 2026-2030, 103 senators (100 elected nationally, 2 indigenous seats and 1 seat for the presidential runner-up) and 183 representatives, including 16 peace seats for conflict victims until 2030.
High courts and the JEPThe Supreme Court investigates and tries members of Congress; the Constitutional Court reviews laws and protects rights (tutela); the Council of State judges the administration and can strip a member of Congress of the seat. The JEP, born of the 2016 peace agreement, judges crimes of the armed conflict.
Control and electoral bodiesThe Procuraduría disciplines officials, the Contraloría audits public money, the Fiscalía prosecutes. The Registraduría runs elections and the National Electoral Council (CNE) oversees parties and campaign money.
32 departmentsEach has an elected governor and assembly; they coordinate health, education and roads with the municipalities.
MunicipalitiesMore than 1,100 municipalities with elected mayors and councils; Bogotá is a capital district.

How people vote

  • Voting is voluntary from 18. You vote where your cédula (ID card) is registered; the Registraduría publishes polling places before each election.
  • The President needs more than half of the valid votes in the first round; otherwise the top two meet in a run-off three weeks later.
  • Congress is elected every four years in March: the Senate in one national constituency, the Chamber by department, with closed or preferential lists and a 3% threshold for the Senate.

How a law is passed

  1. Bills can come from the government, members of Congress, the high courts, control bodies or citizens with signatures.
  2. An ordinary law needs four debates: committee and plenary in the Senate and in the Chamber; differing texts go to conciliation.
  3. The President signs it or objects to it; Congress can insist with an absolute majority, and objections on constitutional grounds go to the Constitutional Court.
  4. Statutory laws and constitutional amendments are reviewed by the Constitutional Court, which can send flawed articles back to Congress, as it did with the pension reform in 2026.

Why it's complicated

Colombia swung from its first left-wing government (Gustavo Petro, 2022-2026) to Abelardo de la Espriella, a right-wing lawyer elected outside the parties, who won the 21 June 2026 run-off with 49.66% against Petro's heir Iván Cepeda (48.70%). Petro passed a tax, a labour and a pension reform but lost his health reform twice in the Senate; poverty fell to 28% in 2025, yet the fiscal council calls public finances its main worry and Medicina Legal counted 14,780 homicides in 2025, the most in a decade. Congress is fragmented: the Pacto Histórico is the largest party in both chambers but in opposition, while seven parties declared themselves pro-government. Courts weigh on both camps: two of Petro's ministers and the former heads of both chambers are on trial over bribes paid with disaster-relief contracts, a Centro Democrático senator was convicted at first instance in 2026, and former President Álvaro Uribe's acquittal is before the Supreme Court on cassation.

Why it's like thisThe 1991 Constitution was written by an elected assembly after the peace deal with the M-19 guerrilla, in a country scarred by drug-cartel violence. It created the Constitutional Court, the tutela that lets anyone seek urgent protection of a right, and strong control bodies, to open a system long shared by Liberals and Conservatives. A 2015 reform banned presidential re-election. The 2016 peace agreement with the FARC added the JEP and temporary seats in Congress for victims of the conflict.

Who's at the wheel

President Abelardo de la Espriella since 7 August 2026, sworn in in Cali, with Vice-President José Manuel Restrepo, Finance Minister Miguel Gómez Martínez and Interior Minister Rodrigo Lara (a former director of Cambio Radical).

De la Espriella came first in the 31 May first round (43.77% in the preliminary count) and won the run-off against Iván Cepeda (Pacto Histórico) by 49.66% to 48.70%. He has no party of his own in Congress: Centro Democrático, the Liberal, Conservative and La U parties, Cambio Radical and Salvación Nacional declared themselves government parties; the Pacto Histórico leads the opposition; Alianza Verde, MIRA, Dignidad y Compromiso and Nuevo Liberalismo are independent. The 2027 budget passed 57-1 in the Senate and 112-1 in the Chamber.
