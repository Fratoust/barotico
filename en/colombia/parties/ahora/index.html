Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterBogotá (Nuevo Liberalismo mayor Carlos Fernando Galán): 8 homicides per 100,000 by 2027
Half-wayThe city closed 2024 at 15 homicides per 100,000, and in April 2025 El Espectador found the trend moving away from the goal. Between 1 January and 8 June 2026 Bogotá recorded 468 homicides, against 518 in the same period of 2025, a 10% fall (Asocapitales). source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • Formed in June 2025 as a joint list for Congress, aiming to be 'a third option' that would 'break with those extremes'; its agreement left each party free to promote its own presidential candidate. source
  • Nuevo Liberalismo's Bogotá mayor Carlos Fernando Galán set a target of 8 homicides per 100,000 by 2027; the rate stood at 15 in 2024 and El Espectador found the trend moving away from the goal. source
  • In December 2025 its MIRA senator Ana Paola Agudelo was one of the 8 votes in the Senate's Seventh Committee that archived the Petro government's health reform, 8-5. source
To be fair
  • All three parties declared themselves independent of the De la Espriella government rather than trading support for posts
  • Sergio Fajardo made education the centre of his terms as mayor of Medellín and governor of Antioquia
  • Nuevo Liberalismo stayed neutral in the run-off, saying its commitment was to the 1991 Constitution and individual liberties
Court proceedings (official acts)

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Never tested Apply an ethical filter to its candidates
  • Never tested Act as an independent, transparent bloc in Congress
  • Never tested Unite popular sectors, the middle class and business around lawful development

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Ahora Colombia, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The coalition was formed to be 'a third option' that would 'break with those extremes'. It was built for Congress only, with each party free to back its own presidential candidate.

Its Bogotá mayor promised 8 homicides per 100,000 by 2027. The city closed 2024 at 15.

In the Senate, its MIRA senator was one of the 8 votes that archived Petro's health reform in December 2025.

To be fair, Bogotá recorded 468 homicides between January and early June 2026, 50 fewer than a year earlier (Asocapitales).

Polarizationnot measured yet

How often its leaders attack opponents personally or present them as enemies rather than rivals.
