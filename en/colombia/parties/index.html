Colombia

Unitary presidential republic · about 53 million people

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.

How to read this country profile

The profile has seven tabs. Start with the system to understand the rules of the game, then look at who governs and what the parties do. Every piece of information links to its source: click to check.

  1. How the country is run: institutions, elections, who passes the laws, and the political and economic context of the moment.
  2. Who is in power and how they got there, what they promised, what independent promise trackers say, and the measured effects of their action.
  3. One card per party, opened with a click: its place on the left-right axis, what it says set against what it does, its promises one by one and its democracy barometer.
  4. How to vote in practice, the electoral system, turnout, and what a blank vote changes.
  5. The country's indicators (life expectancy, schooling, homicides, press freedom, freedoms, corruption), each with its source.
  6. The country's main media, who owns them, and what to know about its news landscape.

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. The judicial record only lists cases with an official act (ruling, charge, sanction, expulsion), with the same caps for every party; relatives and non-members are left out. The number of cases partly reflects a party's size and years in power.

Party programme links are left empty except for Cambio Radical, the only party whose platform we could open on its own site. Other stated values come from statements reported by the national press. Promises marked 'untested' come from those statements, not from a verified programme.

DeliveredUnder its government, monetary poverty fell from 31.8% in 2024 to 28.0% in 2025, 1,790,909 fewer poor people, and extreme poverty from 11.7% to 9.6% (DANE). source
On the poster'Total peace' with all armed groups
Half-wayLa República (April 2026) lists total peace among the government's promises that were not achieved or stayed half-way. Medicina Legal recorded 14,780 homicides in 2025, the highest count of the decade, against 13,939 in 2022. source · 2
On the posterLeave oil and coal behind: an energy transition
Half-wayAs promised, it signed no new oil and gas exploration contracts and allowed no fracking. Of the 6 GW of renewable power it promised, 1.9 GW of solar were connected to the grid and none of 16 planned wind farms was operating (XM data cited by La República, April 2026). source
On the posterHealth as a right, not a business
Half-wayIts health-system reform was sunk twice in the Senate's Seventh Committee, the second time on 16 December 2025 by 8 votes to 5, with Conservative, Liberal, La U, Centro Democrático, MIRA and Christian-party senators voting to archive it. Its Interior Minister said its opponents had 'waited until the last day'. source
On the posterA pension reform with a state-funded old-age income for the poorest
Half-wayCongress passed it in 2024 (Law 2381). On 25 August 2026 the Constitutional Court upheld it 7-1 but sent nine articles back to the Chamber over procedural defects; it enters into force on 1 April 2027, after its government left office. source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • Promised 'total peace'; La República counts it among the unmet goals of its government, and Medicina Legal recorded 14,780 homicides in 2025, the highest in a decade. source
  • In June 2025 its government suspended the fiscal rule for three years, a move the Fiscal Rule Committee judged unwarranted, with a deficit projected at 7.1% of GDP. source
  • The National Electoral Council found that its 2022 presidential campaign exceeded the spending limit by more than 3.5 billion pesos and confirmed the sanctions in April 2026; court challenges remain possible. source
To be fair
  • Monetary poverty fell from 31.8% in 2024 to 28.0% in 2025, 1.8 million fewer poor people (DANE)
  • Passed the 2025 labour reform: night pay from 7 p.m., Sunday and holiday surcharge rising to 100%, contracts with health and pension cover for SENA apprentices
  • Its pension reform (Law 2381 of 2024), upheld 7-1 by the Constitutional Court in August 2026, creates a state-funded old-age income for poor elderly people from April 2027
