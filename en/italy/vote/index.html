Italy

Parliamentary republic · two equal chambers of 400 deputies and 200 senators

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
Next vote— General election (2027, date not yet set)
The legislature began on 13 October 2022 and expires in October 2027; the new chambers must be elected within 70 days. Spring 2027 (together with the municipal elections in Rome, Milan, Turin and Naples) and autumn 2027 are both being discussed, and the Prime Minister has named no date. It will be the first vote under the new 'Stabilicum' electoral law. source

Voting in Italy

Before the next vote

How to vote, in practice

Registration is automatic: vote in person at your polling station with an ID and the electoral card (tessera elettorale). Italians registered abroad (AIRE) vote by post. Under the new law, students and workers living in another region for at least 9 months can vote where they live: apply by 31 December for the following year's elections.

Last turnout: 63.9% in the September 2022 general election (Chamber), down from 73% in 2018 source

The electoral system

  • Citizens aged 18 and over elect both chambers: 400 deputies and 200 senators (cut from 945 by the 2020 constitutional referendum).
  • From the next election (Stabilicum, Oct 2026): proportional lists with a 3% threshold; a list or coalition with at least 42% gets a bonus of 70 deputies and 35 senators; the list leader is chosen by the party, then voters can give up to 3 preferences.
  • Italians living abroad vote by post in overseas constituencies (reduced to 2 for the Chamber and 1 for the Senate by the new law).
  • Referendums: abrogative ones need a turnout of 50% + 1 (missed in June 2025, about 30%); constitutional ones have no quorum (March 2026: 58.9% turnout).
Blank vote: what it says, what it changes

In Italy, a blank ballot (scheda bianca) counts among voters but not among valid votes. Its real weight shows in referendums.

What it is

  • Blank ballot: a ballot handed in with no mark. source
  • Spoilt ballot (scheda nulla): a ballot with invalid marks; it counts neither for yes nor for no in a referendum. source

How it is counted

  • Blank ballots count in turnout, because the voter went to the polls, but are excluded from the valid votes used to allocate seats: no effect on results. source
  • Abrogative referendums are valid only if more than half of voters take part. Blank and spoilt ballots count towards that quorum. source

The figures

  • 1.7%of Chamber voters cast a blank ballot in 2022, almost half a million; 2.8% cast a spoilt one (822,000). source
  • 4.2%the record for blank ballots in a general election, in 2001. source

What it changes for the result

  • In a referendum, a voter opposed to the question who votes blank helps reach the quorum; one who abstains helps make it fail. Blank and abstention do not have the same effect. source
  • In general elections, blank ballots weigh on turnout figures, not on seats. source

Questions to ask yourself

  • If no option represents me, do I want that refusal to be counted? Check above how it is counted here.
  • Do I reject every option, or is one of them less far from my ideas than the others?
  • Do I want to weigh on the result, or to make a refusal count?
  • Is the race close, is the country going through a crisis, could the election tip one way or the other? The closer it is, the more the outcome rests on the votes given to candidates.
  • Blank, spoilt or abstention: what does the law of this country do with each one?

Elsewhere, a blank vote has effects

  • FranceCounted separately and published since 2014, but outside the votes cast: no effect on the result or on thresholds. source
  • SpainBlank votes count as valid votes. They raise the number of votes a list needs to pass the 3% bar for seats. Only an empty envelope is blank: a blank sheet inside is spoilt. source source
  • ColombiaIf blank votes win a majority of valid votes (first presidential round, governors, mayors, assemblies), the election is held again once, without the same candidates. source
  • PeruIf spoilt and blank votes, together or separately, exceed two thirds of votes cast, the election is annulled. source
  • Nevada (United States)Since 1975, ballots offer “None of these candidates” in statewide and presidential races. It can come first, but it never wins: the candidate with the most votes is elected. source
  • IndiaSince a 2013 Supreme Court ruling, voting machines offer “None of the above”. Some states (Maharashtra, Haryana) rerun local elections when that option comes first. source

For lawmakers and citizens who want to change the rule: each model has a cost. Rerunning an election takes time and money, a low threshold can leave seats empty, and counting blank votes changes majorities and thresholds.
