Italy

Parliamentary republic · two equal chambers of 400 deputies and 200 senators

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote— General election (2027, date not yet set)
The legislature began on 13 October 2022 and expires in October 2027; the new chambers must be elected within 70 days. Spring 2027 (together with the municipal elections in Rome, Milan, Turin and Naples) and autumn 2027 are both being discussed, and the Prime Minister has named no date. It will be the first vote under the new 'Stabilicum' electoral law. source

Reading codes

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results

  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Who governs in Italy?

Who's at the wheel

Giorgia Meloni (Fratelli d'Italia), Prime Minister since 22 October 2022, leading a coalition of Fratelli d'Italia, Lega, Forza Italia and Noi Moderati.

The centre-right coalition won a majority of seats in both chambers in September 2022 with 44.5% of the vote, thanks to the single-member constituencies of the old law. In 2026 it lost the justice referendum, replaced the tourism minister (Gianmarco Mazzi, April 2026) and passed a new electoral law.

Sources: Sky TG24: the Meloni government sworn in (22 October 2022) · Pagella Politica: post-election guide, 2022 · Pagella Politica: seats won in single-member constituencies · Il Sole 24 Ore: Mazzi sworn in (3 April 2026) · Pagella Politica: the new electoral law · Chamber of Deputies: composition of the parliamentary groups (consulted 15 November 2026) · Ipsos: approval of the government, 31 October 2026

Who holds power

President of the Republic: Sergio Mattarella. Prime Minister (President of the Council of Ministers): Giorgia Meloni (FdI), since 22 October 2022.

In government: 231 absolute majority: 201

The Meloni government rests on a coalition of Fratelli d'Italia, Lega, Forza Italia and Noi Moderati, formed after the September 2022 election; with 231 of the 400 deputies listed, the four coalition groups hold a majority in the Chamber. Futuro Nazionale, the party Roberto Vannacci launched in 2026, sits as a component of the Mixed group and is shown here as not in government. No count of ministers by party was checked, so no cabinet chart is shown.

Seats by group from the Chamber's official group composition page (XIX legislature), consulted on 15 November 2026; the page gives no update date. The groups and Mixed-group components listed add up to 398 deputies; the 2 remaining seats (400 minus 398) are shown as vacant, a figure derived by us, not read on the page.

Groups are ordered by the left-right positions used on the party cards. Italia Viva and Azione share the same position; the Mixed-group components (25 deputies in all) have no card, and their place in the chart is indicative. Only the Chamber is shown; the Senate (200 elected senators plus senators for life) is not.

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • ISTAT: public deficit 3.1% of GDP in 2025 (3.4% in 2024), so Italy stays in the EU excessive-deficit procedure; GDP growth +0.6%. source
  • ISTAT: tax burden 42.9% of GDP in 2025, up 0.7 points on 2024. source
  • ISTAT: unemployment 5.7% and employment rate 62.9% in June 2026 (24.31 million in work). source
  • Corte dei conti (audit court), 29 Oct 2025: refused to register the €13.5 billion CIPESS resolution approving the Strait of Messina bridge. source
Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards cover the nine parties with a group of their own in the Chamber of Deputies, ordered by group size on the Chamber's composition page (consulted 15 November 2026); senators are given as elected in 2022, since current Senate group sizes were not checked. Futuro Nazionale (Roberto Vannacci's party, 9 deputies), +Europa, Sud chiama Nord and the linguistic minorities sit only as components of the Mixed group and did not reach 3% under their own name in 2022, so they have no card.

Left-right positions are approximate readings of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that were not re-checked; Noi Moderati is not in the survey, so its position is an estimate. Party finance is broken down only for Noi Moderati, from its own 2025 accounts; for the others only the 2x1000 tax allocation is given, because the audited accounts filed with Parliament were not read. A programme link is given only where one was found on an official site; Pagella Politica's promise tracker is the independent tally used for the government.

Press links come only from the validated outlets in the media panel: ANSA (its dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as ANSA), Pagella Politica, Sky TG24, Il Sole 24 Ore and the regional press. The RSF and Transparency International figures are taken from national reports on those indices. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, referral, ruling, parliamentary vote); acquittals older than twelve months and private matters are left out.
