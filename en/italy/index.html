Italy

Parliamentary republic · two equal chambers of 400 deputies and 200 senators

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
Next vote— General election (2027, date not yet set)
The legislature began on 13 October 2022 and expires in October 2027; the new chambers must be elected within 70 days. Spring 2027 (together with the municipal elections in Rome, Milan, Turin and Naples) and autumn 2027 are both being discussed, and the Prime Minister has named no date. It will be the first vote under the new 'Stabilicum' electoral law. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

68 governments since 1946, lasting 431 days on average.

In September 2026 the Meloni government became the longest-lasting in the Republic's history (1,413 days), beating Berlusconi II by one day. In Italy, simply staying in office is a record. Source: Il Post, 4 Sept 2026.

Who decides what

StateParliament (Chamber of Deputies and Senate, with equal powers) votes laws and confidence; the government is led by the President of the Council of Ministers; the President of the Republic, elected by Parliament for 7 years, appoints the PM and can dissolve the chambers.
Regions20 regions (5 with special statutes) run health care, regional transport and much planning, with elected presidents and councils.
Provinces and metropolitan citiesIntermediate bodies (roads, secondary-school buildings), mostly elected indirectly by local councillors.
MunicipalitiesNearly 8,000 comuni with directly elected mayors; in towns above 15,000 inhabitants a runoff is held if nobody wins outright.
European UnionItaly elects 76 members of the European Parliament; EU law and the EU deficit rules weigh on the national budget.

How a law is passed

  1. A bill is tabled by the government, MPs, regions, the CNEL or 50,000 citizens.
  2. Both chambers must pass the identical text; any change sends it back to the other chamber (the 'navetta'). The government often uses decree-laws, which must be converted by Parliament within 60 days, and confidence votes.
  3. The President of the Republic promulgates the law and can send it back once; it is then published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale.
  4. The Constitutional Court can strike it down; 500,000 voters can ask for a referendum to repeal it.

Why it's complicated

In March 2026 voters rejected the government's justice reform (No 54.6%). An undersecretary and the tourism minister resigned in the following days. Roberto Vannacci's new party Futuro Nazionale then drew deputies away from the Lega and Forza Italia. A new electoral law passed in October 2026 by secret ballot, the opposition protested, and the election date is still open. Italy also remains in the EU excessive-deficit procedure (deficit 3.1% of GDP in 2025).

Why it's like thisThe 1948 Constitution was written after Fascism. Its authors wanted no single power to dominate: two chambers with identical powers, a government that depends on Parliament, an independent judiciary governed by its own council (CSM) and a Constitutional Court. Every big attempt to concentrate power or reshape that balance through a confirmatory referendum has failed: in 2006, in 2016 and in 2026.

Trust in news fell to 32% in 2026, 4 points down on 2025 and below the 37% global average (Reuters Institute Digital News Report). ANSA is the most trusted brand for the ninth year running (74%). source

Who owns what

  • RAI: public broadcaster; its governance has not changed and the appointment of its chair has been blocked since 2024.
  • Fininvest (Berlusconi heirs): keeps a very significant role in the national media through its TV holdings.
  • Antenna Group (Greece): bought the GEDI group; La Stampa went to the SAE group.
  • LMDV Capital: entered the capital of the daily Il Giornale.

source

Documented facts to know

  • EU Commission Rule of Law report 2026: concerns about RAI's independent functioning and financing, and no progress on defamation reform or on protecting journalists' sources; investigations into the Paragon spyware used against journalists are ongoing. source
  • AGCOM decision 39/26/CONS (Feb 2026): dismissed a complaint by the referendum Yes committee against RAI's investigative programme Report, finding no breach of pluralism. source
  • Media Pluralism Monitor (EUI): overall risk 51% (medium-high); market plurality at high risk (69%). source
  • RSF: Italy fell from 41st (2023) to 56th (2026) in the World Press Freedom Index. source
