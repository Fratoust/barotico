Italy

Parliamentary republic · two equal chambers of 400 deputies and 200 senators

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote— General election (2027, date not yet set)
The legislature began on 13 October 2022 and expires in October 2027; the new chambers must be elected within 70 days. Spring 2027 (together with the municipal elections in Rome, Milan, Turin and Naples) and autumn 2027 are both being discussed, and the Prime Minister has named no date. It will be the first vote under the new 'Stabilicum' electoral law. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

How to read the profiles

Positions on the left-right axis come from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (or the closest expert survey where it does not cover the country). Profiles are ordered by number of seats. On the posterOn the record
On the posterA safety net against poverty
⚠ Rights alertIn government with the Lega it created the citizens' income (2019); with three other schemes it kept about one million people out of absolute poverty in 2020 (ISTAT). Its ten-year residence requirement was ruled indirect discrimination by the EU Court of Justice (29 July 2024 for long-term residents, 7 May 2026 for refugees), and the Constitutional Court found it contrary to equality and cut it to five years (31/2025, 20 March 2025). source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterFight corruption and politicians' privileges
Half-wayIts 2019 'Spazzacorrotti' law stopped the statute of limitations after a first-instance verdict; in August 2021, in the Draghi government, it voted for the Cartabia reform (passed 396 to 57), which sets time limits on appeal trials, after obtaining unlimited extensions for mafia and terrorism cases. source
On the posterEcological transition
ContradictionIt campaigned in 2018 to stop the TAP gas pipeline in Puglia; on 26 October 2018 the Conte government (M5S-Lega) let it go ahead, Di Maio citing penalties of almost €20 billion that 'we did not know about'. Pagella Politica later found it was wrong to speak of actual penalties: compensation claims had been foreseeable since the project's approval in 2015, before the campaign. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterLegality and security (its 2018 government contract with the Lega)
⚠ Rights alertThe Conte I government (M5S-Lega) adopted the 2018 security decree, passed by the Chamber 396 to 99 with M5S votes, 14 of its deputies not voting; it barred asylum seekers from municipal residence registers. On 31 July 2020 the Constitutional Court (186/2020) struck the ban down for breaching equal social dignity and equality before the law (art. 3 of the Constitution). The M5S's Giuseppe Brescia then said the party had warned the Lega, accusing it of 'mistaking laws for adverts'. source · 2 · 3 · 4
Also worth knowing
  • The two-term limit for elected members was a hallmark rule; at the constituent assembly in November 2024 members voted to change it. source
  • Conte said the Superbonus building subsidy, launched by his government, added no debt; Pagella Politica rated that misleading: about €71 billion of costs by January 2023, roughly 3% of GDP. source
  • It campaigned in 2018 to stop the TAP gas pipeline in Puglia; in October 2018 the Conte government let it go ahead, Di Maio citing almost €20 billion of penalties 'we did not know about'. Pagella Politica later found it was wrong to speak of actual penalties: compensation claims had been foreseeable since the project's 2015 approval. source
To be fair
  • Introduced the citizens' income (reddito di cittadinanza), Italy's first national minimum-income scheme (2019).
  • Promoted the cut in the number of MPs from 945 to 600, approved by referendum in 2020.
  • Passed the 2019 'Spazzacorrotti' anti-corruption law.

Some kept, some stretched.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept, made a difference A safety net against poverty, the reddito di cittadinanza (2019, defended in 2022): in 2020 it and other support schemes kept about one million people out of absolute poverty (ISTAT); replaced by the Meloni government from 2024 source · measured effect
  • Kept on paper Cut politicians' costs by reducing MPs from 945 to 600 (approved by referendum in 2020): net saving about €57 million a year, 0.007% of public spending (Osservatorio CPI) source
  • Partly kept A €9-an-hour statutory minimum wage: its joint opposition bill was turned by the majority into a delegation law (September 2025) that no longer mentions a minimum wage source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the M5S, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It dropped its two-term limit. It campaigned to stop the TAP pipeline; in government, it let it through, citing penalties the fact-checkers could not find.

It says the Superbonus added no debt. Pagella Politica counts about €71 billion of costs.

Its cut of 345 MPs saves about €57 million a year, or 0.007% of public spending. Its €9 minimum wage became a law without one.

To be fair, ISTAT estimated that in 2020 its citizens' income, with other support schemes, kept about one million people out of absolute poverty.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards cover the nine parties with a group of their own in the Chamber of Deputies, ordered by group size on the Chamber's composition page (consulted 15 November 2026); senators are given as elected in 2022, since current Senate group sizes were not checked. Futuro Nazionale (Roberto Vannacci's party, 9 deputies), +Europa, Sud chiama Nord and the linguistic minorities sit only as components of the Mixed group and did not reach 3% under their own name in 2022, so they have no card.

Left-right positions are approximate readings of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that were not re-checked; Noi Moderati is not in the survey, so its position is an estimate. Party finance is broken down only for Noi Moderati, from its own 2025 accounts; for the others only the 2x1000 tax allocation is given, because the audited accounts filed with Parliament were not read. A programme link is given only where one was found on an official site; Pagella Politica's promise tracker is the independent tally used for the government.

Press links come only from the validated outlets in the media panel: ANSA (its dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as ANSA), Pagella Politica, Sky TG24, Il Sole 24 Ore and the regional press. The RSF and Transparency International figures are taken from national reports on those indices. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, referral, ruling, parliamentary vote); acquittals older than twelve months and private matters are left out.
