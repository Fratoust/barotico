Italy

Parliamentary republic · two equal chambers of 400 deputies and 200 senators

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
Next vote— General election (2027, date not yet set)
The legislature began on 13 October 2022 and expires in October 2027; the new chambers must be elected within 70 days. Spring 2027 (together with the municipal elections in Rome, Milan, Turin and Naples) and autumn 2027 are both being discussed, and the Prime Minister has named no date. It will be the first vote under the new 'Stabilicum' electoral law. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterA statutory minimum wage
Half-wayIn government, its Jobs Act delegation law (December 2014) allowed a statutory hourly minimum wage; the implementing decrees left it out. In opposition it co-filed a €9 bill in 2023, which the majority turned into a delegation law with no minimum wage (2025). source
On the posterDefend and fund the public health service
DeliveredIn Emilia-Romagna, the regional government of Michele de Pascale (PD) raised the regional income-tax surcharge, Irap and the car tax in its 2025-27 budget, about €400 million a year once in force, rather than cut €250 million from health; an income of €60,000 pays about €340 more a year. source
On the posterEqual rights and citizenship for children of immigrants
Half-wayThe ius soli bill passed the Chamber in 2015 under its government. In the Senate its coalition ally Alternativa Popolare refused to back it and said its ministers would vote no on a confidence vote (September 2017); on 23 December 2017 it fell for lack of quorum, missed by 33 senators, with 29 of the PD's 89 absent and AP, M5S, Lega and almost all of FI absent too. source · 2 · 3
On the posterNo to cutting the number of MPs (voted against in the first three readings, 2019)
ContradictionOn 8 October 2019, a month after forming the Conte II government with the M5S, it voted for the cut (553 in favour); Serracchiani said the yes had been 'put in the political agreement to create this government'. Its stated reason was a package of correctives agreed in return, an electoral reform by December and three constitutional changes; by August 2020 PD deputy Matteo Orfini said 'those commitments were not realised'. source · 2 · 3
On the posterStable jobs and safe work (Renzi government, 2014-2016)
⚠ Rights alertTwo measures of its Renzi government were struck down. The Jobs Act fixed unfair-dismissal compensation at two months' pay per year of service: the Constitutional Court ruled this breached equality and the right to work (194/2018, 8 November 2018; arts. 3, 4 and 35 of the Constitution), and the Council of Europe's European Committee of Social Rights found its caps contrary to the right to protection against unfair dismissal (11 September 2019). A July 2015 decree let a seized Ilva blast furnace in Taranto keep running on a safety plan written by the company alone: struck down for sacrificing the rights to health, life and a safe workplace (58/2018, 23 March 2018). Under Elly Schlein, the PD backed the 2025 referendum to repeal the Jobs Act's dismissal rules. source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6
On the posterEqual rights for immigrant families
⚠ Rights alertIts Renzi government's 2015 baby bonus excluded non-EU workers without a long-term permit, a rule renewed by the next three governments; the EU Court of Justice (2 September 2021) and the Constitutional Court (54/2022, 4 March 2022) found this breached equal access to social security and the protection of motherhood. In regions it ran, residence-length rules were struck down for unequal treatment (art. 3): Tuscany's aid for disabled children (42/2024) and housing points (1/2026, 8 January 2026), and Piedmont's housing access (147/2024). source · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5
Also worth knowing
  • In June 2025 the PD officially backed repealing key parts of the Jobs Act, the labour reform passed under Matteo Renzi's government when he led the PD; Schlein, who had opposed the law from the start, called it 'a battle for the dignity of work', while Guerini, Madia, Gori and Picierno announced No votes on the three Jobs Act questions. source
  • The PD voted three times against cutting the number of MPs, then for it on 8 October 2019, once the cut had become one of the M5S's main conditions for the Conte II government; the correctives it asked in return, starting with a new electoral law, had not passed by the 2020 referendum. source
  • The Italy-Libya migration memorandum was signed in 2017 by the PD-led Gentiloni government; in October 2025 the PD co-signed an opposition motion on it, with AVS, Italia Viva and +Europa, which the Chamber rejected while approving the government's motion on the renewal. source
To be fair
  • Largest party of the opposition and first for taxpayers' 2x1000 allocations (about €10.5 million in 2025).
  • Campaigned for the No side, which won the March 2026 justice referendum.
  • Its delegation of 21 MEPs is one of the largest in the Socialists and Democrats group.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Partly kept A €9-an-hour statutory minimum wage: its joint opposition bill (2023) was turned by the majority into a delegation law, passed in September 2025, that no longer mentions a minimum wage source
  • Partly kept Easier citizenship for children of immigrants: in opposition it backed the June 2025 referendum to halve the residence requirement, which failed with about 30% turnout source
  • Never tested More money for the public health service (2022 programme): in opposition since 2022, untested

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for the PD, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It voted three times against cutting the number of MPs. Then it voted for it, as the price of a government with the M5S.

The Libya migration deal it signed in 2017 is, it now says, a failure.

From opposition, its €9 minimum wage became a law without a minimum wage. The citizenship referendum it backed missed the quorum.

To be fair, a Bank of Italy study found that the Jobs Act it now wants to undo had a significant effect on job growth, through both its new contract and its hiring tax relief.

Method and missing data What is missing from this page, why, and how the choices were made.

Party cards cover the nine parties with a group of their own in the Chamber of Deputies, ordered by group size on the Chamber's composition page (consulted 15 November 2026); senators are given as elected in 2022, since current Senate group sizes were not checked. Futuro Nazionale (Roberto Vannacci's party, 9 deputies), +Europa, Sud chiama Nord and the linguistic minorities sit only as components of the Mixed group and did not reach 3% under their own name in 2022, so they have no card.

Left-right positions are approximate readings of the Chapel Hill Expert Survey that were not re-checked; Noi Moderati is not in the survey, so its position is an estimate. Party finance is broken down only for Noi Moderati, from its own 2025 accounts; for the others only the 2x1000 tax allocation is given, because the audited accounts filed with Parliament were not read. A programme link is given only where one was found on an official site; Pagella Politica's promise tracker is the independent tally used for the government.

Press links come only from the validated outlets in the media panel: ANSA (its dispatches republished elsewhere with a visible credit count as ANSA), Pagella Politica, Sky TG24, Il Sole 24 Ore and the regional press. The RSF and Transparency International figures are taken from national reports on those indices. Cases are listed only with an official act (charge, referral, ruling, parliamentary vote); acquittals older than twelve months and private matters are left out.
