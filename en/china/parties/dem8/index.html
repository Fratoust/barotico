China

One-party socialist republic led by the Chinese Communist Party · 1.405 billion people (end of 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
no free national vote≈ 11 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national step is the Communist Party's 21st National Congress, to be held in Beijing in the second half of 2027
No national office is filled by a free, competitive vote: Freedom House scores China 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework. Power is allocated inside the Party: its National Congress meets every five years (the 20th was held in October 2022) and elects the Central Committee, which elects the General Secretary. On 29 October 2026 the Central Committee decided that the 21st Congress will meet in Beijing in the second half of 2027; analysts expect Xi Jinping to seek a fourth five-year term there. No exact date has been set, so the countdown uses mid-October 2027 as an estimate. Before that, the National People's Congress holds its annual session in March 2027. Citizens vote directly only for county and township people's congresses, whose new round of elections is being prepared in 2026-2027. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

DeliveredThe parties took on counties in Bijie one by one from 1989 (the Democratic League first, the Taiwan League in 2005). In 1987, 3.15 million people there, 56% of the population, lacked enough food and clothing; the united front ran 3,773 projects worth 2.461 billion yuan, and Bijie completed its poverty-alleviation drive on schedule in 2020 (official figures; no independent measure for Bijie found). source · 2
Also worth knowing
  • Called 'democratic parties', yet the State Council's 2021 white paper states that they are 'not opposition parties' and that they 'voluntarily accept' the leadership of the Communist Party. source
  • Their mission includes 'democratic supervision'; at a December 2017 forum their leaders endorsed the Communist Party's proposal to amend the Constitution, which removed the presidential term limit and passed 2,958 to 2. source
  • Six of their chairs sat as vice-chairs of the NPC Standing Committee elected in 2018, the body that passed Hong Kong's National Security Law unanimously, 162 to 0, in June 2020. source
To be fair
  • From 1989 (the Democratic League first) the parties each took on a county in Bijie (Guizhou), where 3.15 million people lacked enough food and clothing in 1987; the united front ran 3,773 projects worth 2.461 billion yuan, and Bijie completed its poverty-alleviation tasks in 2020
  • A route into office for non-Communists: 14 hold top posts in the Supreme Court, the Supreme Procuratorate and State Council ministries, and 29 are vice-governors or deputy mayors of provinces (2021 white paper, with non-party figures)
  • Over 152,000 non-Communist deputies at all levels of people's congresses and over 410,000 CPPCC seats nationwide, together with non-party figures (2021 white paper)

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're wondering what these parties have delivered, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

The eight parties have about 1.3 million members. Their job description includes 'democratic supervision' of a party they 'voluntarily accept' as leader.

From 2016 to 2020 they supervised the anti-poverty drive in eight provinces: more than 2,400 suggestions and 80 reports. On the Constitution, their leaders supported lifting the presidential term limit.

Their chairs sat on the Standing Committee that passed Hong Kong's security law 162 to 0.

To be fair, their partner counties in Bijie, where 3.15 million people lacked basic food and clothing in 1987, completed the poverty drive in 2020.
