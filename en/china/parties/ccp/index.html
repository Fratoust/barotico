China

One-party socialist republic led by the Chinese Communist Party · 1.405 billion people (end of 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check.
no free national vote≈ 11 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national step is the Communist Party's 21st National Congress, to be held in Beijing in the second half of 2027
No national office is filled by a free, competitive vote: Freedom House scores China 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework. Power is allocated inside the Party: its National Congress meets every five years (the 20th was held in October 2022) and elects the Central Committee, which elects the General Secretary. On 29 October 2026 the Central Committee decided that the 21st Congress will meet in Beijing in the second half of 2027; analysts expect Xi Jinping to seek a fourth five-year term there. No exact date has been set, so the countdown uses mid-October 2027 as an estimate. Before that, the National People's Congress holds its annual session in March 2027. Citizens vote directly only for county and township people's congresses, whose new round of elections is being prepared in 2026-2027. source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

Leaders

Founded
1921 · 105 years old Long-established partysource
In power
Has led the national government

Not placed on the left-right axisIn a single-party system with no competitive election, expert surveys do not place parties on a left-right scale. The Party's doctrine (Marxism-Leninism, 'socialism with Chinese characteristics') and its economic record (markets, a large private sector) point in different directions, so we give it no position and no 'far left' or 'far right' label.

On the posterOn the record
On the posterLift every rural resident out of absolute poverty by 2020 (13th Five-Year Plan)
DeliveredBy the end of 2020 the government declared all 98.99 million rural poor under the national standard out of poverty, and all 832 poor counties and 128,000 poor villages taken off the list (State Council white paper, April 2021). The World Bank (2022) counts close to 800 million fewer people in China below $1.90 a day over 40 years, about three-quarters of the global fall in extreme poverty. source · 2
On the posterCut CO2 emissions per unit of GDP by 18% over 2021-2025 (14th Five-Year Plan)
Half-wayThe official figure is a 17.7% cut (People's Daily, October 2026), under a scope that changed without an official announcement. Under the previous scope, Carbon Brief calculates 12.4%, and finds about 380 million tonnes of CO2 that the change of scope does not explain (May 2026). Over the same period non-fossil energy rose from 16.0% to 21.7% of consumption. source · 2
On the posterAllow a third child and markedly lower the cost of having and raising children, for a 'moderately' higher fertility level by 2025 (2021 decision)
Half-wayThe three-child policy was adopted in 2021, and a national childcare subsidy of 3,600 yuan a year per child under three, with about 90 billion yuan of central funds in its first year, applies from 2025. Births went from 10.62 million in 2021 to 7.92 million in 2025, when the population fell by 3.39 million (National Bureau of Statistics). source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the posterEquality and unity of all ethnic groups; in Xinjiang, education and training to remove the 'soil' of terrorism and religious extremism
⚠ Rights alertUyghurs and other Muslims in Xinjiang were held in what the authorities call vocational education and training centres. In August 2018 the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination asked China to halt detention without lawful charge, trial and conviction and to release those held. On 3 March 2023 the UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights found that the centres system 'amounts to large-scale arbitrary deprivation of liberty', linked it to forced labour and asked China to end it. China's foreign ministry called the UN human rights office's 2022 Xinjiang assessment 'completely illegal and invalid'. source · 2 · 3 · 4
On the poster'One country, two systems' and a 'high degree of autonomy' for Hong Kong
⚠ Rights alertOn 30 June 2020 the NPC Standing Committee passed Hong Kong's National Security Law unanimously. On 22 July 2022 the UN Human Rights Committee found that the law 'overrides' Covenant rights, has 'unduly restricted' them and 'substantially undermine[s]' judicial independence, and asked for its repeal; it also found the electoral system, which 'further deteriorated' after the 2021 changes, out of line with the right to vote (Article 25). China's mission to the EU said the committee had shown 'ideological bias'. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing
  • In 2018 the People's Daily explained that removing the two-term limit for the President 'does not mean life tenure'; the amendment passed 2,958 to 2, and in 2023 Xi Jinping began a third term, elected 2,952 to 0. source
  • Promised Hong Kong a 'high degree of autonomy' under 'one country, two systems'; in 2020 the NPC Standing Committee itself wrote Hong Kong's National Security Law and passed it unanimously, with 162 members present. In 2022 the UN Human Rights Committee found that the law 'overrides' rights protected by the Covenant and asked for its repeal; China's mission to the EU said the committee had shown 'ideological bias'. source
  • The Constitution proclaims the equality and unity of all ethnic groups; in 2022 the UN Human Rights Office found that the detention of Uyghurs and other Muslims in Xinjiang 'may constitute international crimes, in particular crimes against humanity'. China rejects the findings. source
To be fair
  • Close to 800 million fewer people living on under $1.90 a day over 40 years, about three-quarters of the world's reduction in extreme poverty (World Bank, 2022)
  • Life expectancy reached 79.25 years in 2025, 1.32 years more than in 2020 (National Health Commission)
  • Wind and solar capacity passed 1.2 billion kW in July 2024, about six and a half years ahead of schedule; solar has since overtaken coal as the largest power source (National Energy Administration; State Council white paper, 2026)
Court proceedings (official acts)

  • Convicted, 1st instance Wei Fenghe and Li Shangfu (defence ministers 2018-2023): convicted at first instance by a military court on 7 May 2026, Wei of taking bribes and Li of taking and giving bribes; both sentenced to death with a two-year reprieve, to be commuted to life without parole
  • Convicted, 1st instance Tang Renjian (Minister of Agriculture and Rural Affairs until 2024): convicted at first instance of taking bribes worth over 268 million yuan (Changchun court, 28 September 2025); death sentence with a two-year reprieve
  • Convicted, 1st instance Fu Zhenghua (Minister of Justice 2018-2020): convicted at first instance of taking bribes worth over 117 million yuan and of bending the law for personal gain (Changchun court, 22 September 2022); death sentence with a two-year reprieve, then life without parole; no appeal reported in the source

Who picks up the bill

Members pay dues of 0.5% to 2% of their monthly after-tax pay (0.5% to 1% for pensioners). With 101.3 million members at the end of 2025 this is a large income, but the Party publishes no audited accounts, no total of dues and no breakdown of other resources, and no outside body audits its finances.

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're wondering what this party has delivered, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It set itself an 18% cut in carbon intensity for 2021-2025 and reports 17.7%, under a revised scope. Under the old one, Carbon Brief calculates 12.4%.

It allowed a third child in 2021 and has paid 3,600 yuan a year per child under three since 2025. Births, meanwhile, went from 10.62 million in 2021 to 7.92 million in 2025.

It promised Hong Kong a high degree of autonomy, then wrote the city's security law itself. In 2022 a UN treaty body asked for the law's repeal; Beijing cited 'ideological bias'.

In 2023 another UN committee found that Xinjiang's training-centre system 'amounts to large-scale arbitrary deprivation of liberty'.

To be fair, close to 800 million fewer people live in extreme poverty than forty years ago, about three-quarters of the world's reduction (World Bank).
