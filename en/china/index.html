Country profile · 31 Oct 2026

China

One-party socialist republic led by the Chinese Communist Party · 1.405 billion people (end of 2025)

A giant summary, not the whole story

Barotico condenses complex realities into a few boxes. We try to be as neutral as possible, but politics is far richer than these profiles. Read the programmes, follow several media, talk about it, and make up your own mind. If Barotico makes you want to dig deeper, it has done its job.

no free national vote≈ 11 monthsNo free national vote: the next formal national step is the Communist Party's 21st National Congress, expected in late 2027
No national office is filled by a free, competitive vote: Freedom House scores China 0 out of 4 for the election of the head of government, of the legislature and for the electoral framework. Power is allocated inside the Party: its National Congress meets every five years (the 20th was held in October 2022) and elects the Central Committee, which elects the General Secretary. The 21st Congress is expected in late 2027, where analysts expect Xi Jinping to seek a fourth five-year term; no date has been announced, so the countdown uses mid-October 2027 as an estimate. Before that, the National People's Congress holds its annual session in March 2027. Citizens vote directly only for county and township people's congresses, whose new round of elections is being prepared in 2026-2027. source

How to vote, in practice

Citizens aged 18 and over are registered by local election committees where they live or where their household is registered; migrants are encouraged to take part where they reside. Only county and township deputies are elected directly. There is no voting from abroad for national offices, which are filled by indirect election.

Last turnout: No national vote. Official turnout in the 2021-2022 county-level people's congress elections: 86.49% of 1.064 billion registered voters (85.63% at township level) source

Who decides what

Communist PartyThe Chinese Communist Party (101.3 million members at the end of 2025) leads the state. Its National Congress meets every five years and elects a Central Committee, which elects the Politburo, its Standing Committee and the General Secretary, Xi Jinping since 2012. Party committees sit alongside every ministry, province, court, army unit and large company.
State: President and State CouncilThe President is elected by the National People's Congress, with no term limit since 2018; Xi Jinping holds the post alongside that of Party leader. The State Council, the government, is led by Premier Li Qiang since March 2023 and presents an annual work report with growth and jobs targets.
National People's Congress (NPC)The legislature has 2,977 deputies, elected indirectly by provincial congresses for five years, and meets for about a week each March. Its Standing Committee of about 175 members meets every two months and passes most laws. Freedom House describes its role as a 'rubber stamp'; the state media describe 'whole-process people's democracy'.
CPPCC and the eight partiesThe Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference is an advisory body that meets at the same time as the NPC. It brings together the Communist Party, the eight legally recognised 'democratic parties', mass organisations and personalities; non-Communists held 60.2% of its national seats in 2018. It gives opinions but does not vote laws.
Army (PLA) and courtsThe People's Liberation Army answers to the Party's Central Military Commission, chaired by Xi Jinping. Both vice-chairs chosen in 2022 were later expelled from the Party (He Weidong in 2025, Zhang Youxia in 2026). Courts are supervised by Party political-legal committees; Freedom House rates judicial independence 1 out of 4.

How people vote

  • Citizens vote directly only for county and township people's congresses, every five years. In the 2021-2022 round, 86.49% of registered voters took part at county level and 85.63% at township level, electing 2.63 million deputies (official figures). Independent candidates may stand with 10 supporters' signatures, but Freedom House reports they are often kept off the ballot.
  • Each higher congress, up to the NPC, is elected by the one below it; the Communist Party vets the candidates.
  • The NPC elects the President, the Premier (on the President's nomination) and the heads of the courts; the leadership is decided in advance inside the Party. In March 2023 Xi Jinping was re-elected President by 2,952 votes to 0.

How a law is passed

  1. Drafts come mainly from the State Council, NPC committees or Party proposals; the Party approves the legislative plan and must sign off on major laws.
  2. The NPC Standing Committee generally reviews a draft three times; between readings it is published for public comment for at least 30 days (a 2023 draft on public security penalties drew over 109,900 suggestions).
  3. Basic laws and constitutional amendments go to the full NPC in March; amendments need a two-thirds majority.
  4. The President promulgates the law. There is no constitutional court: the NPC Standing Committee itself interprets the Constitution and the laws, including Hong Kong's Basic Law.

Why it's complicated

China has no free national election, and this page cannot rank parties by votes. The Constitution names the Communist Party's leadership as the defining feature of the system. Eight smaller 'democratic parties' are legal, but the State Council's 2021 white paper states that they are 'not opposition parties' and 'voluntarily accept' Communist leadership; they are summarised here in a single card. Freedom House scores China 9 out of 100 (Not Free); courts are supervised by the Party, so this page notes when a rights body considers a conviction political: Human Rights Watch called the 2023 subversion sentences of lawyers Xu Zhiyong (14 years) and Ding Jiaxi (12 years) unfounded, and Amnesty called citizen journalist Zhang Zhan's second four-year sentence (2025) baseless. In 2022 the UN Human Rights Office found that detentions in Xinjiang 'may constitute international crimes, in particular crimes against humanity'; China rejects the findings. The Party's own disciplinary system is busy too: since 2023 two defence ministers and a farm minister have received suspended death sentences, and both army vice-chairs picked in 2022 were expelled. The official charges are bribery and, for the generals, disloyalty; some analysts read the military purges as political. Hong Kong and Macau have separate systems and are not covered here.

Why it's like thisThe People's Republic was founded in 1949 by the Communist Party after a civil war, and the eight democratic parties, which had sided with it, joined the new consultative system. Under Mao Zedong, campaigns such as the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution concentrated power in one man. From 1978 Deng Xiaoping launched market reforms, and the 1982 Constitution limited the President to two terms to prevent another personal rule; the 1989 Tiananmen protests were put down by the army. Since 2012 Xi Jinping has centralised power again, with a sweeping anti-corruption campaign and the 2018 removal of the presidential term limit. Supporters credit the system with stability and with lifting close to 800 million people out of extreme poverty; critics point to the absence of checks on the leadership.

Who's at the wheel

Xi Jinping, General Secretary of the Communist Party since 2012, President since 2013 and chairman of the Central Military Commission, all in their third term; Premier Li Qiang heads the State Council since March 2023.

The 20th Party Congress (October 2022) renewed Xi Jinping as Party leader; in March 2023 the NPC elected him President by 2,952 votes to 0 and approved the new government. In October 2026 the Central Committee met for its fifth plenary session (26-29 October) on 'full and rigorous Party self-governance'.

The audience rating

No free polling exists. The most cited academic survey, by Harvard's Ash Center (eight waves, 2003-2016, about 32,000 face-to-face interviews through a Chinese firm), found 95.5% 'relatively' or 'highly' satisfied with the central government in 2016, but only 11.3% 'very satisfied' with township government; its authors warn that polling is heavily scrutinised, that migrant workers are excluded and that censorship may shape answers. It is dated and foreign, so the gauge stays empty.

Not placed on the left-right axisIn a single-party system with no competitive election, expert surveys do not place parties on a left-right scale. The Party's doctrine (Marxism-Leninism, 'socialism with Chinese characteristics') and its economic record (markets, a large private sector) point in different directions, so we give it no position and no 'far left' or 'far right' label.

On the poster
  • Serve the people wholeheartedly; Marxism-Leninism and socialism with Chinese characteristics
  • 'Whole-process people's democracy'
  • Common prosperity and the end of poverty
  • Full and rigorous Party self-governance, zero tolerance for corruption
On the record
  • In 2018 the People's Daily explained that removing the two-term limit for the President 'does not mean life tenure'; the amendment passed 2,958 to 2, and in 2023 Xi Jinping began a third term, elected 2,952 to 0. source
  • Promised Hong Kong a 'high degree of autonomy' under 'one country, two systems'; in 2020 the NPC Standing Committee itself wrote Hong Kong's National Security Law, passed 162 to 0, under which publisher Jimmy Lai was sentenced to 20 years in 2026. Human Rights Watch calls the charges baseless and asks for his release. source
  • The Constitution proclaims the equality and unity of all ethnic groups; in 2022 the UN Human Rights Office found that the detention of Uyghurs and other Muslims in Xinjiang 'may constitute international crimes, in particular crimes against humanity'. China rejects the findings. source
To be fair
  • Close to 800 million fewer people living on under $1.90 a day over 40 years, about three-quarters of the world's reduction in extreme poverty (World Bank, 2022)
  • Life expectancy reached 79.25 years in 2025, 1.32 years more than in 2020 (National Health Commission)
  • Wind and solar capacity passed 1.2 billion kW in July 2024, about six and a half years ahead of schedule; solar has since overtaken coal as the largest power source (National Energy Administration; State Council white paper, 2026)

Who picks up the bill

Members pay dues of 0.5% to 2% of their monthly after-tax pay (0.5% to 1% for pensioners). With 101.3 million members at the end of 2025 this is a large income, but the Party publishes no audited accounts, no total of dues and no breakdown of other resources, and no outside body audits its finances.
