Next vote50 daysInauguration of the re-elected president: 5 January 2027
The general election has just taken place: Lula (PT) won the runoff of 25 October 2026 with 51.79% of valid votes against Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), after trailing him in the first round on 4 October (45.16% to 47.03%). Since Constitutional Amendment 111 of 2021, the president takes office on 5 January instead of 1 January, and governors on 6 January. Winners must be formally certified (diplomação) by 18 December; the newly elected Congress starts in February 2027. The next national vote is the general election of October 2030 (municipal elections take place in 2028). source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

Who governs in Brazil?

Who's at the wheel

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT) since 1 January 2023, with Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin (PSB). Re-elected on 25 October 2026, he starts a fourth term on 5 January 2027.

Lula beat Jair Bolsonaro in 2022 with 50.90% of the vote and governed with a broad coalition that gave ministries to centre and centre-right parties (MDB, PSD, União Brasil, PP, Republicanos). União Brasil and PP formally left the government in September 2025. In 2026 he was re-elected with a record 64.3 million votes (51.79%).

Who holds power

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT) since 1 January 2023, with Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin (PSB). Re-elected on 25 October 2026, he starts a fourth term on 5 January 2027.

In government: 85 absolute majority: 257

The parties in government hold 85 of 513 seats; an absolute majority is 257. In a presidential system the government does not need the chamber's confidence to stay in office, but to pass laws it needs 172 more votes from other groups. With the parties that support it (79 seats), the total is 164.

The chamber shown is the one elected on 4 October 2026, which takes office in February 2027; the outgoing chamber sits until then. “Other parties” groups the parties without a card, including the PT's federation partners PCdoB and PV.

What the government promised

  • Lula's 2026 programme: end the 6-day working week and cut it to 40 hours with no pay cut, keep Bolsa Família and real minimum-wage rises, keep the fiscal framework, create a Ministry of Public Security, expand Mais Médicos and full-time schools
  • Law signed on 26 November 2025: no income tax on earnings up to R$5,000 a month from 2026, paid for by a minimum tax on very high incomes (a 2022 campaign pledge)

What independent promise trackers found

  • Folha de S.Paulo's count of 103 campaign promises (independent), at mid-term in January 2025: 28% fulfilled, 29% in progress, 25% moving slowly and 17% stalled. No later independent count was found. source

The results, as measured by independent bodies

Findings from independent public research bodies. No label, no verdict: the figures speak.

  • IBGE: unemployment at 5.2% in July-September 2026, the lowest for that quarter since the series began in 2012, with a record 103.6 million people in work. source
  • IBGE: consumer prices (IPCA) up 4.58% over 12 months to September 2026, above the target band; September alone rose 0.82%. source
  • Central Bank: general government gross debt at 82.9% of GDP in August 2026 (82.6% in July); the record was 87.6% in December 2020. source
