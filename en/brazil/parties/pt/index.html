Next vote50 daysInauguration of the re-elected president: 5 January 2027
The general election has just taken place: Lula (PT) won the runoff of 25 October 2026 with 51.79% of valid votes against Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), after trailing him in the first round on 4 October (45.16% to 47.03%). Since Constitutional Amendment 111 of 2021, the president takes office on 5 January instead of 1 January, and governors on 6 January. Winners must be formally certified (diplomação) by 18 December; the newly elected Congress starts in February 2027. The next national vote is the general election of October 2030 (municipal elections take place in 2028). source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterEnd hunger
DeliveredBrazil, back on the UN Hunger Map in 2018-2020, left it in FAO's 2025 report (undernourishment below 2.5% in 2022-2024) and stayed off in 2026. For 2023-2025 moderate or severe food insecurity fell to 9.6% of the population, from 13.3% in 2022-2024 and 22.1% in 2020-2022, and severe food insecurity to 0.6%, the lowest in the series (FAO, SOFI 2026). source · 2
On the posterJobs
DeliveredUnemployment fell to 5.2% in July-September 2026, the lowest for that quarter since IBGE's series began in 2012, with a record 103.6 million people in work (IBGE, Pnad Contínua). source
On the posterTax justice: no income tax up to R$5,000 a month, the richest pay more
Half-wayThe bill exempting monthly incomes up to R$5,000, funded by a tax of up to 10% on incomes above R$600,000 a year, passed the Chamber 493 to 0 on 1 October 2025 and applies from 2026 (estimated cost R$25.8bn in 2026). No independent measure of its effect has been published yet. source
On the posterEnd the 'secret budget' of parliamentary amendments (2022 campaign)
Half-wayIn 2025 its government paid a record R$31.5bn in amendments chosen by members of Congress. Most of them were amendments the government must pay; R$5.3bn were committee amendments, which are not mandatory, and it paid 47.4% of those (Siop data). source
On the posterKeep public accounts within the 2023 fiscal framework
Half-wayIts government designed the 2023 fiscal framework and met its 2025 zero-deficit target (tolerance 0.25% of GDP) with a deficit of R$13.0bn, about 0.1% of GDP, after excluding R$48.7bn of spending such as court-ordered debts; the full deficit was R$61.7bn, 0.48% of GDP. source
Also worth knowing
  • On 25 October 2022 Lula wrote: 'If elected, I will be a one-term president.' In June 2026 he said he was running again 'to ensure that Brazil remains a democratic country', and he won a fourth term. source
  • At mid-term (January 2025), Folha de S.Paulo found that only 28% of Lula's 103 campaign promises had been fulfilled and 17% were stalled; reversing the Eletrobras privatisation had not gone beyond a dispute over board seats. source
  • Campaigned in 2022 against the 'secret budget' of parliamentary amendments; in 2025 its government paid a record R$31.5bn in amendments chosen by members of Congress, R$5.3bn of them committee amendments it was not obliged to pay. source
To be fair
  • Passed the income-tax exemption for earnings up to R$5,000 a month, funded by a minimum tax on very high incomes (law of November 2025)
  • Unemployment fell to 5.2% in July-September 2026, the lowest for that quarter since IBGE's series began in 2012
  • Lula won 64.3 million votes in the 2026 runoff, a record for a Brazilian presidential election

More broken than kept.

A sample of promises, one by one
  • Kept on paper No income tax on earnings up to R$5,000 a month (2022 pledge): law of November 2025, in force since January 2026; so far only advance estimates (about 10 million people fully exempt), no independent measure of its effect yet source
  • Partly kept Review the 2022 privatisation of Eletrobras: a 2025 settlement gave the government 3 of 10 board seats, without reversing the sale source
  • Broken Lula as 'a one-term president' (October 2022) source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for PT, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

Its leader promised a single term. He has just won his fourth, to keep Brazil 'a democratic country', he says.

It denounced the 'secret budget'. Its government then paid Congress a record R$31.5bn in amendments, most of them mandatory, R$5.3bn not.

It met its 2025 zero-deficit target, once R$48.7bn of spending had been left out of the count. The R$5,000 income-tax exemption is in force, but its effect has not been measured yet.

To be fair, Brazil is off the UN Hunger Map, severe food insecurity is down to 0.6%, the lowest on record, and unemployment to 5.2%.
