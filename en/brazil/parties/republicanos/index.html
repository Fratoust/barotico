Next vote50 daysInauguration of the re-elected president: 5 January 2027
The general election has just taken place: Lula (PT) won the runoff of 25 October 2026 with 51.79% of valid votes against Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), after trailing him in the first round on 4 October (45.16% to 47.03%). Since Constitutional Amendment 111 of 2021, the president takes office on 5 January instead of 1 January, and governors on 6 January. Winners must be formally certified (diplomação) by 18 December; the newly elected Congress starts in February 2027. The next national vote is the general election of October 2030 (municipal elections take place in 2028). source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

On the posterPublic security
Half-waySão Paulo, governed by its Tarcísio de Freitas since 2023, had the country's lowest homicide rate in 2024, 6.6 per 100,000 (Atlas da Violência 2026). Killings by the state's military police rose 69.8% in the same period, from 457 in 2023 to 776 in 2024 (SSP-SP data). source · 2
On the posterLegal certainty for rural landowners: indigenous lands limited to those occupied in 1988
⚠ Rights alertOn 14 December 2023 its deputies voted 31 to 3 to override Lula's veto and restore the 'time frame' in Law 14,701: indigenous peoples could claim only lands they occupied on 5 October 1988. The party then held a ministry in his government. On 19 December 2025 the Supreme Court struck the time frame down as contrary to indigenous peoples' constitutional rights to their traditional lands. No party statement contesting the ruling was found. source · 2 · 3
On the posterA market economy with social programmes
⚠ Rights alertUnder its former name, PRB, its deputies voted 15 to 4 for the 2017 labour reform (Law 13,467) on 26 April 2017. On 20 October 2021 the Supreme Court struck down the clauses making workers with free legal aid pay expert and lawyers' fees when they lose a case, as contrary to the constitutional right of access to justice and free legal assistance (art. 5, LXXIV). The rest of the reform stands. No party statement contesting the ruling was found. source · 2 · 3
Also worth knowing

  • Its deputies voted almost unanimously in September 2025 for the 'Shield PEC', which would have made it harder to arrest or prosecute members of Congress; the Senate buried it. source
  • After signalling neutrality in the presidential race, it joined Flávio Bolsonaro's runoff coalition in October 2026. source

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for Republicanos, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It kept a minister in Lula's cabinet. Its deputies, meanwhile, voted 32 to 2 against Lula's line on the Dosimetria bill, and backed the Shield PEC almost to a man.

Its runoff choice lost, so the programme it backed stays untested.

In São Paulo, which it governs, killings by the military police rose 69.8% in 2024, to 776.

To be fair, São Paulo, which it has governed since 2023, had the country's lowest homicide rate in 2024: 6.6 per 100,000 (Atlas da Violência).
