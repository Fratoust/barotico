Brazil

Federal presidential republic · 213.4 million people (IBGE, 2025)

Before you read
  • The approach: we set what parties say against what they do, and every fact links to an official or independent source.
  • Neutrality: the same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico recommends no vote.
  • A summary, not the whole story: read the programmes, follow several media, and make up your own mind.
  • Dates: the date at the top is the profile's last edit. Each section gives its own source and, where it applies, the date of the data or of the last check; in “Watch list”, some dates are upcoming deadlines.
Next vote50 daysInauguration of the re-elected president: 5 January 2027
The general election has just taken place: Lula (PT) won the runoff of 25 October 2026 with 51.79% of valid votes against Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), after trailing him in the first round on 4 October (45.16% to 47.03%). Since Constitutional Amendment 111 of 2021, the president takes office on 5 January instead of 1 January, and governors on 6 January. Winners must be formally certified (diplomação) by 18 December; the newly elected Congress starts in February 2027. The next national vote is the general election of October 2030 (municipal elections take place in 2028). source

Reading codes

Red: alarming. Green: reassuring. Amber: half-way. Grey: neutral or consistent.

Promise statuses

  • Delivered done, with a measured effect for people
  • Half-way partly done, diluted, or with mixed results
  • Consistent in opposition, the party voted for its promise but was outvoted
  • Contradiction the party acted against what it claims, established by at least three sources
  • Rights alert an independent authority found the measure breached fundamental rights

⚠ Democratic watch point (in red, on party cards): a programme promise that would restrict a democratic guarantee (free elections, independent courts, freedoms, equality before the law, checks and balances, personal rights) and that an independent authority has found contrary to fundamental rights. Political goals are never flagged, only the mechanism.

The same rules apply to every party, left or right, in government or in opposition. Barotico does not recommend any vote.

That government met its 2025 zero-deficit target (tolerance 0.25% of GDP) with a deficit of R$13.0bn, about 0.1% of GDP, after excluding R$48.7bn of spending such as court-ordered debts; the full deficit was R$61.7bn, 0.48% of GDP. source · 2
On the posterLegal certainty for rural landowners: indigenous lands limited to those occupied in 1988
⚠ Rights alertOn 14 December 2023 its deputies voted 47 to 4 to override Lula's veto and restore the 'time frame' in Law 14,701: indigenous peoples could claim only lands they occupied on 5 October 1988. The party then held ministries in his government. On 19 December 2025 the Supreme Court struck the time frame down as contrary to indigenous peoples' constitutional rights to their traditional lands. No party statement contesting the ruling was found. source · 2 · 3
On the posterA market economy and a leaner state
⚠ Rights alertIts predecessor DEM, which merged with the PSL in 2022 to form União Brasil, voted 29 to 0 for the 2017 labour reform (Law 13,467) on 26 April 2017. On 20 October 2021 the Supreme Court struck down the clauses making workers with free legal aid pay expert and lawyers' fees when they lose a case, as contrary to the constitutional right of access to justice and free legal assistance (art. 5, LXXIV). The rest of the reform stands. No party statement contesting the ruling was found. source · 2 · 3

3 to 4 concrete promises from its latest programme, each checked against an official act or an independent tracker. “Made a difference” needs an independent measure of the effect on people; a promise adopted without measurable effect, symbolic or quickly reversed counts as “on paper” (a quarter of a point). “Partly” also covers a promise the party pushed but others blocked. A promise that needed power the party never held cannot be tested.

If you're considering voting for União Brasil, it's worth looking beyond the slogans.

It ran ministries for Lula, then left in 2025, threatening to punish anyone who stayed.

While still in his government, its deputies voted 47 to 4 to limit indigenous lands to those occupied in 1988, a rule the Supreme Court struck down in 2025.

It tried neutrality, then joined Flávio Bolsonaro for the runoff, so the programme it backed remains untested.

To be fair, homicides in Goiás fell 43% between 2019 and 2024 (Atlas da Violência) under governor Ronaldo Caiado, who took that record with him to the PSD in January 2026.
